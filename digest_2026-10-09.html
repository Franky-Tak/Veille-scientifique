Aldosterone, the brain, and the blood pressure ceiling: a neurological case for aldosterone synthase inhibitors in resistant hypertension.

Revue : American journal of hypertension | Tier : 3 | IF estime : ~3-5

Date de publication : 2026-10-01

Resultats principaux : This narrative review evaluates the neurological consequences of hypertension and the therapeutic potential of aldosterone synthase inhibitors (ASIs) in reducing cerebrovascular risk. The review covers large population data showing hypertension to be the leading modifiable risk factor for stroke, contributing to cognitive impairment through endothelial dysfunction, blood-brain barrier disruption, small vessel disease, and accelerated atherosclerosis. A blood pressure target of less than 130/80 mmHg is strongly endorsed for both primary and secondary stroke prevention, with evidence associating controlled BP with reduced risk of recurrent stroke and cognitive decline. The renin-angiotensin-aldosterone system (RAAS) is highlighted as central to hypertension-related cerebrovascular injury, with aldosterone excess specifically linked to vascular inflammation, oxidative stress, and impaired cerebral autoregulation. Phase 2 and 3 clinical trials of ASIs such as lorundrostat and baxdrostat show significant systolic BP reductions of 5-15 mmHg with acceptable safety profiles in patients with uncontrolled or resistant hypertension, though direct randomized evidence on stroke or dementia endpoints is currently lacking.

Implications Medical Affairs : This review positions ASIs as a compelling emerging drug class with potential dual benefit in hypertension management and neuroprotection, offering Medical Affairs a strong scientific narrative for KOL engagement at the intersection of cardiology and neurology. The absence of dedicated hard-outcome trials for stroke and dementia represents a significant evidence gap that could justify Medical Affairs-sponsored investigator-initiated studies or scientific exchange programs with academic neurologists. From a positioning standpoint, the ASI class can be differentiated from mineralocorticoid receptor antagonists (MRAs) by its upstream mechanism targeting aldosterone synthesis, which could be leveraged in personalized phenotype-driven treatment algorithms for resistant hypertension with suspected aldosterone excess.

Revue : American journal of hypertension | Tier : 3 | IF estime : ~3-5

Date de publication : 2026-10-01

Resultats principaux : This comprehensive review evaluates the role of aldosterone dysregulation in hypertension complicating chronic kidney disease (CKD), highlighting that global CKD prevalence is approximately 10% with estimates of 14.4% in North America, and that hypertension is uncontrolled in 60-80% of CKD patients. Resistant hypertension is estimated at 40-50% in CKD populations, far exceeding general population rates, underscoring an unmet need. The established four-pillar approach using RAAS blockade (ACE inhibitors or ARBs), SGLT-2 inhibitors, non-steroidal mineralocorticoid receptor antagonists (nsMRAs), and GLP-1 agonists is well characterized, but aldosterone dysregulation emerging as a key unaddressed driver is discussed. Phase 3 randomized controlled trials of ASIs including baxdrostat and lorundrostat demonstrated substantial office and ambulatory blood pressure reductions with acceptable safety profiles in patients with hypertension and CKD. Early phase 2 trial data additionally suggest a reduction in albuminuria, hinting at potential reno-protective properties that would require confirmation in dedicated endpoint trials.

Implications Medical Affairs : The framing of aldosterone synthase inhibition as a fifth therapeutic pillar in CKD offers Medical Affairs a differentiated scientific message positioned at the nexus of nephrology and hypertension, potentially expanding addressable patient populations beyond the classical resistant hypertension label. The early albuminuria reduction signal from phase 2 trials is a pivotal biomarker finding that, if confirmed in outcome trials, could enable a reno-protective claim and significantly alter guideline positioning alongside established RAAS-blocking agents. Medical Affairs teams should proactively develop scientific exchange programs with nephrology KOLs and prepare evidence summaries highlighting the unmet need in resistant hypertension-CKD, anticipating the competitive launch environment following baxdrostat FDA approval in May 2026.

Hypertension Trajectories and Frailty Among Middle-Aged and Older Chinese Adults: A CHARLS Cohort Study.

Revue : Geriatrics & gerontology international | Tier : NA | IF estime : NA

Date de publication : 2026-10

Resultats principaux : This longitudinal cohort study analyzed 6,770 participants from the China Health and Retirement Longitudinal Study (CHARLS) across four waves to assess the association between hypertension trajectories and frailty index (FI) in middle-aged and older Chinese adults. Hypertension trajectories were classified into four patterns: persistent no hypertension, incident/progressive hypertension, remission/fluctuation hypertension, and persistent hypertension. Compared to persistent normotension, all three hypertensive trajectory groups were independently associated with higher frailty index scores: incident/progressive hypertension (beta 0.013, 95% CI 0.006-0.020), remission/fluctuation (beta 0.009, 95% CI 0.003-0.015), and persistent hypertension (beta 0.008, 95% CI 0.002-0.014). Higher baseline systolic blood pressure was positively associated with FI, with nonlinear dose-response characteristics identified by restricted cubic spline analyses. Mediation analyses suggested that cystatin C and C-reactive protein partly mediated the persistent hypertension-frailty association, implicating inflammatory and renal pathways as mechanistic links.

Implications Medical Affairs : This study provides epidemiological evidence that dynamic hypertension control patterns, not just blood pressure levels at a single time point, independently predict frailty burden in a large Asian elderly cohort, supporting Medical Affairs narratives that emphasize the long-term benefits of early and sustained blood pressure control. The mediation role of inflammatory markers and renal biomarkers suggests that antihypertensive regimens with anti-inflammatory or reno-protective properties may confer additional benefits beyond BP reduction in preventing frailty, a clinically relevant endpoint increasingly prioritized by regulatory agencies. Medical Affairs can use these findings to support KOL engagement around the concept of hypertension as a driver of multimorbidity in aging populations, promoting holistic treatment goals aligned with geriatric cardiovascular medicine.

Aldosterone synthase inhibitors may alter how primary care treats hypertension.

Revue : American journal of hypertension | Tier : 3 | IF estime : ~3-5

Date de publication : 2026-10-01

Resultats principaux : This expert commentary examines how aldosterone synthase inhibitors (ASIs) could transform primary care management of hypertension by addressing the diagnostic and therapeutic gap in primary aldosteronism (PA). The author highlights that PA testing remains infrequent in primary care despite its recognized prevalence, largely due to the perceived complexity of interpreting the aldosterone-renin ratio. The paper proposes an innovative concept of an ASI Challenge using a single agent such as lorundrostat or baxdrostat: if systolic blood pressure drops by more than 10 mmHg, it suggests aldosterone as a primary driver warranting formal PA workup, while a drop of less than 10 mmHg suggests limited aldosterone contribution. The same agent would serve dual diagnostic and therapeutic purposes, streamlining the workup in primary care without requiring complex biochemical testing infrastructure. Phase 3 trial evidence demonstrates that ASIs typically produce 5-15 mmHg reductions in systolic blood pressure, with main safety concerns including hyperkalemia, hyponatremia, and transient reductions in eGFR.

Implications Medical Affairs : The ASI Challenge concept represents a novel diagnostic utility for the drug class that, if validated prospectively, could dramatically increase the rate of PA identification in primary care and position ASIs as both diagnostic and therapeutic tools, a commercially differentiated strategy that Medical Affairs can actively communicate to primary care physician KOLs. Medical Affairs should engage with health technology assessment (HTA) bodies early to propose this dual diagnostic-therapeutic paradigm as a value-based healthcare model, potentially influencing reimbursement frameworks ahead of broader market access. The proposal to use ASIs for population-level screening of aldosterone-driven hypertension aligns with the strategic interest in identifying and treating resistant and secondary hypertension earlier, which could expand the treatable patient population and support market positioning.

Revue : American journal of hypertension | Tier : 3 | IF estime : ~3-5

Date de publication : 2026-10-01

Resultats principaux : This review proposes an endocrinological framework repositioning chronic hypertension, including resistant hypertension, as a consequence of dysregulated hormonal control of renal sodium handling mediated by renin-angiotensin II activity, inappropriate aldosterone excess, or both. The author argues against the traditional binary distinction between essential and secondary hypertension, replacing it with a mechanistic continuum characterized by biochemical phenotyping using renin and aldosterone profiling. Primary aldosteronism is described as existing across a broader severity spectrum than classical diagnostic thresholds define, with subclinical aldosterone dysregulation conferring cardiorenal outcomes worse than renin-dependent hypertension. Second-generation ASIs, including baxdrostat and lorundrostat, directly suppress aldosterone production and mirror the biochemical response seen after surgical adrenalectomy in PA patients, offering potential disease-modifying effects. The review concludes that integrating ASIs and MRAs into mechanism-first clinical algorithms driven by renin and aldosterone profiling could transform hypertension management from empirical add-on drug strategies toward individualized, pathophysiology-guided care.

Implications Medical Affairs : This conceptual reframing of hypertension as an aldosterone-driven endocrinopathy on a spectrum rather than a binary essential vs. secondary classification is scientifically compelling and provides Medical Affairs with a strong disease education platform to shift KOL thinking toward biochemical phenotyping in routine hypertension management. The comparison of ASI biochemical efficacy to surgical adrenalectomy outcomes is a powerful clinical anchor that Medical Affairs can utilize in scientific exchange meetings to communicate the depth of aldosterone suppression achievable with novel ASIs, differentiating them from MRAs. Establishing Medical Affairs-supported registries using renin-aldosterone phenotyping could generate real-world evidence to validate this mechanistic framework and support future guideline revisions incorporating biochemical testing into standard hypertension treatment algorithms.

Effects of Blood Pressure Lowering by Hematoma Location in Acute Intracerebral Hemorrhage.

Revue : Neurology | Tier : NA | IF estime : NA

Date de publication : 2026-10-08

Resultats principaux : This pooled analysis of individual patient-level data from five international randomized controlled trials (INTERACT1, INTERACT2, INTERACT3, INTERACT4, and ATACH-II) included 11,288 patients with acute intracerebral hemorrhage (ICH) to examine whether hematoma location modifies the treatment effect of intensive blood pressure lowering on functional outcome. Patient characteristics showed 35.9% women, mean age 62.5 years, with ICH locations of 81.5% deep hemisphere, 10.1% lobar, and 8.4% infratentorial. The primary outcome was death or disability (mRS 3-6) at 90 days. A statistically significant interaction between treatment and hematoma location was demonstrated (p for interaction = 0.033). Benefit from intensive BP lowering was observed exclusively in deep ICH (adjusted OR 0.81, 95% CI 0.74-0.89, p < 0.001), with no significant benefit in lobar or infratentorial ICH. This is the largest pooled individual patient data analysis to date on this specific subgroup question.

Implications Medical Affairs : This landmark pooled analysis provides precision medicine evidence that intensive blood pressure lowering in acute ICH is beneficial only in patients with deep hematomas, which has immediate implications for guideline updates and clinical decision-making that Medical Affairs should communicate proactively to neurology and emergency medicine KOLs. From a product positioning perspective, this finding reinforces the relevance of rapid blood pressure control agents in the acute stroke care setting, particularly for patients presenting with deep ICH, and supports the development of acute-use antihypertensive regimens optimized for this population. Medical Affairs teams engaged in neurology should monitor the clinical guideline revision processes at AHA/ASA and ESO for incorporation of this subgroup finding, which could create a new precision indication for acute BP management in deep ICH.

Date de publication : 2026-09-07

Resultats principaux : This nationwide prospective cohort study analyzed 12,607 elderly participants (mean age 86.81 years) from 2008 to 2018 to assess the association between indoor PM2.5 and its chemical constituents with all-cause mortality. Using time-varying Cox proportional hazards models, PM2.5 overall was associated with 0.3% higher mortality risk per 1 microg/m3 increase (HR 1.003, 95% CI 1.001-1.005).
