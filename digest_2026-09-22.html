Short-Term 24-h Systolic Blood Pressure Variability and Subclinical Target-Organ Damage in White-Coat Hypertension: A Retrospective Cross-Sectional Study.

Revue : Vascular Health and Risk Management | Tier : NA | IF estimé : NA

Date de publication : 2026-09-16

Résultats principaux : This single-center retrospective cross-sectional study enrolled 267 hospitalized participants (91 normotensive, 93 white-coat hypertension [WCH], 83 mild-to-moderate hypertensive) who underwent ambulatory blood pressure monitoring between 2012 and 2021 at a hospital in Guangxi, China. Primary endpoints were subclinical target-organ damage (TOD) markers — left ventricular hypertrophy (LVH), left ventricular mass index (LVMI), and microalbuminuria (MA) — compared across groups and in relation to 24-hour systolic blood pressure (SBP) variability within the WCH subgroup. Any subclinical TOD occurred in 26.4%, 52.7%, and 71.1% of normotensive, WCH, and hypertensive participants respectively (P<0.001), confirming WCH occupies an intermediate risk position. Within WCH patients, continuous 24-hour SBP variability was independently associated with any TOD (OR 1.25, 95% CI 1.05–1.52), LVH (OR 1.30, 95% CI 1.08–1.60), higher LVMI (beta = 2.23 g/m, 95% CI 0.33–4.12), and log-transformed microalbuminuria (beta = 0.105, 95% CI 0.052–0.157) after adjusting for 24-hour mean SBP and baseline clinical factors. Adding 24-hour SBP variability to a base model improved the area under the ROC curve from 0.613 to 0.678 (likelihood-ratio P=0.009), although the single-center retrospective cross-sectional design precludes causal inference and prospective multicenter validation is required.

Implications Medical Affairs : These findings support incorporating blood pressure variability assessments in the evaluation of WCH patients to stratify subclinical organ damage risk, which may influence future guideline recommendations on ambulatory monitoring intensity. For pharmaceutical companies with antihypertensive agents that reduce BP variability (notably calcium channel blockers), this represents a potential differentiating efficacy signal worth exploring prospectively in WCH populations. Medical affairs teams should engage ambulatory blood pressure monitoring KOLs to develop consensus statements on optimal management of high-variability WCH patients and to generate Phase IV evidence in this underserved subgroup.

Plan-related inequalities in costs, disease progression and mortality among hypertensive patients in China.

Revue : Nature Communications | Tier : NA | IF estimé : NA

Date de publication : 2026-08-21

Résultats principaux : This large-scale observational study analyzed 8,004,039 inpatient admissions among 4,675,738 hypertensive patients in Henan province, China (2019–2024), using mixed-effects, multistate, and survival models; external validation was performed in Xinjiang. Compared with self-pay patients, urban employee insurance was associated with 24.95% higher total inpatient expenditure, while all non-self-pay plans were associated with 89.47–97.56% lower out-of-pocket spending — a major financial protection benefit. Urban employee insurance showed the most favorable all-cause mortality (adjusted HR 0.72, 95% CI 0.68–0.76), while government-supported or medical assistance arrangements showed the poorest survival (HR 1.33, 95% CI 1.23–1.43) and the greatest expected time in multimorbidity states. Sensitivity analyses and external validation in Xinjiang supported the direction of the main non-mortality findings, strengthening internal consistency; however, the observed associations may reflect differences in benefit scope, care access, and participants' underlying health or social vulnerability rather than isolated payment-plan causal effects. The observational nature means confounding cannot be excluded, and the authors caution that narrowing reimbursement gaps alone may be insufficient without broader improvements in service coverage and healthcare access for vulnerable populations.

Implications Medical Affairs : The finding that government-subsidized insurance beneficiaries have the worst survival outcomes among hypertensive patients highlights critical equity gaps that could inform targeted pharmaceutical access programs and policy advocacy in China and similar healthcare systems. For Medical Affairs, this study underscores the importance of designing adherence support programs and access initiatives targeting the most vulnerable insurance segments, as market development strategies for antihypertensive agents should account for the heterogeneous outcomes across reimbursement tiers. KOL engagement should focus on health economists and healthcare policy experts to leverage these findings when advocating for improved government formulary inclusion and reimbursement of evidence-based antihypertensive therapies in government-supported plans.

Antihypertensive drug targets and pancreatitis reporting signals: An integrative Mendelian randomization and FAERS pharmacovigilance study.

Revue : Science Progress | Tier : NA | IF estimé : NA

Date de publication : 2026-09-21

Résultats principaux : This integrative genetic epidemiology and pharmacovigilance study applied linkage disequilibrium score regression, two-sample Mendelian randomization, summary-data-based Mendelian randomization, Bayesian colocalization, and FAERS disproportionality analysis to investigate 21 antihypertensive drug targets and their potential association with pancreatic exocrine diseases. GWAS summary statistics were predominantly derived from European-ancestry participants, with whole-blood cis-eQTLs used to genetically proxy drug-target expression, and FAERS reports from 2004 to 2023 analyzed for pancreatitis-related reporting signals. Among the 21 antihypertensive drug targets analyzed, ADRB2, CACNA1I, JUN, NR3C2, and SLC12A4 showed nominal associations with pancreatic outcomes; however, none remained statistically significant after Bonferroni correction, and absolute PP.H4 values did not support robust colocalization. Several antihypertensive agents also generated pancreatitis-related reporting signals in FAERS, though these signals cannot quantify incidence or establish causality given inherent pharmacovigilance database limitations. Key limitations include the predominantly European-ancestry genetic data limiting multi-ethnic applicability, the exploratory nature of all analyses, FAERS susceptibility to confounding and reporting biases, and the absence of prospective pharmacoepidemiological validation.

Implications Medical Affairs : While exploratory, these safety signals for selected antihypertensive targets (ADRB2 and CACNA1I) warrant continued monitoring by Medical Affairs pharmacovigilance teams to ensure updated safety communications remain informed by emerging genetic and spontaneous reporting evidence. The absence of Bonferroni-corrected significance and lack of robust colocalization are reassuring for most antihypertensive drug classes and can be cited in competitive positioning discussions; however, companies with ADRB2- or CACNA1I-targeting agents should proactively review their pharmacovigilance databases for pancreatitis signals. Medical Affairs should engage Mendelian randomization and pharmacoepidemiology KOLs to design adequately powered, multi-ethnic replication studies that can definitively confirm or refute these exploratory findings before they influence prescribing behavior.

Beta-blockers in cardiovascular medicine — current status: Where are there still 'strong' indications for beta-blockers?

Revue : Deutsche Medizinische Wochenschrift | Tier : NA | IF estimé : NA

Date de publication : 2026-09-21

Résultats principaux : This narrative review synthesizes current evidence and guideline recommendations on the clinical use of beta-blockers in cardiovascular medicine, with particular focus on their evolving and reduced role in arterial hypertension management. Until the last decade, beta-blockers were standard first-line therapy for uncomplicated hypertension; this recommendation was withdrawn after studies demonstrated consistently lower overall efficacy compared to renin-angiotensin-aldosterone system inhibitors, calcium channel blockers, and diuretics. Current cardiology guidelines retain class I (or equivalent) recommendations for beta-blockers specifically in symptomatic heart failure with reduced ejection fraction (HFrEF), angina pectoris, post-myocardial infarction management, atrial fibrillation rate control, and as an adjunct option in resistant hypertension. The review does not present original quantitative trial data but synthesizes existing meta-analytic and guideline evidence to define the remaining 'strong' indications where benefit clearly outweighs risk. As a narrative review without formal systematic methodology or quality assessment of underlying evidence, the article is subject to selection bias in the studies cited and may not capture all relevant emerging data.

Implications Medical Affairs : The demotion of beta-blockers from first-line hypertension therapy, as formally confirmed across European and North American guidelines, represents a significant competitive landscape shift that Medical Affairs teams should incorporate into comparative effectiveness messaging when positioning renin-angiotensin system inhibitors, calcium channel blockers, and diuretics as preferred first-line options. This review reinforces the continued niche value of beta-blockers in specific cardiovascular comorbidities such as HFrEF and post-MI settings, suggesting that Medical Affairs strategy for beta-blocker products should refocus on the cardiovascular disease continuum and combination therapy use cases rather than hypertension monotherapy. KOL engagement initiatives targeting German-speaking cardiologists and internists should address residual prescribing variation in beta-blocker use for uncomplicated hypertension, incorporating the updated guideline landscape into continued medical education programs.

Burden of non-communicable diseases and their attributable risk factors in Africa from 1990 to 2023: a systematic analysis for the Global Burden of Disease Study 2023.

Revue : The Lancet Global Health | Tier : NA | IF estimé : NA

Date de publication : 2026-09-21

Résultats principaux : This systematic analysis applied GBD 2023 methodology to estimate incidence, mortality, DALYs, and attributable risk factors for non-communicable diseases (NCDs) across all 54 African countries from 1990 to 2023, using data from vital registration, verbal autopsy, surveillance, and surveys. In 2023, NCDs in Africa accounted for an estimated 2.33 billion incident cases (95% UI 2.22–2.50 billion), 4.70 million deaths (4.47–4.93 million), and 263 million DALYs (234–293 million); while age-standardised rates declined modestly (mortality by 9.0%, DALY rate by 6.4%), absolute counts rose substantially due to population growth. The five leading causes of NCD death included hypertensive heart disease (246,000 deaths, 95% UI 170,000–337,000), and critically, high systolic blood pressure contributed the largest attributable proportion of all NCD deaths in Africa at 26.2% (21.6–30.9%) — substantially exceeding dietary risks (14.7%) and air pollution (14.2%). These findings reveal a demographic-driven paradox where declining age-standardised rates coexist with rising absolute NCD burdens, placing uncontrolled hypertension at the center of Africa's NCD crisis. Limitations include reliance on heterogeneous and sparse data sources across African countries, large uncertainty intervals reflecting data quality variability, and potential systematic biases in cause-of-death attribution in settings with limited vital registration.

Implications Medical Affairs : The identification of high systolic blood pressure as the single largest modifiable risk factor for NCD mortality in Africa — ahead of all dietary and environmental exposures — establishes a compelling scientific and public health rationale for prioritizing antihypertensive therapy as the cornerstone of Africa's NCD prevention strategy, representing a major market development opportunity. From a Medical Affairs perspective, these GBD 2023 findings should be leveraged in regulatory submissions, access program proposals, and policy advocacy to strengthen the case for improved antihypertensive drug reimbursement and formulary inclusion across African national health systems. KOL engagement initiatives should target African cardiologists, public health epidemiologists, and Ministry of Health officials to co-develop hypertension prevention roadmaps that incorporate pharmaceutical partnerships and access programs aligned with WHO NCD reduction targets for 2030.

Prevalence, associated factors, and machine learning classification of low back pain among postmenopausal women attending rehabilitation and physiotherapy facilities in Bangladesh.

Revue : PLoS ONE | Tier : NA | IF estimé : NA

Date de publication : 2026-09-21

Résultats principaux : This clinic-based cross-sectional study enrolled 566 postmenopausal women attending two specialized rehabilitation centers in Bangladesh to estimate the past-year prevalence of low back pain (LBP) and evaluate associated factors and machine learning classification performance. The past-year prevalence of LBP was 46.6% in this clinic-based sample; multivariable logistic regression identified lower LBP odds among women aged 51–57 years vs. 43–50 years (AOR 0.50, 95% CI 0.31–0.81), and higher odds among housewives (AOR 2.20, 95% CI 1.33–3.64), women with hypertension (AOR 1.60, 95% CI 1.09–2.35), and women with respiratory disease (AOR 2.46, 95% CI 1.28–4.71). SHAP analysis identified sunlight exposure, hypertension, and socioeconomic status as the most influential predictors in machine learning models, with the SVM-RBF classifier achieving the highest ROC-AUC of 0.890 on the held-out test set, followed by random forest (0.885). The independent association between hypertension and LBP after multivariable adjustment is notable, though the cross-sectional design and clinic-based sampling preclude causal inference and likely overestimate prevalence relative to the general population. External validation of the machine learning models in community-based and multi-center samples is required before broader clinical application can be considered.

Implications Medical Affairs : The significant association between hypertension and low back pain in postmenopausal women — even after adjustment for other comorbidities — adds to the growing evidence on multimorbidity clustering in this age group, suggesting that antihypertensive management programs should be integrated with musculoskeletal care pathways for women's health optimization. From a Medical Affairs perspective, this emerging evidence base creates a rationale for disease awareness campaigns targeting gynecologists, rehabilitation specialists, and primary care physicians in South Asian and other low- and middle-income country markets, where multimorbidity in postmenopausal women is frequently undertreated. KOL engagement should include women's health and geriatric medicine specialists to explore whether effective blood pressure control in postmenopausal women may independently reduce musculoskeletal disability risk — a hypothesis worth investigating in prospective studies.

Cardiovascular Disease in Complex Emergencies: A Neglected Burden and Evidence on Public Health Interventions From a Scoping Review.

Revue : Disaster Medicine and Public Health Preparedness | Tier : NA | IF estimé : NA

Date de publication : 2026-09-21

Résultats principaux : This scoping review searched PubMed, the Directory of Open Access Journals, ReliefWeb, and Google Scholar for studies published between 2014 and April 2024 examining cardiovascular disease prevention, management, or outcomes in complex emergency settings, ultimately including 31 studies. Evidence predominantly focused on hypertension management, with documented interventions targeting primary prevention (risk factor control, health workforce training, health system strengthening) and secondary prevention (screening, early diagnosis, treatment continuity). Common barriers to cardiovascular care in complex emergencies included disrupted care continuity, population displacement, attacks on health services, undertrained staff, medication supply interruptions, and inadequate humanitarian funding. The WHO Noncommunicable Diseases Kit showed mixed effectiveness depending on contextual adaptation and local health system capacity, and evidence on coronary artery disease management and cardiac rehabilitation (tertiary prevention) was notably limited. Key limitations include significant heterogeneity in study designs and settings, potential publication bias toward intervention successes, limited grey literature inclusion, and the evolving evidence base since the April 2024 search cut-off.

Implications Medical Affairs : The predominance of hypertension as the focus of cardiovascular evidence in complex emergency settings highlights a critical need for scalable, simplified antihypertensive regimens validated for resource-constrained environments — a differentiation opportunity for pharmaceutical companies offering affordable, stable, fixed-dose combination therapies with proven outcomes. From a Medical Affairs perspective, this evidence gap presents an opportunity to partner with humanitarian organizations (MSF, WHO, ICRC, UNHCR) to include specific antihypertensive agents in emergency NCD formularies, building both access programs and real-world evidence generation in crisis-affected populations. KOL engagement should target global health and humanitarian medicine specialists as well as WHO NCD emergency program leads, to co-develop clinical protocols addressing the unique management challenges of hypertension in conflict-affected and displaced populations.

Hypertensive Disorders of Pregnancy and Cardiovascular-Kidney-Metabolic Syndrome.

Revue : Hypertension | Tier : 2 | IF estimé : ~8.0

Date de publication : 2026-09-21

Résultats principaux : This study analyzed 219,639 parous UK Biobank participants (mean age 56.9 years) and 3,591 Women's Health Initiative (WHI) participants (mean age 61.7 years at baseline), examining the association between hypertensive disorders of pregnancy (HDP) history and cardiovascular-kidney-metabolic (CKM) syndrome staging. In the UK Biobank, 2,836 participants (1.3%) reported a history of HDP, and HDP was significantly associated with higher CKM stage in ordinal logistic regression (adjusted OR 3.40, 95% CI 3.12–3.71; P<0.001), with markedly elevated odds for CKM stages 2, 3, and 4 (adjusted ORs 6.34, 11.20, and 7.43 respectively; all P<0.001). Over a median follow-up of 13.6 years, prior HDP was associated with progression to advanced CKM stage 4 (adjusted HR 1.24, 95% CI 1.10–1.40; P<0.001), independent of baseline CKM stage, age, race, socioeconomic deprivation, and smoking status. Replication in the WHI cohort produced fully consistent results, strengthening the generalizability across two large, independent cohorts with different ascertainment methods. Limitations include the observational design with potential residual confounding, reliance on self-reported HDP history in the UK Biobank, and the predominantly White European populations in both cohorts, which may limit generalizability to other ethnic groups.

Implications Medical Affairs : The strong, consistent, and replicated association between HDP history and advanced CKM syndrome — with a 3.4-fold increase in CKM stage odds — establishes HDP as a clinically actionable early-life predictor of cardiometabolic risk that could expand the treatment-eligible population for early antihypertensive and cardiometabolic interventions. For Medical Affairs, this evidence strengthens the scientific narrative for proactive, long-term antihypertensive follow-up in women with prior HDP, supporting disease awareness campaigns targeting obstetricians and gynecologists as critical referral gateways and supporting label expansion discussions in the women's cardiovascular health space. Medical Affairs KOL strategy should prioritize collaboration with maternal-fetal medicine specialists, cardiologists, and nephrologists to develop integrated post-HDP cardiovascular screening protocols and prospective intervention trials evaluating whether early antihypertensive therapy mitigates CKM progression in this high-risk population.

Anti-inflammatory dietary patterns and cognitive aging in older adults: a scoping review of mechanistic, clinical, and nursing implications.

Revue : Frontiers in Nutrition | Tier : NA | IF estimé : NA

Date de publication : 2026-09-18

Résultats principaux : This scoping review searched PubMed, Embase, Web of Science Core Collection, and Scopus from inception to June 30, 2026, including 38 reports (27 longitudinal observational studies, 7 randomized intervention reports, 3 cross-sectional biomarker/neuroimaging studies) evaluating dietary patterns — including Mediterranean, DASH (Dietary Approaches to Stop Hypertension), MIND, and dietary inflammatory indices — in relation to cognitive aging outcomes. Mediterranean- and MIND-oriented dietary patterns and lower dietary inflammatory potential were recurrently associated with more favorable cognitive outcomes across observational studies, though findings varied across dietary definitions, populations, cognitive measures, and follow-up durations. Randomized evidence was mixed and non-definitive, and no study demonstrated conclusively that any specific anti-inflammatory diet prevents dementia. Fourteen reports measured inflammatory, proteomic, microbiome, neuroimaging, cerebrospinal fluid, or neuropathological outcomes, supporting selected biological links without establishing a complete diet-neuroinflammation-cognition pathway. Key limitations include heterogeneity in dietary exposure definitions, lack of standardized cognitive endpoints, predominantly observational evidence, and absence of tested nurse-led dietary implementation frameworks — meaning clinical translation remains premature.

Implications Medical Affairs : The prominent role of the DASH diet — originally developed to reduce blood pressure — in cognitive aging evidence creates a compelling dual-benefit narrative that Medical Affairs teams for antihypertensive companies can leverage in KOL engagement and patient education programs targeting the aging, multimorbid population. If future definitive trials confirm DASH-diet adherence as a cognitive-protective intervention, hypertension management guidelines could incorporate cognitive outcome endpoints, substantially strengthening the value proposition of antihypertensive therapies beyond blood pressure reduction and creating new evidence pillars for Medical Affairs communications. Medical Affairs should proactively engage neurology, geriatrics, and nutrition KOLs to co-develop research programs exploring shared mechanisms between dietary inflammation, blood pressure control, and cognitive trajectories, positioning the company as a scientific leader at the intersection of cardiometabolic and brain health.
