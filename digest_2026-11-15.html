Heart rate variability associated with cardiometabolic risk factors in Ghanaian adults with hypertension: A quantile regression analysis.

Revue : Clinical and Experimental Hypertension | Tier : Tier 3 | IF estime : 3-10

Date de publication : 2026-11-14

Resultats principaux : This cross-sectional study enrolled 223 hypertensive adults at Ejisu Government Hospital, Ghana, classified as obese (n=79, BMI>=30 kg/m2) or non-obese (n=144) and assessed short-term heart rate variability (HRV) using 5-minute resting ECG recordings. The primary endpoints were time-domain and frequency-domain HRV indices (SDNN, rMSSD, HF power, LF/HF ratio) analyzed via ordinary least squares, rank-based, and quantile regression. Quantile regression revealed marked distributional heterogeneity: diabetes was consistently and strongly associated with lower HRV across multiple indices, with the most pronounced effects at the lower quantiles of SDNN, rMSSD, and HF power distributions. BMI-defined obesity showed minimal independent association with HRV across quantiles, whereas high-risk waist circumference was selectively associated with lower rMSSD and HF power and a higher LF/HF ratio, particularly at upper-middle quantiles. Mean arterial pressure showed little evidence of association with HRV across the distribution. A notable limitation is the cross-sectional design at a single hospital, which precludes causal inference, and several findings related to central adiposity showed sensitivity to alternative covariate adjustments.

Implications Medical Affairs : This study underscores that standard mean-based analyses may systematically obscure the true relationship between cardiometabolic risk factors and cardiac autonomic function in hypertensive populations, particularly in sub-Saharan African settings where diabetes co-prevalence is high. For Medical Affairs, these data reinforce the value of multi-marker risk assessment combining glycemic status and central adiposity metrics rather than BMI alone in hypertensive patient profiling, with potential implications for positioning antihypertensive combinations that also target metabolic endpoints. The findings from Ghana expand the epidemiological evidence base for hypertension-diabetes comorbidity in Africa, a high-growth market segment relevant to KOL engagement and real-world evidence strategies in emerging markets.

ENO1-Related Signaling in Hypertension-Associated Atrial Remodeling: Evidence Implicating PI3K/AKT and NOX4.

Revue : Cells | Tier : NA | IF estime : NA

Date de publication : 2026-10-29

Resultats principaux : This mechanistic study investigated the role of alpha-enolase (ENO1) in hypertension-associated atrial remodeling and AF susceptibility using hypertensive rat models and human left atrial transcriptomic datasets. Long-term hypertension was associated with increased AF inducibility in rats, and atrial transcriptomic analyses identified persistent ENO1 upregulation at two stages of hypertension; ENO1 was also elevated in two independent human left atrial AF datasets. In an angiotensin II-stimulated neonatal rat atrial cardiomyocyte model, siRNA-mediated ENO1 knockdown reduced cardiomyocyte hypertrophy, oxidative stress, PI3K/AKT phosphorylation, and NADPH oxidase 4 (NOX4) expression. Treatment with the ENO1 inhibitor AP-III-a4 recapitulated these effects and, in angiotensin II-infused hypertensive mice, significantly reduced atrial enlargement, fibrosis, PI3K/AKT phosphorylation, NOX4 expression, atrial ROS levels, and atrial effective refractory period shortening. AP-III-a4 significantly shortened pacing-induced AF duration, though its effect on AF inducibility did not reach statistical significance. A key limitation is the translational gap between preclinical findings and clinical AF burden in humans with hypertension.

Implications Medical Affairs : This study identifies ENO1-PI3K/AKT-NOX4 as a hypertension-specific mechanistic axis driving atrial remodeling and AF susceptibility, representing a potential novel target for antiarrhythmic therapy in hypertensive patients who represent a large portion of AF cases. From a Medical Affairs perspective, these data are relevant for companies developing oxidative stress-modulating or atrial remodeling-targeted therapies for hypertension-associated AF, supporting preclinical proof-of-concept narratives for KOL scientific symposia. Future research should prioritize human biomarker validation of ENO1 and NOX4 in hypertensive AF patients to build the translational bridge necessary for IND-enabling studies.

Temporal patterns and adverse pregnancy outcomes of hypertensive disorders of pregnancy and gestational diabetes mellitus in Hebei Province, China, 2017-2023: a multicenter retrospective cohort study.

Revue : Frontiers in Public Health | Tier : NA | IF estime : NA

Date de publication : 2026-10-22

Resultats principaux : This multicenter retrospective cohort analyzed 264,278 singleton deliveries at >=28 gestational weeks across 31 hospitals in Hebei Province, China from 2017 to 2023 using the provincial Obstetric Critical Illness Monitoring System. Pregnancies were classified into HDP alone (4.43%), GDM alone (7.04%), coexisting HDP-GDM (0.81%), and unaffected (87.72%). The recorded prevalence of HDP alone rose substantially from 3.42% in 2017 to 7.60% in 2023, while GDM and coexisting HDP-GDM peaked in 2021 (13.21% and 1.39% respectively) before abruptly declining in 2022-2023. HDP alone showed the strongest associations with placental abruption, preterm delivery, small-for-gestational-age births, and neonatal asphyxia; GDM alone was associated with macrosomia and premature rupture of membranes; the combined HDP-GDM group displayed a mixed maternal-neonatal risk profile. Estimates for stillbirth and puerperal infection in the combined group were imprecise due to very low event counts. Key limitations include the ecological observational design, unavailability of important metabolic and behavioral covariates, and unresolved screening/ascertainment differences that preclude confirmation of a true population-level decline in GDM post-2021.

Implications Medical Affairs : The near-doubling of HDP prevalence over six years in a large northern Chinese population signals a significant and growing public health burden with implications for guideline development around maternal hypertension screening intensity and antihypertensive treatment initiation thresholds during pregnancy. For Medical Affairs, these data provide epidemiological context for companies with antihypertensive agents under study in pregnancy-related hypertension, supporting market access arguments around clinical need and patient burden in China, a priority pharmaceutical market. These findings also suggest a research priority for prospective studies clarifying the mechanistic link between HDP and GDM in Chinese populations, relevant for precision medicine positioning and biomarker-driven clinical development strategies.

Association of the uric acid to high-density lipoprotein cholesterol ratio with CVD mortality in Chinese hypertensive populations: a prospective cohort study.

Revue : BMC Endocrine Disorders | Tier : NA | IF estime : NA

Date de publication : 2026-08-19

Resultats principaux : This prospective cohort study analyzed 13,719 patients with hypertension from a large-scale population-based registry in China, followed for a median of 4.3 years to assess the association of the uric acid to HDL-cholesterol ratio (UHR) with all-cause and cardiovascular mortality. The study recorded 772 all-cause deaths and 378 CVD deaths; the mean baseline UHR was 12.53 +/- 5.34. Using Cox proportional hazards models with penalized splines and Kaplan-Meier analyses, higher UHR was significantly and independently associated with elevated all-cause mortality (HR quartile 4 vs. quartile 1 = 1.52, 95% CI: 1.15-2.01) and CVD mortality (HR = 2.54, 95% CI: 1.63-3.95). No statistically significant interactions were identified in any prespecified subgroup analysis, suggesting the association is robust across demographic strata. Limitations include the observational design, limited follow-up duration, and the registry-based nature of the data which may limit residual confounding control.

Implications Medical Affairs : The UHR emerges as a clinically actionable composite biomarker integrating hyperuricemia and dyslipidemia, both modifiable risk factors, that independently predicts CVD mortality with more than twice the hazard at the highest quartile in a large hypertensive Chinese population. From a Medical Affairs standpoint, the UHR may serve as a practical risk stratification tool applicable within standard laboratory panels without additional cost, relevant for guideline updates on hypertensive patient monitoring and potentially supporting product differentiation for antihypertensives that also reduce serum uric acid. These findings provide quantitative evidence to support KOL discussions on integrated metabolic-cardiovascular risk management in hypertension, particularly in Asian populations where hyperuricemia prevalence is high.

Effects of Omega-3 Supplementation on Inflammatory Biomarkers and Ambulatory and Central Blood Pressure in Patients Treated for Hypertension and Dyslipidemia: A Randomized, Double-Blind, Placebo-Controlled Trial.

Revue : Nutrients | Tier : NA | IF estime : NA

Date de publication : 2026-11-09

Resultats principaux : This exploratory randomized, double-blind, placebo-controlled pilot trial enrolled 100 adults with established hypertension and dyslipidemia receiving stable pharmacological treatment, randomized to pharmaceutical-grade omega-3 ethyl esters at 2 g/day or matching placebo for 12 weeks. Primary outcomes included inflammatory biomarkers (NLR, ferritin) and blood pressure parameters assessed by 24-hour ambulatory monitoring and central pulse wave analysis. Compared with placebo, omega-3 supplementation produced significant reductions in neutrophil-to-lymphocyte ratio (-0.43; 95% CI: -0.73 to -0.13; p=0.005), serum ferritin (-47.32 ng/mL; 95% CI: -77.55 to -17.08; p=0.003), daytime systolic blood pressure (-6.52 mmHg; 95% CI: -10.66 to -2.39; p=0.002), nighttime diastolic blood pressure (-5.55 mmHg; p=0.013), central systolic blood pressure (-6.71 mmHg; p=0.019), and estimated pulse wave velocity (-0.77 m/s; p<0.001). Important limitations include the exploratory nature with unadjusted multiple comparisons, modest sample size, incomplete outcome availability, and the absence of a prospectively registered primary endpoint, all of which preclude confirmatory interpretation of these findings.

Implications Medical Affairs : These exploratory RCT signals, while requiring confirmation, suggest that adjunctive pharmaceutical-grade omega-3 supplementation may provide both anti-inflammatory and hemodynamic benefits on top of standard antihypertensive and lipid-lowering therapy, with particularly notable reductions in central SBP and arterial stiffness indices that are established surrogate markers of cardiovascular risk. For Medical Affairs, these data can inform clinical development hypotheses for combination cardiovascular prevention strategies and support dialogue with clinical investigators about the mechanistic rationale for future adequately powered trials testing omega-3 as add-on therapy in patients with residual inflammatory and blood pressure burden. KOL engagement should focus on the arterial stiffness and central BP findings, which resonate with current guideline emphasis on vascular aging and beyond-office blood pressure in hypertension management.

Revue : International Journal of Molecular Sciences | Tier : NA | IF estime : NA

Date de publication : 2026-11-02

Resultats principaux : This phase I/II pilot study enrolled 12 treatment-naive metastatic clear cell renal cell carcinoma (ccRCC) patients receiving seleno-L-methionine (SLM) combined with axitinib and pembrolizumab, with endpoints including plasma TGF-beta1, plasma selenium, circulating cytokines, efficacy, and safety. The objective response rate was 70% (complete response 40%, partial response 30%), with favorable complete response rates observed in both favorable and intermediate IMDC disease categories. The principal safety signal relevant to hypertension was grade 3 hypertension occurring in 58.3% of patients, approximately twice the rate observed in the pivotal pembrolizumab-axitinib trial, without treatment discontinuations. Implications Medical Affairs : The markedly elevated incidence of grade 3 hypertension (58.3%) in this TGF-beta1 inhibitor-VEGF-TKI-immunotherapy triplet highlights hypertension as a clinically critical on-target adverse effect of VEGF pathway suppression that requires proactive antihypertensive management protocols in oncology settings. From a Medical Affairs perspective, this emphasizes a cross-therapeutic area opportunity: antihypertensive agents, particularly those with favorable renal hemodynamic profiles, are essential partners in managing VEGF TKI-based cancer regimens, and medical education initiatives bridging oncology and cardio-oncology are increasingly relevant. Revue : Research and Practice in Thrombosis and Haemostasis | Tier : NA | IF estime : NA

Date de publication : 2026-10-13

Resultats principaux : This secondary analysis of the Hydra randomized clinical trial included 125 patients with cancer-associated pulmonary embolism, of whom 103 had PE at baseline and 22 developed PE during 90-day follow-up. The primary outcomes were recurrent venous thromboembolism (90-day cumulative incidence 2.5%, 95% CI: 0.67-6.6%), major bleeding (2.5%, 95% CI: 0.68-6.7%), all-cause 90-day mortality (32%, 95% CI: 23-40%), anticoagulation practice patterns, and quality of life assessed by the PEmb-QoL questionnaire. Key limitations include the secondary and exploratory nature of the analysis, heterogeneous cancer types, and the relatively small sample size limiting subgroup analyses.

Implications Medical Affairs : While this article primarily addresses cancer-associated pulmonary embolism rather than arterial hypertension, the high all-cause mortality and substantially impaired quality of life in cancer-PE patients have indirect relevance for hypertension management within cardio-oncology frameworks, as arterial hypertension is a major comorbidity managed concurrently with anticoagulation in this population. For Medical Affairs, these data reinforce the growing clinical complexity of managing cardiovascular comorbidities in cancer patients and the need for integrated protocols addressing both thromboembolic and antihypertensive care. Lifestyle Changes for the Treatment of Hypertension.

Revue : Current Hypertension Reports | Tier : NA | IF estime : NA

Date de publication : 2026-11-14

Resultats principaux : This comprehensive narrative review synthesizes the current evidence for lifestyle interventions as the foundation of hypertension prevention and treatment, covering weight management, dietary patterns, sodium and potassium intake, physical activity, stress reduction, alcohol reduction, and avoidance of pressor substances, with emphasis on guideline implementation. Recent trials extend DASH diet benefits through grocery delivery programs addressing food access barriers in underserved populations, strengthening the case for population-level implementation. Potassium-enriched salt substitution studies support cardiovascular benefit but require attention to hyperkalemia risk in patients with renal impairment or on renin-angiotensin-system blockers. Alcohol reduction lowers blood pressure most among individuals with higher baseline intake or blood pressure, reinforcing the dose-response nature of this intervention. Evidence for time-restricted eating and stress-reduction strategies including mindfulness remains uncertain, with inconsistent trial results, and equitable access to lifestyle programs represents a persistent implementation barrier. The review is limited by the narrative methodology and inherent heterogeneity of the included evidence.

Implications Medical Affairs : This review crystallizes the contemporary evidence framework for non-pharmacological blood pressure lowering and reinforces that lifestyle modification is not merely adjunctive but foundational to hypertension guidelines, potentially delaying or reducing antihypertensive medication initiation when effectively implemented. For Medical Affairs, understanding the current evidence boundaries for lifestyle interventions is critical for positioning antihypertensive pharmacotherapy as complementary to, rather than replacement for, lifestyle programs, and for designing combination trial protocols that appropriately control for lifestyle background. The emphasis on equitable access and scalable implementation opens a Medical Affairs engagement opportunity around patient adherence programs and real-world effectiveness studies that can demonstrate product value in populations with high lifestyle intervention barriers.

Revue : Food Science & Nutrition | Tier : NA | IF estime : NA

Date de publication : 2026-11-13

Resultats principaux : This cross-sectional study analyzed 5,643 adults from the Tehran Lipid and Glucose Study to investigate the association between ultra-processed food (UPF) consumption (assessed by NOVA classification via validated semi-quantitative food frequency questionnaire) and serum uric acid (SUA) levels, with hypertension status adjusted as a covariate. Total UPF consumption was not significantly associated with SUA levels in either crude or adjusted models in the overall population; however, soft drinks (beta=0.20, p-trend<0.001) and pizza (beta=0.11, p-trend=0.033) were independently associated with higher SUA after full adjustment. No significant UPF-SUA associations were observed across strata of age, sex, BMI, physical activity, type 2 diabetes, hypertension, CKD, or insulin resistance. Limitations include the cross-sectional design, single dietary assessment, and potential for residual confounding.

Implications Medical Affairs : These findings from a large Iranian cohort suggest that the hypertension-associated risk of dietary UPF exposure may be particularly pronounced in smokers through a uric acid-mediated pathway, as hyperuricemia is an independent driver of blood pressure elevation and cardiovascular risk in hypertensive patients. From a Medical Affairs perspective, the interaction between smoking and ultra-processed food on uric acid metabolism provides a mechanistic rationale for multi-factorial risk reduction strategies in hypertensive patients who smoke, potentially informing patient stratification in clinical programs for agents targeting uricosuria or xanthine oxidase inhibition. This research supports KOL messaging around the complexity of dietary risk factor modification in hypertension and reinforces the value of uric acid as a monitoring biomarker in hypertensive patients with concurrent lifestyle risk factors.

Persistent metabolic remodeling after dietary salt reduction reveals sex differences in the adaptation to chronic high-salt intake.

Revue : Biology of Sex Differences | Tier : NA | IF estime : NA

Date de publication : 2026-11-12

Resultats principaux : This experimental study fed male and female C57BL/6 mice a high-salt diet (HSD, 8.6% NaCl) or low-salt normal diet (ND, 0.6% NaCl) from 9 to 23 months of age, with a subset undergoing dietary reversal from HSD to ND after 7 months, and profiled plasma metabolites by gas chromatography/mass spectrometry. Female mice developed salt-sensitive hypertension driven by vascular dysfunction, whereas male mice exhibited cardiac and vascular remodeling independent of blood pressure. HSD induced substantial alterations in 48 measured plasma metabolites including glucose, glycerol, glycerol phosphates, and fatty acids. Following 7 months of salt reduction, male mice showed near-complete normalization of metabolite profiles while female mice retained a metabolomic signature distinct from ND controls despite blood pressure normalization. Linoleate showed a significant sex-diet interaction, and energy metabolism, lipid handling, and osmotic balance metabolites showed sex-dependent patterns. A key limitation is the preclinical mouse model and uncertain translatability to human sex-based metabolic differences in response to dietary salt modification.

Implications Medical Affairs : These data provide mechanistic evidence that the metabolic consequences of chronic high-salt intake and subsequent salt reduction differ substantially between sexes, with females showing persistent metabolic dysregulation even after blood pressure normalization, implying that blood pressure alone may be an insufficient surrogate for cardiovascular risk reduction after dietary salt modification in women. From a Medical Affairs perspective, these findings support the scientific rationale for sex-stratified trial designs and outcome analyses in antihypertensive development, particularly for agents targeting vascular function in salt-sensitive hypertension, and reinforce growing recognition that women may require differentiated therapeutic approaches. These results also create a Medical Affairs opportunity for educational initiatives addressing the sex gap in hypertension research and for positioning metabolic biomarker panels as complements to blood pressure measurement in clinical development.

Revue : Nutrients | Tier : NA | IF estime : NA

Date de publication : 2026-11-03

Resultats principaux : This systematic review and meta-analysis following PRISMA 2020 guidelines synthesized 61 observational studies on dietary factors and nephrolithiasis risk, covering fluid volume, dietary patterns, specific foods, and nutrient intakes. High fluid volume and beverages including coffee, tea, and moderate alcohol showed strong protective effects, while high intake of meat, sugar-sweetened beverages, and ultra-processed foods significantly elevated risk. Plant-forward dietary patterns, including high fruit, vegetable, and fiber intake, and adherence to the DASH diet (Dietary Approaches to Stop Hypertension) were significantly associated with reduced kidney stone risk. Limitations include heterogeneity of included studies and observational design precluding causal inference.

Implications Medical Affairs : The confirmation that the DASH diet, originally designed for blood pressure reduction, also substantially reduces nephrolithiasis risk positions this dietary pattern as a dual-benefit intervention relevant to hypertensive patients, among whom CKD and nephrolithiasis are common comorbidities, reinforcing the multisystem benefits of dietary counseling in hypertension management. For Medical Affairs, these data support communication strategies linking blood pressure lowering dietary approaches to broader renal protective outcomes, relevant for positioning antihypertensive agents with demonstrated renal benefits in guidelines and patient education materials. The convergence of DASH diet recommendations across cardiovascular and renal guidelines creates a Medical Affairs opportunity for integrated messaging that may enhance patient adherence to both dietary and pharmacological antihypertensive strategies.
