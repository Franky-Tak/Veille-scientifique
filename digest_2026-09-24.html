Stroke in the Young: Incidence, 30-Day Case Fatality Rates, and Risk Factors Over Time in a Population-Based Study.

Revue : Neurology | Tier : NA | IF estime : NA

Date de publication : 2026-09-23

Resultats principaux : This population-based study from the Greater Cincinnati/Northern Kentucky region used validated physician-adjudicated methods to assess first-ever stroke across 6 time periods from 1993/94 to 2020, identifying 2,076 events in young adults aged 20 to 54 years. Stroke incidence in young adults rose sharply from 33.90 to 62.18 cases per 100,000 person-years over the study period (p-trend=0.04), an approximately 83% increase, while rates in older adults simultaneously declined. Ischemic stroke drove this increase, with incidence nearly doubling from 23.8 to 47.3 cases per 100,000 person-years. Hypertension, diabetes, atrial fibrillation, and substance use all became significantly more prevalent over time (p<0.001 for all), implicating worsening risk factor burden as the key driver of rising incidence. The 30-day case fatality rate modestly declined from 11.7% to 9.4%, largely attributable to improved outcomes in hemorrhagic stroke subtypes, though this suggests advances in acute care rather than prevention; the retrospective observational design limits causal attribution.

Implications Medical Affairs : The dramatic rise in stroke incidence in young adults despite improving acute outcomes underscores a critical failure of preventive hypertension control in patients under 55, representing a compelling epidemiological argument for earlier pharmacological intervention and product positioning targeting younger hypertensive patients. This longitudinal population-level data provides powerful support for Medical Affairs messaging around the cardiovascular consequences of uncontrolled blood pressure in younger demographics, a segment frequently undertreated due to physician and patient inertia. KOL engagement with neurologists and preventive cardiologists should leverage this data to strengthen guideline advocacy around earlier antihypertensive initiation and aggressive risk factor modification in young adults.

Association between Early-Onset Metabolic Diseases and Adverse Mental Health Conditions: A Nationwide Cross-Sectional Study.

Revue : Yonsei Medical Journal | Tier : NA | IF estime : NA

Date de publication : 2026-10

Resultats principaux : This nationwide cross-sectional study analyzed data from the 2014-2023 Korea National Health and Nutrition Examination Survey to examine associations between age at metabolic disease diagnosis and mental health outcomes in Korean adults, comparing early-onset (age <40 years) to late-onset (age ≥40 years) cases. Early-onset hypertension was strongly associated with depression (OR=2.67; 95% CI: 1.91-3.74) compared to both undiagnosed individuals and late-onset cases (p<0.001 for group difference), a finding persisting after Bonferroni correction. Early-onset hypertension was also associated with higher perceived stress (OR=1.41; 95% CI: 1.21-1.66 vs. late-onset, p<0.001), while early-onset diabetes showed the strongest suicidality link (OR=2.76; 95% CI: 1.79-4.25). These associations appeared independent of disease duration, suggesting the age at diagnosis rather than time lived with disease carries unique psychological impact. The cross-sectional design precludes causal inference and self-reported diagnosis ages may introduce recall bias, while generalizability beyond a Korean population remains uncertain.

Implications Medical Affairs : The robust association between early-onset hypertension and both depression and perceived stress in a large national cohort highlights a clinically significant comorbidity burden that is largely absent from current treatment algorithms, creating an opportunity to position antihypertensives with favorable tolerability profiles as particularly suitable for younger patients at mental health risk. From a Medical Affairs perspective, this evidence supports developing educational programs that encourage mental health screening in young hypertensive patients, which could differentiate product positioning through a holistic patient-centered narrative. KOL engagement should bridge psychiatry and cardiology to explore integrated care pathways for early-onset hypertension, ultimately strengthening the scientific rationale for cross-specialty collaboration and comprehensive risk management in this underserved segment.

Impact of obstructive sleep apnoea on nocturnal blood pressure: mechanisms and treatment responses - a narrative review.

Revue : European Respiratory Review | Tier : NA | IF estime : NA

Date de publication : 2026-09-23

Resultats principaux : This narrative review from the University Hospital Zurich synthesizes current evidence on nocturnal blood pressure abnormalities — particularly non-dipping and nocturnal hypertension — in patients with obstructive sleep apnoea (OSA), evaluating physiological mechanisms, epidemiology, prognostic significance, and treatment effects. OSA is shown to disrupt normal nocturnal blood pressure regulation through intermittent hypoxia, sympathetic activation, and impaired baroreflex sensitivity, resulting in a distinct cardiovascular risk profile beyond that explained by daytime blood pressure alone. CPAP has the strongest evidence base for nocturnal BP reduction, with greater effects observed in patients with uncontrolled or resistant hypertension and better treatment adherence, though average BP reductions remain modest across the overall population. Non-CPAP therapies including mandibular advancement devices, supplemental oxygen, hypoglossal nerve stimulation, and upper-airway surgery show limited and inconsistent evidence for nocturnal BP effects. A key limitation identified across the evidence base is the chronic underutilization of 24-hour ambulatory blood pressure monitoring (ABPM) — which is essential to detect non-dipping and nocturnal hypertension — due to cost, patient acceptance, and reimbursement barriers.

Implications Medical Affairs : The under-recognition of nocturnal hypertension in the large and growing OSA patient population represents a substantial unmet need that provides a compelling differentiating angle for antihypertensive therapies with proven 24-hour or nocturnal blood pressure efficacy profiles. Medical Affairs should use this evidence to advocate for wider ABPM adoption in clinical practice guidelines, supporting generation of real-world evidence demonstrating that products with nocturnal blood pressure control translate to improved cardiovascular outcomes in OSA comorbid patients. Strategic KOL engagement at the interface of sleep medicine, pulmonology, and hypertension will be essential to build scientific exchange programs that position comprehensive blood pressure management — beyond office BP control — as standard of care in resistant and OSA-related hypertension.

Evaluation of sleep quality and obstructive sleep apnea in non-alcoholic fatty liver disease: a cross-sectional study.

Revue : BMC Gastroenterology | Tier : NA | IF estime : NA

Date de publication : 2026-09-02

Resultats principaux : This cross-sectional study utilized registry data from the PERSIAN Organizational Cohort Study at Mashhad University of Medical Sciences to evaluate sleep quality (Pittsburgh Sleep Quality Index) and OSA risk (STOP-BANG questionnaire) in 958 NAFLD/MASLD patients (mean age 46.7 years, 69.6% male) from Iran. Sleep disturbance was identified in 35.4% of participants (31.6% mild, 3.8% moderate) and was significantly associated with hypertension in unadjusted analysis (p=0.007), female sex (p=0.022), single marital status (p=0.019), and higher BMI (p=0.026). More than half of participants (53.4%) were at intermediate OSA risk, while hepatic steatosis grade correlated positively with STOP-BANG scores (r=0.232, p<0.001) but not with PSQI sleep disturbance scores. Subgroup analyses were not performed due to sample size constraints, and the absence of polysomnography for confirmatory OSA diagnosis is a notable methodological limitation. The cross-sectional design precludes any causal interpretation, and the single-center Iranian cohort limits generalizability to other populations.

Implications Medical Affairs : The significant association between hypertension and sleep disturbance in this large NAFLD cohort adds to a growing body of evidence that hypertension frequently coexists with complex multimorbidity, creating strategic opportunities to promote comprehensive cardiometabolic risk management approaches. The high prevalence of both sleep disorders and hypertension in this hepatological patient population suggests a clinically meaningful cardiovascular risk amplifier that is likely underdiagnosed in routine hepatology practice, supporting arguments for ABPM use in metabolic multimorbidity patients. KOL engagement with hepatologists and metabolic medicine specialists should focus on developing interdisciplinary case management frameworks that position antihypertensive therapies within an integrated sleep-liver-cardiovascular care model.

Blood Pressure Phenotypes in Patients Recently Diagnosed With Type 2 Diabetes Mellitus and Matched Controls: A Study in Urban Mozambique.

Revue : Journal of Clinical Hypertension (Greenwich, Conn.) | Tier : Tier 3 | IF estime : 3-10

Date de publication : 2026-09

Resultats principaux : This cross-sectional case-control study in urban Mozambique evaluated blood pressure phenotypes using four measurement methods in 151 recently diagnosed T2DM patients and 151 matched controls, comparing standard office BP (OBPM), unattended automated office BP (u-AOBP), 24-hour ambulatory BP monitoring (ABPM), and home BP monitoring (HBPM). Using ABPM as the reference standard, T2DM patients had significantly lower normotension rates (32.5% vs. 48.3%) and significantly higher masked hypertension rates (23.2% vs. 9.9%) compared to controls (p=0.004). HBPM demonstrated better concordance with ABPM than u-AOBP (κ=0.62 vs. 0.51 in T2DM), though both methods substantially underestimated masked hypertension and overestimated white-coat hypertension, particularly in the T2DM group. This study provides rare rigorous BP phenotyping data from sub-Saharan Africa, highlighting the unique diagnostic challenge in a population with high undetected cardiovascular risk. Limitations include the single-center design, recruitment from one urban hospital, and restriction to recently diagnosed T2DM limiting applicability to longer-duration disease.

Implications Medical Affairs : The disproportionately high prevalence of masked hypertension in newly diagnosed T2DM patients in sub-Saharan Africa underscores a critical diagnostic gap that could be directly addressed by promoting ABPM and HBPM in diabetic care pathways, supporting product positioning for antihypertensives with robust masked hypertension efficacy data. Medical Affairs should leverage this emerging-market evidence to build the scientific case for ABPM-guided antihypertensive therapy in diabetic patients, a strategy with strong differentiation potential given that standard office measurements systematically miss nearly one-quarter of affected individuals. This study is a call to action for KOL engagement in Africa and other emerging markets where the dual burden of diabetes and hypertension is escalating, supporting market development efforts aligned with local epidemiological evidence.

Implementation of a standardized electronic medical record-integrated alternating hyperosmolar therapy protocol in neurocritical care: a single-center retrospective feasibility study.

Revue : Frontiers in Neurology | Tier : NA | IF estime : NA

Date de publication : 2026-09-08

Resultats principaux : This retrospective single-center observational feasibility study at the American University of Beirut Medical Center evaluated a standardized EMR-integrated protocol alternating 3% hypertonic saline and 20% mannitol every 12 hours over 4 days in 17 consecutive adult neurocritical care patients with cerebral edema or suspected intracranial hypertension between January 2024 and January 2026. Protocol completion was achieved in 6 of 17 patients (35.3%), while 16 (94.1%) received at least 2 days of therapy; discontinuation primarily reflected underlying neurological disease progression or changes in goals of care rather than treatment-related toxicity. Hypernatremia occurred in 3 patients (17.6%) and led to protocol discontinuation in 1, and elevated serum osmolality was noted in 1 patient (5.9%). No protocol-related acute kidney injury, pulmonary edema, or decompensated heart failure events were observed during protocol administration. In-hospital mortality was 52.9%, consistent with the severity of underlying neurological conditions; the retrospective design, absence of a control group, and very small sample size (n=17) preclude definitive conclusions about protocol safety or efficacy.

Implications Medical Affairs : While this study addresses intracranial rather than systemic hypertension, its use of osmotic diuretics (mannitol) reflects a distinct therapeutic application of the diuretic class, and the safety profile observed — particularly the low rate of renal and cardiac adverse events — may have limited peripheral relevance for comparative safety discussions in the diuretics class. From a Medical Affairs perspective, the weak evidence base for standardized osmotic diuretic protocols in neurocritical care highlights an unmet need for rigorous trial design in this population, underscoring broader messages about protocol-driven care and EMR integration that could be relevant to systematic antihypertensive management. Engagement with neurocritical care and neurology KOLs around the intersection of intracranial pressure management and systemic blood pressure control could identify emerging collaborative research priorities bridging neurology and cardiovascular medicine.

An exploratory study of before hospitalization nutrients associated with N-terminal pro b-type natriuretic peptide during the exacerbation of chronic heart failure.

Revue : Clinical Nutrition ESPEN | Tier : NA | IF estime : NA

Date de publication : 2026-09-23

Resultats principaux : This exploratory cross-sectional study of 63 elderly Japanese CHF patients (median age 82 years, 41% female) used a brief self-administered diet history questionnaire to investigate pre-hospitalization dietary patterns associated with NT-proBNP levels at the time of CHF exacerbation, using a cut-off of 2,000 pg/mL. Hypertension was the most prevalent coronary risk factor, present in 67% of patients (42/63). Multivariate logistic regression across four adjustment models consistently identified high dietary n-3 polyunsaturated fatty acid intake as independently associated with lower odds of NT-proBNP >2,000 pg/mL (OR ranging from 0.18 to 0.29 across models, all p<0.05), while high vitamin D intake showed similar protective associations (OR 0.84-0.89, all p<0.05). Secondary analysis suggested that alpha-tocopherol (vitamin E) also associated independently with lower high NT-proBNP odds in one model. Major limitations include the very small sample size (n=63), the elderly single-ethnic Japanese population restricting generalizability, reliance on self-reported dietary assessment, and the cross-sectional exploratory design, which cannot establish causality between dietary intake and CHF severity.

Implications Medical Affairs : The high prevalence of hypertension (67%) as the dominant comorbidity in this CHF cohort reinforces the critical role of antihypertensive control as a foundational element of heart failure prevention, supporting Medical Affairs narratives that link early blood pressure management to reduced downstream cardiovascular burden including CHF exacerbation and hospitalization. The protective associations observed for dietary omega-3 fatty acids and vitamin D in this hypertensive-heavy CHF population align with broader cardiometabolic messaging around integrated nutritional and pharmacological strategies, providing scientific context for cross-disciplinary discussions with cardiologists and clinical nutritionists. Future Medical Affairs-sponsored research could explore whether optimized antihypertensive management in combination with nutritional guidance in elderly CHF patients translates to reduced hospitalization rates, a high-value outcome for payer and reimbursement discussions.

Associations between intuitive eating and health behaviors and physical health in U.S. adults.

Revue : Journal of Behavioral Medicine | Tier : NA | IF estime : NA

Date de publication : 2026-09-23

Resultats principaux : This cross-sectional online survey study enrolled 1,057 racially diverse U.S. adults (mean age 36 years, 52% female) recruited from CloudResearch to examine associations between intuitive eating scores and health behaviors and chronic disease diagnoses, after adjusting for age, sex, and race/ethnicity. Higher intuitive eating scores were significantly associated with greater physical activity (p=0.01), more sleep (p<0.001), better self-reported health (p<0.001), and lower self-reported stress (p<0.001), while no significant associations were found for fruit/vegetable intake or substance use. Importantly, intuitive eating was associated with significantly lower odds of self-reported hypertension (p<0.001) and hyperlipidemia (p<0.001) with moderate effects, as well as lower odds of type 2 diabetes (p=0.01) with a small effect size. The study included a notably diverse sample with 30% food insecurity, addressing a population often underrepresented in dietary research. Key limitations include the cross-sectional online design, self-reported chronic disease diagnoses (subject to recall bias and underdiagnosis), the self-selected online sample potentially introducing healthy user bias, and the inability to determine causal directionality.

Implications Medical Affairs : The significant association between intuitive eating — a non-restrictive, appetite-responsive dietary approach — and lower prevalence of hypertension supports the integration of lifestyle modification messaging into antihypertensive treatment narratives, particularly for primary prevention in younger adults where modifiable dietary behaviors are influential. Medical Affairs teams can leverage this evidence in educational initiatives that combine lifestyle counseling frameworks with pharmacological approaches, engaging KOLs in behavioral medicine, preventive cardiology, and primary care to develop holistic hypertension prevention pathways. This dataset also provides supporting arguments for health economics and outcomes research linking adherent lifestyle behaviors to reduced chronic disease burden, which could strengthen formulary and access discussions for products positioned in primary cardiovascular prevention.

Behavioral, neuroanatomic, and neuroinflammatory characteristics of metabolic syndrome in cafeteria diet-induced obese male rats.

Revue : Metabolic Brain Disease | Tier : NA | IF estime : NA

Date de publication : 2026-09-23

Resultats principaux : This preclinical study allocated 12 male Wistar rats (6 per group) to a standard diet or a cafeteria diet for 5 months from weaning to model diet-induced metabolic syndrome, then assessed metabolic parameters, behavioral outcomes, brain regional volumes by 4.7T MRI, and neuroinflammatory markers in the hypothalamus and olfactory bulb. Cafeteria diet rats developed a metabolic syndrome phenotype comprising abdominal adiposity, insulin resistance, systemic hypertension, and dyslipidemia (all p<0.05). Hypothalamic TNF-alpha and GFAP levels were significantly elevated in cafeteria-fed animals, and relative orbitofrontal cortex volume was increased (p<0.05), while reductions in hypothalamic and corpus callosum volumes did not reach significance. Behaviorally, cafeteria-fed rats showed significantly reduced locomotor activity, impaired short-term memory, and increased grooming — evidence of cognitive and behavioral decline. Multivariate modeling identified hypothalamic neuroinflammation, metabolic syndrome severity, and reduced corpus callosum volume as the strongest predictors of cognitive and behavioral impairment. Limitations include the very small sample size (n=6 per group), exclusive use of male rodents precluding sex-stratified conclusions, and the inherent translational challenges of dietary animal models applied to human metabolic syndrome.

Implications Medical Affairs : This preclinical study provides mechanistic evidence linking diet-induced hypertension and metabolic syndrome to hypothalamic neuroinflammation and structural brain alterations, supporting a narrative connecting optimal cardiometabolic control — including blood pressure management — to neuroprotection and cognitive preservation. From a Medical Affairs perspective, the emerging brain-cardiovascular axis represents an opportunity to position antihypertensive therapies within a broader neurocardiovascular disease prevention framework, a growing area of interest among KOLs working at the intersection of dementia prevention and cardiovascular risk. Translational research initiatives exploring whether pharmacological blood pressure reduction in obese patients modifies neuroinflammatory biomarkers would be a strategically valuable and scientifically differentiated endpoint to pursue in future clinical studies.

US Life Expectancy and Increasing Mortality Rates: An Analysis of the Crisis.

Revue : The Milbank Quarterly | Tier : NA | IF estime : NA

Date de publication : 2026-09-23

Resultats principaux : This comprehensive analytical study by Steven Woolf from Virginia Commonwealth University used multiple national and international mortality databases (UN DESA, US Mortality Database, CDC, OECD) to examine US life expectancy trends, cause-specific mortality patterns, and comparisons with 16 high-income countries. As of 2023, US life expectancy ranked 38th globally, with populations of 60 countries facing lower risk of dying before age 40 than Americans; in young adults aged 25-44, all-cause mortality increased by a striking 71.2% between 2010 and 2021. Hypertensive disease was specifically identified as a major driver of rising mortality in both young-to-middle-aged and middle-aged adults, alongside drug overdoses, metabolic disorders, and external causes. The analysis found that the widening mortality gap between the US and peer nations since 2010 is driven by cardiovascular, external, nervous system, respiratory, and metabolic causes, with state-level life expectancy increasingly correlating with political and policy orientation. Structural contributors identified include healthcare system deficiencies, socioeconomic precarity, an obesogenic food environment, and structural racism; the aggregate national data design limits individual-level causal inference but provides important context for policy prioritization.

Implications Medical Affairs : The explicit identification of hypertensive disease as a key contributor to the unprecedented rise in US young adult mortality provides powerful epidemiological evidence for strengthening treatment guidelines and broadening therapeutic access, creating an urgent public health narrative that Medical Affairs can leverage in payer, formulary, and health policy discussions. This high-level policy analysis underscores systemic failures in hypertension detection and control across socioeconomic strata, supporting Medical Affairs advocacy for equitable access to antihypertensive therapies and engagement in health equity initiatives that could drive market expansion. KOL strategy should incorporate health policy experts, public health academics, and health equity advocates to build a science-policy interface that positions proactive hypertension management as a national mortality reduction strategy.

Interaction between adiposity and genetic risk score on blood pressure traits in Mexican adults.

Revue : Journal of Human Hypertension | Tier : Tier 3 | IF estime : 3-10

Date de publication : 2026-09-22

Resultats principaux : This longitudinal study from the Health Workers Cohort Study (Mexico) followed 1,424 adults across three assessment waves (2004-2006, 2010-2012, 2016-2019) to evaluate interactions between adiposity indicators (BMI, waist circumference, total and trunk body fat percentage) and a 6-SNP genetic risk score (GRS) for blood pressure. Significant longitudinal within-subject and between-subject associations were found between all adiposity measures and both systolic and diastolic blood pressure using hybrid mixed-effects regression. A positive gene-adiposity interaction was identified specifically for diastolic blood pressure, with subjects in the highest GRS tertile showing significantly greater DBP increases per unit rise in BMI compared to those in the lowest GRS tertile (p=0.001), persisting after adjustment for potential confounders. The study represents one of few longitudinal assessments of gene-environment interaction on blood pressure in a Latin American population, providing data relevant to an underrepresented population in hypertension genetics research. Limitations include the simplified 6-SNP GRS not capturing the full genetic architecture of hypertension, the occupational cohort limiting generalizability, and residual confounding from unmeasured lifestyle and dietary factors.

Implications Medical Affairs : The demonstration of a gene-adiposity interaction amplifying diastolic blood pressure in genetically susceptible Mexican adults provides mechanistic evidence supporting precision medicine approaches to hypertension risk stratification, with direct relevance to developing personalized treatment algorithms for high-risk cardiometabolic patients. Medical Affairs should engage KOLs in pharmacogenomics and preventive cardiology to develop scientific messaging around genetically tailored hypertension prevention, particularly in Latin American markets where metabolic risk factors and genetic hypertension susceptibility represent an increasingly recognized dual burden. This study supports investment in real-world evidence generation combining genetic profiling with metabolic phenotyping in Latin American cohorts, enabling differentiated product positioning for antihypertensive therapies targeting the high-risk gene-obesity interaction subpopulation.
