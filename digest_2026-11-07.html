Association of the triglyceride glucose-waist circumference index with hypertension among US adults: A cross-sectional study from NHANES.

Revue : Medicine (Baltimore) | Tier : NA | IF estime : NA

Date de publication : 2026-11-06

Resultats principaux : This cross-sectional study analyzed 2,716 adults aged 20 years and older drawn from the US National Health and Nutrition Examination Survey (NHANES) 2015-2018, with hypertension defined by self-reported physician diagnosis. The primary endpoint was the association between the triglyceride glucose-weight-adjusted waist index (TyG-WWI), a composite marker of glycolipid metabolism and central obesity, and hypertension prevalence, evaluated using multivariable logistic regression with restricted cubic splines and subgroup analyses. Each unit increase in TyG-WWI was independently associated with a 2% increase in the odds of hypertension (OR = 1.02, 95% CI: 1.01-1.03, P < 0.001) after full covariate adjustment, with a statistically significant nonlinear dose-response relationship (P for nonlinearity < 0.05). Participants in the highest TyG-WWI quartile had 72% greater odds of hypertension compared to the lowest quartile (OR = 1.72, 95% CI: 1.21-2.46, P for trend < 0.001), with subgroup analyses revealing stronger associations in females and in normal-weight individuals. Exploratory mediation analyses suggested BMI and uric acid may account for approximately 30.5% and 7.9% of the association, respectively, though these estimates are descriptive and require prospective confirmation in longitudinal designs.

Implications Medical Affairs : TyG-WWI as a composite metabolic marker adds meaningful value to hypertension risk stratification beyond traditional lipid panels, particularly for identifying metabolically dysregulated patients who may benefit from early pharmacological and behavioral intervention. From a Medical Affairs perspective, this supports product positioning of antihypertensive agents with favorable metabolic profiles in obese and insulin-resistant populations, where metabolic burden compounds cardiovascular risk. Future clinical research should validate TyG-WWI-guided patient selection in prospective trials and investigate whether specific antihypertensive drug classes provide differential benefits in this metabolically defined subgroup.

Protocol for investigating the association between Helicobacter pylori exposure, antigen-specific serological responses, and resistant versus non-resistant hypertension in a South African cohort.

Revue : PloS one | Tier : NA | IF estime : NA

Date de publication : 2026-11-06

Resultats principaux : This study protocol describes enrollment of 200 adults aged 18 years and older at a tertiary hospital in Cape Town, South Africa, divided into three groups: 80 with resistant hypertension, 80 with non-resistant hypertension, and 40 normotensive controls, to investigate the association between Helicobacter pylori (H. pylori) exposure and hypertension phenotype. The primary exposure variable is H. pylori seropositivity assessed via the Pylori DuoTect point-of-care assay detecting antibodies to CagA and FliD antigens, with laboratory outcomes encompassing lipid profile, inflammatory markers, nitric oxide-related markers, homocysteine, insulin indices, complete blood count, ghrelin, multiplex cytokine profiling, and flow cytometric immunophenotyping. Multivariable logistic regression will evaluate associations between H. pylori exposure and hypertension phenotype with adjustment for demographic, lifestyle, and clinical covariates; PAXgene RNA samples will be retained for future exploratory analyses. The study is described as the first African investigation to integrate point-of-care H. pylori serology with detailed clinical, inflammatory, metabolic, and immunological profiling across resistant hypertension, non-resistant hypertension, and normotension phenotypes. Key limitations are inherent to the protocol design: results are not yet available, the sample size is modest, and the cross-sectional nature will preclude causal inference once completed.

Implications Medical Affairs : This protocol addresses a critical evidence gap regarding the inflammatory mechanisms linking H. pylori to treatment-resistant hypertension in sub-Saharan Africa, a region with both high H. pylori prevalence and inadequately controlled hypertension. From a Medical Affairs standpoint, findings could support the hypothesis that H. pylori eradication therapy may serve as an adjunctive intervention to improve blood pressure control in treatment-resistant cases, potentially broadening the therapeutic paradigm. KOL engagement with African cardiovascular researchers and global health organizations should be prioritized now to shape the clinical narrative as results from this landmark African study emerge.

Association of aspartate aminotransferase to alanine aminotransferase ratio with hypertension in Chinese adults: a cross-sectional study.

Revue : Frontiers in public health | Tier : NA | IF estime : NA

Date de publication : 2026-10-22

Resultats principaux : This large cross-sectional study analyzed 87,869 Chinese adults (mean age 42.1 years, 57% male) from the Rich Healthcare Group health screening program, with hypertension defined as systolic BP >= 140 mmHg or diastolic BP >= 90 mmHg. The primary finding was an independent inverse association between the AST/ALT ratio and hypertension risk: each unit increase in the ratio was associated with a 20% reduction in hypertension odds in the age/sex/BMI-adjusted model (OR = 0.80, 95% CI: 0.76-0.84, P < 0.001), and 17% in the fully adjusted model (OR = 0.83, 95% CI: 0.78-0.87, P < 0.001). Quartile analysis demonstrated a stepwise inverse pattern with Q2, Q3, and Q4 showing 15%, 22%, and 19% lower odds of hypertension compared to Q1, respectively. A nonlinear dose-response relationship was identified with an inflection point at an AST/ALT ratio of approximately 1.010, suggesting a threshold effect rather than a linear gradient. Stratified analyses revealed significant effect modification by age (P for interaction < 0.001) and BMI (P for interaction < 0.001), with stronger protective associations among individuals younger than 60 years and those with BMI >= 25 kg/m2; cross-sectional design and use of secondary health screening data are primary limitations.

Implications Medical Affairs : The AST/ALT ratio, obtained at no additional cost from routine liver function testing, may provide incremental value as an inexpensive metabolic marker for hypertension risk assessment that could be integrated into systematic cardiometabolic screening protocols. For Medical Affairs, this finding reinforces the metabolic-liver-cardiovascular disease continuum, supporting product positioning of antihypertensives in patients with metabolic syndrome and concurrent hepatic involvement as a clinically differentiated subgroup. Prospective validation studies and mechanistic research exploring hepatic metabolism pathways in blood pressure regulation are warranted to convert this large observational finding into a clinically actionable screening tool.

Long-term mobile use and incident hypertension: findings from the Shahrekord PERSIAN cohort study.

Revue : BMC public health | Tier : NA | IF estime : NA

Date de publication : 2026-08-11

Resultats principaux : This prospective cohort study enrolled 7,490 hypertension-free adults aged 35-70 years from the Shahrekord PERSIAN Cohort Study, Iran, with a total follow-up of 61,895.9 person-years, during which 236 participants developed incident hypertension (incidence rate 3.81 per 1,000 person-years). The primary exposure, mobile phone use duration, was categorized as less than 5, 5-10, and 10 or more years; in multivariable Cox regression, those with 5-10 years of use had a significantly higher hazard ratio of 1.60 (95% CI: 1.05-2.43), and those with 10 or more years had an adjusted HR of 1.42 (95% CI: 1.03-1.96) compared to less-than-5-year users. Additional independent predictors of incident hypertension identified in this study included older age, obesity (BMI >= 30 kg/m2), depression, hypertriglyceridemia, and alcohol non-use. These associations were robust after adjustment for established cardiovascular risk factors, suggesting that mobile phone use duration may represent an independent, novel behavioral risk factor. Key limitations include potential misclassification of mobile use duration by self-report, absence of data on call patterns and electromagnetic frequency intensity, residual confounding, and limited generalizability beyond the Iranian study population.

Implications Medical Affairs : Long-term mobile phone use as an independent predictor of incident hypertension introduces a novel modifiable behavioral variable with significant public health implications as global smartphone adoption among all age groups continues to accelerate. From a Medical Affairs perspective, this finding could inform cardiovascular prevention messaging and enriches the clinical narrative around lifestyle modification as a complement to pharmacological antihypertensive management. Collaboration with digital health researchers and public health KOLs to explore whether mobile health interventions can simultaneously mitigate this risk while improving antihypertensive medication adherence would represent a strategically innovative research direction.

Population-level trends, risk factors and predictors of hypertensive disorders of pregnancy in the U.S, 2014-2023.

Revue : BMC pregnancy and childbirth | Tier : NA | IF estime : NA

Date de publication : 2026-08-11

Resultats principaux : This population-level cohort study used National Center for Health Statistics National Vital Statistics System natality data from 2014 to 2023, encompassing 37.7 million US births, to examine temporal trends, risk factors, and outcomes for hypertensive disorders of pregnancy (HDP), including chronic hypertension, gestational hypertension, and eclampsia. HDP was identified in 9.9% of all pregnancies, with prevalence substantially increasing across all racial and ethnic groups over the 10-year study period, indicating a true epidemiological shift beyond improved diagnostic awareness. Maternal obesity emerged as the strongest modifiable predictor, with extreme obesity conferring greater than 6-fold higher odds of HDP compared to normal-weight mothers. Non-Hispanic Black mothers consistently showed the highest prevalence of every HDP subtype compared to other racial groups, highlighting persistent health equity disparities in maternal cardiovascular outcomes. HDP was strongly associated with preterm birth and low birth weight, underscoring its impact on perinatal outcomes; primary limitations include reliance on birth certificate data with potential diagnostic misclassification and lack of detailed clinical data.

Implications Medical Affairs : The rising HDP burden, particularly in Black women and obese mothers, signals an urgent need for equity-focused prenatal and preconception care strategies that will directly expand the addressable patient population for antihypertensive therapies safe during pregnancy. From a Medical Affairs standpoint, the intersection of HDP with maternal obesity creates an opportunity to position antihypertensives with favorable safety profiles in pregnancy and to partner with obstetric societies on joint cardiovascular prevention guidelines addressing this growing clinical unmet need. Investment in KOL relationships with maternal-fetal medicine specialists and health equity researchers should be prioritized to develop educational programs targeting the disproportionate HDP burden in underserved populations and to shape emerging obstetric hypertension guidelines.

The impact of maternal cardiovascular risk factors and diseases on offspring cardiovascular health.

Revue : Cardiovascular research | Tier : NA | IF estime : NA

Date de publication : 2026-11-07

Resultats principaux : This narrative review synthesized population-level evidence examining how maternal cardiovascular risk factors and established cardiovascular diseases during pregnancy influence offspring cardiovascular health across the life course. Maternal conditions including hypertension, diabetes, obesity, dyslipidemia, and elevated lipoprotein(a) were found to disrupt fetal cardiovascular development through impaired placental blood flow, inflammation, oxidative stress, and disrupted nutrient transfer, leading to changes in cardiac structure, vascular function, and metabolism. Data from large population cohorts demonstrate that children born to mothers with cardiovascular conditions have approximately twice the risk of developing cardiovascular disease compared to those born to healthy mothers, with risks persisting into adulthood. The rising maternal age in developed countries further amplifies this intergenerational cardiovascular risk, as older mothers are more likely to carry established cardiovascular risk factors during pregnancy. Key limitations include heterogeneity across reviewed studies, difficulty separating genetic from intrauterine environmental contributions, and uncertainty about whether current lifestyle interventions in pregnancy translate to meaningful long-term benefits for offspring cardiovascular health.

Implications Medical Affairs : The finding that maternal hypertension doubles offspring cardiovascular disease risk positions optimal blood pressure management in women of reproductive age as not merely a maternal health imperative but an intergenerational cardiovascular disease prevention strategy. From a Medical Affairs standpoint, this evidence strengthens the value proposition of well-tolerated antihypertensive agents suitable for fertile women and supports engagement with preconception care guideline committees to integrate blood pressure targets into reproductive health planning. Partnership with perinatal cardiologists and women's cardiovascular health advocates represents a strategic KOL opportunity to shape emerging guidelines on maternal cardiovascular health and expand educational programming on the multigenerational consequences of undertreated hypertension.

Lifestyle counseling including the DASH diet improves multiple metabolomic profiles.

Revue : The Journal of nutrition | Tier : NA | IF estime : NA

Date de publication : 2026-11-06

Resultats principaux : This secondary analysis of the randomized PREMIER trial examined metabolomic changes in 690 adult participants assigned to three lifestyle interventions: Advice-only, Established, and Established plus DASH diet, with fasting plasma metabolites quantified by NMR spectroscopy at baseline and 6 months. Principal component analysis identified 13 distinct metabolomic patterns spanning lipoprotein, triglyceride-rich, amino acid, fatty acid, inflammatory, and ketone-related profiles. Compared to Advice-only, the Established intervention reduced PC4 (beta = -0.50, P = 0.04) including VLDL cholesterol and saturated fatty acids; Established plus DASH had broader effects, reducing PC9 (beta = -0.33, P = 0.01), PC11 (beta = -0.28, P = 0.004), and PC12 (beta = -0.23, P = 0.02) while increasing omega-3 fatty acids and decreasing branched-chain amino acids, lactate, citrate, and the GlycA inflammatory marker. BMI change was identified as the primary driver of metabolomic shifts, with the DASH component providing additive but independent metabolic benefits beyond weight loss. Limitations include the 6-month observational window, NMR spectroscopy with lower metabolite coverage compared to mass spectrometry platforms, and potential selection bias inherent to the trial population.

Implications Medical Affairs : The identification of GlycA and branched-chain amino acid reduction as specific DASH-associated metabolomic signatures provides mechanistic insight into how dietary interventions reduce cardiovascular risk beyond blood pressure lowering, reinforcing the multi-modal benefit of lifestyle modification as complementary to pharmacological antihypertensive therapy. Medical Affairs should leverage these findings to support communications positioning antihypertensive therapy within a comprehensive cardiovascular risk reduction framework that explicitly includes dietary optimization, particularly for patients with high metabolic inflammatory burden. Future research should investigate whether DASH-associated metabolomic improvements predict long-term cardiovascular outcomes and whether specific patient phenotypes benefit differentially from DASH in conjunction with pharmacotherapy, potentially informing precision medicine approaches to hypertension management.

Fruit and vegetable consumption among people living with HIV at a large Urban HIV Clinic in Central Uganda: A cross-sectional study.

Revue : PloS one | Tier : NA | IF estime : NA

Date de publication : 2026-11-06

Resultats principaux : This cross-sectional study at the Infectious Diseases Institute HIV clinic in Kampala, Uganda administered an adapted WHO STEPwise questionnaire to 431 people living with HIV aged 18 and older to assess fruit and vegetable consumption patterns and their determinants. Only 44 of 417 respondents (10.6%) met the WHO recommendation of five or more servings of fruits and vegetables per day, representing critically low adherence to cardioprotective dietary guidelines in this population at elevated cardiometabolic risk. Multivariable robust Poisson regression identified unemployment (APR = 0.1, 95% CI: 0.0-0.8), higher household income (APR = 0.99, 95% CI: 0.90-0.998), and longer duration on antiretroviral therapy (APR = 0.9, 95% CI: 0.89-0.996) as independently associated with inadequate consumption. Perceived barriers to fruit and vegetable consumption (mean score 3.2 out of 5) were notably high despite overall favorable attitudes (mean 3.7), suggesting structural and economic factors rather than knowledge deficits as the primary drivers of poor dietary behavior. Limitations include the cross-sectional design preventing causal inference, potential self-report bias in dietary recall, single-center setting limiting generalizability, and the absence of direct blood pressure measurements.

Implications Medical Affairs : The critically low fruit and vegetable intake among people living with HIV, a population already at elevated cardiometabolic risk due to antiretroviral therapy effects and chronic inflammation, represents a compound vulnerability that may substantially accelerate hypertension incidence in the growing PLHIV population across sub-Saharan Africa. From a Medical Affairs standpoint, this underscores the importance of integrating cardiovascular risk management, including antihypertensive therapy initiation thresholds, into HIV care pathways in low- and middle-income countries, an emerging market with significant unmet clinical need. KOL engagement with HIV and cardiovascular medicine specialists in Africa should focus on developing integrated chronic disease management guidelines that systematically address both HIV and cardiovascular risk factors within resource-constrained healthcare settings.

Cardiometabolic Outcomes in Dahl Salt-Sensitive Rat Pregnancies: Impact of a Maternal Hypertensive High-Fat Diet.

Revue : American journal of physiology. Endocrinology and metabolism | Tier : NA | IF estime : NA

Date de publication : 2026-11-06

Resultats principaux : This experimental study used high-fat diet (HFD)-fed Dahl salt-sensitive (DSS) rats as a preclinical model to investigate cardiovascular and metabolic adaptations during pregnancy in the context of obesity-driven chronic hypertension, with the primary hypothesis that HFD-fed dams would develop a superimposed preeclampsia-like phenotype. HFD-fed dams exhibited attenuated gestational weight gain, increased ketogenesis, significant cardiovascular dysfunction, and blunted uteroplacental vascular adaptation compared to control diet-fed animals, with blood pressure consistently higher pre-pregnancy and during most of gestation and lactation. Unexpectedly, both groups showed a blood pressure drop during late gestation, with HFD-fed dams displaying a lower blood pressure nadir and lower urinary protein excretion than controls, failing to replicate the hallmark preeclampsia features of late-gestation BP rise and proteinuria. Short-term pregnancy outcomes (litter size, pup viability, and placental efficiency) were largely preserved in HFD-fed dams, but long-term dietary normalization failed to reverse hypertension, with blood pressure remaining 10-15 mmHg higher than controls for at least 14 weeks post-weaning. Key limitations include inherent species differences between rats and humans, the artificial dietary model, and the absence of pharmacological intervention arms that would directly test therapeutic strategies.

Implications Medical Affairs : The demonstration of persistent postpartum hypertension following HFD-induced chronic hypertension during pregnancy provides a valuable preclinical platform for testing antihypertensive interventions designed for the peripartum period without compromising fetal outcomes. From a Medical Affairs perspective, this model could support early-phase investigation of antihypertensive agents with favorable metabolic profiles suitable for use across the pregnancy-to-postpartum continuum, addressing an area of significant regulatory and clinical unmet need. Mechanistic insights into adaptive uteroplacental responses that may attenuate preeclampsia risk in obese and chronically hypertensive patients could guide KOL-driven research priorities and inform the design of prospective clinical trials in hypertensive disorders of pregnancy.

The prevalence and risk factors associated with obesity among adults in Hawtat Bani Tamim: a cross-sectional study.

Revue : Frontiers in public health | Tier : NA | IF estime : NA

Date de publication : 2026-10-22

Resultats principaux : This cross-sectional study enrolled 384 adults residing in Hawtat Bani Tamim, Saudi Arabia, and characterized obesity prevalence and independent risk factors using standardized questionnaires and anthropometric measurements, providing the first city-level epidemiological data for this region. The overall obesity prevalence was 28.9%, with multivariate logistic regression identifying the following independent predictors: age 41-50 years (OR = 1.45, P = 0.001), age 51-60 years (OR = 2.40, P < 0.001), male sex (OR = 1.20, P = 0.007), lower education level at secondary school or below (OR = 4.18, P = 0.008), short sleep duration (OR = 1.25, P = 0.021), smoking (OR = 1.30, P = 0.008), and physical inactivity (OR = 1.60, P = 0.005). Hypertension was significantly associated with obesity in this population, consistent with the well-established bidirectional relationship between these conditions observed globally. No significant associations were detected between obesity and marital status, occupation, or most dietary factors in this cohort, with moderate physical activity identified as protective (OR = 0.75, P = 0.028). Key limitations include the modest sample size of 384 participants, single-site cross-sectional design, potential selection bias, and limited generalizability beyond the specific geographic area studied.

Implications Medical Affairs : The high prevalence of comorbid obesity and hypertension in this Saudi Arabian cohort, compounded by modifiable behavioral risk factors including physical inactivity and smoking, reflects an accelerating cardiometabolic disease burden across the Gulf Cooperation Council region requiring urgent public health intervention. Medical Affairs should recognize this growing Middle Eastern market as a priority target for evidence-based educational initiatives addressing the obesity-hypertension continuum and for positioning antihypertensive agents with weight-neutral or favorable metabolic profiles in KOL communications targeted at regional cardiovascular specialists. These regional data support strategic investment in partnerships with Middle Eastern public health authorities and cardiovascular societies to co-develop locally relevant prevention and treatment guidelines that reflect the specific epidemiological profile of hypertension in this population.

Machine learning-based identification of depression in older patients with type 2 diabetes mellitus: A multicenter retrospective study.

Revue : Science progress | Tier : NA | IF estime : NA

Date de publication : 2026-11-06

Resultats principaux : This multicenter retrospective study developed and validated nine machine learning classifiers to identify clinically documented depression in 31,980 older patients (aged 65 and older) with type 2 diabetes mellitus across six centers in China, using electronic health records. LASSO regression selected seven final predictors retained in the models: age, sex, hypertension, cerebrovascular disease, alcohol history, surgical history, and neutrophil-to-albumin ratio. The XGBoost model achieved AUCs of 0.836 (training), 0.784 (internal validation), and 0.772 (external validation), with performance comparable to logistic regression in external validation (AUC = 0.772), indicating no clear discriminative advantage of more complex machine learning algorithms. Hypertension was consistently identified as one of the key predictors associated with model-predicted depression risk, highlighting its bidirectional relationship with mental health outcomes in elderly diabetic patients. Limitations include the retrospective electronic health record design which may underdiagnose depression, lack of non-diabetic comparator groups, geographic restriction to China, and the moderate external validation AUC suggesting room for improvement in model performance.

Implications Medical Affairs : The identification of hypertension as a key predictor of comorbid depression in elderly type 2 diabetic patients reinforces the clinical importance of holistic cardiometabolic management that addresses both cardiovascular and psychological endpoints when treating hypertension in this high-risk demographic. From a Medical Affairs perspective, the hypertension-depression comorbidity burden in elderly diabetic populations represents an opportunity to differentiate antihypertensive agents with demonstrably neutral or beneficial effects on psychological wellbeing as preferred options in this complex patient segment. KOL partnerships spanning geriatric medicine, psychiatry, and cardiology should explore integrated care pathway development that systematically screens for depression in hypertensive T2DM patients to optimize treatment outcomes and reduce overall healthcare utilization.

Disease susceptibility and risk of dementia in patients with chronic kidney disease: a prospective cohort study based on UK Biobank.

Revue : BMC neurology | Tier : NA | IF estime : NA

Date de publication : 2026-08-10

Resultats principaux : This prospective cohort study analyzed 16,083 CKD participants from the UK Biobank over a mean follow-up of 13.8 years, identifying 806 incident dementia cases across a wide range of lifestyle, medical, social, electronic device use, environmental, and sensory risk factor exposures. Cox proportional hazards models with inverse probability weighting and competing risk analyses were applied; after false discovery rate (FDR) correction, hypertension remained significantly associated with higher dementia risk in CKD patients alongside diabetes, depression, hearing loss, and reduced social engagement. Moderate physical activity and moderate computer use remained protective after FDR correction, while no significant associations were found for BMI, LDL cholesterol, air pollution, or visual impairment in this CKD-specific cohort. Exploratory subgroup analyses suggested that associations may differ by kidney function severity and genetic susceptibility, warranting further investigation in stratified analyses. Key limitations include the population-based UK Biobank design which may not represent all CKD severity stages, absence of a concurrent non-CKD comparator arm, potential for residual confounding, and the need for independent external validation.

Implications Medical Affairs : The persistence of hypertension as a significant dementia risk factor in CKD patients after rigorous FDR correction strengthens the clinical case for aggressive blood pressure management in this high-risk comorbid population to prevent cognitive decline, a patient-centered outcome increasingly prioritized in clinical trial endpoints. For Medical Affairs, these findings underscore the importance of positioning antihypertensive therapies with proven renoprotective and neuroprotective properties in the growing CKD segment, where dual-organ protection represents a compelling and clinically differentiated value proposition. Future KOL-driven research should investigate whether specific antihypertensive drug classes provide differential protection against dementia in CKD patients, a question directly relevant to product differentiation strategy and potential label expansion discussions with regulatory authorities.
