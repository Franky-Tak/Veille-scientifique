Epidemiological Transition of Renal Mortality in Chiapas (2000-2024): Autonomy of CKDu and the Rise of Hypertensive Nephropathy-A Time-Series Ecological Study.

Revue : Nephrology (Carlton, Vic.) | Tier : NA | IF estime : NA

Date de publication : 2026 Sep

Resultats principaux : This ecological time-series study analysed 46,301 renal deaths in Chiapas, Mexico (2000-2024) using DGIS microdata, classifying deaths into diabetic kidney disease (DKD), hypertensive nephropathy (HN), chronic kidney disease of uncertain aetiology (CKDu), reflux nephropathy and other renal causes across five age strata. Primary endpoints were age-standardised mortality rates and annual percent change (APC), with sociodemographic contrasts assessed by binomial logistic regression and diagnostic substitution ruled out via trend correlation and binomial testing. Among adults aged 15-44 years, CKDu was the leading renal cause of death at 44.2% versus 27.5% for DKD, with men showing higher CKDu proportion (32.9% vs 26.2%) and agricultural workers displaying an odds ratio of 1.61 for CKDu versus DKD attribution. The fastest growth was seen for hypertensive nephropathy (APC 6.72%), while CKDu mortality in men aged 30-44 grew at APC 4.01% (p<0.001); 64.5% of CKDu temporal variance was independent of diabetes trends (R=0.3547, p=0.7 for substitution). Limitations include the ecological design precluding causal inference, potential misclassification of ICD-10 renal codes, and lack of individual-level clinical or exposure data on occupational and environmental determinants.

Implications Medical Affairs : This dual burden of hypertensive nephropathy and CKDu highlights the urgent need to strengthen early antihypertensive intervention pathways in Latin American agricultural populations, an angle currently under-addressed in international nephrology and hypertension guidelines. For a company with renin-angiotensin blockers or SGLT2 agents, the findings support positioning renoprotective therapy in populations where hypertensive nephropathy is emerging as the fastest-growing driver of end-stage kidney disease. KOL engagement should prioritise Latin American nephrology and public health networks to co-design real-world evidence protocols quantifying blood pressure control impact on renal mortality trajectories.

The Congenital Heart Disease in Children: Kidney-Associated Conditions with Epidemiologic Endpoints (CHICKADEE) study: Study design and protocol.

Revue : PloS one | Tier : NA | IF estime : NA

Date de publication : 2026 08 20

Resultats principaux : The CHICKADEE study is a multicentre prospective cohort protocol enrolling 300 children aged 4-16 years, 4-12 years after congenital heart disease (CHD) surgery, across three US clinical sites with enrichment for hypoplastic left heart syndrome and single-ventricle physiology. Primary endpoints include prevalent and incident hypertension, chronic kidney disease (CKD) and kidney failure, characterised via standardised clinical assessments, echocardiography, ambulatory blood pressure monitoring, and targeted plus untargeted proteomic biomarker discovery. The rationale draws on prior data showing that more than 2 million Americans live with CHD and face elevated hypertension and CKD risk that appears to originate in childhood but remains under-recognised in paediatric populations. A biorepository will be established to enable future ancillary studies on mechanisms of cardio-renal coupling in this vulnerable cohort. Study limitations include the observational design, restricted geographic representation of only three US sites, and the fact that the protocol paper reports design rather than outcomes.

Implications Medical Affairs : The study foreshadows a new evidence base likely to reshape paediatric cardiology and nephrology guidelines by embedding routine hypertension screening into CHD long-term follow-up. For Medical Affairs, this creates an opportunity to position antihypertensive portfolios and biomarker platforms into a well-defined cardio-renal-paediatric niche that currently lacks label-based recommendations. Early KOL engagement with the CHICKADEE investigators can shape ancillary study proposals and secure a role in future risk-stratification algorithms and paediatric formulation development.

The association between body mass index and lower extremity deep venous abnormalities identified by computed tomography venography.

Revue : Frontiers in public health | Tier : NA | IF estime : NA

Date de publication : 2026 08 05

Resultats principaux : This retrospective cross-sectional study analysed 349 patients (217 males, 132 females) undergoing computed tomography venography (CTV) to evaluate the association between body mass index (BMI) and lower-extremity deep venous (LEDV) abnormalities, with stratification by sex, age, and comorbidities including hypertension, diabetes and hyperlipidemia. Overweight or obesity prevalence in patients with LEDV abnormalities was 86.4% in males and 79.0% in females, and males had more stenoses per person (1.19 vs 0.77, p<0.05); coexistence of overweight/obesity with hypertension, diabetes, or hyperlipidemia amplified thrombosis prevalence in females and stenosis burden in both sexes. Multivariable logistic regression found no factor including BMI to be an independent predictor of LEDV prevalence, but negative binomial regression identified male sex as an independent correlate of stenosis count (adjusted IRR 1.65, 95% CI 1.19-2.28, p=0.002) and overall lesion burden (aIRR 1.32, p=0.048), with hypertension borderline (aIRR 1.39, p=0.05). Subgroup findings underscore a sex-differentiated comorbidity effect that is more pronounced in males for stenosis and in females for thrombosis. Limitations include the single-centre retrospective design, selection bias from CTV referral, cross-sectional inability to establish causality, and unmeasured confounders such as anticoagulant use or physical activity.

Implications Medical Affairs : The signal that hypertension approaches independent association with lower-extremity venous stenosis burden broadens the vascular comorbidity conversation beyond arterial disease and may support future guideline attention to venous complications in hypertensive obese populations. For Medical Affairs, this offers a differentiated narrative to reinforce blood pressure control as a broader vascular-protective strategy, useful for internal medicine and vascular KOL engagement. A targeted real-world evidence study coupling antihypertensive intensification with venous imaging endpoints would fill an evidence gap identified by the authors.

Adverse cardio-metabolic risk profile and epicardial adiposity in patients with adrenal adenoma: a cross-sectional study.

Revue : Frontiers in endocrinology | Tier : NA | IF estime : NA

Date de publication : 2026 08 05

Resultats principaux : This cross-sectional exploratory study from Medical University of Vienna enrolled 74 individuals stratified into mild autonomous cortisol secretion (MACS, n=25), non-functioning adrenal adenoma (NFA, n=25), and controls without adrenal tumour (CON, n=24), with a majority of women and median age 58-63 years across groups. Visceral and ectopic fat stores plus cardiac function were assessed by MRI and spectroscopy, blood pressure by standardised protocol, and glucose and lipid metabolism via fasting bloods and oral glucose tolerance test. Prevalence of hypertension differed significantly across MACS (84%), NFA (76%) and CON (43.5%), as did dyslipidemia (96%/80%/62.5%) and prediabetes (56%/72%/29.2%); HbA1c and HOMA-IR were worst in MACS, and epicardial adipose tissue area was highest in MACS (1159.68 mm²) followed by NFA and CON (p<0.001). No between-group differences were seen for cardiac morphology, intramyocardial or intrahepatic fat. Limitations include the small exploratory sample, cross-sectional design without longitudinal cardiovascular outcomes, single-centre recruitment, and lack of standardised confirmatory endocrinology testing for MACS across all cases.

Implications Medical Affairs : These data reinforce that even non-functioning adrenal adenomas confer cardio-metabolic risk beyond the general population, supporting proactive hypertension and metabolic screening in these subclinical endocrine populations. For Medical Affairs, this creates a positioning angle for antihypertensive and cardio-metabolic therapy programmes at the interface with endocrinology, where guideline gaps remain. KOL strategy should target hypertension-endocrinology cross-specialty networks and consider co-sponsoring prospective outcome studies to convert this signal into evidence-based intervention thresholds.

Maternal Hypertension, Diabetes, and Preterm Birth Risk Among Infants With Trisomy 21 in Texas, 1999-2018.

Revue : Birth defects research | Tier : NA | IF estime : NA

Date de publication : 2026 Aug

Resultats principaux : This registry-based cohort used Texas Birth Defects Registry data for 9,355 deliveries of infants with trisomy 21 between 1999 and 2018 to quantify the association of maternal hypertensive disorders and diabetes with preterm birth. Poisson regression estimated crude and adjusted risk ratios for hypertension, diabetes, their subtypes (pregestational and gestational), and co-occurring exposure, with stratification by racial/ethnic subgroup, preterm subtype (<32 weeks and 32-36 weeks), and presence or absence of congenital heart defects. Maternal hypertension was associated with 50% higher preterm birth risk and diabetes with 21% higher risk, while co-occurrence conferred a 71% higher risk; the hypertension association was substantially stronger for delivery before 32 weeks (aRR 2.60, 95% CI 1.83-3.70) than for 32-36 weeks (aRR 1.46, 95% CI 1.28-1.66). Subgroup analyses stratified by congenital heart defect status reinforced consistent direction of association. Limitations include reliance on registry-coded exposures potentially subject to misclassification, absence of gestational blood pressure or medication data, and residual confounding by unmeasured maternal characteristics.

Implications Medical Affairs : This work strengthens the case for tighter blood pressure management in pregnancies complicated by fetal aneuploidy, a subgroup currently absent from mainstream hypertension guideline algorithms. For Medical Affairs, it opens a niche for maternal-fetal medicine engagement around antihypertensive safety and efficacy in high-risk pregnancies, particularly for very early preterm birth prevention. KOL strategy should include obstetric medicine specialists and preterm-birth research networks, potentially seeding registry-based real-world studies of specific antihypertensive classes in this population.

Risk factors for carfilzomib-related cardiovascular adverse events in Chinese patients with multiple myeloma: A real-world retrospective cohort study.

Revue : The Journal of international medical research | Tier : NA | IF estime : NA

Date de publication : 2026 08 20

Resultats principaux : This retrospective real-world cohort from Tianjin First Central Hospital analysed 74 Chinese patients with multiple myeloma treated with carfilzomib between August 2022 and April 2026, to characterise the incidence and predictors of cardiovascular adverse events (CVAEs) including severe hypertension, arrhythmia, heart failure, venous thromboembolism, myocardial infarction and angina. CVAEs occurred in 48.6% of patients (36/74), with a median time to first event of 56 days and 67% occurring within the first three months; severe hypertension accounted for 50% of CVAEs and arrhythmia for 47%. Multivariable logistic regression identified elevated baseline N-terminal pro-B-type natriuretic peptide (NT-proBNP) as the single independent predictor of subsequent CVAEs (odds ratio 6.16, p=0.002). The authors propose baseline NT-proBNP as a stratification biomarker to prioritise cardiovascular monitoring during carfilzomib therapy. Limitations include the small single-centre sample, retrospective design, restricted Chinese population generalisability, and lack of ambulatory blood pressure monitoring or standardised echocardiographic follow-up.

Implications Medical Affairs : These findings support formalising cardio-oncology hypertension management pathways during proteasome inhibitor therapy, an area with heterogeneous guideline coverage. For Medical Affairs teams positioning antihypertensive agents in cardio-oncology, this reinforces the value of blood pressure monitoring bundles integrated with NT-proBNP triage for high-risk oncology cohorts. KOL engagement should focus on cardio-oncology thought leaders and haematology-oncology societies to co-develop prospective validation studies and educational content on preventive antihypertensive strategies during carfilzomib exposure.

Phase-Resolved Functional Lung MRI-derived Pulmonary Arterial Pulse Wave Velocity Correlates with 12-Month Mortality in Participants with Pulmonary Hypertension: A Post Hoc Analysis of a Multicenter Study.

Revue : Radiology. Cardiothoracic imaging | Tier : NA | IF estime : NA

Date de publication : 2026 Aug

Resultats principaux : This post hoc analysis of the multicentre CHANGE MRI study evaluated pulmonary arterial pulse wave velocity (PWV) derived from phase-resolved functional lung (PREFUL) MRI in 632 participants with suspected pulmonary hypertension (median age 66 years, 58% female) recruited between July 2016 and November 2023. PWV was calculated from 1.5 T PREFUL MRI images and correlated with right heart catheterisation and cardiac MRI parameters, with 12-month mortality assessed via multivariable Cox proportional hazard modelling. Among 415 participants with confirmed pulmonary hypertension, 13 died within 12 months; PWV was higher in pulmonary arterial hypertension than in chronic thromboembolic pulmonary hypertension (2.76 vs 1.84 m/sec, p<0.001) and independently associated with mortality (hazard ratio 2.28 per SD increase, p<0.001) and with right ventricular ejection fraction (β=-0.024 m/sec per percentage point, p<0.001). PWV also correlated with perfusion defect percentage (β=0.014, p=0.02). Limitations include the post hoc nature of the analysis, relatively small number of mortality events, cohort restriction to suspected chronic thromboembolic pulmonary hypertension referrals, and limited external validation of the PREFUL-based PWV metric.

Implications Medical Affairs : PREFUL-MRI-derived pulmonary PWV shows promise as a non-invasive prognostic biomarker in pulmonary hypertension, which could influence future imaging guidelines and risk stratification pathways. For a Medical Affairs organisation with a pulmonary hypertension portfolio, this signal supports co-developing imaging-based enrichment strategies in future trial designs and reinforces the narrative around comprehensive right-ventricular-pulmonary vascular coupling assessment. Engagement with imaging-oriented pulmonary hypertension KOLs and academic centres running CHANGE MRI can pave the way for prospective validation and companion diagnostic strategies.

Ventricular arrhythmogenesis in pulmonary hypertension: From right ventricular remodeling to clinical implications.

Revue : Physiological reports | Tier : NA | IF estime : NA

Date de publication : 2026 Aug

Resultats principaux : This narrative review synthesises preclinical and clinical evidence on ventricular arrhythmogenesis in pulmonary hypertension (PH), integrating findings on right-ventricular structural, metabolic, neurohumoral, and electrophysiological remodelling. The authors describe premature ventricular contractions and non-sustained ventricular tachycardia as the most frequently reported ventricular arrhythmias in PH, while sustained ventricular tachycardia and ventricular fibrillation remain uncommon. Mechanisms discussed include ion channel remodelling, abnormal calcium handling, hypertrophy, fibrosis and gap junction alterations, autonomic imbalance, myocardial ischemia, inflammation, and oxidative stress, each offering potential therapeutic targets. The review highlights that although atrial arrhythmias dominate PH populations, ventricular arrhythmias contribute meaningfully to poor outcomes and are inadequately characterised in current registries. Limitations include the narrative rather than systematic methodology, absence of quantitative pooled estimates, and heterogeneity of underlying PH subtypes in the source studies.

Implications Medical Affairs : The review reinforces a research agenda in PH-associated ventricular arrhythmia risk that could inform future ESC/ERS pulmonary hypertension guideline updates and cardiac monitoring recommendations. For Medical Affairs, this creates a natural bridge between pulmonary vascular disease portfolios and cardiac electrophysiology teams, opening space for combination-strategy positioning. KOL engagement should target the intersection of PH clinicians and electrophysiologists to shape prospective arrhythmia burden registries and translational research programmes.

T cell receptor repertoire dynamics and newly identified functional regulators of autologous tumor-infiltrating lymphocyte therapy in advanced solid tumors.

Revue : Journal of translational medicine | Tier : NA | IF estime : NA

Date de publication : 2026 08 11

Resultats principaux : This investigator-initiated phase I trial enrolled 12 patients with advanced melanoma, cervical, lung or head and neck cancers between August 2022 and December 2024 to evaluate the safety and preliminary efficacy of autologous tumour-infiltrating lymphocyte (TIL) therapy after cyclophosphamide/fludarabine lymphodepletion and high-dose interleukin-2 support. Primary endpoints included safety, feasibility, objective response rate (ORR) by RECIST 1.1, and correlative T-cell receptor sequencing and transcriptomic profiling of TIL products. The most common adverse events included fever, anemia, nausea, hypertension and hyponatremia; the ORR was 33.3% (one complete and three partial responses) with a disease control rate of 75%. Correlative analyses showed dynamic TCR clonal remodelling in responders and identified ASS1 and CEP20 as genes negatively associated with efficacy, with CRISPR/Cas9 knockout enhancing TIL memory phenotype, cytokine production and cytotoxicity in vitro and in vivo. Limitations include the small phase I sample size, mixed tumour indications, single-centre Asian population restricting generalisability, and lack of a comparator arm or randomised design.

Implications Medical Affairs : While the trial focuses on cell therapy oncology, the emergence of hypertension as a prominent adverse event during high-dose IL-2 supportive care reinforces the need for structured cardio-oncology and antihypertensive support pathways in the TIL-era immunotherapy landscape. For Medical Affairs, this creates an opportunity to reposition antihypertensive management within cellular therapy toxicity guidelines and integrated cardio-oncology programmes. Association of pulmonary artery radiation with pulmonary hypertension after lung cancer radiotherapy.

Revue : Thorax | Tier : NA | IF estime : NA

Date de publication : 2026 08 20

Resultats principaux : This multi-institutional retrospective cohort of 848 non-small cell lung cancer patients (746 discovery, 102 validation; 49.9% women, median age 65 years) evaluated whether radiation dose to the pulmonary arteries (PA) predicts post-radiotherapy pulmonary hypertension (PH). Structures including PA, distal pulmonary vasculature, lungs and heart were segmented and dose-volume parameters extracted at 5 Gy increments, with area under the receiver operating curve (AUROC) analyses and Fine-Gray regressions performed. The 2-year cumulative incidence of PH was 11.6%; PA V10 Gy showed the highest AUROC (0.58) and was significantly associated with PH in both discovery (subdistribution HR 1.01, 95% CI 1.00-1.02, p=0.013) and validation cohorts (sHR 1.03, 95% CI 1.00-1.05, p=0.019). Patients developing post-radiotherapy PH were more likely to have moderate or greater tricuspid disease (18.7% vs 0.2%, p<0.001) and right ventricular systolic dysfunction (3.3% vs 0.2%, p=0.003). Limitations include the retrospective observational design, moderate discrimination of the identified PA V10 Gy metric, potential residual confounding by baseline pulmonary and cardiac comorbidity, and the hypothesis-generating nature acknowledged by the authors.

Implications Medical Affairs : The finding that PA radiation dose independently predicts pulmonary hypertension supports emerging cardio-oncology guideline discussion about PA-sparing radiotherapy planning and prospective PH surveillance in thoracic radiation survivors. For a Medical Affairs organisation with a pulmonary hypertension portfolio, this creates a defined post-radiotherapy PH population for screening, education and clinical development. KOL engagement should target radiation oncology and cardio-oncology thought leaders to co-develop imaging-based dose planning constraints and prospective PH surveillance protocols.

Beyond Heatwaves and Cold Spells: Long-Term Exposure to Short-Duration Extremes, Behavioural Pathways, and Hypertension Risk in a National Chinese Cohort.

Revue : Environmental research | Tier : NA | IF estime : NA

Date de publication : 2026 08 20

Resultats principaux : This prospective cohort analysis used China Health and Nutrition Survey data (n=8,147) to examine whether long-term exposure to short-duration temperature extremes contributes to incident hypertension, using an identification strategy that jointly modelled cold spells, short-duration cold extremes (SD-CEs), heatwaves and short-duration heat extremes (SD-HEs). Cox models and four-way decomposition were applied to quantify direct and behavioural pathway effects, with interactions tested for physical activity, sleep duration and sedentary behaviour. Higher SD-CE and SD-HE exposure was associated with increased hypertension risk (hazard ratio range 1.05-1.06 and 1.04-1.05, respectively), with SD-CE showing a non-linear dose response; cold spells conferred elevated risk only above high-frequency thresholds, and heatwaves were not significantly associated with hypertension. Behavioural interactions revealed that low physical activity attenuated risk while short sleep duration and prolonged sedentary time amplified it. Limitations include exposure assessment based on ambient rather than personal temperature, self-reported lifestyle variables, potential residual confounding by air pollution or socioeconomic status, and cohort restriction to the Chinese population.

Implications Medical Affairs : These findings elevate short-duration temperature extremes as a novel modifiable environmental determinant of hypertension, potentially informing WHO and climate-health guideline updates on cardiovascular risk under climate change. For Medical Affairs, this offers a compelling narrative to embed antihypertensive product positioning within climate-sensitive cardiovascular prevention programmes and public health campaigns. KOL engagement should extend to environmental epidemiology and public health networks to co-develop real-world evidence programmes quantifying antihypertensive impact under climate-vulnerable conditions.

Parental Obesity and Offspring Risk for Cardiorenal Diseases.

Revue : Current hypertension reports | Tier : NA | IF estime : NA

Date de publication : 2026 08 20

Resultats principaux : This narrative review examines current evidence linking parental obesity to offspring cardiorenal and metabolic disease susceptibility, in a context where 40.3% of US men and 39.7% of US women aged 20-39 years are classified as obese. The authors synthesise mechanistic literature indicating that maternal (and, increasingly, paternal) obesity programs offspring vulnerability to hypertension, type 2 diabetes and chronic kidney disease that can emerge early and persist into adulthood, even after adjustment for lifestyle factors. Proposed mechanisms include dysregulation of neurohumoral pathways, chronic inflammation, mitochondrial dysfunction, and altered intercellular signalling transmitted across generations. The review discusses developmental programming during intrauterine and early postnatal periods as central to long-term offspring cardiovascular risk. Limitations include the narrative rather than systematic review design, absence of pooled quantitative estimates, reliance on heterogeneous preclinical and clinical evidence, and limited data on preconception versus prenatal versus postnatal intervention windows.

Implications Medical Affairs : The developmental origins framework outlined here strengthens the argument for repositioning antihypertensive and cardio-metabolic prevention strategies into preconception and early-life windows, an angle largely absent from current adult hypertension guidelines. For Medical Affairs, this creates an integrated cardio-metabolic and maternal health narrative that spans multiple therapeutic areas. KOL strategy should target developmental origins of health and disease investigators and maternal-fetal medicine leaders to seed early-intervention trial concepts and family-based prevention programmes.

NCD-SCAN: A Digital Health Tool for Community-Based Non-Communicable Disease Risk Screening by Lady Health Workers in Pakistan.

Revue : Research square | Tier : NA | IF estime : NA

Date de publication : 2026 08 06

Resultats principaux : This prospective mixed-methods implementation study in Karachi and Hyderabad, Pakistan, evaluated NCD-SCAN, a mobile application integrating validated cardiovascular, metabolic, cerebrovascular, lifestyle and mental health screening tools deployed by Lady Health Workers (LHWs) between June and August 2024. Formative qualitative data were drawn from 13 focus groups with 128 LHWs, and 22 trained LHWs conducted household-based screening in 1,087 community members, with implementation determinants examined via the Consolidated Framework for Implementation Research. High proportions of screen-positive results were observed for stroke symptoms (83.7%), poor mental well-being (64.4%), hypertension (46.3%), angina (32.8%) and diabetes risk (30.1%). LHWs reported strong community trust, enhanced counselling capacity and improved professional credibility, and no data loss or technical failures were reported. Facilitators included multilingual design, workflow integration and community engagement, while barriers included indirect costs, limited digital access and weak referral linkages. Limitations include the short implementation window, restriction to two urban settings, reliance on self-reported feasibility measures, and preprint status pending peer review.

Implications Medical Affairs : The 46.3% hypertension screen-positive rate in a community setting underscores the massive undiagnosed hypertension burden in low- and middle-income countries and supports scaling digital community-based screening as a foundation for public health interventions. For Medical Affairs teams operating in emerging markets, this creates a platform to partner on downstream referral, diagnosis and treatment initiation pathways integrated with government primary care programmes. KOL engagement should extend to public health, digital health and government stakeholders in South Asia to co-develop referral and treatment intensification protocols that convert screening yield into sustained blood pressure control.
