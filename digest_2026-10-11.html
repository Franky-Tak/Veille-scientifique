Modifiable Cardiovascular Risk Factors and Hemorrhagic Presentation in Dural Arteriovenous Fistulas: A Systematic Review With Exploratory Quantitative Synthesis.

Revue : Brain and Behavior | Tier : NA | IF estime : NA

Date de publication : 2026-10

Resultats principaux : This systematic review with exploratory meta-analysis examined the influence of modifiable cardiovascular risk factors (CVRFs) — including hypertension, diabetes, smoking, alcohol use, dyslipidemia, and obesity — on hemorrhagic presentation in dural arteriovenous fistulas (dAVFs), following PRISMA 2020 guidelines across PubMed, Embase, and Scopus. Of 918 records screened, only 3 observational studies met inclusion criteria, totaling 543 patients, with 2 studies (n=281) contributing to the quantitative synthesis and 1 hemorrhage-only registry cohort (n=262) analyzed narratively. Hypertension was the most prevalent CVRF (31.7% of 281 patients) but was not associated with hemorrhagic presentation (OR 0.89, 95% CI 0.47-1.68; I squared=0%), while hyperlipidemia showed extreme heterogeneity (I squared=93.6%) preventing any clinical inference. Data on smoking, alcohol, statins, and ACE inhibitors were sparse, unadjusted, and inconsistent, limiting conclusions about these factors in the dAVF setting. Key limitations include the very small number of qualifying studies, retrospective designs, lack of adjusted analyses, and heterogeneous outcome definitions, confirming that angiographic features remain the most reliable predictors of hemorrhagic presentation in dAVFs.

Implications Medical Affairs : The absence of a significant association between hypertension and hemorrhagic dAVF presentation, while consistent with current angiographic risk-stratification paradigms, does not diminish the critical need for BP control in this population given broader cardiovascular risk reduction imperatives, and underscores the opportunity to position antihypertensives within integrated neurovascular risk management frameworks. From a product positioning perspective, this study highlights a substantial evidence gap regarding hypertension-modifying interventions in neurovascular conditions, which could be leveraged to develop differentiated messaging around antihypertensive use in high-risk neurological comorbidities. The paucity of high-quality prospective data on CVRFs in dAVFs represents an unmet medical need, suggesting an actionable opportunity for KOL engagement in designing multicenter prospective studies that could generate data supporting broader indications for cardiovascular risk management therapies.

Knowledge, Attitudes, and Reported Practices Regarding Prediabetes Among Hypertensive Patients With Abdominal Obesity: A Cross-Sectional Structural Equation Modeling Analysis.

Revue : Journal of Diabetes Research | Tier : NA | IF estime : NA

Date de publication : 2026

Resultats principaux : This cross-sectional survey assessed prediabetes-related knowledge, attitudes, and practices (KAP) among 446 hypertensive adults with abdominal obesity recruited between April and June 2025 from community health service centers in Nantong, China, using validated questionnaires and structural equation modeling (SEM). Mean scores were 8.90 plus or minus 2.14 for knowledge, 37.31 plus or minus 3.68 for attitude, and 10.75 plus or minus 2.38 for the four-item practice scale; SEM showed acceptable model fit (CMIN/DF=2.127, RMSEA=0.050, CFI=0.925). Knowledge was weakly but significantly associated with attitude (beta=0.160, p=0.012) and attitude was more strongly associated with practice (beta=0.281, p=0.004), while the direct knowledge-to-practice path was not statistically significant (beta=0.069, p=0.402). The model-based indirect effect of knowledge on practice via attitude was 0.045 (95% CI 0.016-0.091; p=0.004), confirming attitude as the key mediating pathway between knowledge and behavior change in this population. Significant limitations include low convergent validity of the practice construct (AVE=0.199; CR=0.464), marked ceiling effects in attitude responses, cross-sectional design precluding causal inference, and absence of objective glycemic assessment to validate self-reported practices.

Implications Medical Affairs : The identification of attitude as the critical mediator between knowledge and practice in hypertensive patients with abdominal obesity underscores the need for behavioral change interventions that move beyond patient education to address motivational and attitudinal barriers, directly relevant to developing patient support programs for antihypertensive and cardiometabolic product lines. This study supports the value proposition of integrated patient support programs that combine educational components with motivational strategies and behavioral coaching, which can serve as differentiators for antihypertensive product portfolios targeting high-risk metabolic syndrome populations. The well-defined triple comorbidity profile of this study population (hypertension plus abdominal obesity plus prediabetes) represents a compelling target for KOL-led real-world evidence initiatives and joint scientific symposia, particularly as combination cardiometabolic therapies increasingly address multiple risk factors within a single treatment strategy.

The Japanese Society of Hypertension Practical Consensus Guidelines for Seasonal Blood Pressure Management.

Revue : Hypertension Research | Tier : NA | IF estime : NA

Date de publication : 2026-10-10

Resultats principaux : This expert consensus document from the Japanese Society of Hypertension (JSH) addresses seasonal blood pressure (BP) variation management, covering ten key clinical questions related to cold-season BP elevations and cardiovascular events, summer dehydration with excessive BP reduction, and nighttime BP increases, establishing seasonal BP management as an emerging discipline termed "Implementation Hypertension." The document synthesizes evidence linking not only outdoor temperature but also indoor temperature, within-home temperature differentials, living environments, and socioeconomic factors to BP fluctuations, reflecting recent advances in environmental epidemiology. Evidence-based and consensus-derived practical guidance is provided for BP assessment modalities, lifestyle interventions targeting thermal environmental factors (including appropriate indoor temperature ranges), and pharmacological adjustments of antihypertensive therapy across seasonal changes. The guidelines explicitly call for standardization of temperature exposure indices, accumulation of year-round home BP monitoring data, and development of intervention models for vulnerable populations (elderly, low-income housing). Limitations include the consensus-based nature of many recommendations, geographic specificity to Japan with its distinct climate patterns and traditional housing characteristics, and the relatively nascent evidence base for residential environment-level interventions on BP outcomes.

Implications Medical Affairs : These JSH guidelines elevate seasonal BP management to a formal clinical priority, creating a strategic opportunity for antihypertensive product positioning that emphasizes dosing flexibility, tolerability across seasonal BP variation, and suitability for year-round home blood pressure monitoring programs that align with the guidelines' data collection imperatives. The concept of "Implementation Hypertension" that bridges individual patient management with housing, community, and governmental interventions represents a public health narrative that Medical Affairs teams can leverage to engage a broader and more influential ecosystem of KOLs including epidemiologists, environmental health scientists, housing policy experts, and community medicine specialists. The explicit call for year-round home BP data collection and digital monitoring infrastructure aligns directly with digital health and connected device strategies, making this guideline immediately actionable for companies with BP monitoring companion products, telehealth platforms, or integrated care solutions.

Hypertension: a missing piece in HFpEF?

Revue : Hypertension Research | Tier : NA | IF estime : NA

Date de publication : 2026-10-09

Resultats principaux : This narrative review examines the pathophysiological link between hypertension and heart failure with preserved ejection fraction (HFpEF), characterizing hypertension as a central hub connecting cardiovascular and metabolic abnormalities including obesity and atrial fibrillation (AF), as the burden of HFpEF continues to rise with population aging and prevalent unhealthy lifestyles. The review synthesizes current evidence on sympathovagal imbalance as a common underlying mechanism for hypertension, obesity, and AF, with particular focus on autonomic dysregulation as a pivotal driver of HFpEF pathogenesis and disease progression. Based on current evidence synthesis, the authors propose that a systolic blood pressure target of less than 130 mmHg may be appropriate for patients with HFpEF, aligning with broader cardiovascular risk reduction goals and potentially tightening treatment thresholds in this difficult-to-treat population. Emerging neuromodulatory approaches — specifically low-level transcutaneous tragus stimulation targeting parasympathetic activation — are highlighted as promising adjunctive strategies that may complement pharmacological antihypertensive therapy in the management of HFpEF. The review is limited by its narrative, non-systematic design, the heterogeneity of the HFpEF population studied in included evidence, and the relatively early stage of validation for neuromodulatory interventions, most of which require large randomized controlled trials.

Implications Medical Affairs : The positioning of hypertension as a pivotal, modifiable pathophysiological link in HFpEF directly strengthens the therapeutic rationale for early and intensive BP control in this population, reinforcing the value proposition for antihypertensive agents with cardiometabolic profile benefits in a growing and currently undertreated heart failure indication. The proposal of a systolic BP target below 130 mmHg in HFpEF patients carries significant Medical Affairs implications for guideline alignment strategies and label positioning, particularly for products that have demonstrated efficacy at aggressive BP targets in heart failure-adjacent or comorbid populations, supporting advocacy for inclusion in upcoming HFpEF-specific guideline updates. The identification of autonomic modulation as an emerging therapeutic pathway creates novel cross-specialty KOL engagement opportunities, enabling Medical Affairs strategy to bridge cardiologists, electrophysiologists, and heart failure specialists around hypertension's central and underappreciated role in the HFpEF syndrome.
