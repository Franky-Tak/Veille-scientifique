HIV-associated pulmonary arterial hypertension: improving survival despite persistent treatment gaps - a systematic review and meta-analysis.

Revue : AIDS (London, England) | Tier : NA | IF estime : NA

Date de publication : 2026-10-01

Resultats principaux : This systematic review and meta-analysis pooled data from 12 right heart catheterization-confirmed cohorts comprising 537 patients with HIV-associated pulmonary arterial hypertension (HIV-PAH) observed between 1986 and 2021. HIV-PAH presents with severe hemodynamic compromise and advanced functional limitation at the time of diagnosis, underscoring the late stage at which patients typically come to clinical attention. Only 64% of patients were receiving PAH-targeted therapy at the time of diagnosis, revealing a substantial and persistent treatment gap across the entire study period. Survival rates improved progressively over the three decades studied, closely paralleling the increasing use of PAH-targeted combination therapies over time. The study did not stratify findings by geographic region or antiretroviral therapy status, which limits generalizability to modern HIV treatment paradigms where effective viral suppression is near-universal.

Implications Medical Affairs : This meta-analysis quantifies the underdiagnosis and undertreatment of HIV-PAH, creating a compelling platform for educational initiatives targeting HIV care specialists and cardiologists on the importance of early PAH screening and referral in HIV-positive patients. From a Medical Affairs perspective, the demonstrated improvement in survival with PAH-targeted combination therapy supports positioning modern PAH agents within the HIV comorbidity space as an addressable unmet clinical need with a clear evidence base. Engagement with pulmonary hypertension and HIV KOLs to advocate for routine right heart catheterization screening protocols in high-risk HIV-positive populations represents a high-priority action for advancing both patient outcomes and medical strategy.

Effect of Hypertension on the Health-related Quality of Life of People Living with Hypertension in Dschang, Cameroon: Comparative Analytical Cross-section.

Revue : Journal of Epidemiology and Global Health | Tier : NA | IF estime : NA

Date de publication : 2026-09-25

Resultats principaux : This comparative analytical cross-sectional study enrolled 196 participants (98 hypertensive, 98 age-, sex-, and location-matched controls) across three health facilities in the Dschang Health District of Cameroon, with a median age of 66 years and 62.2% female. Health-related quality of life (HRQoL) was assessed using the Health Utilities Index Mark 3 (HUI3), and comparisons used multivariable linear and logistic regression. People with hypertension had significantly poorer HRQoL than non-hypertensive controls, with a mean HUI3 score difference of 0.2017 (p<0.001), more than sixfold greater than the minimal clinically important difference of 0.03. Diabetes was markedly more prevalent among hypertensive participants (OR=30.96, 95% CI: 6.87–139.51, p=0.001), while neither duration of hypertension nor overall comorbidity burden independently predicted HRQoL reduction, suggesting that even early-stage disease substantially impairs quality of life. Limitations include the cross-sectional design, facility-based recruitment introducing selection bias, and small sample size restricting subgroup analyses.

Implications Medical Affairs : These findings quantify the substantial HRQoL burden of hypertension in sub-Saharan Africa, providing patient-reported outcome evidence that can anchor health economic models and value-based arguments for antihypertensive pharmacotherapy in this region. For Medical Affairs, data from Cameroon reinforce the case for addressing the hypertension treatment gap in low-resource African environments, aligning with global cardiovascular disease prevention initiatives and supporting access programs. KOL engagement with African academic cardiologists and public health leaders on patient-reported outcomes in hypertension management could strengthen market positioning strategies for products with documented HRQoL benefits.

Association of monocyte to high-density lipoprotein cholesterol ratio with non-calcified plaques in subclinical coronary atherosclerosis: a cross-sectional study in hypertensive patients.

Revue : Frontiers in Endocrinology | Tier : NA | IF estime : NA

Date de publication : 2026-09-17

Resultats principaux : This cross-sectional study enrolled 283 asymptomatic hypertensive patients who underwent coronary CT angiography (CCTA) at Shenzhen Traditional Chinese Medicine Hospital between January 2019 and September 2024, stratified into quartiles by monocyte-to-HDL-cholesterol ratio (MHR). After full multivariable adjustment for age, sex, smoking, BMI, triglycerides, LDL-C, serum creatinine, statin use, and diabetes, patients in the highest MHR quartile had a 5.97-fold greater risk of non-calcified plaques compared to the lowest quartile (OR=5.97, 95% CI: 2.63–14.1, p<0.001). Restricted cubic spline analysis demonstrated a significant non-linear positive association between MHR and non-calcified plaque risk (p for nonlinearity=0.007). The AUC of MHR for identifying non-calcified plaques was 0.700, indicating moderate discriminatory ability. No significant interaction was detected between MHR and age, sex, diabetes, smoking, BMI, or statin use, suggesting consistent association across subgroups, though the single-center Chinese cohort limits broader generalizability.

Implications Medical Affairs : MHR emerges as a simple, accessible composite biomarker integrating inflammation and lipid metabolism that may enhance cardiovascular risk stratification beyond traditional factors in asymptomatic hypertensive patients, offering a novel tool for identifying those at highest risk for vulnerable plaque. For Medical Affairs, these findings support the growing evidence base for inflammatory-lipid pathways in atherosclerotic plaque formation in hypertension, enriching the scientific narrative for anti-inflammatory or lipid-lowering agents with plaque-modifying properties. Collaboration with Chinese cardiology KOLs on prospective multicenter validation of MHR-guided risk stratification could strengthen evidence generation for hypertension complications prevention and support differentiation of the product portfolio.

Revue : Frontiers in Neurology | Tier : NA | IF estime : NA

Date de publication : 2026-09-17

Resultats principaux : This retrospective cohort study reviewed 314 patients aged 18–49 with radiologically confirmed stroke admitted to a secondary-care hospital in Ras Al Khaimah, UAE, between January 2020 and March 2026, of whom 81.2% were male and 65.6% were South Asian, reflecting the UAE labor force demographic. Despite only 49.0% of patients having documented hypertension prior to admission, 74.5% had elevated blood pressure on admission, revealing a profound systematic underdiagnosis gap. Similarly, dyslipidemia was documented in only 15.9% of patients, yet an abnormal lipid profile was found in 73.9% of those tested, and 29% of all diabetes cases were newly diagnosed at the time of stroke admission. Small-vessel occlusion was the leading ischemic subtype, and diabetes was the only factor independently associated with ischemic stroke presentation (adjusted OR=2.34, 95% CI: 1.35–4.07). Limitations include the retrospective single-center design, reliance on administrative records for pre-existing condition documentation, and incomplete laboratory testing in some patients.

Implications Medical Affairs : The profound underdiagnosis of hypertension among young South Asian workers in the UAE underscores an urgent public health need for occupational health screening programs in Gulf region migrant populations, representing both an ethical imperative and an unmet pharmaceutical market. For Medical Affairs, this study highlights a large population of undiagnosed hypertensive patients in Gulf region workforces as a target for disease awareness campaigns and real-world evidence programs demonstrating the impact of early treatment on stroke prevention. Strategic partnerships with occupational health organizations and South Asian cardiology networks in the Gulf Cooperation Council region could support KOL engagement, early intervention program development, and market access discussions.

The relationship between overweight or obesity, elevated blood pressure and the overall quality of life in medical students, Poland.

Revue : Frontiers in Public Health | Tier : NA | IF estime : NA

Date de publication : 2026-09-17

Resultats principaux : This prospective longitudinal POLLEK study enrolled 887 first-year medical students from the Medical University of Silesia in Poland, with 705 followed at the end of year two, assessing anthropometrics, blood pressure, and QoL using the WHOQOL-BREF questionnaire at both time points. Prevalence of overweight or obesity increased slightly from 12.9% to 13.6% over follow-up and was significantly higher among male students at both time points. A small but statistically significant increase in both BMI and blood pressure values was observed over the two years, with the highest rates of elevated blood pressure found in obese students (p<0.0001). Overall QoL scores did not differ significantly across BMI categories, and logistic regression identified poor financial situation (OR=1.669–1.910) and chronic disease history (OR=2.153–2.228) as the primary independent predictors of reduced QoL. Limitations include the single-institution study design, self-reported QoL data, and potential healthy-worker effect, as medical students represent a select, health-conscious population.

Implications Medical Affairs : This longitudinal study demonstrates that blood pressure elevation tracks with progressive weight gain even in young, educated healthcare professionals, supporting early preventive intervention before clinical hypertension is established and providing a rationale for pharmacological and lifestyle interventions in pre-hypertensive populations. For Medical Affairs, these findings reinforce the value of targeting cardiometabolic risk clusters early in life, which could support positioning of preventive antihypertensive approaches and weight management therapies within younger demographic segments. Engagement with academic medical education institutions and health behavior KOLs in Poland and Central Europe could generate complementary evidence for the case that early-stage intervention reduces long-term hypertension burden.

Hobby engagement and hypertension risk: evidence from two large prospective cohorts.

Revue : BMC Public Health | Tier : NA | IF estime : NA

Date de publication : 2026-07-27

Resultats principaux : This prospective cohort study analyzed data from 7,112 participants aged ≥50 from the China Health and Retirement Longitudinal Study (CHARLS) and 4,937 participants aged ≥50 from the US Health and Retirement Study (HRS), using Cox proportional hazards models to estimate the relationship between hobby engagement and incident hypertension. Baseline hobby engagement was associated with significantly lower hypertension risk in both CHARLS (HR=0.84, 95% CI: 0.76–0.93) and HRS (HR=0.86, 95% CI: 0.78–0.96) after full covariate adjustment. Persistent hobby engagement over time yielded the strongest protective effect in both cohorts (CHARLS: HR=0.77, 95% CI: 0.68–0.88; HRS: HR=0.83, 95% CI: 0.73–0.95). Hobby engagement was also associated with reductions in systolic BP (−0.52 mmHg/year) and diastolic BP (−0.24 mmHg/year) in CHARLS, though this blood pressure reduction was not replicated in HRS, suggesting potential cultural or measurement heterogeneity. Limitations include self-reported hobby assessment, potential residual confounding by unmeasured physical activity, and between-cohort differences in how hobby participation is defined and captured.

Implications Medical Affairs : The consistent protective association between hobby engagement and hypertension incidence across two large cohorts from distinct cultural contexts strengthens the evidence base for psychosocial and behavioral factors as modifiable hypertension risk reducers, supporting a holistic non-pharmacological prevention paradigm. For Medical Affairs, these findings can enrich the scientific messaging around hypertension management to position antihypertensive agents within a comprehensive lifestyle-plus-medication treatment framework that resonates with both clinicians and patients. Leveraging these data in KOL-directed scientific exchange on non-pharmacological interventions could support guideline advocacy and patient education initiatives emphasizing the complementary role of behavioral health in blood pressure control.

Diagnostic challenges in primary aldosteronism evaluation and finerenone use.

Revue : Journal of Medical Case Reports | Tier : NA | IF estime : NA

Date de publication : 2026-09-19

Resultats principaux : This case report describes a 70-year-old Han Chinese male with a 20-year history of treatment-resistant hypertension who presented with normokalemia and normal plasma aldosterone levels, creating a challenging diagnostic scenario for primary aldosteronism (PA). Despite normal absolute aldosterone, laboratory evaluation revealed suppressed direct renin concentration and a markedly elevated aldosterone-to-renin ratio (ARR), raising PA suspicion, confirmed radiologically by bilateral nodular adrenal thickening on CT. Blood pressure remained persistently uncontrolled on triple antihypertensive therapy with amlodipine besylate, valsartan/hydrochlorothiazide, and bisoprolol, highlighting the resistance pattern typical of secondary hypertension. Spironolactone was added but subsequently discontinued due to intolerance; finerenone, a nonsteroidal mineralocorticoid receptor antagonist (MRA), was then substituted with successful blood pressure control and normalization of plasma renin concentration and ARR at follow-up. As a single case report, the findings lack statistical power and cannot be generalized, but provide clinically valuable detail on finerenone as an alternative MRA in PA-with-spironolactone-intolerance, a context underrepresented in the literature.

Implications Medical Affairs : This case report adds to the emerging clinical literature positioning finerenone as a well-tolerated alternative mineralocorticoid receptor antagonist for patients with primary aldosteronism who are intolerant to spironolactone, strengthening its scientific narrative in secondary and resistant hypertension beyond its established indication in chronic kidney disease with type 2 diabetes. For Medical Affairs, this real-world clinical experience represents an opportunity to build a case series and registry-based evidence program around finerenone use in PA, targeting nephrologists, endocrinologists, and cardiologists who encounter normokalemic PA patients who are often underdiagnosed. Development of medical education materials on the diagnostic complexity of PA—particularly normal-range aldosterone presentations—and the role of nonsteroidal MRAs could be a high-value strategic action to address this underdiagnosis gap.

Cardiovascular Outcomes Among Adults with Type 2 Diabetes or Obesity Receiving Carbohydrate-Reduced Nutrition via Telemedicine: A Retrospective Claims-Based Cohort Study.

Revue : Cardiology and Therapy | Tier : NA | IF estime : NA

Date de publication : 2026-10-02

Resultats principaux : This retrospective propensity score-matched cohort study compared 4,877 adults with type 2 diabetes or obesity receiving individualized telemedicine-based carbohydrate-restricted nutritional therapy (INT) to 4,877 propensity-matched controls, with a mean age of 51 years and 60.3% female. INT was significantly associated with lower incidence of MACE-3 (4.1 vs. 9.3 per 1,000 person-years; HR=0.44, 95% CI: 0.29–0.65, p<0.001), MACE-6 (5.7 vs. 10.8; HR=0.52, 95% CI: 0.37–0.73, p<0.001), all new-onset CVD (27.7 vs. 36.9; HR=0.70, 95% CI: 0.59–0.82, p<0.001), and new-onset hypertension (41.8 vs. 49.3 per 1,000 person-years; HR=0.81, 95% CI: 0.70–0.93, p<0.001). Notably, the 19% relative risk reduction for new-onset hypertension is clinically relevant given the large at-risk population of adults with diabetes and obesity. Limitations include the retrospective claims-based design introducing potential selection bias, the US-based single-provider setting limiting global generalizability, and the absence of direct adherence measurement for the dietary intervention.

Implications Medical Affairs : This large propensity-matched study provides compelling evidence that individualized carbohydrate-restricted telemedicine-delivered nutritional interventions can substantially reduce cardiovascular events and incident hypertension in high-risk metabolic populations, reinforcing the clinical value of integrated digital lifestyle approaches as a primary prevention strategy. For Medical Affairs, these data support the narrative that digital therapeutic programs and pharmacotherapy are complementary in hypertension and cardiometabolic disease management, strengthening the positioning of antihypertensive agents within multimodal treatment frameworks increasingly endorsed by payers and guidelines. Collaborating with digital health and nutrition KOLs to design prospective randomized trials combining pharmacological and telemedicine-delivered dietary interventions could yield high-impact evidence suitable for guideline-level recommendations and label expansion discussions.

Development and validation of a LASSO-selected machine learning prediction pipeline for predicting coronary artery calcification in a health check-up population.

Revue : Frontiers in Medicine | Tier : NA | IF estime : NA

Date de publication : 2026-09-17

Resultats principaux : This retrospective study developed and internally validated a LASSO-selected, class-weighted, and probability-calibrated logistic regression machine learning model to predict coronary artery calcification (CAC) positivity in 4,465 asymptomatic adults undergoing routine health check-ups, using stratified sampling to split participants into training (n=3,125) and testing (n=1,340) cohorts. LASSO regression selected 13 predictors including sex, age, pulse, systolic and diastolic blood pressure, hypertension, diabetes, LDL-C, serum creatinine, uric acid, non-alcoholic fatty liver disease, smoking, and alcohol use. The final model achieved an AUC of 0.816 in training and 0.771 (95% CI: 0.713–0.824) in testing, demonstrating acceptable discrimination. SHAP (SHapley Additive exPlanations) analysis identified age, sex, hypertension, pulse, and NAFLD as the five most influential predictors, confirming hypertension as a dominant driver of CAC risk in this screening population. Limitations include single-center internal validation only, requirement for multicenter external validation before clinical implementation, and incomplete standardization of risk factor collection across health check-up sites.

Implications Medical Affairs : Machine learning-based CAC prediction models incorporating hypertension as a key predictor can serve as cost-effective, radiation-free gatekeeping tools to identify asymptomatic hypertensive patients who warrant advanced cardiovascular imaging or intensified preventive treatment, representing a precision medicine advance with significant clinical utility. For Medical Affairs, this type of AI-assisted cardiovascular risk stratification aligns with the broader scientific narrative of early identification and targeted intervention in hypertension-related subclinical atherosclerosis, supporting educational and publication strategies positioning the company as a leader in precision hypertension management. Collaborating with digital health and AI cardiology KOLs on prospective multicenter external validation studies could accelerate translation of such models into clinical practice and provide differentiated real-world evidence for the benefit of early antihypertensive intervention.
