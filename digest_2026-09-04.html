Body composition displays expected and unexpected contributions to blood pressure and glycemia in young South African women.

Revue : BMJ Open Diabetes Research & Care | Tier : NA | IF estime : NA

Date de publication : 2026-09-03

Resultats principaux : This cross-sectional study enrolled 7,735 young women (median age 22.4 years) in Soweto, South Africa, undergoing screening for a clinical trial, using whole-body DEXA scanning to characterize 12 body composition measures as predictors of glycemia (HbA1c), systolic BP, and diastolic BP. The study population had high rates of overweight/obesity (49.6%), pre-diabetes/diabetes (10.9%), and hypertension (11.9%), reflecting the rapid cardiometabolic transition underway in sub-Saharan Africa. All 12 body composition measures were significantly associated with both systolic and diastolic BP; lean mass indices explained 5.8-6.3% of variance in systolic BP, while adiposity measures (android fat, subcutaneous adipose tissue, total fat mass) explained 4.6-4.8% of variance in diastolic BP. Paradoxically, adiposity measures showed only exceedingly modest associations with HbA1c (explaining 0.14-0.37% of variance), suggesting an atypical cardiometabolic phenotype in which the link between adiposity and dysglycemia is much weaker than expected. Key limitations include the cross-sectional design preventing causal inference, the exclusively young female and single-site study sample limiting generalizability, and clinical-trial recruitment context introducing potential selection bias.

Implications Medical Affairs : The finding that lean mass is a stronger predictor of systolic BP than adiposity in young African women challenges traditional adiposity-centric paradigms, with direct implications for hypertension risk stratification models used in sub-Saharan African markets and requiring attention from Medical Affairs teams operating in these regions. For KOL engagement, this data supports collaboration with sub-Saharan epidemiologists and cardiometabolic specialists to co-develop regionally tailored educational content on hypertension risk factors that goes beyond BMI and general obesity metrics. Investment in real-world evidence studies capturing body composition-BP relationships across African urban and rural populations could strengthen product differentiation and advocacy for earlier hypertension screening programs in this rapidly urbanizing demographic.

Addressing risk factors for hypertensive disorders in pregnancy: A case study of a quality improvement program in two states in Nigeria.

Revue : PloS One | Tier : NA | IF estime : NA

Date de publication : 2026-09-03

Resultats principaux : This prospective quality improvement (QI) study was implemented from 2019 to 2022 across 40 public and private health facilities in the Federal Capital Territory and Lagos State of Nigeria, targeting early detection and management of hypertensive disorders of pregnancy (HDP) and co-morbid risk factors (anemia, diabetes) at antenatal care (ANC), postnatal care (PNC), and family planning (FP) touchpoints. Among 93,384 ANC visits, the median proportion of women receiving blood pressure, anemia, and diabetes checks increased substantially from 44%, 48%, and 32% at baseline (October 2019-March 2020) to 89%, 91%, and 87% by program end (July-December 2022). PNC BP documentation improved from 81% to 90% across 57,658 PNC visits, and FP visit BP checks rose from 73% to 98% across 105,237 FP encounters. Pre-program facility assessments revealed critical gaps in provider knowledge and observed adherence to HDP management best practices, underscoring the effectiveness of targeted training and system-level interventions in this high-burden, resource-limited context. Limitations include the absence of a concurrent control group preventing definitive causal attribution, potential Hawthorne effects in participating facilities, and limited data on clinical outcomes (maternal mortality, severe morbidity) beyond process indicators.

Implications Medical Affairs : The dramatic improvements in HDP screening coverage achieved in this QI program demonstrate the scalability of structured intervention models in sub-Saharan Africa, creating an evidence base that Medical Affairs teams can reference when engaging international maternal health organizations and governmental stakeholders on the importance of systematic hypertension monitoring in pregnancy. Companies with antihypertensive products relevant to pregnancy or the peripartum period should monitor emerging WHO and African regional guidelines, as evidence from scalable HDP programs like this one may accelerate updated treatment recommendations and expand therapeutic indications. Engaging leading maternal-fetal medicine specialists and public health researchers from Nigeria and similar high-burden settings as scientific advisors would strengthen real-world evidence generation, data dissemination, and Medical Affairs scientific credibility in an underrepresented but clinically significant global population.

Therapeutic Reversal of PH-HFpEF via Lung Spheroid Cell Exosomes.

Revue : ACS Nano | Tier : NA | IF estime : NA

Date de publication : 2026-09-01

Resultats principaux : This preclinical study investigated lung spheroid cell-derived exosomes (LSC-Exos) as a therapeutic modality in a murine model (C57BL/6 male mice) of pulmonary hypertension-associated heart failure with preserved ejection fraction (PH-HFpEF) induced by high-fat diet feeding, with disease phenotype validated by echocardiography and histologic assessment. Mice with established PH-HFpEF receiving daily nebulized LSC-Exo for 3 weeks showed significant improvements in right and left ventricular structural parameters, reduced regurgitation velocity, and attenuated pulmonary vascular wall thickening compared with untreated controls. Transcriptomic profiling revealed activation of PI3K-Akt signaling in the lung and suppression of downstream mTOR signaling in the heart, with marked upregulation of miR-150-5p in cardiac tissue following LSC-Exo treatment. Mechanistic validation using miR-150-5p-enriched exosomes recapitulated the majority of functional and structural improvements, while miR-150-5p inhibitor-loaded exosomes attenuated therapeutic efficacy, identifying this microRNA as the key active mediator of LSC-Exo benefit. Limitations include the animal-only study design without human validation, single-sex study population (male mice), single disease model, unresolved manufacturing scalability challenges, and open questions around clinical dosing and inhaled delivery of exosome-based biologics.

Implications Medical Affairs : Although this study concerns pulmonary rather than systemic hypertension, the PI3K-Akt-mTOR pathway modulation via inhaled exosomal miRNA represents an emerging therapeutic concept in cardiopulmonary disease that Medical Affairs innovation intelligence functions should track, particularly given the close clinical association between pulmonary hypertension, HFpEF, and systemic hypertension in real-world patients. The broader field of extracellular vesicle therapeutics is advancing rapidly, and positioning of conventional antihypertensive agents alongside novel cardioprotective biologics may require proactive competitive intelligence monitoring as these modalities approach clinical stages. Engagement with academic groups working on exosome delivery platforms—particularly in the context of HFpEF, an area of high unmet medical need strongly linked to inadequately controlled arterial hypertension—could support long-term scientific leadership and KOL network development at the cardiovascular biology frontier.

Diagnosis, clinical assessment, and staging of hepatocellular carcinoma: a Brazilian multidisciplinary consensus.

Revue : Arquivos brasileiros de cirurgia digestiva (ABCD) | Tier : NA | IF estime : NA

Date de publication : 2026-08-31

Resultats principaux : This Brazilian multidisciplinary expert consensus was developed by 43 specialists across 10 disciplines (surgical oncology, hepatology, clinical oncology, radiology, interventional radiology, pathology, liver transplantation, gastroenterology, radiation oncology, and palliative care) under the coordination of the Brazilian Society of Surgical Oncology and 13 collaborating national medical societies, covering the diagnosis, clinical assessment, and staging of hepatocellular carcinoma (HCC). The panel formulated 18 recommendations addressing radiological and histopathological diagnosis (including standardized LI-RADS application), management of indeterminate lesions, biopsy indications, immunohistochemical markers, assessment of hepatic function and portal hypertension, staging systems, diagnostic work-up, and multidisciplinary care. Portal hypertension was specifically identified as a key prognostic factor and staging determinant in HCC management, integrated alongside tumor burden, liver function, and patient performance status for individualized therapeutic decision-making. While portal hypertension is central to HCC staging, this consensus document does not address systemic arterial hypertension management or antihypertensive pharmacotherapy; its relevance to arterial hypertension surveillance is indirect, pertaining to the cardiovascular-hepatic comorbidity interface. Limitations include the inherent limitations of consensus methodology in the absence of high-quality randomized trial evidence for all recommendations, and the Brazilian-specific regulatory and healthcare context limiting direct international applicability.

Implications Medical Affairs : Although this consensus primarily addresses portal hypertension in the context of liver malignancy, it underscores the clinical importance of comprehensive hypertension assessment—including portal hypertension—in oncology settings, which is relevant for Medical Affairs teams tracking comorbidity management at the oncology-cardiovascular interface. For companies with antihypertensive products used in patients with concurrent liver disease or malignancy, understanding how portal hypertension is assessed and staged in national guidelines can inform labeling considerations, drug interaction communications, and support for multidisciplinary tumor board discussions. Building scientific relationships with hepatology and surgical oncology KOLs in Brazil who are active in multidisciplinary consensus development may provide Medical Affairs access to emerging evidence and policymaking discussions on hypertension management in patients with complex oncological and hepatic comorbidities.

A decomposition analysis to evaluate the role of social, chemical, and neighborhood factors on the Black/White disparity in preterm birth in Greater Houston, Texas.

Revue : Environmental Epidemiology (Philadelphia, Pa.) | Tier : NA | IF estime : NA

Date de publication : 2026-09-01

Resultats principaux : This cross-sectional study recruited 1,847 Black and White women at time of delivery between 2021 and 2023 in Greater Houston, Texas, using two-fold Blinder-Oaxaca decomposition to quantify contributions of individual- and neighborhood-level factors across six domains (sociodemographics, perinatal history, comorbidities/health behaviors, chemical exposures, social factors, neighborhood characteristics) to the Black/White disparity in preterm birth. Black mothers had a 6.6% higher prevalence of preterm birth compared with White mothers; the included variables explained 63.0% of this disparity, with perinatal history explaining 36.3% and neighborhood characteristics 32.8% of the explained portion. Chronic hypertension before pregnancy (16.8%) and hypertensive disorders during the index pregnancy (16.7%) were among the individual variables contributing most substantially to the racial disparity, surpassed only by residing in a racially minoritized and economically disadvantaged neighborhood (19.2%) and having a prior pregnancy complication (18.5%). Chemical exposures contributed relatively less to the disparity within this analytic framework, highlighting the dominant role of structural and medical risk factors. Limitations include the cross-sectional design precluding causal inference, recruitment from a single metropolitan area limiting geographic generalizability, and reliance on self-report for some exposure variables.

Implications Medical Affairs : The substantial contribution of both chronic hypertension and hypertensive disorders of pregnancy to racial disparities in preterm birth reinforces the urgent public health case for equitable hypertension screening and management among women of reproductive age, an area where Medical Affairs can proactively support awareness programs and equity-focused treatment access initiatives. This epidemiological evidence is directly actionable for KOL strategy: engaging maternal-fetal medicine specialists, environmental health researchers, and social determinants of health experts in hypertension-focused advisory boards can broaden the scientific dialogue around prevention opportunities and underscore the systemic burden of undertreated hypertension in racially and socioeconomically disadvantaged populations. Real-world evidence demonstrating the cardiovascular and obstetric burden attributable to uncontrolled hypertension in underserved populations can strengthen payer engagement and health equity frameworks for antihypertensive therapies, particularly in the context of value-based care models emerging in US healthcare.

Specific aspects of kidney care for Africans living in Europe.

Revue : Nephrology, Dialysis, Transplantation | Tier : NA | IF estime : NA

Date de publication : 2026-09-03

Resultats principaux : This narrative review synthesized evidence on the disproportionate burden of chronic kidney disease (CKD) among people of African ancestry living in Europe, examining genetic susceptibility factors (APOL1 risk variants, sickle-cell nephropathy), early-life determinants (low birth weight), modifiable risk factors (hypertension, diabetes, recurrent acute kidney injury), and structural drivers (poverty, limited nephrology access, systemic racism, medical mistrust). Hypertension was explicitly identified as the leading modifiable CKD risk factor in African-ancestry populations, often presenting with greater severity and worse control than in other ethnic groups; culturally-tailored community interventions (barbershop and church-based blood pressure programs) were cited as evidence-supported strategies to improve BP control in this population. The review critically addressed the historical inclusion of a race coefficient in eGFR equations, arguing it embeds a social label into a biological formula and potentially delays CKD recognition; current guidance supports race-free equations and selective use of ancestry-informed genetic tools such as APOL1 testing within ethical frameworks. Across Africa, glomerulonephritis, HIV-related nephropathy, and heat- or toxin-related kidney injury remain major CKD causes, compounded by scarce diagnostic capacity and low nephrologist density, likely contributing to CKD burden in diaspora populations. Key limitations include the narrative review design prone to selection bias, European healthcare focus potentially limiting broader applicability, and absence of quantitative evidence synthesis from primary studies.

Implications Medical Affairs : Hypertension's identification as the leading modifiable CKD risk factor in African-ancestry European populations strengthens the scientific case for equity-focused antihypertensive treatment programs and supports positioning of products with established CKD-protective properties (RAS inhibitors, SGLT2 inhibitors) in this high-risk, ethnically diverse population segment within EU markets. Medical Affairs teams should closely monitor evolving European nephrology guidelines on race-free eGFR calculation and APOL1-informed risk stratification, as these regulatory and algorithmic changes may substantially impact prescribing patterns, clinical decision-support tools, and future labeling discussions across the European Union. KOL engagement should prioritize nephrologists and cardiologists with expertise in health disparities and African-diaspora populations, supporting collaborative research on culturally-adapted blood pressure control strategies that address structural, biological, and social determinants simultaneously and generate evidence relevant to payer and health technology assessment bodies in Europe.

Lifestyle behaviors as Effect Modifiers in the Association Between Oral Health and Carotid Artery Calcification: A Cross-Sectional Study.

Revue : International Dental Journal | Tier : NA | IF estime : NA

Date de publication : 2026-09-02

Resultats principaux : This cross-sectional study in Taiwan assessed 3,116 patients (670 with carotid artery calcification [CAC] detected by panoramic radiography, prevalence 21.49%) examining associations between CAC and oral health outcomes (DMFT index, periodontal disease, apical periodontitis) with rigorous adjustment for confounders including hypertension, hyperlipidemia, and diabetes mellitus, using directed acyclic graphs to define the minimal sufficient adjustment set. After adjustment for cardiovascular risk factor confounders, associations between CAC and oral health outcomes (DMFT, periodontal disease, apical periodontitis) disappeared, indicating the crude oral-cardiovascular association was largely explained by underlying cardiometabolic risk factors including hypertension. A significant interaction between CAC and alcohol consumption was identified: among drinkers, CAC was significantly associated with periodontal disease (OR = 9.051), an association absent in non-drinkers, suggesting alcohol modifies the oral-cardiovascular relationship in a clinically meaningful way. Age was significantly and independently associated with higher DMFT index, more teeth with apical periodontitis, and higher odds of concurrent disease across all stratifications (all P < 0.001). Limitations include the cross-sectional design preventing causal inference, use of panoramic radiography rather than CT (gold standard) for CAC detection, potential residual confounding from unmeasured variables, and the reliance on multiple imputation for missing values.

Implications Medical Affairs : This study reinforces hypertension as a critical confounding variable in the oral health-cardiovascular disease relationship, providing Medical Affairs with evidence to support messaging around the importance of total cardiovascular risk management in hypertensive patients, emphasizing that BP control is part of a broader cardiometabolic risk reduction strategy. The interaction between alcohol consumption, CAC, and periodontal disease may interest KOLs working at the cardiovascular-dental medicine interface, presenting an opportunity for Medical Affairs to support collaborative interdisciplinary research on lifestyle-mediated mechanisms of cardiovascular risk in hypertensive patients and to position antihypertensive therapies within a comprehensive risk factor modification framework. Incorporating lifestyle interaction data (alcohol, smoking, diet) into risk stratification models may support differentiated messaging for antihypertensive products in discussions with cardiologists and internists focused on subclinical cardiovascular marker-guided risk management.

Revue : British Journal of Hospital Medicine (London, England: 2005) | Tier : NA | IF estime : NA

Date de publication : 2026-08-21

Resultats principaux : This comprehensive narrative review synthesized contemporary literature (2017-2026) and international guideline recommendations on phase-specific nutritional strategies for patients undergoing cardiac surgery, covering pre-operative, early post-operative, in-hospital recovery, discharge, and long-term phases with a focus on attenuating post-surgical catabolism, supporting wound healing, immune function, and myocardial recovery. In the early postoperative period, evidence supports early initiation of enteral nutrition, adequate protein provision (1.2-2.0 g/kg/day), and careful fluid and glycemic management to reduce catabolic stress and postoperative complications. During the long-term phase, the review explicitly endorsed cardioprotective dietary patterns—specifically the Mediterranean diet and the Dietary Approaches to Stop Hypertension (DASH) diet—for secondary prevention and cardiovascular risk reduction, positioning hypertension management through dietary intervention as a key long-term recovery goal. Special populations requiring individualized nutritional protocols included elderly or sarcopenic patients, those with diabetes mellitus or chronic kidney disease, overweight individuals, and patients on anticoagulation therapy, each presenting unique nutritional challenges in the cardiac surgical context. Limitations include the narrative review methodology subject to selection bias, the absence of procedure-specific dietary protocols for diverse cardiac surgery types, limited randomized controlled trial evidence in this specific surgical population, and the primarily retrospective nature of most reviewed data.

Implications Medical Affairs : The explicit endorsement of the DASH diet for hypertension management in cardiac surgical patients reinforces lifestyle modification as an essential pillar of secondary prevention, providing Medical Affairs with evidence to support positioning antihypertensive therapies within a comprehensive, multimodal cardiovascular risk reduction program discussed with cardiologists, cardiac surgeons, and cardiac rehabilitation teams. KOL engagement in cardiac surgery and critical care nutrition represents a valuable avenue for Medical Affairs to disseminate aligned scientific messaging on integrated blood pressure management in high-risk post-surgical patient populations, particularly as multidisciplinary perioperative care pathways (ERACS) become increasingly standard of care. Collaboration with cardiac rehabilitation programs that advocate DASH-aligned dietary intervention creates opportunities to generate real-world evidence datasets linking dietary adherence to BP outcome trajectories, supporting the value case for antihypertensive therapy combined with structured lifestyle modification in the post-cardiac surgery population.

Prevalence and Associated Factors of Metabolic Syndrome in Patients With Systemic Lupus Erythematosus: A Cross-Sectional Study.

Revue : Cureus | Tier : NA | IF estime : NA

Date de publication : 2026-08-02

Resultats principaux : This hospital-based cross-sectional study conducted from January 2024 to December 2025 enrolled 427 adults diagnosed with SLE according to 2019 EULAR/ACR criteria using consecutive sampling, and performed multivariable logistic regression to identify factors independently associated with metabolic syndrome (MetS), defined by NCEP criteria. MetS prevalence was 39.34% in this SLE cohort, substantially elevated compared with the general population; the most frequent MetS components were low HDL cholesterol, central obesity, hypertension, hypertriglyceridemia, and elevated fasting plasma glucose, with hypertension ranking as the third most prevalent component. Independent risk factors for MetS in multivariable analysis included older age, obesity (BMI ≥30 kg/m2), longer disease duration, lupus nephritis, physical inactivity (less than 150 minutes per week of moderate-intensity activity), family history of diabetes mellitus, and corticosteroid use; conversely, hydroxychloroquine use was independently associated with significantly lower odds of MetS. Subgroup findings highlighted physical inactivity and corticosteroid use as key modifiable and treatment-related determinants of MetS in SLE, providing actionable targets for integrated clinical management strategies combining rheumatological and cardiometabolic care. Limitations include the single-center, cross-sectional design preventing causal inference, consecutive sampling method potentially introducing selection bias, inability to control for all potential confounders, and the call for prospective longitudinal studies to validate temporal relationships.

Implications Medical Affairs : The high prevalence of hypertension as a MetS component in SLE patients (MetS overall at 39.34%) designates this autoimmune population as a high-risk group where antihypertensive therapies must be carefully selected considering drug interactions with immunosuppressive regimens—a key educational priority for Medical Affairs engagement with rheumatology and internal medicine KOLs. The protective association of hydroxychloroquine with MetS prevention has indirect implications for the management of cardiovascular risk in lupus patients and may influence future guideline recommendations on treatment prioritization for SLE patients with concurrent hypertension and cardiometabolic risk. Real-World Evidence initiatives examining longitudinal BP control trajectories and cardiovascular outcomes in SLE patients would fill a critical evidence gap and support differentiated scientific positioning of RAS inhibitors or other antihypertensives in the immune-mediated disease landscape, an area of growing Medical Affairs scientific interest.

Decade of Risk: Predicting Ten-year Cardiovascular Disease Events (Stroke/myocardial Infarction) in Hypertensive Patients at a Tertiary Care Institute in Jharkhand.

Revue : Indian Journal of Community Medicine | Tier : NA | IF estime : NA

Date de publication : 2026-08-13

Resultats principaux : This cross-sectional study conducted from February to June 2024 enrolled 365 hypertensive patients aged 30 years or older attending the Medicine OPD at AIIMS Deoghar, Jharkhand, India, using the validated QRISK3 algorithm to estimate individual 10-year CVD risk (stroke/myocardial infarction) and assess contributing risk factors in a resource-limited tertiary care setting. The mean 10-year QRISK3 score was 11.67% (SD = 11.16), indicating a moderate-risk profile, with a mean relative risk of 1.53 (SD = 1.01) and a mean QRISK3 Healthy Heart Age of 58.68 years (SD = 12.44)—substantially above chronological age—indicating poor cardiovascular health optimization in this population. Smoking prevalence was very high (88.8%), 22.7% had diabetes, and only 20.8% were receiving antihypertensive treatment despite 21.6% having documented uncontrolled hypertension, exposing critical gaps in hypertension diagnosis, treatment initiation, and BP control. Correlation analysis confirmed significant associations between age, systolic blood pressure, and random blood sugar with QRISK3 CVD risk scores, identifying these as the primary modifiable contributors to the high observed risk burden. Limitations include the cross-sectional single-center design using purposive sampling limiting generalizability, use of a UK-derived QRISK3 tool in an Indian population where external validation is limited, and the short five-month data collection window which may not capture seasonal or temporal variation in risk factor profiles.

Implications Medical Affairs : The high rate of undertreated hypertension (only 20.8% on treatment despite 21.6% with uncontrolled BP) in a tertiary care setting in Jharkhand reveals a substantial treatment gap in India, reinforcing the strategic opportunity for Medical Affairs to support physician education programs and guideline adherence campaigns targeting cardiometabolic risk management in South Asian populations, where the hypertension treatment cascade remains significantly suboptimal. The mean QRISK3 score of 11.67% combined with a very high smoking prevalence (88.8%) and diabetes co-prevalence (22.7%) positions this hypertensive cohort as a high-priority target for intensive multifactorial risk intervention, and Medical Affairs should engage with national guideline bodies in India to advocate for earlier antihypertensive treatment initiation thresholds supported by locally validated risk-scoring tools. KOL strategy in India should prioritize community medicine and internal medicine specialists at AIIMS and equivalent national institutions who are actively conducting CVD risk profiling studies, as these physicians are highly influential in shaping regional prescribing behavior, designing national cardiovascular prevention policies, and generating locally-relevant evidence that can support health technology assessments in one of the world's largest and fastest-growing pharmaceutical markets.
