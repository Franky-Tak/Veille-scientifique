Orthostatic Hypertension: Clinical Spectrum and Gaps in Knowledge.

Revue : Current Hypertension Reports | Tier : NA | IF estime : NA

Date de publication : 2026-09-16

Resultats principaux : This narrative review examines orthostatic hypertension (oHT) — a rise in blood pressure upon standing — across a broad spectrum of clinical populations including younger adults prone to incident hypertension and older adults at risk for cardiovascular, cerebrovascular, renal, and cognitive complications. The primary focus areas include pathophysiology, diagnostic criteria standardization, and prognostic significance, drawing on accumulating observational and mechanistic evidence published to 2026. Excessive sympathetically mediated vasoconstriction is identified as the predominant mechanism, with arterial stiffness, impaired baroreflex buffering, and abnormal vascular responsiveness as important contributing factors depending on the population studied. In younger adults, oHT is independently linked to incident hypertension, while in older cohorts it is associated with adverse cardiovascular and end-organ outcomes, though whether oHT represents an independent risk factor or primarily a marker of vascular aging and established hypertension remains unresolved. Consensus diagnostic definitions have recently been established, but uncertainties persist around optimal thresholds, measurement reproducibility, and heterogeneity of clinical presentation; critically, no evidence yet supports oHT-specific therapeutic strategies, representing a major clinical gap identified by this review.

Implications Medical Affairs : This review highlights oHT as an emerging, undercharacterized hypertension phenotype not adequately captured by current cardiovascular risk assessment frameworks, creating potential impetus for future guideline updates that incorporate standing blood pressure criteria. From a Medical Affairs perspective, oHT represents a strategic positioning opportunity for antihypertensive agents with favorable autonomic profiles — such as sympatholytic or vasodilatory mechanisms — if clinical evidence can be generated demonstrating efficacy in this subgroup. KOL engagement should target autonomic medicine and cardiology specialists to design proof-of-concept clinical trials using oHT as a primary endpoint, given the current therapeutic vacuum and unmet medical need.

Sex Differences in Sleep Duration and Quality: Implications for Hypertension Risk.

Revue : Current Hypertension Reports | Tier : NA | IF estime : NA

Date de publication : 2026-09-16

Resultats principaux : This review synthesizes five years of evidence on the association between sleep and hypertension, with a specific emphasis on sex differences in blood pressure regulation among male and female adults from multiple population-based and clinical studies. Sleep disturbances — including insomnia, short sleep duration, and obstructive sleep apnea (OSA) — are confirmed as modifiable risk factors for hypertension, with the strength and direction of associations differing meaningfully by sex. Females demonstrate distinct OSA presentations and greater susceptibility to blood pressure dysregulation arising from sleep disturbances, attributed in part to sex-specific hormonal influences — including estrogen fluctuation — and differences in autonomic nervous system function. No uniform pooled quantitative effect sizes are provided due to the narrative synthesis design; however, the review integrates consistent evidence across heterogeneous study populations that sleep represents a multidimensional cardiovascular risk determinant with sex-specific clinical significance. Key limitations include that most included studies were not designed with sex-stratified hypertension risk as a primary aim, and the causal direction between sleep disorders and hypertension remains difficult to establish from observational evidence.

Implications Medical Affairs : The identification of sex-specific sleep-hypertension pathways supports the incorporation of sleep health as a sex-stratified cardiovascular risk modifier in future guideline iterations, particularly for women with OSA or chronic insomnia. Medical Affairs teams should consider positioning antihypertensive products within broader cardiovascular risk reduction programs that integrate sleep assessment tools, especially targeting female patients in whom sleep disorder-related blood pressure elevation may be underrecognized and undertreated. KOL strategy should engage sleep medicine specialists and preventive cardiologists to co-develop research initiatives examining how sex modulates the blood pressure-lowering efficacy of existing antihypertensive therapies in patients with comorbid sleep disorders.

Increased prevalence of ambulatory and isolated nocturnal hypertension in children with idiopathic intracranial hypertension: a case-control study.

Revue : European Journal of Pediatrics | Tier : NA | IF estime : NA

Date de publication : 2026-09-15

Resultats principaux : This cross-sectional case-control study evaluated 24-hour ambulatory blood pressure monitoring (ABPM) in 42 children and adolescents with idiopathic intracranial hypertension (IIH) compared with 34 age-, sex-, and BMI-matched controls at a Turkish tertiary center. The primary endpoint was prevalence of ambulatory hypertension, with secondary assessments of nighttime blood pressure load, dipping status, left ventricular mass index (LVMI), and left ventricular hypertrophy (LVH). Ambulatory hypertension was significantly more prevalent in IIH patients compared to controls (28.6% vs. 5.9%; OR 6.40, 95% CI 1.32-31.00; p=0.011), driven predominantly by isolated nocturnal hypertension (19.0% vs. 2.9%; p=0.031), which accounted for two-thirds of all ambulatory hypertension cases in the IIH group. Nighttime diastolic blood pressure load was significantly higher in IIH children [15.4% vs. 6.2%; p=0.016], while office blood pressure measurements, daytime ambulatory blood pressure, LVMI, LVH prevalence, and dipping patterns did not differ significantly between groups. Key limitations include the cross-sectional design preventing causal inference, a moderate sample size (n=76 total), and the single-center setting, which may limit generalizability; the absence of overt cardiac target organ damage does not exclude subclinical or future risk progression.

Implications Medical Affairs : These findings demonstrate that subclinical nocturnal blood pressure dysregulation in pediatric IIH is detectable only by ABPM and not by routine office measurements, establishing a compelling case for broader ABPM adoption in at-risk pediatric populations and informing paediatric hypertension screening guidelines. Medical Affairs teams can leverage this evidence to support market education around 24-hour blood pressure monitoring devices in pediatric patients with neurological comorbidities, identifying a niche but growing segment for ABPM technology adoption. KOL strategy should engage pediatric nephrologists and neurologists in collaborative research addressing whether antihypertensive intervention targeting nocturnal hypertension in IIH alters long-term cardiovascular and neurological outcomes.

Associations of Genetic Variants in Uric Acid Transporter Genes With Salt Sensitivity, Longitudinal Blood Pressure Changes, and Incident Hypertension in Chinese Adults.

Revue : Journal of Clinical Hypertension (Greenwich, Conn.) | Tier : 3 | IF estime : 3-10

Date de publication : 2026-09

Resultats principaux : This prospective family-based cohort study enrolled 514 Chinese adults from the Baoji Salt-Sensitivity Study, who underwent a controlled dietary sodium intervention (low-salt and high-salt phases) and were followed for 14 years to assess longitudinal blood pressure changes and incident hypertension. Eight urate transporter genes (SLC2A9, ABCG2, SLC17A3, SLC22A7, SLC22A6, SLC22A11, SLC22A12, ABCC4) were genotyped, and their single-nucleotide polymorphisms (SNPs) were tested for associations with salt-sensitive blood pressure response, longitudinal BP change, and incident hypertension. After multivariable adjustment and multiple-testing correction, several variants — including ABCG2, SLC22A6, SLC22A12, and ABCC4 SNPs — were significantly associated with diastolic and mean arterial pressure responses to low- and high-sodium dietary interventions, while SLC2A9 rs3733591 and SLC17A3 rs1165165 were associated with longitudinal systolic BP change over 14 years. Notably, ABCC4 rs7982809 and rs869951 were independently associated with incident hypertension during the 14-year follow-up, providing the first human longitudinal evidence linking urate transport pathway variation to hypertension development. Limitations include the restriction to a single ethnic Han Chinese cohort (limiting global generalizability), the potential for residual confounding in a family-based design, and the need for functional in vitro and replication studies to validate the identified SNP-phenotype associations.

Implications Medical Affairs : These findings establish a novel mechanistic framework linking genetic variation in urate transporter pathways to salt sensitivity, blood pressure progression, and incident hypertension, which may inform future guidelines advocating for pharmacogenomic or dietary risk stratification in populations with high salt intake. Medical Affairs should proactively monitor emerging evidence on uric acid-lowering agents (e.g., xanthine oxidase inhibitors such as febuxostat) as potential adjuncts for blood pressure management in salt-sensitive, hyperuricemic patients, representing a scientifically grounded combination therapy positioning opportunity. KOL collaboration with cardiogenomics specialists in Chinese and Asian populations could catalyze pharmacogenomic studies examining whether urate-modulating interventions reduce incident hypertension in genetically defined high-risk subgroups.

Hopes and doubts about zilebesiran, a new antihypertensive drug.

Revue : Wiadomosci lekarskie (Warsaw, Poland) | Tier : NA | IF estime : NA

Date de publication : 2026

Resultats principaux : This narrative review critically evaluates zilebesiran, a first-in-class subcutaneous small-interfering RNA (siRNA) therapy targeting hepatic angiotensinogen synthesis for the treatment of arterial hypertension, based on a comprehensive literature search of publications from January 2010 to December 2025 in PubMed and Google Scholar. Zilebesiran's mechanism involves RNA-induced silencing complex (RISC)-mediated silencing of the angiotensinogen gene, resulting in sustained suppression of the entire renin-angiotensin system with a single subcutaneous injection every 3 to 6 months. In the KARDIA 1 and KARDIA 2 clinical trials, a single subcutaneous dose reduced angiotensinogen concentrations by approximately 90% and lowered systolic blood pressure by 10-20 mmHg compared to placebo, with effects maintained for 3-6 months and with minimal adverse events — primarily rare orthostatic hypotension and a slight, transient creatinine elevation. No serious drug-related adverse events were observed in completed trials, and the therapeutic durability of up to 6 months distinguishes zilebesiran from all existing antihypertensive drug classes. The critical limitation identified by the authors is the absence of cardiovascular hard endpoint data (myocardial infarction, stroke, mortality) and the lack of long-term safety evidence beyond 6 months, with the ongoing KARDIA 3 trial designed to address these gaps.

Implications Medical Affairs : Zilebesiran's once-per-3-to-6-month dosing paradigm represents a potentially transformative innovation in antihypertensive therapy, directly addressing the public health challenge of medication non-adherence, which could force significant portfolio repositioning for incumbent antihypertensive product lines. Medical Affairs teams should proactively prepare comparative scientific communications that contextualize existing therapy classes relative to this emerging siRNA approach, particularly for treatment-resistant hypertension and adherence-challenged patient segments where zilebesiran may gain early market traction following KARDIA 3 results. KOL strategy should prioritize hypertension specialists and interventional cardiologists to shape clinical practice expectations around optimal patient profiles, dosing sequences, and combination regimens involving zilebesiran, positioning the company as a scientific leader in next-generation antihypertensive care.

Control Rates and Health-Seeking Behaviour Among People With Diabetes and Hypertension, Tamil Nadu, India - a Community Baseline Survey.

Revue : Journal of Primary Care and Community Health | Tier : NA | IF estime : NA

Date de publication : 2026-09-16

Resultats principaux : This cross-sectional community survey was conducted in November 2023 across the Villupuram and Cuddalore districts of Tamil Nadu, India, enrolling 1,572 participants aged 18-69 years who had received treatment for hypertension and/or diabetes for more than six months, using a cluster sampling methodology (MTM line list) with a 95% response rate. The study measured blood pressure and HbA1c control rates as primary outcomes and assessed medication adherence, sociodemographic characteristics, and health-seeking behavior through a validated structured questionnaire; data were analyzed using Stata version 16. Among 757 hypertensive participants, only 49.1% achieved optimal blood pressure control despite a self-reported medication adherence rate of 75.6%, revealing a substantial adherence-to-control gap; among 738 diabetic participants, glycaemic control was even more inadequate at 25.7% with 76.2% adherence. Over 70% of all participants were classified as overweight or obese, highlighting the concurrent burden of obesity as a major contributor to NCD treatment failure in this community setting. Limitations include the cross-sectional design precluding causal inference, potential social desirability bias in self-reported adherence, restriction to two districts limiting national generalizability, and the absence of data on treatment regimen complexity or healthcare access barriers.

Implications Medical Affairs : The striking divergence between medication adherence (75%) and hypertension control rates (49%) in this South Indian community underscores that pill-taking compliance alone is insufficient for cardiovascular risk control, reinforcing the clinical value of fixed-dose combination therapies and lifestyle interventions in LMIC settings. Medical Affairs teams operating in India and similar low- and middle-income markets should leverage these data to strengthen market access arguments for once-daily single-pill combination antihypertensives, framing them as strategic tools to bridge the adherence-to-control gap at the population level. KOL engagement should target primary care physicians, public health officials, and community health worker networks in South Asia to embed pharmaceutical programs within broader national NCD control initiatives, maximizing both public health impact and commercial positioning.

Preliminary Outcomes of iStent Inject vs Kahook Dual Blade Goniotomy Combined With Phacoemulsification: A Randomized Controlled Trial.

Revue : Medical Science Monitor | Tier : NA | IF estime : NA

Date de publication : 2026-09-16

Resultats principaux : This prospective randomized controlled trial conducted between 2024 and 2025 at a single ophthalmology center in Poland compared two canal-based minimally invasive glaucoma surgery (CB-MIGS) approaches — phacoemulsification with iStent inject implantation (phaco-iStent; n=35 eyes) versus goniotomy using the Kahook Dual Blade (phaco-KDB; n=33 eyes) — in patients with open-angle glaucoma or ocular hypertension, with a mean follow-up of 8±2.4 months. The primary endpoint was surgical success (IOP reduction ≥20% from baseline or IOP ≤15 mmHg); secondary endpoints included medication burden reduction, postoperative complication rate, and complete success rates. Both procedures produced comparable intraocular pressure reductions (~14%): mean IOP decreased from 19.37±4.03 to 16.58±3.64 mmHg in the phaco-iStent group and from 18.15±3.76 to 15.46±3.15 mmHg in the phaco-KDB group, with no statistically significant difference between groups (P=0.355 at 6 months). Complete surgical success at 6 months was achieved in 68.8% of phaco-iStent eyes and 62.5% of phaco-KDB eyes; glaucoma medication burden was eliminated postoperatively in both groups (mean reduction from ~1.1 to 0 medications per patient). Note: this study addresses ocular hypertension (intraocular pressure), not systemic arterial hypertension. Limitations include the single-center design, limited sample size, short mean follow-up (8 months), and decreasing follow-up numbers over time, which reduces confidence in long-term outcome interpretation.

Implications Medical Affairs : While this RCT pertains to ocular rather than systemic hypertension, it is relevant to Medical Affairs insofar as patients with systemic hypertension frequently carry co-existing intraocular pressure abnormalities and glaucoma risk, creating educational opportunities around integrated cardiovascular-ophthalmology care pathways. Medical Affairs teams focused on arterial hypertension should note this study as contextual background when engaging with healthcare providers who manage patients with combined systemic and ocular hypertension comorbidity, supporting holistic cardiovascular risk management messaging. KOL strategy opportunities in this domain are limited for direct antihypertensive product positioning, but collaborative initiatives with ophthalmologists treating cardiovascular comorbidities in glaucoma patients may offer indirect value for comprehensive cardiometabolic education programs.

Revue : Netherlands Heart Journal | Tier : NA | IF estime : NA

Date de publication : 2026-09-16

Resultats principaux : This narrative review examines obesity in women as a multifactorial public health challenge, focusing on sex-specific cardiometabolic trajectories across the female life course — from menarche through pregnancy to menopause — and the emerging role of incretin-based pharmacological therapies including GLP-1 receptor agonists in managing obesity-related cardiovascular risk in women. Key findings establish that hormonal fluctuations across the female life cycle significantly influence adipose tissue distribution, energy balance, and metabolic risk, with women-specific conditions — including hypertensive disorders of pregnancy, pregnancy-related weight retention, and polycystic ovarian syndrome — substantially amplifying long-term cardiometabolic risk. Limitations include the narrative review format relying on heterogeneous literature, the lack of sex-stratified data from major cardiovascular outcome trials for GLP-1 receptor agonists, and the absence of meta-analytic quantitative estimates.

Implications Medical Affairs : The recognition of hypertensive disorders of pregnancy and sex-specific obesity trajectories as major drivers of long-term hypertension risk in women highlights an underserved patient segment where cardiometabolic intervention — including blood pressure management during and after pregnancy — could meaningfully alter long-term cardiovascular trajectories. A multistate prediction model for hypertension and cardiovascular disease after pregnancy using routine care data in the Netherlands.

Revue : European Journal of Preventive Cardiology | Tier : NA | IF estime : NA

Date de publication : 2026-09-16

Resultats principaux : This nationwide, registry-based retrospective cohort study developed and internally validated a multistate prediction model for hypertension and cardiovascular disease (CVD) among 737,257 primiparous women in the Netherlands, using routine care data with maximum follow-up of 17 years. Candidate predictors included hypertensive disorders of pregnancy (HDP: pregnancy-induced hypertension [PIH] and pre-eclampsia), preterm birth, small-for-gestational-age birth, recurrent miscarriages, gestational diabetes, country of origin, and socioeconomic status (income and education); a stratified time-dependent Cox model accounting for competing non-CVD mortality was used. During follow-up, 1.7% of women developed hypertension and 0.3% developed CVD; HDP demonstrated the strongest associations with both outcomes — with effect sizes attenuating with increasing age — and PIH combined with low socioeconomic status yielded hypertension risks of 15% vs. 5% for high SES in Dutch women. Surinamese women faced significantly higher hypertension risks than Dutch women, and PIH combined with preterm birth and low socioeconomic status produced the highest absolute risk estimates in the entire cohort. Discriminatory performance was modest to good (AUCs 0.65-0.89), with adequate calibration; key limitations include the registry-based data source limiting granularity of clinical variables, absence of external validation, and the need for prospective validation before clinical implementation.

Implications Medical Affairs : This large-scale prediction model provides the most robust available evidence that HDP — especially PIH — is the dominant early predictor of long-term hypertension and CVD, supporting the clinical rationale for systematic early postpartum cardiovascular risk stratification beginning 6 weeks after delivery. Medical Affairs teams should leverage these data to support product positioning for antihypertensive agents in women with prior HDP, advocating for guideline-supported earlier treatment initiation protocols and systematic monitoring programs in high-risk postpartum women identified through validated prediction tools. KOL strategy should engage preventive cardiologists, obstetricians, and public health researchers in guideline development to formally incorporate postpartum hypertension prediction models into standard cardiovascular risk assessment frameworks for women, creating durable scientific influence.

Mechanisms of Aging in Phenylketonuria (PKU).

Revue : American Journal of Medical Genetics. Part A | Tier : NA | IF estime : NA

Date de publication : 2026-09-15

Resultats principaux : This narrative review synthesizes current evidence on accelerated aging in phenylketonuria (PKU), an autosomal recessive metabolic disorder characterized by elevated phenylalanine levels, drawing on clinical observational studies, biomarker research, and mechanistic data in adults with PKU managed at metabolic disease centers across Europe. The study population is adult PKU patients maintained on low-protein dietary management with amino acid supplementation; the review examines cardiovascular, cognitive, bone, and metabolic aging trajectories. Adults with PKU exhibit greater cardiovascular aging including arterial stiffness and dyslipidemia, alongside significantly higher rates of obesity, diabetes, and hypertension compared to adults without PKU — findings consistent with an accelerated vascular aging phenotype driven by chronic hyperphenylalaninemia. Oxidative stress (OS) is identified as the primary underlying mechanism, leading to secondary mitochondrial dysfunction, DNA damage, and epigenetic alterations; these pathways collectively produce premature aging features — including hypertension — beyond what would be predicted by traditional metabolic risk factor accumulation alone. Limitations include the narrative review methodology, the small and heterogeneous literature base on adult PKU outcomes, the absence of prospective longitudinal designs specifically powered to quantify cardiovascular aging trajectories, and the challenge of controlling for confounding dietary and treatment variables.

Implications Medical Affairs : The linkage between PKU-related metabolic disturbance, oxidative stress, and accelerated hypertension risk identifies an underserved rare disease population requiring specific cardiovascular monitoring protocols, offering niche but strategically relevant opportunities for hypertension-focused Medical Affairs engagement. Antihypertensive therapy selection in PKU patients may require individualization to account for underlying oxidative stress, metabolic fragility, and renal function considerations, providing an evidence-based argument for the preferential use of agents with favorable metabolic and renal profiles in this population. Resultats principaux : This quantitative cross-sectional observational study enrolled 299 Lebanese adults with confirmed heart failure (mean age 59.95±15.89 years, predominantly male) from outpatient clinics and primary healthcare services across multiple Lebanese geographic regions between January 2024 and April 2025, using convenience sampling and Arabic-validated psychometric instruments. The study assessed resilience as the primary outcome alongside depressive symptoms, quality of life, and heart failure self-care behaviors; hypertension, diabetes, and hyperlipidemia were highly prevalent comorbidities, and mean LVEF was 37.52%±10.51% with most patients classified as NYHA class II-III. Association of the geriatric nutritional risk index and medical or social factors in elderly patients with regular home-visit medical care: A cross-sectional study.

Revue : Nihon Ronen Igakkai Zasshi (Japanese Journal of Geriatrics) | Tier : NA | IF estime : NA

Date de publication : 2026

Resultats principaux : This cross-sectional study enrolled 265 elderly Japanese patients (median age 87 years, 71.7% women) receiving regular home-visit medical care at a single primary care clinic, examining associations between the Geriatric Nutritional Risk Index (GNRI) — a composite albumin- and body weight-based nutritional assessment tool — and medical, physical, cognitive, and social factors. The study used ordinal logistic regression with GNRI as the outcome variable (categorized into normal nutrition, mild, moderate, and severe malnutrition) and evaluated a range of candidate predictors including mobility status, living environment, diet type, feeding modality, and chronic disease diagnoses. Counterintuitively, hypertension (OR 0.58, 95% CI 0.35-0.95, p=0.033) and dyslipidemia (OR 0.54, 95% CI 0.32-0.91, p=0.023) were independently associated with better nutritional status (higher GNRI), interpreted as a reflection of preserved functional independence and ambulatory capacity in patients with these well-managed chronic conditions. Conversely, wheelchair mobility (OR 2.03, p=0.012), nursing home residence (OR 1.88, p=0.010), blended diet use (OR 3.21, p=0.004), and tube feeding (OR 4.09, p=0.045) were significantly associated with worse nutritional status, reflecting the frailty burden in the most dependent patients. Limitations include the single-center design, cross-sectional methodology, restriction to a home-visit patient population, and the use of GNRI as a surrogate rather than directly measured nutritional assessment.

Implications Medical Affairs : The paradoxical positive association between hypertension and better nutritional status in this frail geriatric population highlights the complexity of interpreting cardiovascular risk factor management in very elderly home-care patients, where functional independence and nutritional adequacy may be stronger determinants of health outcomes than blood pressure levels alone. Medical Affairs teams should recognize the geriatric home-care population as a distinct segment where antihypertensive treatment goals must be individualized based on nutritional status, frailty markers, and functional capacity, supporting positioning of well-tolerated antihypertensive agents with minimal metabolic and functional side effects in this population. KOL engagement should include geriatricians, home-care physicians, and clinical pharmacologists specializing in polypharmacy management to develop evidence-based recommendations for blood pressure management in frail elderly patients, where overly aggressive treatment may paradoxically worsen outcomes.
