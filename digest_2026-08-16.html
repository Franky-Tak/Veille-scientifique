Global, regional, and national burden of chronic kidney disease attributable to dietary risks, 1990 to 2021: A systematic analysis for the Global Burden of Disease Study 2021.

Revue : Medicine (Baltimore) | Tier : NA | IF estime : NA

Date de publication : 2026-08-14

Resultats principaux : This systematic analysis used Global Burden of Disease (GBD) 2021 data to quantify disability-adjusted life-years (DALYs) attributable to dietary risk factors for chronic kidney disease (CKD) across 204 countries from 1990 to 2021. In 2021, the global age-standardized DALY rate for CKD attributable to dietary risks was 93.52 (95% UI: 54.29–134.38) per 100,000 population, with low fruit intake (38.68/100,000), low vegetable intake (30.84/100,000), and high sodium intake (19.81/100,000) as leading contributors. DALY rates increased sharply with age and were higher in males, peaking at 920.46 per 100,000 in males aged 95+ years for low fruit intake. Low Socio-demographic Index (SDI) regions bore a disproportionate burden from CKD due to hypertension attributable to fruit and vegetable deficiencies (54.23 and 55.99 per 100,000, respectively), while high SDI regions showed a greater burden from CKD due to type 2 diabetes, driven by processed meat and red meat intake. A notable limitation is reliance on modeled GBD estimates with wide uncertainty intervals, and residual confounding by unmeasured dietary or socioeconomic variables cannot be excluded.

Implications Medical Affairs : These GBD findings reinforce the significant role of dietary factors — particularly sodium — in driving CKD burden attributable to hypertension, providing epidemiological support for dietary modification as a cornerstone of hypertension management guidelines, especially in low SDI regions where the hypertension-CKD link is strongest. For Medical Affairs, the data on sodium intake as a key modifiable driver supports positioning antihypertensive agents alongside dietary counseling in high-risk populations, particularly in resource-limited settings where pharmacological intervention remains underutilized. KOL engagement strategies should target nephrologists and preventive cardiologists to champion integrated care approaches combining pharmacotherapy with dietary interventions in hypertensive CKD patients globally.

Blood pressure and renal outcomes after renal denervation in patients with chronic kidney disease: A systematic review and meta-analysis.

Revue : Medicine (Baltimore) | Tier : NA | IF estime : NA

Date de publication : 2026-08-14

Resultats principaux : This systematic review and meta-analysis analyzed 12 studies involving 202 patients with CKD stages 2–5, including dialysis populations, who underwent renal denervation (RDN) for resistant hypertension. Pooled analyses demonstrated significant reductions in office systolic BP (−20 mmHg) and diastolic BP (−8 mmHg), as well as ambulatory systolic (−15 mmHg) and diastolic BP (−6 mmHg) following RDN. Renal function remained stable throughout follow-up, with no evidence of accelerated estimated glomerular filtration rate (eGFR) decline, and the safety profile was favorable, with complications limited to isolated access-site events and no excess risk of renal artery stenosis. Heterogeneity was moderate (I² = 58%), and the Egger test showed no significant small-study effects (p = 0.33). Key limitations include the small total sample size (n=202), the heterogeneity of study designs (randomized and non-randomized studies combined), and the lack of long-term cardiovascular outcome data.

Implications Medical Affairs : This meta-analysis supports renal denervation as an effective adjunctive intervention in resistant hypertension with CKD — a population with limited pharmacological options — and demonstrates preserved renal function, which has direct implications for positioning device-based therapies within existing antihypertensive treatment algorithms. From a Medical Affairs perspective, the stable renal function data provides reassurance regarding RDN safety in this vulnerable population, potentially clarifying the niche for pharmacotherapy combination strategies alongside catheter-based interventions. KOL strategy should focus on interventional cardiologists and nephrologists to define optimal sequencing of antihypertensive medications and device-based therapies in resistant hypertension patients with CKD.

The association between obesity indices and hypertension in adults with different glycemic status: A population-based cross-sectional study of the Bandare-Kong cohort.

Revue : Medicine (Baltimore) | Tier : NA | IF estime : NA

Date de publication : 2026-08-14

Resultats principaux : This population-based cross-sectional study examined 3,916 adults aged 35–70 from the Bandare-Kong cohort in Iran, stratifying participants by glycemic status (normoglycemic, prediabetic, diabetic) and comparing the associations of BMI-based general obesity versus waist circumference (WC)-based central obesity with hypertension. In diabetic individuals, high WC alone (aOR 3.19, 95% CI: 1.18–5.60) and combined obesity (aOR 3.09, 95% CI: 1.77–5.39) were significantly associated with higher odds of hypertension. In the prediabetic group, combined obesity showed the highest odds of hypertension (OR 2.53, 95% CI: 1.58–4.15), followed by high WC alone (OR 1.99, 95% CI: 1.21–3.27). Among normoglycemic participants, combined obesity was also associated with higher hypertension odds (aOR 2.63, 95% CI: 2.01–3.44), with WC-only obesity conferring moderate risk (aOR 1.44). Key limitations are the cross-sectional design precluding causal inference, the small number of diabetic participants with BMI-only obesity yielding unstable estimates, and the single-center regional cohort limiting generalizability.

Implications Medical Affairs : These findings confirm that central obesity, as measured by waist circumference, is a stronger and more consistent predictor of hypertension than BMI alone across all glycemic states, reinforcing the need for waist circumference assessment in clinical risk stratification and hypertension treatment initiation thresholds. For Medical Affairs, this data supports therapeutic strategies targeting metabolic syndrome components simultaneously in diabetic and prediabetic patients where hypertension risk is substantially elevated, highlighting the value of antihypertensive agents with favorable metabolic profiles in this population. KOL engagement should prioritize endocrinologists and primary care physicians to advocate for comprehensive cardiometabolic risk assessment — including routine WC measurement — when discussing antihypertensive treatment initiation and selection.

Trends in peripheral vascular disease-related mortality among U.S. adults with co-listed hypertension and type 2 diabetes, 1999-2023: A retrospective observational study.

Revue : Medicine (Baltimore) | Tier : NA | IF estime : NA

Date de publication : 2026-08-14

Resultats principaux : This retrospective observational study analyzed 785,358 peripheral vascular disease deaths with co-listed hypertension and type 2 diabetes (PVD-HD) in US adults aged ≥45 years from the CDC WONDER database spanning 1999–2023, using Joinpoint regression to estimate temporal trends in age-adjusted mortality rates (AAMR). Overall AAMR declined substantially over 25 years, with the steepest decrease during 2003–2009, followed by a slower decline during 2009–2019; after 2019, the trend was not statistically significant, indicating a deceleration in improvement. In 2023, non-Hispanic Black/African American individuals had the highest AAMR (24.23 per 100,000) compared to Hispanic/Latino individuals at the lowest (11.84), demonstrating persistent racial disparities. Geographic disparities were substantial, with an approximately two-fold state-level AAMR difference — West Virginia highest (27.90) and Louisiana lowest (13.86 per 100,000). Limitations include reliance on death certificate coding with potential misclassification, and the inability to capture changes in treatment quality or guideline adherence over time.

Implications Medical Affairs : The post-2019 deceleration in PVD-HD mortality improvement and persistent racial and geographic disparities indicate that current treatment paradigms are insufficient for high-risk subgroups, highlighting opportunities for targeted antihypertensive and vascular-protective therapies. Medical Affairs should prioritize real-world evidence generation focused on Black/African American patients and geographically underserved populations, demonstrating effectiveness of guideline-directed antihypertensive therapies in reducing PVD burden in the hypertension-diabetes comorbidity context. Engagement with vascular medicine KOLs and health equity advocacy organizations should be prioritized to amplify awareness of persistent disparities and advocate for equitable access to comprehensive guideline-directed cardiovascular care.

Preconception Blood Pressure and Sex of the Baby: A Large Prospective Cohort Study in China.

Revue : Journal of clinical hypertension (Greenwich, Conn.) | Tier : Tier 3 | IF estime : 3–10

Date de publication : 2026-08

Resultats principaux : This large prospective cohort study from the China-US Collaborative Project for Neural Tube Defects Prevention examined 44,399 singleton pregnancies in seven counties in southern China to evaluate whether preconception hypertension is associated with increased likelihood of male delivery. The prevalence of preconception hypertension was 4.61% (2,045/44,399), and the prevalence of male delivery was 51.10% in the hypertension group versus 51.79% in the non-hypertension group. Logistic regression adjusting for potential confounders found no significant association between preconception hypertension and male delivery (adjusted RR = 0.99, 95% CI: 0.94–1.03). The study had 99% statistical power to detect a 50% increase in male delivery rates over the baseline of 51.94%, making a false-negative result unlikely. Key limitations are the generalizability restricted to a Chinese rural population and the reliance on self-reported hypertension history or single blood pressure measurement.

Implications Medical Affairs : This well-powered study provides reassuring null findings effectively refuting the hypothesis that preconception hypertension influences fetal sex ratios, removing a potential confounding concern in preconception antihypertensive counseling and simplifying the risk communication for hypertensive women planning pregnancy. For Medical Affairs, these data reinforce that preconception blood pressure management decisions should be guided solely by cardiovascular risk considerations without factoring in fetal sex ratio outcomes. Engagement with maternal-fetal medicine and obstetric KOLs should emphasize evidence-based preconception blood pressure management strategies, focusing on safety and efficacy of antihypertensive therapies in women of childbearing age rather than fetal sex determinism.

Subclinical primary aldosteronism: toward a conceptual framework linking primary aldosteronism and low-renin hypertension.

Revue : Frontiers in endocrinology | Tier : NA | IF estime : NA

Date de publication : 2026-07-31

Resultats principaux : This review synthesizes current evidence on subclinical primary aldosteronism (SPA) — a milder phenotype of renin-independent aldosterone excess observed in normotensive or mildly hypertensive populations — as an emerging cardiometabolic risk entity without universally accepted diagnostic criteria. Low-renin phenotypes are observed in a substantial proportion of the general population, though the true prevalence of SPA remains uncertain due to the absence of consensus diagnostic criteria. Observational studies associate SPA with arterial stiffness, adverse cardiac remodeling, major adverse cardiovascular events, albuminuria, and CKD progression, with effects not fully explained by blood pressure elevation alone, suggesting direct aldosterone-mediated organ damage. Conventional screening strategies based on the aldosterone-to-renin ratio have limitations in detecting this subclinical spectrum; mineralocorticoid receptor antagonists and aldosterone synthase inhibitors are discussed as hypothesis-generating therapeutic strategies. Key limitations are the narrative review design, lack of prospective interventional evidence in SPA populations, and absence of validated biomarkers for SPA diagnosis in routine clinical practice.

Implications Medical Affairs : The conceptualization of SPA as an emerging risk-associated biochemical phenotype beyond overt primary aldosteronism significantly expands the potential addressable patient population for mineralocorticoid pathway therapies, particularly aldosterone synthase inhibitors currently in development. For Medical Affairs, this review provides a scientific foundation for positioning MRAs and emerging aldosterone synthase inhibitors in the broader spectrum of low-renin hypertension, representing a potential market expansion opportunity well beyond confirmed primary aldosteronism. KOL strategy should prioritize endocrinologists and hypertension specialists to drive awareness of SPA as a diagnostic and therapeutic frontier, supporting investigator-initiated research to establish consensus diagnostic criteria and validate clinically applicable screening strategies.

Trends and Characteristics in Gestational Hypertension: United States, 2016-2024.

Revue : National vital statistics reports : from the Centers for Disease Control and Prevention, National Center for Health Statistics, National Vital Statistics System | Tier : NA | IF estime : NA

Date de publication : 2026-07

Resultats principaux : This national vital statistics report analyzed 100% of US birth registrations from 2016 through 2024 to describe trends in gestational hypertension (hypertension diagnosed during pregnancy) by maternal race/ethnicity, age, BMI, state of residence, and plurality. The overall rate of gestational hypertension increased 73% from 6.0% in 2016 to 10.4% in 2024, representing a dramatic eight-year rise. In 2024, rates varied significantly by race/ethnicity, from 7.1% in Asian non-Hispanic mothers to 13.2% in American Indian and Alaska Native non-Hispanic mothers, reflecting persistent disparities. Gestational hypertension rates rose progressively with prepregnancy BMI and were nearly two-fold higher in mothers with multiple gestations compared to singleton births. Geographic variation was substantial, with nine states plus DC having rates below 9.0% and eight states (including Minnesota, Oregon, Alaska, and Alabama) reporting rates of 13.0% or more.

Implications Medical Affairs : The 73% increase in gestational hypertension rates over eight years constitutes an urgent public health signal with significant implications for maternal-fetal medicine, generating a growing demand for safe and effective antihypertensive therapies approved or appropriate for use in pregnancy. Medical Affairs should closely monitor the expanding burden of gestational hypertension as it creates a larger cohort of women at risk for post-partum chronic hypertension, representing a pipeline for long-term antihypertensive therapy and a compelling target for early intervention studies. KOL engagement with maternal-fetal medicine specialists, obstetricians, and health equity advocates is critical to address the substantial racial and geographic disparities identified and to position evidence-based antihypertensive strategies in this high-priority population.

Assessing the Effectiveness of Lifestyle Clinic Interventions on Cardiometabolic Risk Factors: A Retrospective Study.

Revue : Georgian medical news | Tier : NA | IF estime : NA

Date de publication : 2026-06

Resultats principaux : This retrospective cohort study analyzed data from 281 participants enrolled in a comprehensive lifestyle clinic program at King Abdulaziz Medical City in Jeddah, Saudi Arabia, stratified into metabolic (n=137) and smoking cessation (n=144) cohorts, using linear mixed-effects models to assess changes in BMI, systolic blood pressure, HbA1c, and total cholesterol at baseline, 3 months, and 6 months. At 6 months, participants demonstrated significant reductions in BMI (−0.92 kg/m², 95% CI: −1.79 to −0.05; p=0.036) and total cholesterol (−0.25 mmol/L, 95% CI: −0.48 to −0.03; p=0.022). Among active smokers at baseline, 24.1% had quit smoking at 6 months, representing a meaningful cessation rate within a structured program. No significant changes were observed in systolic blood pressure or HbA1c levels over 6 months, which is a notable finding given the intensity of the lifestyle intervention. Limitations include the retrospective design, high attrition potential, single-center setting in Saudi Arabia limiting generalizability, and the relatively short 6-month follow-up period for assessing blood pressure changes.

Implications Medical Affairs : The absence of significant systolic blood pressure reduction in a structured lifestyle intervention program underscores the limitations of lifestyle modification alone in achieving blood pressure targets in patients with cardiometabolic comorbidities, reinforcing the indispensable role of pharmacotherapy in guideline-directed hypertension management. Medical Affairs can leverage these real-world data to reinforce to primary care physicians and preventive medicine specialists that lifestyle interventions, while valuable for metabolic parameters, are insufficient alone for blood pressure control, supporting early and robust pharmacological treatment initiation. This evidence strengthens the case for prioritizing KOL discussions on antihypertensive therapy as a necessary complement to lifestyle programs, particularly in Middle Eastern populations with high cardiometabolic comorbidity burden.

Effects of Combined Intravenous and Topical Magnesium Sulfate Administration on Pain, Stress, and Functional Recovery After Total Knee Arthroplasty: A Prospective, Randomized, Blinded Controlled Trial.

Revue : Drug design, development and therapy | Tier : NA | IF estime : NA

Date de publication : 2026-08-10

Resultats principaux : This prospective, randomized, blinded controlled trial enrolled 150 patients undergoing total knee arthroplasty (TKA) and assigned them to intravenous magnesium sulfate (IV Group), topical magnesium sulfate added to local infiltration analgesia (Topical Group), or combined IV and topical administration (Combined Group). The primary outcome — resting VAS pain score at 24 hours postoperatively — showed no significant difference among the three groups. Secondary outcomes including postoperative morphine consumption for rescue analgesia, knee range of motion, walking ability, and hospital length of stay did not significantly differ between groups. Intraoperative hypertension incidence was examined as a component of intraoperative stress response assessment, with no significant between-group differences reported. Key limitations include the single-center design, relatively short follow-up, and the marginal clinical significance of the small VAS reductions observed on the day of surgery in the Combined Group.

Implications Medical Affairs : While this RCT does not directly address antihypertensive therapy, the perioperative blood pressure data positions intraoperative hypertension as a clinically relevant secondary endpoint in major orthopedic surgery trials, underscoring the need for optimized perioperative antihypertensive protocols in surgical patients. From a Medical Affairs perspective, perioperative blood pressure management in hypertensive surgical patients represents an underexplored area with significant patient safety implications, warranting dedicated pharmacological research and guideline development with hospital pharmacists and anesthesiology KOLs. Future research should specifically investigate optimal antihypertensive management protocols for hypertensive patients undergoing high-risk elective surgeries to reduce intraoperative cardiovascular events and improve perioperative outcomes.

Association between dietary flavonoid intake and stroke in Adults: The National Health and Nutrition Examination Survey 2007-2010 and 2017-2018.

Revue : Medicine (Baltimore) | Tier : NA | IF estime : NA

Date de publication : 2026-08-14

Resultats principaux : This cross-sectional study analyzed NHANES data from 12,424 US adults (2007–2010 and 2017–2018) to evaluate associations between dietary flavonoid subclass intake and stroke prevalence using multivariable logistic regression, restricted cubic splines (RCS), and network pharmacology. Higher anthocyanin intake was inversely associated with stroke prevalence (Q4 vs Q1: OR 0.69; 95% CI: 0.48–0.98; p for trend = 0.047), with a significant nonlinear relationship (p for nonlinearity = 0.018). Subgroup analysis revealed the protective association was particularly evident in non-hypertensive individuals, suggesting that hypertension may modify the relationship between dietary flavonoids and stroke risk. Higher flavanone intake was significantly associated with lower stroke prevalence in females (Q4 vs Q1: OR 0.49; 95% CI: 0.30–0.82; p for trend = 0.04). Network pharmacology identified 193 anthocyanin-stroke overlapping targets and 89 flavanone-stroke targets, enriched in lipid/atherosclerosis, MAPK, and PI3K-Akt signaling pathways; however, cross-sectional design limits causal inference.

Implications Medical Affairs : The finding that dietary flavonoid protection against stroke is more pronounced in non-hypertensive individuals suggests that effective hypertension control may be a necessary prerequisite for dietary bioactives to deliver their full cardiovascular benefit, reinforcing the primacy of blood pressure normalization through pharmacotherapy. Medical Affairs teams should monitor emerging evidence on dietary bioactives as complementary prevention strategies, incorporating this data into holistic treatment narratives combining antihypertensive pharmacotherapy with dietary modification for stroke prevention. The mechanistic data implicating inflammatory and atherosclerotic pathways creates a foundation for exploring potential synergies between flavonoid-rich dietary interventions and antihypertensive or lipid-lowering therapies in future clinical trials involving hypertensive stroke-risk populations.

Dietary vitamin B6 intake and gallstone prevalence in US adults: A cross-sectional analysis of NHANES 2017-2023.

Revue : Medicine (Baltimore) | Tier : NA | IF estime : NA

Date de publication : 2026-08-14

Resultats principaux : This cross-sectional analysis of NHANES 2017–2023 data (n=9,273) examined the association between dietary vitamin B6 intake and self-reported gallstone history, adjusting for sociodemographic, lifestyle, and clinical factors including hypertension, using weighted multivariable logistic regression and restricted cubic splines. Overall gallstone prevalence was 10.47%; in fully adjusted models, higher vitamin B6 intake was inversely associated with gallstone risk (OR per mg increase: 0.90; 95% CI: 0.82–0.99; p=0.029). RCS analysis revealed a linear dose-response gradient reaching statistical significance at intake levels exceeding 1.66 mg/day. Stratified analyses demonstrated that among individuals with comorbid hypertension, the inverse association between vitamin B6 and gallstone risk was robust and statistically significant. Limitations include the cross-sectional design precluding causal inference, reliance on self-reported dietary recall subject to measurement error, and potential residual confounding by unmeasured clinical variables.

Implications Medical Affairs : The stronger inverse association between vitamin B6 intake and gallstone risk in individuals with hypertension indicates that hypertensive patients may be a particularly high-risk subgroup for gallstone disease, representing a relevant comorbidity that could affect antihypertensive treatment adherence and overall health management. Medical Affairs teams should be aware of the potential hepatobiliary comorbidity burden in hypertensive patients when developing comprehensive disease management narratives, as gallstone disease may complicate the clinical picture and affect quality of life outcomes in this population. Multidisciplinary KOL engagement with gastroenterologists and cardiologists could explore the intersection of micronutrient intake, cardiometabolic risk, and hepatobiliary disease burden, potentially informing holistic management guidelines for hypertensive patients.

Exploring the Facilitators and Barriers to Physical Activity among Adults Experiencing Food Insecurity During the Healthy Food First Trial: A Qualitative Study.

Revue : Current developments in nutrition | Tier : NA | IF estime : NA

Date de publication : 2026-07-21

Resultats principaux : This qualitative study embedded within the Healthy Food First randomized clinical trial enrolled 38 adults with hypertension experiencing food insecurity, predominantly female (66%) and Black (53%), assigned to food support with or without community health worker (CHW) lifestyle coaching. Semi-structured telephone interviews at 12 or 18 months revealed that motivation for physical activity was high, but sustained engagement was consistently constrained by time scarcity, fatigue, occupational demands, environmental safety concerns, weather conditions, health complications, and financial limitations. Social connection and access to physical activity-friendly environments were identified as key facilitators. CHW support was viewed as encouraging but predominantly diet-focused, limiting its impact on physical activity engagement. Participants described bidirectional links between diet quality, energy levels, and physical activity capacity, with physically demanding occupational activity often substituting for but not replacing the benefits of structured leisure-time physical activity.

Implications Medical Affairs : These qualitative findings demonstrate that hypertensive patients experiencing food insecurity face substantial structural and contextual barriers to lifestyle modification — a key non-pharmacological component of hypertension management guidelines — underscoring the critical importance of pharmacological therapy adherence in this population. Medical Affairs should leverage such data to support integrated social determinants of health programs alongside antihypertensive treatment, positioning pharmaceutical solutions as essential rather than complementary in resource-limited settings where lifestyle modification is structurally constrained. KOL engagement with community medicine specialists, social epidemiologists, and health equity researchers could inform Medical Affairs communications on the unmet pharmacological need in disadvantaged hypertensive populations, driving advocacy for simplified treatment regimens and access programs.

Incidence and risk factors for stillbirth in Kenya, Mozambique and the Gambia: the PRECISE cohort study.

Revue : The Lancet regional health. Africa | Tier : NA | IF estime : NA

Date de publication : 2026-08

Resultats principaux : This prospective multi-country cohort study pooled data from 5,772 women aged 16–49 across seven health facilities in Kenya, Mozambique, and The Gambia (PRECISE cohort, 2019–2022) to determine stillbirth rates and associated risk factors. Overall stillbirth incidence was 29.2 per 1,000 total births (95% CI: 24.8–33.6), with significant country variation: 17.5 in Kenya, 32.8 in Mozambique, and 49.8 per 1,000 in The Gambia; 89.1% occurred at ≥28 weeks gestation. Stage 2 hypertension emerged as a significant independent risk factor for stillbirth (aRR 3.25, 95% CI: 1.42–7.44), alongside previous stillbirth (aRR 3.18), antepartum haemorrhage (aRR 3.10), Caesarean delivery (aRR 1.74), and small-for-gestational age (aRR 2.05). A notable subgroup finding was that systemic hypertension contributed a three-fold increased risk comparable to other major obstetric complications, highlighting blood pressure control as a key modifiable risk factor in this setting. Key limitations include facility-based recruitment potentially overrepresenting higher-risk pregnancies, potential gestational age misclassification, and limited generalizability beyond sub-Saharan African settings.

Implications Medical Affairs : The three-fold stillbirth risk associated with Stage 2 hypertension in sub-Saharan African populations underscores the critical importance of effective blood pressure management in antenatal care in low-resource settings, with direct implications for global maternal health guidelines and access to antihypertensive therapy. Medical Affairs should engage with global health KOLs and obstetric societies operating in sub-Saharan Africa to highlight the preventable nature of hypertension-related stillbirths and advocate for expanded access to safe, effective antihypertensive therapies for hypertensive disorders of pregnancy in LMICs. These findings provide a compelling evidence base for pharmaceutical companies to support and partner with international health organizations in expanding access to pregnancy-compatible antihypertensive treatments in low-resource settings, directly addressing a major cause of preventable perinatal mortality.

Precursor Lesions in Hepatocellular Carcinogenesis.

Revue : Visceral medicine | Tier : NA | IF estime : NA

Date de publication : 2026-07-10

Resultats principaux : This narrative review discusses the spectrum of hepatocellular carcinoma (HCC) precursor lesions, characterizing the histopathological, molecular, and clinical entities that confer elevated risk of progression to primary hepatic malignancy, with a particular focus on the role of Schistosoma species in hepatocarcinogenesis. Chronic hepatosplenic schistosomiasis is described as an indirect precancerous condition wherein longstanding granulomatous inflammation and periportal fibrosis induce portal hypertension, which — in conjunction with viral hepatitis or other hepatic insults — creates a milieu facilitating hepatocellular carcinogenesis. The review emphasizes that portal hypertension, chronic immune activation, and fibrotic remodeling are key contributors to the carcinogenic microenvironment, even though the parasite does not directly transform hepatocytes. Key regulatory networks involved in liver regeneration and cell cycle control are highlighted as relevant mechanistic targets. Notably, this review addresses portal hypertension in a hepatic oncology context, not arterial hypertension; its relevance to systemic antihypertensive management is indirect and limited.

Implications Medical Affairs : While the primary focus of this review is hepatocarcinogenesis rather than arterial hypertension, the discussion of portal hypertension as a carcinogenesis risk-modifying factor is of limited direct relevance to systemic antihypertensive therapy positioning but may be of interest to companies with hepatic or oncology portfolios. Medical Affairs teams focused on arterial hypertension monitoring should note that this article's inclusion in the search results reflects the broad co-occurrence of the term 'hypertension' in hepatic disease contexts, and refined semantic search strategies could improve future surveillance specificity.
