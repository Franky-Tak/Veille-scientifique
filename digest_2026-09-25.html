[Effectiveness evaluation of heat-sensitive moxibustion self-management in community patients with essential hypertension: a randomized controlled trial]

Revue : Zhen Ci Yan Jiu (Acupuncture Research) | Tier : NA | IF estime : NA

Date de publication : 2026-09-25

Resultats principaux : This randomized controlled trial enrolled 120 community patients with essential hypertension across three communities in China, comparing heat-sensitive moxibustion self-management combined with standard antihypertensive regimen to standard treatment alone over a 6-month period, with 89.2% (107/120) completing follow-up. The primary endpoint was reduction in systolic blood pressure (SBP), and the intervention group demonstrated a significantly greater reduction from baseline compared to controls (p<0.01), while no significant difference was observed in diastolic blood pressure reduction. The antihypertensive effective rate was significantly higher in the moxibustion group (p<0.05), and patients showed marked improvements in EQ-5D-5L quality-of-life scores and traditional Chinese medicine (TCM) hypertension symptom scores including headache, tinnitus, and red eyes (p<0.01 and p<0.05). All moxibustion-related adverse events were mild, and the intervention did not require changes to pharmacological regimens. Limitations include the impossibility of blinding participants to the intervention, the small sample size of 120 patients, and the restriction to Chinese community settings which limits generalizability to non-Asian populations.

Implications Medical Affairs : This RCT provides clinical evidence that adjunctive traditional Chinese medicine approaches can meaningfully reduce systolic blood pressure and improve quality of life alongside standard pharmacotherapy, with implications for combination therapy strategies in markets where TCM is integrated into primary care pathways. These findings reinforce the increasing regulatory and HTA expectation that hypertension trials capture patient-reported outcomes and quality-of-life measures as co-primary or secondary endpoints, and pharmaceutical companies should incorporate these frameworks into future trial designs. Engaging KOLs in integrative cardiology and community hypertension management around the additive value of non-pharmacological adjuncts could differentiate product positioning and strengthen global Medical Affairs narratives on holistic blood pressure management.

Treatment burden, healthcare experiences, and health-related quality of life among youth with chronic kidney disease treated for hypertension

Revue : Quality of Life Research | Tier : NA | IF estime : NA

Date de publication : 2026-09-24

Resultats principaux : This multi-center cross-sectional study used electronic health records from 15 pediatric institutions to assess 395 youth (mean age 16.8 years, 54% female) with chronic kidney disease (CKD) and hypertension, supplemented by online questionnaires from 238 caregivers. Most youth (92%) reported at least one physical symptom, with a median of 5 symptoms (IQR 3-8), and over 95% of caregivers reported generally positive healthcare experiences. Antihypertensive medication use was distributed as follows: 21% took no medications, 56% took one, and 23% took multiple medications. Use of multiple antihypertensive medications was associated with modestly lower global health scores (B = -2.6; 95% CI: -4.9 to -0.3) after adjustment for CKD stage, while no significant associations were found for other health-related quality-of-life domains. Overall, youth with CKD and hypertension reported significantly lower quality of life than the general population, with the most pronounced impairments in global health and fatigue, and the cross-sectional design limits causal inference regarding medication burden and long-term HRQoL trajectories.

Implications Medical Affairs : This study highlights the substantial quality-of-life burden in pediatric patients with CKD and hypertension, a population increasingly targeted by specialized nephrology and cardiovascular therapies, and underscores the importance of minimizing polypharmacy-related HRQoL deterioration when selecting antihypertensive agents. From a Medical Affairs perspective, these findings support the development and validation of pediatric-specific patient-reported outcome instruments for hypertension trials, which can strengthen regulatory dossiers and formulary positioning for medications with favorable tolerability profiles in younger age groups. Engaging KOLs in pediatric nephrology and hypertension to champion HRQoL measurement as a standard of care in pediatric CKD could create scientific differentiation opportunities and strengthen product profiles in this emerging therapeutic area.

Vascular Dysfunction in Diabetes and Hypertension: Insights into HSP70

Revue : Cells | Tier : NA | IF estime : NA

Date de publication : 2026-09-13

Resultats principaux : This comprehensive narrative review examined the emerging role of Heat Shock Protein 70 (HSP70) in vascular dysfunction in diabetes and hypertension, two highly prevalent and frequently co-occurring conditions that significantly elevate cardiovascular risk. The review details how hyperglycemia in diabetes and elevated angiotensin II in hypertension converge on shared molecular pathways promoting oxidative stress, low-grade inflammation, endothelial damage, and vascular remodeling. The protein exists in two functionally opposing forms: intracellular HSP70 (iHSP70) appears to regulate intracellular calcium handling with a cytoprotective role, while extracellular HSP70 (eHSP70) is elevated in both conditions and associated with inflammatory signaling and vascular injury. The authors discuss emerging pharmacological approaches to modulate HSP70 and position it as a candidate biomarker and therapeutic target, though significant mechanistic heterogeneity across experimental models remains a challenge for clinical translation. Key limitations include the narrative (non-systematic) review methodology, absence of clinical outcome data from HSP70-targeted interventions, and variability across in vitro and animal models that constrains direct clinical applicability.

Implications Medical Affairs : HSP70 emerges as a mechanistically plausible molecular target at the intersection of hypertension and diabetes-related vascular dysfunction, offering a potential biomarker pathway for companies developing therapies for cardiometabolic conditions with vascular endpoints. From a Medical Affairs perspective, this review supports investment in translational research programs to validate HSP70 as a surrogate endpoint in clinical trials targeting vascular protection in hypertensive diabetic patients, a growing and high-priority comorbid population. Pharmaceutical companies with RAAS-targeting or anti-inflammatory pipelines should monitor the HSP70 field closely for mechanistic differentiation opportunities and KOL engagement in precision cardiometabolic medicine.

Effect of Perinatal and Maternal Factors on Blood Pressure and Adiposity at Age 3

Revue : Journal of Clinical Hypertension | Tier : 3 | IF estime : 3-10

Date de publication : 2026-09

Resultats principaux : This retrospective case-control study analyzed 128 children and their mothers enrolled in the Northwell Health Generations Project — a longitudinal observational population-based cohort — to evaluate associations between perinatal and maternal factors and childhood blood pressure and adiposity outcomes at age 3. Multivariable regression identified cesarean delivery (beta=0.07, CI 0.04-0.10, p<0.001) and small for gestational age (beta=0.07, CI 0.002-0.13, p=0.04) as independently associated with greater systolic blood pressure index during early childhood. In vitro fertilization (IVF) was associated with significantly greater odds of elevated blood pressure status (OR=3.52, CI 1.04-12.0, p=0.04), a novel finding with potential clinical implications for cardiovascular monitoring in IVF-conceived children. Low birth weight was associated with lower childhood body mass index Z-scores (beta=-0.86, CI -1.64 to -0.08, p=0.03), suggesting divergent trajectories for blood pressure and adiposity in this subgroup. Limitations include the small sample size (n=128), retrospective case-control design, restriction to one health system (Northwell), and use of early-childhood blood pressure measurements as proxies for longer-term cardiovascular risk trajectories.

Implications Medical Affairs : This study extends the developmental origins of cardiovascular disease hypothesis by identifying IVF conception and cesarean delivery as novel perinatal predictors of elevated childhood blood pressure, adding nuance to pediatric hypertension risk stratification that could inform screening guidelines and early intervention protocols. From a Medical Affairs standpoint, these findings reinforce the value of longitudinal pediatric cardiovascular monitoring programs, which may be incorporated into risk-stratification frameworks where pharmaceutical companies can demonstrate product relevance in high-risk pediatric cohorts. Engaging KOLs in maternal-fetal medicine and pediatric cardiology around perinatal cardiovascular risk creates new scientific discourse channels and positions awareness initiatives focused on early identification and prevention of hypertension.

Hypertensive Disorders of Pregnancy and Incident Dementia

Revue : JAMA Network Open | Tier : NA | IF estime : NA

Date de publication : 2026-09-01

Resultats principaux : This nationwide retrospective cohort study used the Korean National Health Insurance Service database to follow 1,370,485 females who delivered between 2002 and 2005 through December 2024 — providing more than two decades of longitudinal follow-up — to assess the association between hypertensive disorders of pregnancy (HDPs) and incident dementia. Among the cohort, 2.1% (28,747 women) experienced HDPs (gestational hypertension, preeclampsia, HELLP syndrome, or eclampsia), and the cumulative incidence of dementia was significantly higher in this group (7.2 vs 2.8 per 10,000 females; incidence density 3.00 vs 1.05 per 100,000 person-years; p<0.001). After adjusting for baseline maternal, socioeconomic, and obstetric factors, HDPs were associated with nearly twice the risk of incident dementia (adjusted HR: 1.96; 95% CI: 1.19-3.23). However, after additional adjustment for postdelivery hypertension, diabetes, myocardial infarction, and stroke, the association was attenuated to non-significance (adjusted HR: 1.15; 95% CI: 0.70-1.91), suggesting the dementia risk is largely mediated through subsequent cardiometabolic morbidity rather than the pregnancy event itself. Key limitations include the small absolute number of dementia events in the HDP subgroup, potential coding inaccuracies in administrative data, and restriction to a Korean population limiting ethnic generalizability.

Implications Medical Affairs : This large-scale cohort study provides compelling evidence that HDPs serve as early markers of long-term neurovascular vulnerability, supporting a cardiovascular continuum model where pregnancy complications predict future neurodegeneration risk, primarily mediated through subsequent vascular and metabolic disease — reinforcing the scientific rationale for sustained cardiovascular risk management after HDP-complicated pregnancies. From a Medical Affairs perspective, these findings potentially expand the treated population for antihypertensive therapies and cardiometabolic agents to women with HDP history, a scientifically differentiated and commercially relevant segment. Pharmaceutical companies should engage KOLs in cardio-obstetrics and neurology to build integrated scientific narratives linking HDP management to downstream cognitive protection, a high-impact and underexplored positioning opportunity.

Endocrine-Disrupting Chemicals and Hypertension: Mechanisms and Clinical Implications

Revue : Journal of Cardiovascular Development and Disease | Tier : NA | IF estime : NA

Date de publication : 2026-09-14

Resultats principaux : This comprehensive review examined the experimental and epidemiological evidence linking endocrine-disrupting chemicals (EDCs) — including per- and polyfluoroalkyl substances (PFASs), bisphenol A, phthalates, polychlorinated biphenyls (PCBs), heavy metals, and pesticides — to the pathogenesis of hypertension at the molecular and clinical levels. The review details multiple converging mechanisms through which EDCs promote blood pressure dysregulation, including oxidative stress, endothelial dysfunction, chronic low-grade inflammation, epigenetic modifications, and activation of the renin-angiotensin-aldosterone system (RAAS), creating a complex interplay that mirrors and amplifies traditional hypertensive pathophysiology. Prenatal EDC exposure was specifically associated with hypertensive disorders of pregnancy, including gestational hypertension and preeclampsia, with proposed long-term implications for maternal cardiovascular health that parallel those observed with HDPs from other etiologies. Significant epidemiological associations were identified between EDC exposure and both prevalent and incident hypertension across multiple population cohorts, though causality has not been definitively established due to the challenge of isolating individual chemical effects from complex real-world co-exposures. Limitations include heterogeneity of exposure assessment methods across studies, the inherent difficulty of disentangling EDC-specific effects from confounding lifestyle and nutritional factors, and the absence of interventional data confirming reversibility of EDC-induced hypertensive effects.

Implications Medical Affairs : The convergence of EDC-driven RAAS activation and endothelial dysfunction with established pharmacological targets of antihypertensive therapy — particularly RAAS inhibitors — suggests a scientifically differentiated messaging opportunity for Medical Affairs in environmentally exposed hypertensive populations where RAAS-targeting agents may provide enhanced mechanistic benefit. This review reflects a growing regulatory and public health interest in environmental determinants of hypertension that may reshape treatment guidelines and expand the scientific context for cardiovascular drug approvals, which pharmaceutical companies should monitor proactively. KOL engagement strategies should incorporate environmental cardiologists and environmental epidemiologists as emerging influencers in the hypertension science landscape, particularly in academic centers conducting research on environmental cardiovascular risk factors.

A Structured Narrative Review of Contemporary Dietary Strategies Following Acute Coronary Syndromes

Revue : Journal of Cardiovascular Development and Disease | Tier : NA | IF estime : NA

Date de publication : 2026-08-26

Resultats principaux : This structured narrative review searched PubMed/MEDLINE through October 2025 to compare eight dietary patterns — Mediterranean, DASH (Dietary Approaches to Stop Hypertension), plant-based, intermittent fasting, low-carbohydrate/ketogenic, Palaeolithic, Atkins, and Carnivore diets — for secondary cardiovascular prevention following acute coronary syndromes (ACS), identifying 28 studies meeting inclusion criteria. Primary outcomes were major adverse cardiovascular events and cardiovascular mortality; secondary outcomes included LDL-cholesterol, blood pressure, body weight, and dietary adherence, with hypertension management being a central secondary focus given the explicit hypertension-targeting design of the DASH diet. The Mediterranean diet demonstrated the strongest and most consistent evidence for reducing recurrent cardiovascular events and improving multiple cardiovascular risk factors including blood pressure after ACS, across five included studies. DASH and plant-based dietary patterns improved cardiovascular risk factors including blood pressure and lipids but lacked robust post-ACS randomized outcome data, while evidence for intermittent fasting, low-carbohydrate/ketogenic, Palaeolithic, Atkins, and Carnivore diets was limited with no randomized trials in post-ACS populations with adequate follow-up. Key limitations include the structured narrative (not systematic or meta-analytic) design, heterogeneous study populations across the eight dietary comparisons, the absence of head-to-head dietary randomized trials, and the inclusion of evidence from broader cardiovascular populations when ACS-specific data were unavailable.

Implications Medical Affairs : The demonstrated efficacy of DASH and Mediterranean diets in reducing blood pressure and cardiovascular risk reinforces the critical role of non-pharmacological interventions as adjuncts to antihypertensive pharmacotherapy in post-ACS patients with comorbid hypertension, informing Medical Affairs communication strategies for combination cardiovascular therapy positioning. This review highlights a significant evidence gap for newer popular dietary approaches in secondary cardiovascular prevention, representing a scientific opportunity for pharmaceutical company-sponsored real-world evidence generation that examines the interaction between dietary patterns and antihypertensive pharmacotherapy outcomes. Engagement with clinical nutrition KOLs and cardiovascular guideline committees around dietary hypertension management can position pharmaceutical companies as comprehensive cardiovascular health partners, beyond pure pharmacological intervention, strengthening brand differentiation in a competitive market.

The authors highlighted that MASLD is independently associated with elevated cardiovascular disease risk in pediatric patients, irrespective of traditional cardiovascular risk factors including hypertension, dyslipidemia, and diabetes mellitus — a finding of major clinical significance for pediatric cardiology and hepatology. Key limitations include the review's narrative design, heterogeneous nature of pediatric MASLD literature, absence of head-to-head treatment trials, and the lack of validated noninvasive biomarkers for pediatric MASLD staging.

Perception of fall risk among cancer survivors receiving Radiotherapy/Chemotherapy in China: A latent profile analysis

Revue : PLoS One | Tier : NA | IF estime : NA

Date de publication : 2026-09-24

Resultats principaux : This cross-sectional study enrolled 534 cancer survivors undergoing radiotherapy or chemotherapy at a tertiary hospital in Zibo, China between June and September 2025, employing latent profile analysis (LPA) to characterize heterogeneous subgroups of perceived fall risk among this vulnerable oncology population. Three distinct fall risk perception profiles were identified: Low-level Perceptually Deficient Group (22.85%), Medium-level Condition-dependent Group (49.25%), and High-level Self-awareness Group (27.90%), with significant heterogeneity in risk awareness suggesting the need for tailored nursing interventions. Hypertension was identified as one of several significant physiological health factors (alongside illness duration, tumor stage, fall history, and vision impairment) associated with fall risk perception subgroup membership in multivariate analysis (p<0.05). Psychological factors (self-assessment of health status, self-efficacy, and health literacy) and social and environmental factors (marital status, education level, caregiver availability, social support, and use of walking aids) were additionally identified as significant determinants. Key limitations include the cross-sectional design precluding causal inference, convenience sampling from a single tertiary center limiting generalizability, and the subjective and potentially culturally specific nature of the fall risk perception instrument used.

Implications Medical Affairs : The identification of hypertension as a significant physiological determinant of fall risk perception in oncology patients has important Medical Affairs implications for antihypertensive drug safety, particularly regarding postural hypotension and fall-related adverse events in elderly or polypharmacy patients receiving concurrent oncological treatments. Pharmaceutical companies with antihypertensive portfolios should proactively review fall-related adverse event data in their oncology and polypharmacy patient subgroups, as regulators are increasingly scrutinizing fall risk in labeling decisions for cardiovascular drugs across comorbid populations. Building real-world evidence in comorbid cancer-hypertension populations could support evidence-based prescribing guidance and KOL engagement at the emerging intersection of oncocardiology and hypertension management.

Revue : Journal of Clinical Laboratory Analysis | Tier : NA | IF estime : NA

Date de publication : 2026-09-23

Resultats principaux : This retrospective case-control study enrolled 210 COVID-19 patients and 210 age- and sex-matched healthy controls from southern Iran to evaluate whether the Interferon-gamma (IFN-gamma) gene +874 T/A polymorphism was associated with COVID-19 susceptibility, disease severity, and inflammatory response. Hypertension was significantly more prevalent among COVID-19 patients compared to controls (29.5% vs. 16.7%), confirming its role as a major comorbidity and risk amplifier for severe COVID-19 disease, with patients also showing elevated neutrophil-to-lymphocyte ratio (8.1 vs. 1.9), CRP (45.5 vs. 3.2 mg/L), and lymphopenia (1.1 vs. 2.1 x 10^9/L; all p<0.001). Key limitations include the small sample size (n=210 per group), restriction to a single region in southern Iran, retrospective design, and potential residual confounding by unmeasured comorbidities.

Implications Medical Affairs : The disproportionate prevalence of hypertension in severe COVID-19 patients in this study corroborates the established cardiovascular vulnerability of hypertensive patients to acute viral respiratory illness, supporting scientific communication strategies emphasizing optimal blood pressure control as a strategy to reduce the severity of intercurrent infections in hypertensive patients. From a Medical Affairs perspective, these findings support the development of real-world evidence studies evaluating whether specific antihypertensive drug classes — particularly RAAS inhibitors with potential immunomodulatory effects — influence immune response and infection outcomes in comorbid populations.
