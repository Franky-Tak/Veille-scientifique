Blood pressure phenotypes and hypertension-associated cardiac damage.

Revue : Journal of Hypertension | Tier : Tier 2 | IF estime : 10-12

Date de publication : 2026-08-26

Resultats principaux : This population-based study included 4,115 individuals who underwent office and home blood pressure measurement alongside computed tomography, coronary CT angiography, and echocardiography to characterize hypertension-associated cardiac damage across four BP phenotypes: sustained normotension (SNT), white coat hypertension (WCHT), masked hypertension (MHT), and sustained hypertension (SHT). The primary endpoints included coronary artery calcium score (CACS) thresholds of 100 and 300, Segment Involvement Score (SIS) >=4, significant coronary stenosis >50%, left ventricular ejection fraction (LVEF) <50%, E/e' ratio >=14, and global longitudinal strain (GLS) beyond sex-specific thresholds. Individuals with WCHT showed significantly elevated odds of cardiac damage compared to SNT, including LVEF <50% (OR 1.83, 95% CI 1.17-2.86) and CACS >=100 (OR 1.58, 95% CI 1.19-2.11), findings previously underappreciated in this phenotype. Most strikingly, masked hypertension was associated with the highest odds of organ damage: LVEF <50% (OR 2.68, 95% CI 1.41-5.10), CACS >=100 (OR 2.15, 95% CI 1.37-3.38), and CACS >=300 (OR 2.34, 95% CI 2.21-4.52), exceeding even sustained hypertension for certain measures. The study is limited by its cross-sectional imaging design, which precludes causal inference, and the population-based sample may not fully represent higher-risk clinical populations typically seen in hypertension specialist settings.

Implications Medical Affairs : These data challenge current diagnostic pathways by demonstrating that masked hypertension carries cardiac damage burden comparable to or exceeding sustained hypertension, with implications for guideline updates recommending routine out-of-office BP monitoring in apparently normotensive patients. From a product-positioning standpoint, these findings reinforce the value of antihypertensive agents with demonstrated efficacy in reducing subclinical cardiac damage across BP phenotypes, providing Medical Affairs teams with compelling evidence-based messaging for out-of-office hypertension detection strategies. Key opinion leaders in hypertension and cardiology should be briefed on this study to support advocacy for expanded home BP monitoring reimbursement and to position 24h BP monitoring technologies and associated pharmacological interventions as integral to cardiovascular risk management.

Nighttime blood pressure versus non-dipping for predicting asymptomatic organ damage in young individuals.

Revue : Journal of Hypertension | Tier : Tier 2 | IF estime : 10-12

Date de publication : 2026-08-26

Resultats principaux : This prospective study enrolled 276 young individuals aged 6-25 years (mean age 13.8 ± 3.8 years, 67% male) who underwent 24-hour ambulatory blood pressure monitoring (ABPM), echocardiography for left ventricular mass index (LVMI), and carotid ultrasonography for intima-media thickness (IMT) to evaluate hypertension-mediated organ damage (HMOD). Circadian BP phenotypes were defined with 23.6% meeting criteria for ABPM hypertension, 26.4% for nighttime hypertension, and 30.4% classified as non-dippers. Non-dippers had significantly higher LVMI (30.5 ± 8.2 vs. 28.4 ± 5.95 g/m2.7, P=0.01) and IMT (0.52 ± 0.05 vs. 0.50 ± 0.05 mm, P=0.02) after adjustment for age, sex, BMI z-score, and 24-h systolic BP. Nighttime hypertension was more powerfully associated with elevated LVMI (32.76 ± 8.02 vs. 27.69 ± 5.65 g/m2.7, P<0.001) than non-dipping status alone in multivariable models, highlighting that absolute nighttime BP level rather than the dipping pattern is the primary driver of subclinical organ damage. The study is limited by its relatively small sample size, single-center design, and cross-sectional nature of the imaging assessments, which restrict extrapolation to broader pediatric hypertensive populations.

Implications Medical Affairs : These findings provide the strongest pediatric evidence to date that nighttime systolic blood pressure is the dominant driver of subclinical cardiac and vascular damage in youth, underscoring the need for ABPM as a standard diagnostic tool even in this low-risk age group. For Medical Affairs, this data supports advocacy for earlier BP phenotyping in children and adolescents, particularly in the context of positioning antihypertensive products with demonstrated reductions in nighttime BP and end-organ protection profiles. This evidence base should be leveraged in KOL engagement with pediatric hypertension specialists and in communications with regulatory bodies and payer organizations to support the clinical utility of nocturnal BP assessment protocols.

Occupational Physical Activity and Hypertension: A Narrative Review.

Revue : Current Hypertension Reports | Tier : NA | IF estime : NA

Date de publication : 2026-08-26

Resultats principaux : This narrative review examined prospective and experimental evidence on the relationship between occupational physical activity (OPA) and hypertension-related outcomes, including resting and ambulatory blood pressure, in working-age adults across diverse occupational sectors. The review found that prospective associations between OPA and hypertension are predominantly null or modestly inverse, with significant heterogeneity across studies attributable to methodological limitations in exposure assessment and failure to account for recovery time and psychosocial job strain. Notably, very high-intensity OPA may paradoxically elevate ambulatory cardiovascular load and impair autonomic recovery in specific subgroups, particularly those with low occupational recovery or high cardiovascular strain, despite minimal effect on resting clinic blood pressure. A key mechanistic distinction emerges: leisure-time physical activity consistently confers antihypertensive benefits, whereas OPA involving sustained low-to-moderate effort without adequate recovery may produce different cardiovascular responses at the molecular and autonomic levels. The review identifies critical methodological gaps including lack of validated OPA exposure assessment tools, inadequate use of ambulatory monitoring in occupational settings, and insufficient mechanistic study designs to elucidate pathways linking workplace physical demands to blood pressure regulation.

Implications Medical Affairs : The distinction between occupational and leisure-time physical activity challenges the prevailing assumption that total daily activity volume is uniformly cardioprotective, with direct relevance for clinical guidelines addressing physical activity recommendations in working adults with or at risk for hypertension. From a Medical Affairs perspective, these findings identify an underserved patient segment — workers with high physical occupational demands — who may not benefit from standard lifestyle-based hypertension prevention messages and who may require pharmacological management earlier than conventionally expected. This represents an actionable insight for KOL strategy, particularly in engaging with occupational medicine specialists and public health authorities to develop targeted screening and treatment algorithms for hypertensive workers in physically demanding professions.

Metabolic score for insulin resistance is associated with metabolic dysfunction-associated steatotic liver disease among adults with hypertension: a cross-sectional secondary analysis of a Chinese single-center health check-up population.

Revue : Frontiers in Endocrinology | Tier : NA | IF estime : NA

Date de publication : 2026-08-11

Resultats principaux : This cross-sectional secondary analysis included 943 hypertensive adults from a Chinese single-center health check-up cohort, investigating the association between the Metabolic Score for Insulin Resistance (METS-IR) — a composite index derived from BMI, fasting glucose, triglycerides, and HDL-cholesterol — and metabolic dysfunction-associated steatotic liver disease (MASLD) defined by ultrasonographic hepatic steatosis. The overall prevalence of MASLD in the hypertensive cohort was remarkably high at 67.9%, with prevalence increasing monotonically across METS-IR quartiles from 40.7% in quartile 1 to 89.0% in quartile 4. In the primary multivariable model adjusted for age, sex, tobacco and alcohol use, and diabetes, each 1 standard deviation increase in METS-IR was associated with an OR of 2.78 (95% CI 2.24-3.44, P<0.001) for MASLD; the extended model yielded an OR of 2.47 (95% CI 1.91-3.18). ROC analysis demonstrated an area under the curve of 0.738, with a data-derived METS-IR cut-point of 42.40 offering 53.0% sensitivity and 81.8% specificity, and a borderline interaction by sex (P=0.046) suggested potentially stronger associations in women. The study is limited by its cross-sectional and single-center design, retrospective data collection, and the use of ultrasonography rather than biopsy for MASLD diagnosis, restricting causal inference and generalizability beyond the Chinese hypertensive population studied.

Implications Medical Affairs : The high MASLD prevalence (68%) in this hypertensive cohort underscores the frequent overlap of metabolic comorbidities in treated hypertension patients, a finding with direct relevance for antihypertensive agents with potential hepatometabolic benefits or that may interact with MASLD pharmacotherapy. This evidence supports Medical Affairs communication strategies that position comprehensive cardiometabolic risk profiling — including METS-IR as a practical screening tool — as an integral component of hypertension management, particularly in Asian populations with high metabolic disease burden. KOL engagement should include hepatologists and endocrinologists in addition to cardiologists to highlight the bidirectional hypertension-MASLD relationship and to advocate for multidisciplinary treatment pathways that could benefit from combination pharmacological strategies.

Exceptional Parental Longevity and Onset of Morbidity and Mortality Across Cohorts.

Revue : JAMA Network Open | Tier : NA | IF estime : NA

Date de publication : 2026-08-03

Resultats principaux : This cohort study analyzed three independent longitudinal cohorts — LonGenity (n=480), the New England Centenarian Study (NECS, n=1,566), and the UK Biobank (n=1,984) — to evaluate whether offspring of centenarians exhibit delayed onset of death and age-associated morbidities including cardiovascular disease, cancer, hypertension, and stroke compared to control offspring of parents with shorter lifespans. Meta-analyses across all three cohorts identified consistently reduced hazard ratios for death (HR 0.58, 95% CI 0.41-0.81), CVD (HR 0.67, 95% CI 0.46-0.97), and hypertension (HR 0.68, 95% CI 0.62-0.75) in offspring of centenarians, with mean delays of 3.12 years for death (95% CI 0.96-5.28) and 5.21 years for hypertension onset (95% CI 2.13-8.29). Stroke risk reduction was observed in LonGenity (HR 0.27, 95% CI 0.09-0.81) and NECS (HR 0.41, 95% CI 0.27-0.63) but not replicated in the UK Biobank, and no significant association with cancer was found. Subgroup consistency across ethnically and geographically diverse cohorts strengthens the generalizability of the hypertension and CVD findings, suggesting a heritable mechanism — whether genetic, epigenetic, or shared environmental — that delays cardiometabolic aging by approximately five years. Limitations include differential enrollment ages and follow-up durations across cohorts, potential for selective survival bias in centenarian identification, and heterogeneous definitions of parental lifespan across the three study designs.

Implications Medical Affairs : The robust 32% reduction in hypertension risk and approximately 5-year delay in hypertension onset in offspring of centenarians provides compelling evidence for a biological resilience phenotype that could serve as a model for future pharmacological and genetic targets in preventive hypertension therapy. From a Medical Affairs standpoint, these findings reinforce the concept of cardiovascular aging delay as a meaningful clinical endpoint, which may be relevant for positioning long-acting antihypertensive agents that target underlying aging mechanisms, such as angiotensin system modulation or inflammation pathways. This publication in JAMA Network Open warrants proactive medical communication and KOL briefing, as the multi-cohort design and high-profile journal placement make it likely to influence upcoming healthy aging guidelines and public health messaging around hypertension prevention.

Theme 5 : Diuretics and Beta-blockers (1 article)

Portal Vein Thrombosis Risk in Cirrhotic Patients With Portal Hypertension on Beta-Blockers: A Multi-Institutional Cohort Study.

Revue : Liver International | Tier : NA | IF estime : NA

Date de publication : 2026-09

Resultats principaux : This retrospective multi-institutional cohort study utilized the TriNetX US Collaborative Network (>110 million patients) to assess portal vein thrombosis (PVT) risk in adults with cirrhosis and portal hypertension who underwent variceal band ligation between 2010 and 2024, stratified by nonselective beta-blocker (NSBB) exposure: propranolol, carvedilol, or no NSBB. After 1:1 propensity score matching on 12 clinical variables (n=4,296 per group), NSBB use overall was associated with significantly higher PVT incidence compared to no NSBB (9.5% vs. 5.4%; RR 1.765, 95% CI 1.500-2.077), indicating an unexpected adverse thrombotic effect of this drug class in this high-risk population. Agent-specific analysis revealed an important differential effect: propranolol use was associated with elevated PVT risk, whereas carvedilol showed PVT incidence comparable to the no-NSBB control group and was associated with significantly lower all-cause mortality compared to both propranolol and no-NSBB use. Time-to-event analyses using Kaplan-Meier methodology confirmed these findings across the follow-up period, with the survival benefit of carvedilol appearing particularly pronounced beyond 12 months post-ligation. Limitations include the retrospective nature of the analysis, potential for residual confounding despite propensity matching, reliance on administrative coding for PVT and portal hypertension diagnosis, and lack of data on NSBB dosing, adherence, and hemodynamic endpoints such as hepatic venous pressure gradient.

Implications Medical Affairs : These real-world data provide the most robust comparative evidence to date distinguishing carvedilol from propranolol in portal hypertension management, with carvedilol demonstrating a dual advantage of no excess PVT risk and improved survival — findings that directly challenge the interchangeability of NSBBs assumed in current hepatology guidelines. For Medical Affairs teams with a cardiovascular-hepatology interface, this evidence supports the development of differentiated messaging around carvedilol's unique pharmacological profile (combined alpha- and beta-blockade) as a mechanism for its superior outcomes in cirrhotic portal hypertension, potentially informing label extensions or supplemental marketing applications. This study warrants urgent dissemination through hepatology and gastroenterology KOL networks and should be considered for inclusion in the next update of portal hypertension management guidelines, where beta-blocker agent selection is currently not differentiated.

Theme 6 : Clinical Trials and Guidelines (1 article)

A pilot randomized controlled trial of the effects of dietary nitrate supplementation on dynamic cerebral autoregulation in older adults with metabolic syndrome.

Revue : Physiological Reports | Tier : NA | IF estime : NA

Date de publication : 2026-08

Resultats principaux : This double-blind, placebo-controlled pilot RCT randomized 22 older adults with metabolic syndrome (aged 55-75 years) to receive nitrate-rich beetroot juice (6.4 mmol NO/day) or nitrate-depleted placebo for 4 weeks, aiming to evaluate the feasibility and preliminary efficacy of dietary nitrate supplementation on dynamic cerebral autoregulation (dCA) — a measure of the brain's ability to maintain stable blood flow despite fluctuating systemic blood pressure. The intervention successfully raised plasma nitrate concentrations in the active arm (P<0.001), confirming adequate compliance and biological engagement, and the protocol demonstrated high adherence and good tolerability across the 4-week treatment period. Primary transfer function analysis (TFA) of frontal dCA phase and gain showed no significant treatment effect versus placebo, and cognitive outcomes were similarly unaffected at the study's primary analysis level. However, exploratory region-specific analyses identified improvements in TFA phase and gain in select frontal areas with beetroot juice supplementation, suggesting potential regional cerebrovascular benefits that were not captured by the aggregate frontal measure. Study limitations include the small sample size (n=22), pilot design with insufficient power for definitive conclusions, heterogeneous metabolic syndrome phenotypes, and use of fNIRS rather than Doppler ultrasound as the cerebral oxygenation measure, which may affect comparability with prior dCA literature.

Implications Medical Affairs : Although the primary endpoint was negative, this pilot RCT establishes feasibility and provides preliminary signal for region-specific cerebrovascular benefits of dietary nitrate — a biologically plausible NO-enhancing intervention — in metabolic syndrome patients who carry a disproportionate burden of cerebrovascular and cognitive risk. From a Medical Affairs perspective, these findings are relevant for positioning antihypertensive agents that enhance nitric oxide bioavailability (e.g., ARBs, ACE inhibitors, and direct NO donors) as potentially beneficial for cerebrovascular protection beyond blood pressure lowering in metabolic syndrome populations. A fully powered RCT informed by these pilot data would provide actionable evidence for guideline bodies and payers regarding adjunctive dietary or pharmacological NO-enhancing strategies in older hypertensive patients with metabolic syndrome and elevated cognitive decline risk.

Exploring the Role of Resistance Training in the Prevention and Management of Chronic Conditions: A Narrative Review.

Revue : Clinics and Practice | Tier : NA | IF estime : NA

Date de publication : 2026-08-21

Resultats principaux : This narrative review synthesized the growing evidence base on resistance training (RT) as a non-pharmacological intervention for the prevention and management of lifestyle-related chronic conditions including type 2 diabetes, obesity, hypertension, cardiovascular disease, dyslipidemia, and osteoporosis in adult populations. The review highlighted a critical epidemiological gap: while approximately half of US adults meet aerobic activity guidelines, only about one quarter meet dual aerobic and muscle-strengthening guidelines, suggesting that RT remains substantially underutilized despite evidence of benefit independent of and additive to aerobic exercise. For hypertension specifically, existing evidence demonstrates that RT reduces resting systolic blood pressure by approximately 2-3 mmHg on average, with some meta-analyses reporting reductions up to 4-5 mmHg, through mechanisms including improved endothelial function, reduced arterial stiffness, and decreased sympathetic nervous system activity. The review identified that current physical activity guidelines for Americans already recommend both aerobic and muscle-strengthening activities for adults, creating a guideline-to-practice gap that represents a major public health implementation challenge. Limitations include the narrative rather than systematic methodology, which introduces potential selection bias in literature coverage, and the heterogeneity of resistance training protocols across included studies limits the ability to provide precise dosing recommendations for hypertension management.

Implications Medical Affairs : The documented underutilization of resistance training — despite robust evidence of its antihypertensive and broader cardiometabolic benefits — signals an opportunity for Medical Affairs teams to support exercise-integrated hypertension management programs as adjuncts to pharmacotherapy, particularly in patients with treatment-resistant or lifestyle-driven hypertension. This evidence reinforces the positioning of antihypertensive medications within a broader lifestyle management framework, as patients engaging in RT may experience enhanced blood pressure response compared to sedentary controls, potentially lowering required pharmacological doses and improving long-term adherence. Collaboration with sports medicine KOLs and cardiovascular rehabilitation specialists should be prioritized to integrate RT recommendations into hypertension management protocols and to design investigator-initiated studies examining the additive effects of RT with specific antihypertensive drug classes.

Factors associated with anxiety, depression, and cognitive impairment in individuals with hypertension: a cross-sectional study based on a Chinese population.

Revue : Frontiers in Cardiovascular Medicine | Tier : NA | IF estime : NA

Date de publication : 2026-08-11

Resultats principaux : This multicenter large-sample cross-sectional study enrolled 1,915 essential hypertension patients from multiple hospitals in Shandong Province, China (May 2022-July 2024), stratified into four groups: hypertension alone (n=1,350), with cognitive impairment (n=191), with anxiety/depression (n=309), and with all three conditions co-occurring (n=65). For the hypertension-with-anxiety/depression group, independent risk factors identified through binary logistic regression included waist circumference (OR=1.06), heart rate (OR=1.02), elevated systolic blood pressure (OR=1.02), poor sleep quality, alcohol cessation, and education level, while protective factors included older age (OR=0.94), greater height (OR=0.96), occasional alcohol use (OR=0.25), family history of hypertension (OR=0.15), and lower LDL-C (OR=0.73). For patients with all three comorbidities (hypertension, anxiety/depression, and cognitive impairment), elevated waist circumference (OR=1.07) and poor sleep quality remained significant risk factors, while lower LDL-C was protective (OR=0.62), suggesting lipid management may have neuropsychiatric benefits beyond cardiovascular outcomes. ROC curves demonstrated good discriminative capacity for the identified factor combinations (AUC=0.895 and 0.887 for the two models respectively), indicating clinical utility for risk stratification. Study limitations include the cross-sectional design precluding causal inference, single-province recruitment limiting national generalizability, and self-reported assessment of sleep quality and alcohol use, which are subject to recall bias.

Implications Medical Affairs : The high co-occurrence of anxiety, depression, and cognitive impairment in hypertensive patients — and their association with modifiable factors such as waist circumference, sleep quality, and LDL-C — highlights the need for integrated mental health and cognitive screening in hypertension management protocols, creating an opportunity for Medical Affairs messaging around holistic patient management. From a product-positioning perspective, antihypertensive agents with favorable metabolic profiles and potential anxiolytic or sleep-improving properties (such as certain beta-blockers or ARBs with neuropsychiatric data) may be differentially positioned in complex hypertensive patients with psychiatric comorbidities. This study's large sample and clinically actionable ROC results should be leveraged in KOL communications targeting psychiatrists, neurologists, and cardiologists to promote multidisciplinary hypertension care pathways that address the full spectrum of cardiometabolic-neuropsychiatric burden.

Interrelationship Between Sarcopenia, Frailty and Cardiovascular Disease — The Crucial Role of Obesity in Hypothesis Generation: A Narrative Review.

Revue : Journal of Personalized Medicine | Tier : NA | IF estime : NA

Date de publication : 2026-08-07

Resultats principaux : This narrative review examined the triangular relationship between sarcopenia, frailty, and cardiovascular disease (CVD), with a particular focus on how obesity modulates this relationship in older adults, a population in which sarcopenic obesity is increasingly recognized as a distinct and highly prevalent phenotype. The review synthesized published studies on body composition, frailty phenotyping, and cardiovascular risk factor profiles, demonstrating that obese sarcopenic and obese frail patients had a substantially higher prevalence of cardiovascular risk factors — including hypertension, diabetes mellitus, dyslipidemia, smoking, and sedentary lifestyle — compared to non-obese sarcopenic or frail patients. In contrast, anorexic, malnourished frail patients with unintentional weight loss or sarcopenia without obesity showed a paradoxically low prevalence of traditional cardiovascular risk factors, suggesting that obesity is the critical mediator connecting muscle-wasting conditions to hypertension and CVD risk, rather than reduced muscle mass per se. The review proposes a biological framework in which visceral adiposity drives shared inflammatory, insulin resistance, and renin-angiotensin-aldosterone system activation pathways that simultaneously promote muscle catabolism, frailty, and hypertensive cardiovascular risk. Limitations include the narrative methodology with potential selection bias, the heterogeneity of sarcopenia and frailty definitions across included studies, and the predominance of cross-sectional data preventing causal pathway confirmation.

Implications Medical Affairs : The obesity-centric model proposed in this review has direct implications for hypertension management in the rapidly growing population of older adults with sarcopenic obesity, where weight management, muscle preservation, and blood pressure control must be simultaneously addressed — a context in which antihypertensive agents with favorable metabolic profiles are particularly relevant. Medical Affairs teams should leverage this evidence to support communication strategies emphasizing comprehensive geriatric cardiovascular risk management, positioning renin-angiotensin system inhibitors and other agents with muscle-protective or metabolically neutral properties in elderly hypertensive patients with frailty and sarcopenia. Collaboration with geriatrics, nephrology, and endocrinology KOLs is recommended to develop consensus statements on hypertension treatment targets and drug selection in the sarcopenic-frail patient, an underrepresented population in major cardiovascular trials.

Incidence and risk factors for cardiovascular mortality in Iranian adults: Findings from a 10-year Yazd Healthy Heart Project Study.

Revue : ARYA Atherosclerosis | Tier : NA | IF estime : NA

Date de publication : 2026

Resultats principaux : This population-based cohort study enrolled 2,000 adults aged 20-74 years (equal numbers of men and women) from Yazd, Iran, between 2005 and 2006, with a mean follow-up of 10 years, to characterize the incidence and lifestyle determinants of cardiovascular mortality in an Iranian urban population using Cox proportional hazards regression. During follow-up, 83 CVD deaths occurred (5.4% overall), with a markedly higher rate in men (6.8%) than women (3.8%), reflecting a significant sex disparity in cardiovascular outcomes in this Middle Eastern population. After adjustment for hypertension, diabetes, and hypercholesterolemia, independent predictors of CVD mortality included age >=65 years (HR=3.63, 95% CI 2.28-5.77), male sex (HR=2.29, 95% CI 1.31-3.34), and smoking (HR=1.95, 95% CI 1.21-3.13), while moderate physical activity was protective (HR=0.38, 95% CI 0.17-0.84) and high school education was also associated with reduced mortality (HR=0.49, 95% CI 0.25-0.98). In sex-stratified analysis among men, age >=65, moderate physical activity, and high school education remained significant independent predictors, while the protective effects of education and physical activity were attenuated or non-significant in women, possibly reflecting residual confounding from gender-specific sociocultural patterns of physical activity reporting. Limitations include the moderate sample size (2,000 participants), limited geographic scope (single city), potential underestimation of CVD mortality through incomplete death registration, and absence of data on dietary patterns, stress, or air pollution as hypertension-relevant exposures.

Implications Medical Affairs : This 10-year prospective cohort from Iran adds important longitudinal evidence on modifiable CVD mortality determinants in the Middle East and North Africa (MENA) region — a geography where hypertension prevalence is rising rapidly but where prospective data remain scarce — with direct relevance for understanding unmet medical need in this market. The prominent protective effect of moderate physical activity (62% relative risk reduction) and the trebling of CVD mortality risk in those aged >=65 years highlight the compounding burden of age and sedentary lifestyle in hypertensive cardiovascular risk, supporting Medical Affairs initiatives advocating for earlier pharmacological intervention combined with lifestyle modification in aging MENA populations. This study's data should inform regional epidemiological slide decks, market access submissions in the MENA region, and KOL engagement strategies targeting cardiologists and public health officials in Iran and neighboring countries where similar demographic and lifestyle patterns prevail.
