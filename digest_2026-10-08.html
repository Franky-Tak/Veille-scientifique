Systolic Blood Pressure Variability and Outcomes in Atrial Fibrillation: A Nationwide Population Study in Korea.

Revue : Journal of Korean Medical Science | Tier : NA | IF estime : NA

Date de publication : 2026-10-05

Resultats principaux : This nationwide retrospective cohort study used the Korean National Health Insurance database to evaluate the relationship between systolic blood pressure variability (SBP-V) and clinical outcomes in 235,091 patients with atrial fibrillation (AF) who underwent at least three health checkups between 2013 and 2014. The primary outcomes were stroke, major bleeding, and all-cause mortality assessed over a median follow-up of 4.9 years (IQR 4.3-5.3 years), during which 5,339 strokes (0.5/100 person-years), 3,432 major bleeding events (0.3/100 person-years), and 16,755 deaths (1.5/100 person-years) were recorded. Patients with SBP 130 mmHg or higher at baseline had significantly higher risks of stroke and major bleeding compared to those with SBP 120 to under 130 mmHg, while all-cause mortality was elevated at both high (150 mmHg or higher) and low (under 120 mmHg) SBP levels, demonstrating a J-curve relationship. The highest quartile of SBP-V was independently associated with greater risks of stroke (adjusted hazard ratio [aHR] 1.211; 95% CI 1.125-1.304), major bleeding (aHR 1.125; 95% CI 1.025-1.234), and all-cause death (aHR 1.189; 95% CI 1.141-1.239) compared to the lowest quartile. Subgroup analyses found no significant interaction between oral anticoagulation use and SBP-V for any clinical outcome, suggesting that BP variability-related risk is independent of anticoagulation status. A key limitation is the retrospective observational design relying on administrative data with limited clinical detail, such as antihypertensive medication types, dosing, and adherence information.

Implications Medical Affairs : These findings reinforce the growing evidence that blood pressure variability — not just mean BP level — is an independent predictor of adverse cardiovascular outcomes in AF patients, and may prompt guideline updates to formally include SBP variability monitoring as a therapeutic target in hypertension management algorithms. For Medical Affairs, this study provides a compelling scientific rationale to position antihypertensive agents with demonstrated benefits in reducing BP variability (such as long-acting calcium channel blockers and certain ARBs) for use in the large and clinically complex AF plus hypertension population. This creates actionable opportunities to engage KOLs in collaborative research on optimal antihypertensive strategies to minimize SBP variability in AF populations, particularly through Asian cardiovascular registries where the disease burden is high.

Comparison of Insomnia Risk between Spironolactone and Esaxerenone in Individuals with Hypertension.

Revue : JMA Journal | Tier : NA | IF estime : NA

Date de publication : 2026-05-29

Resultats principaux : This retrospective pharmacoepidemiological study used the DeSC nationwide claims database in Japan to compare insomnia incidence between hypertensive patients newly initiated on spironolactone (n=1,910) or esaxerenone (n=3,181), using inverse probability of treatment weighting to control for confounding. The 5,091 participants had a median age of 72 years (IQR 67-79), were 55% male, with a median estimated glomerular filtration rate of 66.3 mL/min/1.73 m2, representing an elderly population with mild-to-moderate renal impairment typical of resistant hypertension. Over a median follow-up of 512 days (IQR 240-848), 356 incident insomnia events were recorded, with weighted Cox regression showing spironolactone users had a significantly higher risk of insomnia versus esaxerenone users (HR 1.66; 95% CI 1.32-2.10). Sex-stratified analyses showed consistent effects in both men (HR 1.79; 95% CI 1.30-2.47) and women (HR 1.59; 95% CI 1.15-2.20), indicating no differential sex-based vulnerability. The proposed biological mechanism is that spironolactone's off-target binding to sex hormone receptors may disrupt sleep regulation via hormonal pathways, whereas esaxerenone's high mineralocorticoid receptor selectivity avoids this off-target activity. Limitations include the claims-based database design with potential residual confounding, lack of objective sleep quality measures (polysomnography), absence of data on comedications affecting sleep, and the observational design precluding definitive causal conclusions.

Implications Medical Affairs : This pharmacovigilance study provides important safety differentiation between spironolactone and esaxerenone in hypertensive patients, with insomnia being a highly prevalent and quality-of-life-impacting adverse effect that can compromise medication adherence and long-term blood pressure control, particularly in elderly patients. For Medical Affairs, these findings constitute a meaningful safety differentiator for esaxerenone relative to spironolactone in the mineralocorticoid receptor antagonist class, which can be integrated into prescribing guidance and benefit-risk communication materials targeting resistant hypertension specialists and nephrologists. This dataset could be leveraged in KOL advisory boards to develop medical education content on comparative safety profiles of MRA agents, positioning esaxerenone's receptor selectivity as a clinically meaningful advantage in elderly and polypharmacy hypertensive patients.

Longitudinal associations of dynamic transitions of multidimensional health vulnerability with cardiovascular disease risk: evidence from UK Biobank.

Revue : Frontiers in Public Health | Tier : NA | IF estime : NA

Date de publication : 2026-09-22

Resultats principaux : This longitudinal cohort study included 16,072 participants from the UK Biobank with multidimensional health vulnerability (HV) assessments at two timepoints — baseline (2006-2010) and repeat assessment (2014) — integrating physiological (including hypertension and cardiovascular markers), psychological, and social domain indicators into a composite vulnerability construct. Latent class analysis and latent transition analysis identified four distinct HV trajectory patterns, and Cox proportional hazards models were applied to evaluate associations between HV transitions and incident cardiovascular disease (CVD) over a median follow-up of 8.9 years, during which 3,033 incident CVD events were recorded. Compared to persistently low vulnerability, increasing vulnerability was associated with the highest CVD risk (HR 2.14; 95% CI 1.74-2.63), while persistently elevated vulnerability also showed significantly higher risk (HR 1.18; 95% CI 1.08-1.29). Subgroup analyses revealed significant effect modification by age and smoking status, with stronger associations observed in older participants and current/former smokers. Sensitivity analyses excluding hypertensive diseases from the composite CVD endpoint yielded consistent results, confirming that associations were not driven solely by hypertension reclassification. Limitations include the UK Biobank's predominantly white, educated, and healthier population (healthy volunteer bias), the two-point measurement of vulnerability limiting resolution of transition trajectories, and the observational design precluding causal inference.

Implications Medical Affairs : This study provides a novel multidimensional risk stratification framework that integrates psychosocial and social vulnerability alongside physiological risk factors including hypertension, with implications for developing more comprehensive and actionable cardiovascular risk assessment tools in clinical guidelines. For Medical Affairs, the finding that increasing vulnerability carries a substantially higher CVD risk (HR 2.14) than persistently elevated vulnerability highlights a critical window for earlier antihypertensive intervention when vulnerability is transitioning, presenting an opportunity to position early and sustained BP control as part of holistic cardiovascular risk reduction. Collaboration with KOLs working on multidimensional and digital risk assessment platforms could position the company's therapeutic portfolio at the forefront of personalized cardiovascular prevention, informing real-world evidence strategies aligned with value-based healthcare frameworks.

Smartphone addiction and metabolic health risk in pediatrics.

Revue : European Journal of Pediatrics | Tier : Tier 3 | IF estime : ~4.5

Date de publication : 2026-10-07

Resultats principaux : This systematic review synthesized evidence from 14 original studies published between 2019 and 2026 on the association between problematic smartphone use in children and adolescents aged 5-18 years and metabolic health outcomes, searching PubMed, Scopus, Web of Science, Google Scholar, and IEEE Xplore for peer-reviewed studies meeting predefined eligibility criteria. Prevalence of problematic smartphone use across included studies ranged from approximately 22% to 53%, varying substantially by measurement instrument, cutoff score, and cultural context, with the COVID-19 pandemic associated with a marked temporary increase in both screen time and addiction prevalence. Problematic use was consistently associated with obesity, hypertension, unfavorable dietary patterns, sleep disruption, and reduced physical activity across multiple studies; evidence for a direct association with insulin resistance was more limited and inconsistent. Proposed mechanistic pathways linking smartphone addiction to hypertension and metabolic risk include displacement of physical activity, altered dietary behavior, circadian disruption through melatonin suppression, and dysregulation of reward and stress-response circuitry. There is also a suggested bidirectional relationship with psychiatric comorbidities such as anxiety, depression, and ADHD, which are themselves associated with hypertensive risk in adolescents. The predominance of cross-sectional study designs across the evidence base is the major limitation, preventing causal inference; methodological heterogeneity in diagnostic instruments further limits comparability across studies.

Implications Medical Affairs : This review establishes problematic smartphone use as an emerging and modifiable lifestyle risk factor for hypertension in the pediatric population, with significant implications for primary prevention strategies that may need to be incorporated into updated pediatric hypertension guidelines and cardiovascular risk prevention programs. From a Medical Affairs perspective, this evidence supports development of non-pharmacological intervention programs targeting digital health behaviors as adjuncts to lifestyle modification messaging in adolescent populations, complementing antihypertensive therapy positioning in younger demographics. Engagement with KOLs in pediatric cardiology, public health, and adolescent medicine on prospective trials assessing smartphone restriction and digital wellness interventions as adjuncts to hypertension prevention programs could generate novel data supporting holistic BP control in youth.

Predictive Model for Complications in Total Hip Arthroplasty: Development of a Clinical Tool.

Revue : Revista Brasileira de Ortopedia | Tier : NA | IF estime : NA

Date de publication : 2026-10-06

Resultats principaux : This prospective cohort study enrolled 122 patients (mean age 56 years, 59.8% male) undergoing primary elective total hip arthroplasty (THA) at a public referral hospital in Brazil between November 2021 and April 2022, with the aim of developing a validated predictive model for 30-day postoperative complications. The most prevalent comorbidities in this surgical population were hypertension (61%), diabetes (23%), and dyslipidemia (15%), reflecting a high cardiometabolic burden typical of middle-aged patients requiring orthopedic surgery in Brazil. The 30-day complication rate was 13.1%, with surgical wound infection and anemia as the most frequent adverse events; logistic regression with C-statistic (AUC) analysis was used to build the predictive model incorporated into an online clinical tool. Significant independent risk factors for complications included dyslipidemia (OR 4.70; p=0.010), sedentary lifestyle (OR 4.71; p=0.026), high ASA classification (OR 3.67; p=0.035), and functional capacity below 4 metabolic equivalents of task (METs) (OR 4.08; p=0.040). Notably, hypertension itself was not independently predictive of 30-day complications in this cohort, possibly reflecting adequate perioperative blood pressure management or insufficient statistical power given the small sample. Key limitations include the small single-center sample without a control group, short 30-day follow-up, and absence of external validation of the predictive tool.

Implications Medical Affairs : While primarily an orthopedic study, the 61% hypertension prevalence and the significant independent risk conferred by sedentary lifestyle reinforce the importance of comprehensive cardiovascular risk factor optimization — including hypertension control — prior to elective surgery, with cross-specialty implications for medical affairs engagement in perioperative medicine. From a Medical Affairs perspective, this reinforces the value of long-term antihypertensive and lifestyle intervention programs not only for traditional cardiovascular endpoint reduction but also for improving perioperative outcomes across multiple surgical specialties, broadening the narrative around cardiovascular prevention. Engagement with KOLs in preoperative cardiovascular risk optimization and functional capacity assessment could expand the positioning of antihypertensive therapies and lifestyle interventions beyond cardiological endpoints into surgical risk reduction contexts.

[Percutaneous coronary intervention in Burkina Faso: About the first cases performed in Ouagadougou].

Revue : Annales de Cardiologie et d'Angeiologie | Tier : NA | IF estime : NA

Date de publication : 2026-10-07

Resultats principaux : This descriptive cross-sectional study with prospective follow-up described the first 63 patients to undergo percutaneous coronary intervention (PCI) at Tengandogo University Hospital in Ouagadougou, Burkina Faso, between March 2022 and August 2024, representing the first reported cases of this procedure in the country. The cohort had a mean age of 58.86 +/- 10.59 years with a marked male predominance (83%), and the leading cardiovascular risk factors were hypertension (92.1%), age-correlated sex risk (79.4%), and sedentary lifestyle (55.6%), indicating an extremely high hypertension burden in this population. ST-segment elevation myocardial infarction was the primary indication for PCI in 55.6% of cases, and lesions were multivessel in 66.7% of patients, reflecting advanced coronary artery disease. Stenting was performed in 93.7% of patients with a technical success rate of 93.7%; however, four in-hospital deaths occurred, including one intraoperative death from critical left main coronary artery ostial stenosis. Limitations include the small single-center sample size, retrospective data collection for early cases, the referral-center nature of the institution limiting generalizability, and a lack of longer-term follow-up data to assess post-discharge outcomes and secondary prevention effectiveness.

Implications Medical Affairs : This study documents the extraordinarily high hypertension burden (92.1%) in the first PCI-treated cardiac patients in Burkina Faso, underscoring the critical public health need for hypertension detection, treatment, and control programs in West Africa as the region develops invasive cardiac capabilities that are resource-intensive and potentially avoidable through prevention. From a Medical Affairs standpoint, this report identifies a significant unmet need in sub-Saharan Africa where antihypertensive therapy could prevent progression from hypertension to acute coronary syndromes requiring high-resource interventional procedures, representing an opportunity for strategic public health partnerships, health economics studies, and market access initiatives. The data on hypertension prevalence in this emerging cardiac population in West Africa could be leveraged in KOL educational initiatives on cardiovascular risk factor management and secondary prevention in low-to-middle-income countries, with long-term strategic implications for expanding access to antihypertensive medicines in the region.
