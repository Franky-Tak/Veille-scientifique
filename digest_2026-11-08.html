Prevalence and One-Year Incidence of Patient-Healthcare Professional Discussions About Sexual Health in Hypertensive Adults in France: A Cohort Study.

Revue : Journal of clinical hypertension (Greenwich, Conn.) | Tier : Tier 3 | IF estime : ~4

Date de publication : 2026-11

Resultats principaux : This prospective cohort study enrolled 676 hypertensive adults in France over a one-year follow-up period to quantify the prevalence and incidence of discussions about sexual health between patients and healthcare professionals. Almost half of participants (44.2%) reported having never discussed sexual health prior to study inclusion, revealing a substantial communication gap in routine clinical care. The one-year incidence of sexual health discussions was estimated at 12.8% (95% CI: 8.7-17.0%), indicating that such conversations remain infrequent even during active cardiovascular follow-up. Critically, approximately 23.3% (95% CI: 18.1-28.4%) of participants expressed a wish to discuss sexual health at least once during the follow-up period, yet only 9.1% (95% CI: 1.97-16.2%) of those who expressed this desire ultimately had a discussion, highlighting a significant unmet communication need. Missing data were handled using multiple imputation and attrition bias was corrected via inverse probability weighting, strengthening analytical robustness; however, the single-country design limits generalizability beyond the French healthcare context.

Implications Medical Affairs : These findings expose a critical gap in holistic management of hypertensive patients, where sexual health—a recognized consequence of both hypertension and antihypertensive medications—remains systematically underaddressed in clinical encounters, with direct relevance to treatment adherence and quality of life. From a Medical Affairs perspective, this data supports development of KOL-driven advocacy for systematic integration of sexual health screening into hypertension management guidelines, particularly advantageous for products with favorable sexual function profiles as a differentiating feature. Medical Affairs teams should incorporate sexual health quality-of-life endpoints into future clinical trial designs to build differentiated evidence and equip MSLs with data to address this unmet need in HCP conversations.

Blood Pressure Variability in Neurodegenerative Disease: Evidence From a Large-Scale Remote Patient Monitoring Program.

Revue : Journal of clinical hypertension (Greenwich, Conn.) | Tier : Tier 3 | IF estime : ~4

Date de publication : 2026-11

Resultats principaux : This retrospective analysis used de-identified home blood pressure data from a remote patient monitoring platform to compare day-to-day systolic blood pressure variability between 49 patients with neurodegenerative disease (Alzheimer's disease, Parkinson's disease, or dementia) and 147 propensity-matched controls over a monitoring stretch of at least eight weeks with readings at least three days per week. The neurodegenerative group showed significantly higher average real variability (ARV) in systolic BP (median 12.92 [IQR 10.74-15.35] vs. 11.19 [IQR 9.46-12.99] mmHg; p=0.003) and diastolic BP ARV (7.72 [6.34-8.87] vs. 6.76 [5.67-7.75] mmHg; p=0.002) compared to matched controls. Secondary metrics including standard deviation and variability independent of the mean were concordant across all comparisons (all p ≤ 0.001), confirming the robustness of the BPV elevation finding. Mean blood pressure, monitoring duration, measurement frequency, and reading timing were comparable between groups, isolating neurodegenerative disease as the differentiating factor beyond mean BP levels. Key limitations include the retrospective design, the modest sample size for the neurodegenerative subgroup, and the absence of differentiation between specific neurodegenerative subtypes, which may mask differential effects of Alzheimer's versus Parkinson's pathophysiology on autonomic function.

Implications Medical Affairs : The demonstration of elevated blood pressure variability in neurodegenerative patients despite similar mean BP identifies an important cardiovascular risk dimension in a rapidly growing and often undertreated patient population that is frequently excluded from hypertension clinical trials. From a Medical Affairs perspective, this supports advocating for BPV as a key monitoring and outcome metric in antihypertensive programs targeting elderly patients with cognitive comorbidities, and may justify positioning antihypertensives with demonstrated BPV-reducing properties as preferred options in this population. Medical Affairs teams should explore opportunities to collaborate with neurology and geriatrics KOLs to develop evidence-based treatment algorithms that incorporate BPV monitoring for hypertensive patients with neurodegenerative disease, potentially initiating investigator-sponsored studies in this underserved segment.

Continuous Associations and Predictive Performance of Electrocardiographic Voltage- and Product-Based Indices for Incident Hypertension: A Prospective Cohort Study.

Revue : Journal of clinical hypertension (Greenwich, Conn.) | Tier : Tier 3 | IF estime : ~4

Date de publication : 2026-11

Resultats principaux : This prospective cohort study enrolled 2,860 adults with baseline BP < 130/80 mmHg, no antihypertensive therapy, and no prior cardiovascular disease from a Japanese cohort, examining whether ECG indices—Sokolow-Lyon voltage (SLV), Cornell voltage (CV), and Cornell product (CP)—independently predict incident hypertension over a mean follow-up of 6.2 ± 3.0 years. During follow-up, 943 participants (33.0%) developed hypertension, defined as BP ≥ 140/90 mmHg or initiation of antihypertensive medication, providing adequate statistical power for multivariable analyses. Each 1-SD increase in sex-specific standardized ECG indices was independently associated with elevated hypertension risk: adjusted hazard ratios were 1.30 (95% CI: 1.22-1.38) for SLV, 1.15 (95% CI: 1.08-1.22) for CV, and 1.15 (95% CI: 1.08-1.22) for CP, all statistically significant after multivariable adjustment. Adding ECG indices to conventional risk models improved 5-year discrimination (AUC increased from 0.774 to 0.787 with SLV addition; p<0.001) and reclassification metrics, demonstrating incremental predictive value beyond established risk factors. Limitations include the restriction to a single Japanese ethnic cohort, limiting generalizability to other populations, and the binary BP threshold-based hypertension definition, which may not capture continuous risk relationships.

Implications Medical Affairs : The finding that subclinical ECG changes precede clinical hypertension across the full range of ECG values—not just at traditional LVH thresholds—positions ECG-based risk stratification as a potential early screening tool that could expand the addressable population for preventive antihypertensive treatment. From a Medical Affairs perspective, this evidence supports engagement with cardiology and preventive medicine KOLs to develop consensus positions integrating ECG-based risk indices into hypertension prevention guidelines, potentially identifying a new high-risk pre-hypertensive segment amenable to earlier pharmaceutical intervention. Medical Affairs teams should consider supporting international validation studies of ECG predictive indices in ethnically diverse populations to build the evidence base needed for guideline adoption and commercial positioning of preventive treatment strategies.

Functional capacity and cardiovascular risk burden: a cross-sectional analysis of the six-minute walk test in an at-risk population from 47 primary health centers in Makassar City, Indonesia.

Revue : BMC primary care | Tier : NA | IF estime : NA

Date de publication : 2026-08-10

Resultats principaux : This cross-sectional study enrolled 679 participants with one or more cardiovascular risk factors across 47 primary health centers in Makassar, Indonesia, assessing functional capacity using the six-minute walk test (6MWT) and estimated maximal oxygen consumption (VOmax) in a primary care setting from June to December 2021. The study population was predominantly female (80.1%) with a mean age of 52.3 ± 10.2 years; hypertension, obesity, and smoking were the most prevalent risk factors, and over half (58.6%) presented with four to six concurrent risk factors. The average 6MWT distance was 379.0 ± 97.5 meters, below established healthy reference ranges, and mean VOmax was 15.6 ± 5.3 ml/kg/min, indicating broadly poor functional capacity in the majority of participants. Traditional cardiovascular risk factors including sex, age, hypertension, diabetes, dyslipidemia, and physical inactivity had a statistically significant impact on 6MWT distance and VOmax (p<0.05), with those carrying more than 6 risk factors showing significantly lower VOmax (p=0.008). Key limitations include the cross-sectional design preventing causal inference, the high female predominance limiting sex-balanced generalizability, and restriction to a single Indonesian city which may not reflect LMIC cardiovascular risk profiles nationally or globally.

Implications Medical Affairs : This study provides important real-world primary care data from Indonesia demonstrating how cumulative cardiovascular risk factor burden—including hypertension—significantly impairs functional capacity in a large LMIC population, reinforcing the case for comprehensive multifactorial risk reduction strategies. From a Medical Affairs perspective, this data supports engagement with global health KOLs and health system stakeholders in LMIC settings to position integrated cardiovascular risk management programs that include antihypertensive treatment as central to improving functional outcomes, not just reducing BP. Medical Affairs teams should consider supporting implementation research in LMIC primary care settings to generate real-world evidence linking antihypertensive treatment adherence to functional capacity gains as part of broader global health equity initiatives.

Home Blood Pressure-Guided Lifestyle Intervention for Elevated Blood Pressure Detected at Workplace Health Checkups: A Randomized Pilot Trial.

Revue : Journal of clinical hypertension (Greenwich, Conn.) | Tier : Tier 3 | IF estime : ~4

Date de publication : 2026-11

Resultats principaux : This randomized pilot trial enrolled 58 municipal employees in Japan with elevated BP detected at workplace health checkups, randomizing them to home BP monitoring plus physician-led lifestyle counseling (n=31) or physician-led lifestyle counseling alone without home BP feedback (n=27), with both groups receiving two scheduled counseling sessions. The primary outcome—change in systolic BP at the subsequent annual health checkup—showed significantly greater reductions in the intervention group compared to controls (between-group difference: -9.9 mmHg; 95% CI: -17.0 to -2.8; p=0.007). After excluding three participants who initiated antihypertensive medication during follow-up, the systolic BP reduction difference remained statistically significant (-7.7 mmHg; 95% CI: -14.6 to -0.8; p=0.029), confirming that the intervention effect was not driven solely by pharmacological initiation. No significant differences in adverse events or secondary outcomes were reported, and adherence to home BP monitoring was feasible within the occupational health context. Major limitations include the small sample size with limited statistical power for subgroup analyses, the single-center occupational setting, and the pilot design which precludes definitive efficacy conclusions or generalization beyond Japanese municipal workplace populations.

Implications Medical Affairs : This trial demonstrates that home BP monitoring adds clinically meaningful value—approximately 10 mmHg additional systolic BP reduction—to lifestyle counseling in workplace health programs, supporting the integration of digital health monitoring tools into occupational health management of elevated BP prior to pharmacological intervention. From a Medical Affairs perspective, the results define an important pre-pharmacological intervention window in the occupational setting, relevant for positioning antihypertensive products as the natural evidence-based escalation when home BP-guided lifestyle modifications are insufficient to achieve targets. Medical Affairs teams should engage with occupational medicine KOLs and workplace health program decision-makers to position home BP monitoring guidelines as a cornerstone of hypertension management pathways, while identifying employees reaching BP targets despite lifestyle intervention as a commercially relevant segment for treatment initiation discussions.

WHO HEARTS programs in high-income countries: Implementation, outcomes, and implications for global hypertension control.

Revue : American journal of preventive cardiology | Tier : NA | IF estime : NA

Date de publication : 2026-03-25

Resultats principaux : This structured program report synthesized available evidence on WHO HEARTS technical package implementation and outcomes in high-income countries (HICs), drawing on national registry data, PAHO/WHO program reports, and pooled epidemiological surveys to address the paradox of persistently low hypertension control rates in advanced healthcare systems. The WHO HEARTS package comprises six core components: healthy lifestyle counseling, evidence-based treatment protocols, access to essential medicines and technology, risk-based cardiovascular disease management, team-based care, and monitoring systems; this framework was originally developed for LMICs but is shown to be feasible in HICs. Early HIC adopters of HEARTS demonstrated substantial improvements in hypertension control rates, suggesting the framework's direct transferability to high-resource settings and its potential to reverse stagnating control trends. Key implementation barriers in HICs were identified including therapeutic inertia, physician resistance to standardized protocols, fragmented electronic health record systems, and persistent socioeconomic inequities, while HICs possess distinct advantages such as advanced monitoring infrastructure, trained multidisciplinary teams, and reliable medication supply chains. Limitations include the narrative synthesis design, potential heterogeneity in hypertension control outcome definitions across included programs, limited controlled comparison data, and possible publication bias favoring programs with positive outcomes.

Implications Medical Affairs : This synthesis reinforces that structured, protocol-driven hypertension management frameworks can overcome systemic barriers even in well-resourced settings, creating Medical Affairs opportunities to position evidence-based pharmacological protocols as essential components of HEARTS implementation alongside behavioral interventions. From a Medical Affairs perspective, this data supports strategic engagement with national health ministries, primary care networks, and cardiovascular disease prevention organizations to advocate for HEARTS adoption with the company's antihypertensive portfolio positioned within recommended treatment algorithms. Medical Affairs teams should work with health system KOLs to design real-world evidence studies demonstrating how specific pharmaceutical products contribute to achieving HEARTS-defined hypertension control targets, enabling both guideline positioning and formulary access in HEARTS-adopting health systems.

Evaluation of a postpartum cardiovascular risk clinic after hypertensive disorder of pregnancy: First-year experience.

Revue : Obstetric medicine | Tier : NA | IF estime : NA

Date de publication : 2026-11-05

Resultats principaux : This descriptive single-center prospective cohort study at the Centre Hospitalier Universitaire de Sherbrooke evaluated the first-year experience of a dedicated postpartum cardiovascular risk clinic (CSGPP) for women with hypertensive disorders of pregnancy (HDP), enrolling 65 participants from 104 referrals (80.8% attendance at first visit), with 47 completing the 12-month follow-up. The clinic's primary functions were cardiovascular risk assessment, structured lifestyle counseling, and communication with primary care providers for women with elevated future CVD risk following HDP, using a multidisciplinary approach. Belief in the health benefits of lifestyle changes increased significantly from baseline to 12 months postpartum (p=0.007), suggesting meaningful engagement with preventive health concepts during the program. Patient satisfaction with the clinic was high among completers, and overall adherence (80.8% attending at least one visit) was notably strong compared to typical postpartum care programs. Limitations include the single-center design, small sample size, absence of a control comparison group, the descriptive study design without hard cardiovascular endpoints, and 12-month follow-up which is insufficient to assess long-term cardiovascular event prevention.

Implications Medical Affairs : Hypertensive disorders of pregnancy represent an established cardiovascular risk multiplier, and this study validates the feasibility and acceptability of structured postpartum cardiovascular follow-up programs as a critical entry point for secondary prevention in young women—an area of growing strategic importance for cardiometabolic portfolios. From a Medical Affairs perspective, this data provides clinical justification for engaging obstetric and cardiology KOLs to develop evidence-based multidisciplinary pathways for postpartum cardiovascular risk management, potentially informing new indications or label extensions for established antihypertensives in post-HDP populations. Medical Affairs teams should monitor the emergence of post-HDP prevention guidelines as a growth area for early preventive treatment investment and support long-term observational studies tracking hard cardiovascular endpoints in this young, high-risk female population.

Myeloid deficiency of the PRC2 complex protein JARID2 promotes foam cell formation through elevated plasma lipids without altering atherosclerosis progression.

Revue : Atherosclerosis | Tier : NA | IF estime : NA

Date de publication : 2026-10-30

Resultats principaux : This preclinical murine study generated myeloid-specific Jarid2-deficient mice (Jarid2-KO) and transplanted Jarid2-deficient or control bone marrow into Ldlr-/- recipient mice placed on a high-cholesterol diet for 9 weeks to investigate the role of JARID2—a PRC2 epigenetic co-factor—in macrophage-driven atherosclerosis. Myeloid Jarid2 deficiency modestly increased plasma cholesterol and triglyceride levels and significantly enhanced foam cell formation, two established pro-atherogenic processes driven by macrophage lipid uptake. Despite the elevated plasma lipid levels and increased foam cell formation, atherosclerotic lesion size and composition remained unchanged in Jarid2-deficient versus control mice, indicating a dissociation between foam cell formation and overall plaque burden in this model. Single-cell RNA sequencing of aortic arch plaques revealed complex, context-dependent effects of Jarid2 deficiency, with both pro- and anti-inflammatory gene expression changes across multiple myeloid subsets, suggesting opposing regulatory mechanisms. Limitations include the short 9-week dietary intervention period that may be insufficient to reveal longer-term atherosclerotic differences, the bone marrow transplant model introducing experimental artifacts, and the exclusive use of male mice which may not reflect sex-specific differences in macrophage epigenetic regulation.

Implications Medical Affairs : While primarily focused on atherosclerosis biology, these findings advance understanding of epigenetic regulation of macrophage function in cardiovascular disease, with potential relevance to residual inflammatory risk that persists beyond blood pressure and lipid control in hypertensive patients. From a Medical Affairs perspective, the JARID2/PRC2 pathway represents an emerging epigenetic target in cardiometabolic research that Medical Affairs teams should monitor for future therapeutic development potential, particularly as residual cardiovascular risk reduction beyond conventional targets gains guideline recognition. Medical Affairs teams tracking frontier cardiovascular science should consider supporting investigator-initiated research examining epigenetic modulation of macrophage function in hypertensive cardiometabolic disease models as part of a forward-looking scientific platform strategy.

Association of Diet Quality and Physical Activity through Young Adulthood With Mid-Life Cardiovascular Health Trajectories: The Coronary Artery Risk Development in Young Adults (CARDIA) Study.

Revue : medRxiv : the preprint server for health sciences | Tier : NA | IF estime : NA

Date de publication : 2026-10-22

Resultats principaux : This longitudinal analysis examined 2,806 biracial participants from the CARDIA cohort, applying group-based dual-trajectory modeling to identify latent classes of joint dietary quality (DASH score) and physical activity patterns from ages 25-45, and relating these to midlife cardiovascular health (CVH) trajectories at ages 45-60 using Life's Essential 8 physiologic component scores. Four joint diet/PA behavioral groups were identified: low DASH/very low PA (10.9%), low DASH/moderate-declining PA (25.6%), moderate DASH/high-declining PA (41.5%), and high DASH/high-declining PA (22.0%), demonstrating substantial heterogeneity in sustained lifestyle adherence patterns through young adulthood. Compared with the low DASH/very low PA reference group, participants in the high DASH/high-declining PA group had 21.7-fold higher odds of high versus low midlife CVH (including BP, glucose, lipids, weight, and non-smoking components), substantially exceeding the individual effects of diet quality alone (7.2-fold) or PA alone (10.3-fold). The synergistic joint effect was statistically significant and persisted after adjustment for baseline LE8 score, age, sex, race, and education, indicating a robust independent association between sustained simultaneous lifestyle adherence and favorable midlife cardiovascular trajectories. As a preprint not yet peer-reviewed, findings should be interpreted with caution; additional limitations include reliance on self-reported dietary and PA measures subject to recall bias, and the observational design which cannot establish causality between lifestyle patterns and CVH outcomes.

Implications Medical Affairs : This landmark CARDIA analysis provides compelling longitudinal evidence that synergistic adherence to both dietary and PA guidelines during young adulthood produces multiplicative—not merely additive—benefits on midlife cardiovascular health including hypertension control, reinforcing the centrality of comprehensive lifestyle intervention strategies as the foundation for prevention. From a Medical Affairs perspective, this data supports engagement with preventive cardiology and public health KOLs to position pharmaceutical treatment as a necessary complement to—rather than replacement for—comprehensive lifestyle programs, particularly for patients who have failed to achieve adequate behavioral adherence over time. Medical Affairs teams should monitor this preprint for peer-reviewed publication and consider supporting real-world evidence studies examining how antihypertensive therapies interact synergistically with lifestyle adherence patterns to optimize cardiovascular health trajectories across the life course.
