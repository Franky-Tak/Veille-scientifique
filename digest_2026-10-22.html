Theme 1 : Hypertension Epidemiology (1 article)

How relational continuity shapes recommended chronic disease management: analysis of population-based administrative health data in British Columbia.

Revue : BMC Primary Care | Tier : NA | IF estime : NA

Date de publication : 2026-07-24

Resultats principaux : This retrospective, population-based cohort study enrolled residents of British Columbia, Canada with diabetes and/or hypertension between April 1, 2020 and March 31, 2023, using linked administrative health data from over three years of follow-up. The primary endpoint was a composite outcome of recommended chronic disease management (monitoring and prescribing) comparing patients with complete versus low relational continuity with their primary care provider. Unadjusted comparisons showed slightly higher recommended management rates with complete relational continuity for hypertension (28.4% vs. 26.8%) and diabetes (20.4% vs. 19.2%), but after full adjustment for patient characteristics there was a null overall association (adjusted risk ratio [aRR]: 1.00, 95% CI 0.99-1.01). Significant inverse associations emerged in subgroup analyses among patients with serious mental illness and high primary care use (12+ visits and SMI: aRR 0.64, 95% CI 0.52-0.80) and those with high comorbidity burden (Charlson score >1: aRR 0.91, 95% CI 0.86-0.96), suggesting that more complex patients benefit less from visit-based relational continuity. A key limitation is the reliance on administrative data, which cannot capture quality of care delivered within visits or distinguish intentional complexity-driven care from simple gaps in guideline-recommended management processes.

Implications Medical Affairs : These findings challenge the prevailing policy emphasis on relational continuity as a driver of guideline-recommended chronic disease management for hypertension, suggesting that sustained physician-patient relationships alone do not translate into improved pharmacological adherence or monitoring outcomes at a population level. For Medical Affairs teams, this evidence underscores the importance of positioning antihypertensive therapies within comprehensive care models that emphasize informational and management continuity, particularly for complex patients such as those with serious mental illness or high comorbidity. From a KOL strategy perspective, these data provide an opening for scientific exchange with primary care researchers and health system policymakers on optimizing integrated care pathways that combine pharmacological treatment support with broader coordination tools to improve hypertension outcomes in high-complexity populations.

Theme 6 : Clinical Trials and Guidelines (1 article)

Clinical similarity, economic divergence: cost-effectiveness and population health implications of dietary, pharmacological, and exercise interventions in older adults with type 2 diabetes: a secondary analysis of a randomized controlled trial.

Revue : BMC Geriatrics | Tier : NA | IF estime : NA

Date de publication : 2026-07-25

Resultats principaux : This secondary analysis of the CEV-65 randomized controlled trial enrolled 100 older adults (aged ≥65 years) with type 2 diabetes, comparing a Mediterranean diet intervention (VegMedD), pharmacological treatment with empagliflozin (an SGLT2 inhibitor with established cardiovascular benefits), and circuit resistance training exercise over a 10-week follow-up period. All three interventions produced comparable improvements in glycemic control (HbA1c) with no statistically significant between-group differences, and frailty improvements were modest across all arms. The pharmacological arm achieved the highest composite responder rate (improvement in both HbA1c and frailty), while non-pharmacological interventions incurred substantially lower costs. In cost-utility analysis using quality-adjusted life years (QALYs) derived from SF-12 data via validated mapping, both dietary and exercise interventions were economically dominant over empagliflozin, with the Mediterranean diet showing the highest probability of cost-effectiveness at conventional willingness-to-pay thresholds; however, scenario analyses confirmed that these conclusions were sensitive to pharmacological drug pricing assumptions. The critical limitation is the short 10-week horizon, which cannot capture the well-established long-term cardiovascular, renal, and heart failure benefits of SGLT2 inhibitors that are particularly relevant to hypertensive patients with T2DM.

Implications Medical Affairs : These findings highlight the risk of short-term pharmacoeconomic analyses undervaluing pharmacological agents with established long-term cardioprotective benefits, particularly SGLT2 inhibitors whose advantages in cardiovascular and renal outcomes—highly relevant to hypertensive patients with T2DM—cannot be captured within a 10-week trial window. For Medical Affairs positioning, this study reinforces the strategic importance of communicating the full long-term value proposition of antihypertensive and cardiometabolic agents through robust health economic models that incorporate real-world cardiovascular outcome data and longer time horizons. From a KOL engagement perspective, this evidence base supports collaborative scientific exchange with geriatricians and health economists around integrated pharmacoeconomic frameworks for older patients with comorbid hypertension and diabetes, where the compound cardiovascular risk reduction offered by combination pharmacological strategies represents a key differentiator from lifestyle-only approaches.

Theme 7 : Environment and Risk Factors (1 article)

Food insecurity in the development of metabolic diseases.

Revue : Nature Reviews Endocrinology | Tier : NA | IF estime : NA

Date de publication : 2026-10-20

Resultats principaux : This comprehensive narrative review evaluates the current evidence on the relationship between food insecurity and cardiometabolic diseases, covering obesity, metabolic syndrome, hyperlipidemia, hypertension, cardiovascular disease, and type 2 diabetes mellitus across the life course. The body of evidence is predominantly derived from cross-sectional studies in adults, which consistently demonstrate associations between food insecurity and cardiometabolic risk, with obesity associations particularly pronounced among women. Three mechanistic pathways are identified: food insecurity as a psycho-emotional stressor contributing to anxiety and depressive symptoms; poor dietary quality increasing metabolic syndrome and hypertension risk through visceral adiposity, insulin resistance, systemic inflammation, and an unhealthy gut microbiome; and healthcare behavioral trade-offs such as prioritizing food purchasing over medications, directly impacting treatment adherence and disease prevention. The review evaluates potential mitigating interventions including community-based healthy lifestyle programs, early-life nutrition strategies, and Food as Medicine programs, though it notes that large-scale rigorous evidence for these interventions remains limited. The primary limitation is the predominantly cross-sectional design of the underlying studies, which precludes definitive causal inference, and the insufficient number of intervention trials specifically targeting hypertension outcomes within food-insecure populations.

Implications Medical Affairs : The demonstrated association between food insecurity and hypertension as a social determinant of cardiovascular health represents a critical context for Medical Affairs teams when constructing disease burden narratives and real-world evidence strategies for antihypertensive portfolios, particularly in markets with high socioeconomic disparity. For product positioning, this review strengthens the scientific rationale for patient support programs that address medication adherence barriers in low-income or food-insecure populations, where the documented trade-off between purchasing food and medications directly undermines antihypertensive treatment effectiveness. From a KOL strategy perspective, engaging with researchers in social epidemiology, preventive medicine, and Food as Medicine initiatives around hypertension prevention in vulnerable populations represents a high-impact scientific exchange opportunity fully aligned with current health equity priorities and population health mandates of payers and health systems.
