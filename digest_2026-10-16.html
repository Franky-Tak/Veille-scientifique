Ambulatory blood pressure characteristics and left ventricular hypertrophy in untreated children with primary hypertension.

Revue : European Journal of Pediatrics | Tier : NA | IF estime : NA

Date de publication : 2026-10-15

Resultats principaux : This retrospective study enrolled 306 untreated children and adolescents aged 9 to 17 years with newly diagnosed primary hypertension at Beijing Children's Hospital, evaluating clinical and ambulatory blood pressure monitoring (ABPM)-derived factors associated with left ventricular hypertrophy (LVH), defined as left ventricular mass index (LVMI) greater than or equal to 45 g/m^2.16, using propensity score-based overlap weighting to balance demographic and obesity-related characteristics. The primary endpoint was LVH status, with weighted logistic regression applied to identify independent predictors among clinical, laboratory, and ABPM-derived variables. LVH was present in 89 of 306 patients (29.1%), with grade 2 office hypertension emerging as the strongest associated factor (OR 4.22, 95% CI 2.29-7.77; p less than 0.001). Nighttime mean diastolic blood pressure showed an initial association with LVH (OR 1.54 per 10-mmHg increase, 95% CI 1.02-2.31) that was attenuated after adjustment for hypertension grade, whereas a systolic non-dipper pattern remained independently associated with LVH after full adjustment (OR 2.83, 95% CI 1.18-6.80; p=0.020). Key limitations include the single-center retrospective design, absence of longitudinal follow-up data, and reliance on exploratory associations for metabolic and inflammatory markers without pre-specified hypotheses.

Implications Medical Affairs : These findings reinforce the clinical importance of comprehensive cardiac assessment at diagnosis of pediatric hypertension and may support guideline updates recommending routine ABPM alongside echocardiographic screening in newly diagnosed hypertensive children to detect at-risk patients before overt LVH develops. The independent association of systolic non-dipping with LVH beyond office blood pressure severity strengthens the rationale for antihypertensive agents with demonstrated efficacy in normalizing nocturnal blood pressure profiles, a differentiating attribute for product positioning in the pediatric hypertension segment. Medical Affairs teams should engage pediatric cardiologists and pediatric nephrologists as KOLs to disseminate these findings and support investigator-initiated research on whether early pharmacological normalization of non-dipping patterns reduces LVH burden in untreated pediatric hypertensives.

Risk factors associated with hypertension in patients attending a pediatric sleep clinic: a prospective cohort study.

Revue : European Journal of Pediatrics | Tier : NA | IF estime : NA

Date de publication : 2026-10-14

Resultats principaux : This prospective single-center cohort study enrolled 68 pediatric patients aged 5 to 18 years referred to the sleep clinic at KK Women's and Children's Hospital in Singapore, with 24-hour ABPM performed for all participants to detect hypertension, including masked hypertension (MH), classified per the 2022 AHA ABPM update for children. The primary outcome was hypertension defined as ambulatory hypertension or MH, assessed in the 44 patients with complete ABPM data after 24 (35%) were excluded due to non-attendance or insufficient readings. Hypertension was diagnosed in 18 of 44 patients (41%), comprising 10 (23%) with ambulatory hypertension and 8 (18%) with MH, with prevalence increasing across OSA severity from mild (30%) to moderate (40%) to severe (50%) and reaching 60% in those with primary snoring. Multivariate analysis identified increasing age (RR 1.27, 95% CI 1.07-1.52; p=0.008) and primary snoring (RR 3.52, 95% CI 1.38-8.97; p=0.004) as independent risk factors for hypertension. Key limitations include the small sample with only 44 completing ABPM, a high dropout rate of approximately 35%, single-center design, and cross-sectional ABPM data precluding temporal causality assessment.

Implications Medical Affairs : The high prevalence of masked hypertension (18%) in this pediatric sleep clinic cohort highlights a critical diagnostic gap, with direct implications for guideline updates recommending routine ABPM over office blood pressure measurement in children with any degree of sleep-disordered breathing including primary snoring. The paradoxical finding that hypertension prevalence was highest in primary snoring may reflect under-recognition of cardiovascular risk in this subgroup, potentially widening the addressable population for antihypertensive screening and intervention beyond currently recognized high-risk tiers. Medical Affairs should leverage these findings with KOLs in pediatric sleep medicine, nephrology, and pulmonology to advocate for universal ABPM protocols in sleep clinics, and to build the evidentiary base for antihypertensive therapy indications in pediatric sleep-disordered breathing patients.

Differences by gender in metabolic dysfunction in patients with type 2 diabetes in first-level care.

Revue : Gaceta Medica de Mexico | Tier : NA | IF estime : NA

Date de publication : 2026

Resultats principaux : This analytical cross-sectional study included 5,154 adult patients with type 2 diabetes (T2DM) treated at first-level medical care units in Mexico, comparing metabolic dysfunction profiles between men and women using Student's t-test, Pearson chi-square, and Mann-Whitney U tests; metabolic dysfunction was evaluated against metabolic syndrome criteria. The primary analysis compared the prevalence of obesity, dyslipidemia, hypertension, and microalbuminuria across sexes, with no adjustment for confounders reported. Women exhibited significantly higher BMI (29.91 vs 28.74 kg/m2; p less than 0.001), obesity prevalence (44.2% vs 38.7%), hypercholesterolemia (94.0% vs 90.5%), and hypoalphalipoproteinemia (75.6% vs 24.4%), while men had significantly higher prevalence of hypertension (70.3% vs 66.7%; p less than 0.001) and microalbuminuria (36.4% vs 18.8%; p less than 0.001). No subgroup analyses by diabetes duration, treatment regimen, or comorbidities were reported, limiting mechanistic interpretation of the gender differences observed. Key limitations include the cross-sectional design precluding causal inference, restriction to first-level care which may not represent specialist or tertiary populations, and absence of antihypertensive treatment data that could confound blood pressure outcomes.

Implications Medical Affairs : The significantly higher prevalence of hypertension and microalbuminuria in men with T2DM at first-level care (70.3% and 36.4% respectively) has direct implications for sex-specific cardiovascular risk stratification guidelines and supports positioning antihypertensive agents with dual cardiorenal protection as the preferred first-line option for male T2DM patients in primary care. This large real-world dataset from a Latin American primary care setting demonstrates the substantial unmet medical need for integrated cardiometabolic treatment approaches and provides evidentiary support for broadening antihypertensive prescribing to encompass the diabetic hypertension phenotype prevalent in this population. Medical Affairs teams should engage primary care physicians and endocrinologists in Latin American markets to translate these findings into gender-stratified treatment algorithms, potentially sponsoring educational initiatives on cardiorenal risk in male T2DM patients to influence prescribing behavior at first-level care.

Diet Quality and Physical Activity at 3-4 Years Following a Hypertensive Pregnancy: A Blood Pressure Postpartum Cross-Sectional Sub-Study.

Revue : Journal of Human Nutrition and Dietetics | Tier : NA | IF estime : NA

Date de publication : 2026-10

Resultats principaux : This cross-sectional sub-study was embedded within the three-armed multicenter BPp randomized controlled trial in Australia and enrolled 180 women evaluated 3 to 4 years after hypertensive disorders of pregnancy (HDP), assessing diet quality via the Australian Recommended Food Score (ARFS) derived from the Australian Eating Survey and physical activity via the International Physical Activity Questionnaire (IPAQ). Primary endpoints were diet quality and moderate-to-vigorous physical activity (MVPA) and their linear associations with cardiovascular risk markers including blood pressure, BMI, and waist circumference, compared across BPp intervention strategies (three arms), HDP subtypes, and BMI categories. At 3 to 4 years post-HDP, 61% of participants had suboptimal diet quality (ARFS less than 38 of maximum 73; mean ARFS 33.8 plus or minus 10.5), while 87% met minimum physical activity recommendations with a median MVPA of 515 (IQR 690) minutes per week. No significant differences were observed across the three BPp postpartum intervention arms, HDP subtypes (chronic hypertension, gestational hypertension, preeclampsia, superimposed preeclampsia), or BMI categories, and neither ARFS nor MVPA was significantly associated with blood pressure, BMI, or waist circumference in adjusted linear regression models. Key limitations include the cross-sectional sub-study design with potential survivor bias, reliance on self-reported dietary and activity measures prone to recall bias, the 3-4 year post-HDP follow-up which may dilute acute postpartum intervention effects, and modest power with 180 participants for detecting lifestyle-cardiometabolic associations.

Implications Medical Affairs : The persistence of suboptimal dietary quality in 61% of women 3 to 4 years after HDP despite prior structured lifestyle interventions identifies a clinically significant treatment gap in long-term postpartum cardiovascular risk management, providing a compelling rationale for pharmacological adjuncts to lifestyle modification to sustain risk reduction in this high-risk population. These findings from a multicenter RCT sub-study support Medical Affairs engagement with obstetric medicine physicians and cardiologists to advocate for the integration of antihypertensive therapy into formal postpartum care pathways for HDP survivors, given the established trajectory from HDP to chronic hypertension and cardiovascular disease. A strategic KOL engagement program targeting maternal cardiology conferences should leverage this evidence to promote the need for pharmacological intervention trials within postpartum care frameworks, defining the optimal treatment window and antihypertensive agent selection for women with prior HDP.

Sociodemographic, behavioral, and social support determinants of dyslipidemia, hyperglycemia, and hypertension in a Spanish working population.

Revue : Nutricion Hospitalaria | Tier : NA | IF estime : NA

Date de publication : 2026-10-01

Resultats principaux : This large cross-sectional study included 117,298 Spanish workers and examined associations between sociodemographic variables (age, sex, social class), lifestyle habits (smoking, Mediterranean diet adherence, physical activity), and social support with the prevalence of dyslipidemia, hyperglycemia, and hypertension, using logistic regression models adjusted for all covariates. The primary analysis evaluated multivariable determinants of the three cardiovascular risk conditions simultaneously in a working-age general population. The prevalence of all three cardiovascular risk conditions was significantly higher in men, older individuals, and those of lower social class; smoking, low Mediterranean diet adherence, and physical inactivity were each independently and significantly associated with higher risk for all three conditions. Critically, low social support remained independently associated with higher prevalence of hypertension, dyslipidemia, and hyperglycemia after full adjustment for all sociodemographic and lifestyle variables, identifying psychosocial isolation as a distinct cardiovascular risk dimension beyond traditional modifiable behaviors. Key limitations include the cross-sectional design precluding causal inference, restriction to active employed adults which excludes the most severely ill and underrepresents the retired population, and potential residual confounding from unmeasured psychological and occupational stress variables.

Implications Medical Affairs : The independent association between low social support and hypertension prevalence in this exceptionally large occupational cohort of over 117,000 individuals provides high-powered evidence for incorporating psychosocial determinants into cardiovascular risk stratification frameworks and supports the case for screening hypertension in socially isolated working-age adults as a distinct target population for pharmacological intervention. From a Medical Affairs perspective, these findings from one of the largest real-world occupational health datasets on cardiovascular risk factors create an opportunity to position antihypertensive therapies within integrated workplace health programs addressing both lifestyle and psychosocial risk dimensions, differentiating evidence-based cardiovascular risk profiles for at-risk employee subgroups. Engagement with occupational medicine KOLs, public health stakeholders, and corporate health program managers would allow Medical Affairs to translate these epidemiological findings into actionable prescribing and screening recommendations for the hypertensive working-age population.

4HER-Embedding a Post-Pregnancy Cardiometabolic Health Clinic Into Routine Care.

Revue : The Australian & New Zealand Journal of Obstetrics & Gynaecology | Tier : NA | IF estime : NA

Date de publication : 2026-10

Resultats principaux : This Hybrid Type 2 implementation study evaluated the feasibility and initial effectiveness of the 4HER clinic, a dedicated post-pregnancy cardiometabolic risk reduction program modelled on the Canadian MotHERS program for women with gestational diabetes mellitus (GDM) or hypertensive disorders of pregnancy (HDP), implemented at Mater Mothers' Hospitals in Brisbane, Australia, with post-pregnancy follow-up visits at 3 and 9 months focusing on risk screening, stratification, and connection to primary care. The primary outcomes were clinic attendance and baseline characterization of the cardiometabolic risk profile of the enrolled cohort; 178 eligible women were invited, of whom 126 (70.8%) attended. The attending cohort demonstrated a high-risk cardiometabolic profile with mean BMI of 29.4 kg/m2, mean waist circumference of 99.7 cm, and over 50% classified with a high lifetime cardiovascular risk score; lifestyle and health screening data additionally revealed significant modifiable risk factors in the majority of participants. No significant differences in clinical outcomes were reported at this early implementation phase, and no pharmacological treatment data or blood pressure change metrics were presented. Key limitations include the single-center Hybrid Type 2 design without a control arm, focus on implementation feasibility metrics rather than clinical cardiovascular outcomes, absence of blood pressure and cardiometabolic endpoint data, and the preliminary nature of early-phase implementation results.

Implications Medical Affairs : The high initial engagement rate of 70.8% in this post-pregnancy cardiometabolic clinic demonstrates that structured postpartum cardiovascular risk programs are both feasible and acceptable to high-risk women, providing implementable evidence for health systems seeking to establish formal HDP survivor follow-up pathways that could include pharmacological cardiovascular risk reduction. Medical Affairs teams can leverage these implementation findings to position antihypertensive and cardiometabolic therapies within postpartum care frameworks for women with HDP history, a population with substantially elevated lifetime CVD risk, by partnering with obstetric medicine and maternal health KOLs to develop treatment pathway recommendations within such clinics. Investment in investigator-initiated outcome studies embedded within similar clinic models would generate the long-term cardiometabolic and blood pressure endpoint data needed to establish evidence-based pharmacological treatment guidelines for the postpartum HDP population and strengthen product positioning in this underserved segment.
