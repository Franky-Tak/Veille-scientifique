Identification of novel loci regulating circulating melatonin and its causal relationship with hypertension.

Revue : Human Genetics | Tier : NA | IF estime : NA

Date de publication : 2026-08-07

Resultats principaux : This genome-wide association study employed low-coverage whole-genome sequencing (lcWGS) in 3,605 Han Chinese adults to characterize the genetic architecture of circulating melatonin and its causal relationship with hypertension. Four independent genome-wide significant loci (P<1x10-7) were identified near LINC01807, PTPRD, EDIL3/NBPF22P, and LMO1/STK33, with tissue-specific expression confirmed in neural, vascular, and adrenal tissues via GTEx. Heritability of serum melatonin was estimated at 21.72% (SE=11.2%, P=0.029), with the four loci collectively explaining 3.40% of melatonin variance. One-sample Mendelian randomization demonstrated a significant protective effect of higher serum melatonin on hypertension risk (OR=0.45, P=0.015), systolic blood pressure (beta=-4.14, P=0.014), and diastolic blood pressure (beta=-3.10, P=0.007). Limitations include restriction to a single Han Chinese cohort, modest heritability explained by identified loci, and the exploratory nature of noncoding aggregation signals in the ZSWIM9 promoter region that require further mechanistic validation.

Implications Medical Affairs : These findings establish a putative causal pathway from circulating melatonin to hypertension risk and blood pressure regulation, opening a biologically grounded scientific narrative around chronobiological interventions and melatonin receptor agonists as adjunctive antihypertensive strategies worthy of clinical investigation. From a Medical Affairs perspective, the identified PTPRD and LMO1 loci represent candidate biomarkers that could stratify hypertensive patients most likely to respond to circadian rhythm-targeting therapies, supporting a precision medicine positioning angle. Engagement with KOLs in genetic epidemiology and chronopharmacology is warranted to design biomarker-stratified trials and build an early scientific platform in this emerging area.

Association of non-conventional lipid-inflammatory parameters with incident hypertension risk: a prospective cohort study.

Revue : Journal of Global Health | Tier : NA | IF estime : NA

Date de publication : 2026-08-07

Resultats principaux : This prospective cohort study included 6,891 participants from the China Health and Retirement Longitudinal Study (CHARLS, 2011-2020) to examine non-conventional lipid indices and their inflammatory composites as predictors of incident hypertension using multivariable Cox proportional hazards regression. Over a median follow-up of 7.79 years, 2,532 incident hypertension cases were observed, providing substantial statistical power for exposure-response analyses at both baseline and cumulative time points. Higher tertiles of all non-conventional lipid indices were consistently and progressively associated with increased hypertension incidence across all primary models. The lipoprotein combine index-C-reactive protein composite demonstrated the largest effect size with a hazard ratio of 1.59 (95% CI: 1.44-1.75), while dose-response relationships were characterized using restricted cubic spline modelling. Composite lipid-inflammatory indices provided modest incremental discrimination over individual measures (AUC range 0.55-0.58), and limitations include the observational design, potential residual confounding, and the weak standalone discriminatory performance limiting immediate clinical utility.

Implications Medical Affairs : The identification of non-conventional lipid-inflammatory composites as independent predictors of hypertension incidence in a large representative Asian cohort strengthens the scientific rationale for integrated cardiometabolic monitoring strategies targeting both lipid and inflammatory pathways simultaneously. From a Medical Affairs perspective, these data support messaging around combination therapies addressing dual metabolic derangements in hypertension prevention and could differentiate products with anti-inflammatory or pleiotropic lipid-modulating properties. KOL engagement with preventive cardiology and lipidology specialists in Asian markets should be pursued to position these composite biomarker findings within the evolving hypertension risk stratification framework.

Association Between Sleep Duration and Hypertension Development in Women: A Meta-Analysis.

Revue : Journal of the College of Physicians and Surgeons Pakistan | Tier : NA | IF estime : NA

Date de publication : 2026-08

Resultats principaux : This meta-analysis systematically searched Embase, PubMed, Cochrane Library, Web of Science, CNKI, VIP, and Wanfang databases (up to March 2024), ultimately including 10 articles comprising 12 cohort studies to examine the sex-specific relationship between sleep duration and hypertension risk in women. Inadequate (short) sleep duration was significantly associated with hypertension development in women (RR: 1.10, 95% CI: 1.06-1.13, p<0.001), while long sleep duration showed no significant association (RR: 1.00, 95% CI: 0.96-1.05, p=0.958). Age-stratified subgroup analyses confirmed that short sleep elevated hypertension risk both in women under 50 years (RR=1.19, 95% CI: 1.11-1.28, p<0.001) and in women aged 50 and above (RR=1.06, 95% CI: 1.00-1.12, p=0.036). The dose-response relationship was L-shaped, with the lowest hypertension risk at approximately 7.5 hours per day, with meta-regression identifying age and variability in sleep duration definitions as principal sources of heterogeneity. Limitations include the relatively small number of included studies, heterogeneity in sleep measurement methodologies, and the restriction to female-specific cohorts which precludes sex-comparative conclusions.

Implications Medical Affairs : This meta-analysis reinforces short sleep duration as a significant, modifiable risk factor for hypertension development in women across all age groups, providing quantitative evidence to support sleep health as a formal component of hypertension prevention guidelines. For Medical Affairs, these data underpin patient education initiatives targeting female populations at risk, and support the scientific case for lifestyle-integrated hypertension management programs that include sleep hygiene counseling alongside pharmacotherapy. Engaging KOLs in women's cardiovascular health and sleep medicine would facilitate advocacy for sleep screening as a routine component of cardiovascular risk assessment in women.

Risk prediction for long-term cardiovascular events in patients with concurrent hypertension, HFpEF, and unstable angina: a multicenter machine learning-assisted cohort study.

Revue : Frontiers in Endocrinology | Tier : NA | IF estime : NA

Date de publication : 2026-07-23

Resultats principaux : This multicenter retrospective cohort study enrolled 1,669 patients with concurrent hypertension, heart failure with preserved ejection fraction (HFpEF), and unstable angina pectoris (UAP) across six Chinese medical centers, with patients allocated to development, validation, and independent test cohorts to build and validate a machine learning-assisted prognostic nomogram. During a median follow-up of 48 months, 254 patients (15.2%) experienced major adverse cardiovascular events (MACEs), defined as cardiac death, unplanned coronary revascularization, or rehospitalization for acute heart failure. Five predictors were selected using LASSO regression, the Boruta algorithm, and random forest analysis: diabetes mellitus, previous myocardial infarction, systemic immune-inflammation index, triglyceride, and NT-proBNP. The nomogram achieved AUC values of 0.785, 0.787, and 0.760 for 2-, 3-, and 4-year MACE prediction in the development cohort, with more modest but clinically useful performance in validation (0.674-0.697) and test cohorts (0.685-0.718). Limitations include the retrospective single-country design, moderate external validation performance, absence of imaging-based predictors, and the need for prospective validation before clinical implementation.

Implications Medical Affairs : This machine learning-assisted risk stratification tool addresses a significant unmet need in a clinically complex triple-comorbidity population, where individualized cardiovascular risk prediction can inform treatment intensification decisions and follow-up protocols. From a Medical Affairs perspective, the five identified predictors -- including NT-proBNP and systemic immune-inflammation index -- align with emerging biomarker platforms that could differentiate therapeutic positioning in the intersection of HFpEF and hypertension management. Collaboration with KOLs in heart failure and interventional cardiology to prospectively validate and extend this framework could support a scientific platform around early identification of hypertensive patients at highest risk for adverse cardiovascular outcomes.

In-depth analysis of retinal RNA transcriptome sequencing in a rat model of hypertensive retinopathy.

Revue : Journal of Central South University Medical Sciences | Tier : NA | IF estime : NA

Date de publication : 2026-05-28

Resultats principaux : This experimental study performed comprehensive RNA transcriptome profiling (mRNA, miRNA, lncRNA, circRNA) in retinal tissues from spontaneously hypertensive rats (SHR) as a model of hypertensive retinopathy, comparing control Wistar-Kyoto (WKY) rats, untreated SHRs, and SHRs treated with the calcium channel blocker lacidipine (0.5 mg/kg/day for 8 consecutive weeks). In untreated SHRs versus controls, 870 mRNAs were significantly downregulated and 1,018 upregulated; upregulated mRNAs were enriched in immune-inflammatory pathways including IL-17, TNF, oxidative stress, and lipid inflammatory mediators, while downregulated mRNAs mapped to PI3K-AKT signaling, vascular smooth muscle contraction, and ECM-receptor interaction. Lacidipine treatment reduced blood pressure and alleviated histopathological retinal changes (edema, vascular dilation, increased thickness), reversing 67 upregulated and 54 downregulated mRNAs; differential miRNA, lncRNA, and circRNA analysis identified non-coding RNA regulatory networks implicating retinal rod differentiation, ECM remodeling, the Notch pathway, fatty acid synthesis, and VEGF signaling. Multiple lncRNA/circRNA-miRNA-mRNA competing endogenous RNA (ceRNA) networks were constructed, with key regulatory axes involving rno_circ_Rims2_10, rno-miR-1-3p, and related mRNAs implicated in retinal stress response, inflammatory regulation, and neurovascular remodeling. Limitations include the use of an animal model that may not fully recapitulate human hypertensive retinopathy, the exploratory nature of ceRNA network predictions requiring validation, and restriction to male SHR which limits sex-generalizability of findings.

Implications Medical Affairs : This first integrated multi-modal retinal transcriptome study in the SHR model reveals novel molecular mechanisms through which lacidipine mediates protection against hypertensive retinopathy, providing a mechanistic basis for CCB end-organ protection beyond systemic blood pressure reduction. From a Medical Affairs perspective, these data strengthen the scientific platform for calcium channel blockers in the context of microvascular complication prevention in hypertension, and the identified non-coding RNA targets represent candidates for future biomarker-driven studies linking CCB use to retinal outcomes. Engaging ophthalmology and hypertension KOLs to develop collaborative research programs examining retinal imaging endpoints as surrogates for end-organ protection in CCB clinical trials could generate differentiating data for this drug class.

Care Navigation, Self-Measured Blood Pressure, and Health Coaching for Postpartum Care: A Randomized Clinical Trial.

Revue : JAMA Network Open | Tier : NA | IF estime : NA

Date de publication : 2026-08-03

Resultats principaux : This randomized clinical trial (NCT05685251) enrolled 140 postpartum women with hypertensive disorders of pregnancy (HDP) at a Midwestern US community hospital between February 2023 and April 2025, randomizing them 1:1 to a health coaching intervention (SHARP-HEARTPost) with self-measured blood pressure monitoring versus standard of care. The primary outcome -- attendance at a primary care visit by 12 months postpartum -- was significantly higher in the intervention group (71.0% vs. 39.1%; RR 2.00, 95% CI 1.34-2.97, P<0.001), demonstrating a doubling of healthcare engagement. The intervention yielded significantly greater reductions in systolic BP (mean difference -5.6 mmHg, 95% CI -9.4 to -1.7, P=0.005) and diastolic BP (-4.5 mmHg, 95% CI -7.7 to -1.3, P=0.006) from baseline to 12 months postpartum. BP control to target (<130/80 mmHg) was achieved in 55.3% of intervention participants versus 31.1% of controls (RR 1.73, 95% CI 1.13-2.66, P=0.02), though no statistically significant differences in health behavior change were observed between groups. Limitations include the single-site design, small sample size of 140 participants, lack of blinding inherent to behavioral interventions, and the need for larger multicenter trials to confirm these preliminary findings.

Implications Medical Affairs : This trial provides compelling preliminary evidence that structured health coaching with remote BP monitoring significantly improves both healthcare engagement and blood pressure control among women with hypertensive pregnancy disorders, a population at substantially elevated long-term cardiovascular risk, supporting incorporation of these strategies into postpartum care guidelines. From a Medical Affairs perspective, these results establish a scientific rationale for value-added care navigation services alongside antihypertensive pharmacotherapy in postpartum hypertension management, representing a differentiating patient support framework for products targeting this indication. Prioritizing KOL engagement with obstetrician-gynecologists, maternal-fetal medicine specialists, and cardiologists focused on pregnancy-related cardiovascular risk is recommended to position this evidence within the current guideline update cycle for postpartum hypertension management.

Revue : CNS Neuroscience and Therapeutics | Tier : NA | IF estime : NA

Date de publication : 2026-08

Resultats principaux : This prospective pragmatic multicenter study enrolled 398 stroke survivors aged 35-90 years from five Hong Kong community centers to evaluate individualized Chinese medicine rehabilitation (CMR) comprising acupuncture and Chinese herbal medicine granules administered thrice weekly for 24 weeks (72 sessions total), with hypertension as a key matched covariate in the nested case-control substudy. Of 398 participants, 344 (86.6%) completed at least 75% of sessions, with significant within-group improvements in activities of daily living (ADL; mean change 5.99, 95% CI 4.50-7.48) and mini-mental state examination (MMSE; 1.21, 95% CI 0.87-1.55). In a propensity score-matched nested case-control analysis matching on age, sex, stroke type, baseline ADL, hypertension, diabetes, and dyslipidemia, early CMR initiation (within 6 months post-stroke) yielded superior ADL outcomes at week 24 (mean difference 3.89, 95% CI 1.30-6.48, P=0.003) and improved TCMSSS neurological syndrome scores (-0.86, 95% CI -1.69 to -0.04, P=0.041) versus delayed initiation. Multivariate logistic regression identified baseline ADL dependence (OR=13.45, 95% CI 7.01-25.81), early CMR initiation (OR=2.03, 95% CI 1.23-3.35), and lower baseline neurological syndrome severity as independent predictors of treatment response, with no treatment-related adverse events observed. Limitations include the non-randomized pragmatic design susceptible to selection bias, restriction to Hong Kong community centers limiting generalizability, and inability to separate the contributions of acupuncture and herbal medicine components.

Implications Medical Affairs : While primarily addressing stroke rehabilitation, this pragmatic trial demonstrates that early integration of complementary medicine approaches meaningfully improves functional recovery in stroke patients with hypertension as a major comorbidity, highlighting the importance of holistic post-stroke care strategies that address the full cardiometabolic risk profile. From a Medical Affairs standpoint, the absence of adverse events and the significant functional improvements support patient-centered care positioning that acknowledges the value of rehabilitation alongside pharmacological hypertension management in post-stroke populations, particularly relevant for Southeast Asian and East Asian markets. Engaging rehabilitation medicine specialists and stroke neurologists as KOLs in markets where traditional medicine integration is established could inform real-world evidence programs and patient support services aligned with hypertension management in cerebrovascular disease.

Resultats principaux : This prospective analysis of the CARDIA (Coronary Artery Risk Development in Young Adults) cohort examined DASH diet score trajectories from early to middle adulthood in 2,238 Black and White adults (baseline age 18-30 years) and their association with coronary artery calcium (CAC) progression between study years 20 and 25. Latent class analysis identified two distinct dietary trajectories: a low-increasing and a high-decreasing DASH score pattern across the observation period. For Medical Affairs, these data provide longitudinal evidence supporting lifestyle modification programs as scientifically validated complements to pharmacotherapy in hypertension management, and underscore diastolic blood pressure as a mediating mechanism for dietary benefit that can be incorporated into patient education messaging. Revue : Journal of Global Health | Tier : NA | IF estime : NA

Resultats principaux : This retrospective time series study analyzed four years of clinical emergency visit records (January 2014 to April 2017) from a primary hospital in the Klang Valley, Malaysia, alongside environmental air quality and meteorological data from nearby monitoring stations, using generalized additive models and distributed lag nonlinear models to assess associations between particulate matter (PM10) and asthma emergency visits. Asthma exacerbations accounted for 2.3% of total emergency visits, and daily mean PM10 exceeding the Malaysian Ambient Air Quality Standard was associated with a 21.3% increase (95% CI: 2.2-44.0%) in asthma emergency visits at 200 microg/m3 and a 34.0% increase (95% CI: 4.7-71.7%) at 300 microg/m3, with dose-dependent temporal dynamics observed. Although primarily focused on asthma, the demonstrated air pollution-cardiorespiratory relationship is directly relevant to hypertension risk given established mechanistic links between PM10 exposure, endothelial dysfunction, and elevated systemic blood pressure. Limitations include the single-center retrospective design, four-year data window, and exploratory statistical associations requiring validation in multi-site longitudinal datasets.

Implications Medical Affairs : The dose-dependent and temporally characterized association between PM10 air pollution and acute cardiorespiratory emergency events in an urban Asian setting reinforces environmental particulate matter as a quantifiable trigger for cardiovascular and hypertensive crises, supporting air quality as a modifiable risk factor in hypertension prevention messaging. Factors influencing hemorrhagic transformation after mechanical thrombectomy in patients with acute ischemic stroke.

Revue : Journal of Central South University Medical Sciences | Tier : NA | IF estime : NA

Date de publication : 2026-05-28

Resultats principaux : This single-center prospective observational cohort study enrolled 268 consecutive patients with acute ischemic stroke (AIS) who underwent mechanical thrombectomy (MT) at Beijing Hospital between May 2020 and December 2024, examining clinical, laboratory, and procedural factors associated with hemorrhagic transformation (HT) post-procedure. HT occurred in 92 patients (34.3%), including 29 hemorrhagic infarction type 1, 24 type 2, 16 parenchymal hematoma type 1, and 23 type 2, with symptomatic intracranial hemorrhage in 15 patients (5.6%). Notably, history of hypertension showed no statistically significant difference between HT and non-HT groups (P>0.05), challenging the assumption that antecedent hypertension is a dominant predictor of post-thrombectomy bleeding. Multivariate logistic regression identified baseline NIHSS score greater than or equal to 12 (OR=2.271, 95% CI 1.266-4.076, P<0.01) and history of coronary heart disease (OR=2.144, 95% CI 1.141-4.026, P<0.05) as independent risk factors, while higher fibrinogen level was an independent protective factor (OR=0.651, 95% CI 0.467-0.909, P<0.05). The predictive model incorporating NIHSS score, coronary heart disease history, fibrinogen, and antiplatelet drug use achieved an AUC of 0.680, reflecting moderate discriminatory performance that warrants multicenter validation and augmentation with imaging variables. Limitations include the single-center design, relatively small sample size, absence of imaging-based radiological predictors in the model, and Chinese cohort-specific generalizability constraints.

Implications Medical Affairs : The finding that hypertension history is not an independent predictor of post-thrombectomy hemorrhagic transformation is clinically important, suggesting that acute stroke severity (NIHSS) and coronary comorbidity are stronger determinants of bleeding risk than antecedent blood pressure status, which should inform perioperative antihypertensive management protocols. For Medical Affairs, these results highlight the complexity of hypertension management decisions in the acute stroke setting and support educational programs targeting neurologists and neurocritical care specialists around individualized perioperative blood pressure targets that balance reperfusion benefit against hemorrhagic transformation risk. Engaging stroke neurology and neurocritical care KOLs to design prospective biomarker studies incorporating blood pressure trajectory data alongside coagulation profiles could refine perioperative antihypertensive management guidelines and support evidence-based product positioning in acute neurovascular care.
