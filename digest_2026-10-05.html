CARDIOMETABOLIC RISK PROFILES IN ADULTS WITH DIABETES: ASSOCIATIONS WITH GLYCEMIC CONTROL, DIABETES TYPE, AND DEMOGRAPHIC CHARACTERISTICS.

Revue : Georgian Medical News | Tier : NA | IF estime : NA

Date de publication : 2026

Resultats principaux : This retrospective cross-sectional study analyzed electronic medical records of diabetic patients at the Diabetes and Endocrine Center in Qassim, Saudi Arabia, from December 2024 to May 2025. The study found a high prevalence of obesity (56.6%), physical inactivity (76.3%), and low HDL cholesterol (50.6%) among diabetic patients. Poor glycemic control (HbA1c > 8%) was significantly associated with elevated LDL and total cholesterol levels, with 30.95% of the poor-control group displaying these abnormalities (p < 0.001). Type 2 diabetes mellitus was strongly associated with hypertension, obesity, physical inactivity, and low HDL compared to type 1 diabetes; male participants had higher rates of dyslipidemia and smoking while females showed higher rates of obesity and physical inactivity. The study's notable limitations include its retrospective cross-sectional design, which precludes causal inference, and a single-center sampling frame that may limit generalizability across broader Saudi and global populations.

Implications Medical Affairs : The high co-prevalence of hypertension and modifiable risk factors in diabetic patients from the Qassim region underscores the need for integrated cardio-metabolic treatment guidelines that address both glycemic and blood pressure targets simultaneously. From a Medical Affairs standpoint, this study supports positioning combination antihypertensive-metabolic agents in high-risk diabetic patient segments, particularly in the Middle East region where such data remain scarce. An actionable insight is engaging local key opinion leaders in Saudi Arabia to drive multidisciplinary management protocols that incorporate blood pressure control as a core component of comprehensive diabetes care in the region.

PRO-INFLAMMATORY INTERLEUKINS AND OXIDATIVE IMBALANCE IN L-NAME-INDUCED HYPERTENSION: COMPARATIVE EFFECTS OF BETA-BLOCKERS.

Revue : Georgian Medical News | Tier : NA | IF estime : NA

Date de publication : 2026

Resultats principaux : This experimental comparative study used 70 male Wistar rats randomized into seven groups with hypertension induced by chronic L-NAME administration (40 mg/kg/day intraperitoneally for 8 weeks) to evaluate inflammatory and oxidative mechanisms. Serum pro-inflammatory cytokines (IL-6, IL-17, TNF-alpha), nitric oxide metabolites, and total antioxidant activity (TAA) were measured alongside arterial pressure across four beta-blocker treatment groups: nebivolol (0.5 mg/kg/day), propranolol (0.1 mg/kg/day), metoprolol (3 mg/kg/day), and carvedilol (2 mg/kg/day), administered during weeks 5-8. L-NAME produced time-dependent increases in arterial pressure accompanied by reduced NO metabolites, reduced TAA, and elevated interleukin levels, confirming the inflammatory-oxidative hypertension model. Nebivolol and carvedilol demonstrated greater reductions in arterial pressure, greater restoration of NO metabolites and TAA, and greater attenuation of IL-6, IL-17, and TNF-alpha levels compared with metoprolol and propranolol. Key limitations include the preclinical animal model design that limits direct clinical translation, the relatively short treatment duration of 4 weeks for the intervention phase, and the absence of histopathological vascular endpoints.

Implications Medical Affairs : This preclinical study reinforces the mechanistic differentiation between third-generation beta-blockers (nebivolol, carvedilol) and conventional agents (metoprolol, propranolol) in terms of anti-inflammatory and antioxidant properties beyond blood pressure reduction alone. From a Medical Affairs perspective, these findings provide foundational science to support the positioning of nebivolol and carvedilol as mechanistically superior options in hypertensive patients with concurrent inflammatory burden or elevated oxidative stress markers. An actionable insight is to initiate investigator-sponsored research translating these inflammatory biomarker differentials into human clinical study designs, potentially engaging KOLs in translational cardiology to test nebivolol versus conventional beta-blockers in hypertensive patients with elevated CRP or IL-6 levels.

Effect of interventions for stress management on blood pressure changes in healthy adults: a systematic review and meta-analysis.

Revue : Journal of Human Hypertension | Tier : Tier 3 | IF estime : 3-4

Date de publication : 2026-10-03

Resultats principaux : This systematic review and meta-analysis searched PubMed, Web of Science, Scopus, and Cochrane Library through October 2025, including 22 randomized controlled trials with a maximum total sample of 3,089 healthy normotensive adults to evaluate non-pharmacological stress management interventions on blood pressure. The primary endpoints were between-group mean differences in systolic and diastolic blood pressure changes using a random-effects pooled model. Mind-body exercise significantly reduced systolic blood pressure by -2.17 mmHg [95% CI: -3.29, -1.05], meditation by -1.49 mmHg [95% CI: -2.67, -0.31], and relaxation techniques by -4.76 mmHg [95% CI: -8.48, -1.04] versus controls; corresponding diastolic reductions were -1.69, -1.00, and -1.71 mmHg respectively, with relaxation's diastolic effect not reaching statistical significance. Subgroup analysis across intervention types revealed that relaxation produced the largest systolic blood pressure reductions, though its confidence interval was wider, reflecting greater heterogeneity. Important limitations include heterogeneity across intervention types and populations, limited follow-up durations in many included trials, and the exclusive focus on normotensive adults limiting direct generalizability to overt hypertensive populations.

Implications Medical Affairs : This meta-analysis provides evidence-grade support for stress management as a primary prevention strategy for hypertension, aligning with international guidelines recommending lifestyle modification as first-line therapy, and may prompt updated guideline statements to more specifically endorse mind-body and relaxation interventions in pre-hypertensive populations. For Medical Affairs, this positions non-pharmacological approaches as relevant reference frameworks when communicating the continuum of hypertension risk management, and supports partnerships with professional societies to develop complementary lifestyle guidance alongside pharmacological therapy. An actionable insight is to leverage these data in KOL engagement strategies that frame antihypertensive medications as part of an integrated prevention portfolio, potentially differentiating company-supported patient support programs that incorporate structured stress management and mindfulness coaching.
