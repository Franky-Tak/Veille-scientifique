Early acute kidney injury after pulmonary endarterectomy: incidence, associated perioperative factors, and early outcome in a single-centre retrospective cohort.

Revue : BMC Cardiovascular Disorders | Tier : NA | IF estime : NA

Date de publication : 2026-08-18

Resultats principaux : This single-centre retrospective cohort study enrolled 106 consecutive adults with operable chronic thromboembolic pulmonary hypertension who underwent pulmonary endarterectomy (PEA) at a tertiary referral centre between January 2018 and December 2020. The primary endpoint was the occurrence of acute kidney injury (AKI) within 48 hours post-surgery, defined using 2012 KDIGO serum creatinine and urine-output criteria. AKI occurred in 42 of 106 patients (39.6%), with the majority classified as stage 1 (35 patients), four as stage 2, and three as stage 3. Among perioperative factors, extracorporeal circulation (ECC) duration was the only independently associated variable when analysed alongside deep hypothermic circulatory arrest and aortic cross-clamp times (adjusted OR 1.032 per minute, 95% CI 1.012-1.052; p=0.001), while older age was the sole independent preoperative predictor (adjusted OR 1.034 per year, 95% CI 1.004-1.065; p=0.027). An integrated predictive model combining age and ECC duration achieved an optimism-corrected AUC of 0.706, and 30-day mortality was significantly higher in patients who developed AKI compared to those who did not (14.3% vs. 0%; p=0.003), despite similar haemodynamic recovery in both groups. The study is limited by its retrospective design, single-centre setting, relatively small sample size, and the exploratory nature of the prediction model, which requires external validation.

Implications Medical Affairs : These findings underscore the need to integrate renal risk stratification into preoperative workup protocols for pulmonary hypertension patients scheduled for PEA, which could inform multidisciplinary team guidelines and strengthen the positioning of nephroprotective management strategies in this high-risk surgical population. From a Medical Affairs standpoint, the independent association of ECC duration with AKI provides an actionable lever for surgical teams and reinforces the clinical value of refining perfusion techniques, potentially opening avenues for collaboration with device manufacturers and intensivists on perioperative renal protection bundles. KOL engagement in the pulmonary hypertension and cardiac surgery space should prioritise building real-world evidence registries to validate this exploratory model and guide future interventional studies on renal outcomes in this niche but clinically significant population.

Effect of SEEK-guided versus conventional intubation on hemodynamic responses in elderly hypertensive patients: a randomized controlled trial.

Revue : BMC Anesthesiology | Tier : NA | IF estime : NA

Date de publication : 2026-08-13

Resultats principaux : This prospective randomised controlled trial conducted in China (August–November 2024) enrolled 80 elderly hypertensive patients (ASA grade II) scheduled for elective surgery requiring oral tracheal intubation, randomised equally to SEEK (Safe Easy Endotracheal Kit-flexible) versus conventional stylet-guided intubation. The primary outcomes were mean arterial pressure (MAP) and heart rate (HR) measured at six predefined time points from pre-induction to 3 minutes post-intubation. Following intubation, MAP and HR increased in both groups, but at 30 seconds (T3) and 1 minute (T4) post-intubation, the SEEK group demonstrated significantly lower MAP and HR compared to the conventional group (p<0.05), indicating a more attenuated haemodynamic stress response. The first-attempt intubation success rate was significantly higher in the SEEK group (p<0.05), though intubation time was longer (33.80 ± 3.32 s vs. 23.69 ± 4.70 s, p<0.05). No significant differences were observed in SpO2 or rates of post-intubation respiratory complications between the two groups, and the study is limited by a small sample, single-centre design, short follow-up, and restriction to ASA II patients, limiting generalisability to higher-risk hypertensive populations.

Implications Medical Affairs : The demonstration that a novel intubation device can attenuate the haemodynamic stress response in elderly hypertensive patients is clinically meaningful, as peri-intubation haemodynamic spikes remain a leading precipitant of acute cardiovascular and cerebrovascular events in this population; this finding may support anaesthesiology guideline updates specifically addressing high-risk hypertensive patients. For Medical Affairs, this study identifies an emerging perioperative management niche where hypertension-related cardiovascular risk reduction extends into the intraoperative setting, which could reinforce positioning of antihypertensive products emphasising pre-operative blood pressure optimisation as part of a comprehensive surgical safety strategy. KOL engagement should focus on anaesthesiologists and geriatric medicine specialists to build a broader evidence base linking perioperative haemodynamic control to long-term cardiovascular outcomes in elderly hypertensive patients.

Incidence and predictors of hypertensive heart disease among hypertensive patients: A retrospective cohort study in South Gondar Zone Hospitals, Ethiopia.

Revue : Blood Pressure | Tier : 3 | IF estime : 3–5

Date de publication : 2026-11-16

Resultats principaux : This institution-based retrospective cohort study enrolled 531 hypertensive patients followed across selected hospitals in the South Gondar Zone of Ethiopia from September 2019 to June 2024, with the primary endpoint being the development of hypertensive heart disease (HHD), encompassing left ventricular hypertrophy and heart failure. The incidence rate of HHD was 14.93 per 1,000 person-months (95% CI: 12.94–17.23), with a median survival time to HHD development of only 18 months (IQR: 12–24 months), indicating a rapidly progressing complication course. Cox proportional hazard regression identified four independent predictors of early HHD development: older age (≥60 years, AHR=1.82; 95% CI: 1.12–2.97), obesity (AHR=1.90; 95% CI: 1.29–2.79), low HDL cholesterol (AHR=1.78; 95% CI: 1.21–2.64), and high non-HDL cholesterol (AHR=2.22; 95% CI: 1.51–3.25). No subgroup analyses were reported beyond the main predictors, and the study is limited by its retrospective design, reliance on routine clinical records, single-country context, and the inability to draw causal inferences. The finding that HHD developed in a median of 18 months highlights the compressed timeline of cardiac remodelling in undertreated or sub-optimally managed hypertensive patients in resource-limited settings.

Implications Medical Affairs : The high and rapid incidence of hypertensive heart disease in this sub-Saharan African cohort underscores the urgent unmet need for earlier, more aggressive antihypertensive management and lipid-lowering co-therapy in low-resource settings, which has direct implications for guideline advocacy and the positioning of combination cardiovascular therapies in emerging markets. The identification of modifiable metabolic risk factors — obesity, low HDL, and high non-HDL cholesterol — as independent drivers of HHD progression alongside hypertension provides a compelling scientific rationale for promoting holistic cardiometabolic risk management approaches that go beyond blood pressure control alone, strengthening the value proposition for products addressing lipid disorders in hypertensive patients. Medical Affairs teams should leverage this evidence for KOL engagement with African cardiologists and public health bodies to advocate for integrated hypertension-metabolic risk screening programmes and to design real-world evidence studies exploring the impact of earlier combination therapy on HHD incidence in similar populations.

Factors Contributing to Obesity Among Indigenous Peoples of Canada: A Narrative Review.

Revue : Canadian Journal of Kidney Health and Disease | Tier : NA | IF estime : NA

Date de publication : 2026-09-30

Resultats principaux : This narrative review synthesised evidence from 44 studies selected from 131 eligible articles to characterise the complex, interconnected determinants of obesity among Indigenous Peoples in Canada and their downstream implications for chronic kidney disease (CKD) risk, with hypertension cited as a key mediating pathway between obesity and kidney injury. The study population represents a marginalised group disproportionately burdened by obesity due to structural factors including dietary transition, food insecurity, socioeconomic inequity, reduced physical activity, environmental change, cultural disruption, historical trauma, and systemic racism, all of which compound biological and genetic predispositions. While quantitative pooling was not performed, the review qualitatively underscores that obesity in this population drives CKD risk both directly (obesity-related glomerulopathy) and indirectly through the mediation of diabetes and hypertension, two established drivers of renal decline. Barriers to obesity treatment — including limited bariatric surgery access, restricted pharmacotherapy availability, and culturally unsafe healthcare environments — may further entrench hypertension-mediated renal disparities. The review is limited by its reliance on cross-sectional and self-reported data, underrepresentation of urban Indigenous populations, absence of longitudinal evidence, and limited evaluation of culturally relevant health measures.

Implications Medical Affairs : The delineation of hypertension as a central mediating mechanism between obesity-related risk exposures and kidney disease in Indigenous Canadians highlights an underserved population where antihypertensive therapy is both clinically essential and systematically underutilised, presenting an opportunity for Medical Affairs to advocate for culturally appropriate cardiovascular prevention programmes. This review reinforces the growing body of evidence on social determinants of hypertension, which is increasingly shaping guideline development and health equity frameworks; companies with cardiovascular portfolios should ensure their KOL engagement and health economic messaging are aligned with this paradigm. Actionable insights for Medical Affairs include sponsoring or co-designing community-led hypertension screening and management initiatives for Indigenous populations, and working with health authorities to generate real-world data on treatment adherence and outcomes in this group, which could strengthen product dossiers for markets with significant Indigenous health equity mandates.

Association of the triglyceride-glucose index with multiple colorectal polyps as a precursor-lesion burden phenotype in adults undergoing diagnostic colonoscopy.

Revue : BMC Cancer | Tier : NA | IF estime : NA

Date de publication : 2026-08-20

Resultats principaux : This retrospective single-centre cross-sectional study enrolled 169 adults aged 18–59 years undergoing complete diagnostic colonoscopy with available fasting triglyceride and glucose measurements, categorised into no polyps (n=58), 1–2 polyps (n=60), or 3+ polyps (n=51), with hypertension included as a covariate in the adjusted models. The primary outcome was the presence of 3 or more colorectal polyps as a surrogate for precursor-lesion burden, and mean triglyceride-glucose (TyG) index increased significantly across polyp-burden groups (8.44 ± 0.52, 8.72 ± 0.51, 8.90 ± 0.49; p<0.001). Each 1-unit increase in TyG was associated with higher adjusted odds of multiple polyps (aOR 2.41; 95% CI: 1.54–3.77; p=0.002), and the highest TyG tertile versus lowest showed substantially elevated odds (aOR 4.78; 95% CI: 2.00–11.41); however, AUC discrimination was only fair (0.678). After adjustment for diet, physical activity, alcohol, family history, and colonoscopy-quality indicators, the association attenuated to non-significance (aOR 1.63; 95% CI: 0.97–2.73; p=0.064), indicating that much of the TyG-polyp association is shared with lifestyle confounders, including hypertension-associated metabolic risk factors. The study is limited by its retrospective cross-sectional design, single-centre setting, age restriction to 59 years, and the absence of histological polyp classification limiting the clinical applicability of findings.

Implications Medical Affairs : While this study is primarily focused on colorectal neoplasia rather than hypertension, the inclusion of hypertension as a covariate and the central role of insulin resistance — a core hypertension comorbidity — as the mechanistic driver of TyG-associated polyp burden reinforces the broader message that cardiometabolic risk clustering, anchored by hypertension, extends cancer risk beyond cardiovascular endpoints. From a Medical Affairs perspective, this provides additional scientific rationale for comprehensive cardiometabolic risk management programmes that position antihypertensive and metabolic therapies within a multi-organ risk reduction framework, which may be valuable in health economic arguments and cross-speciality KOL dialogue. The finding should be monitored for future prospective validation, as a confirmed link between insulin resistance biomarkers and colorectal cancer precursors in hypertensive patients could open dialogue with oncology and gastroenterology KOLs about shared metabolic pathophysiology and preventive strategies.

CD22 and CD79A expression level in bladder cancer patients using bioinformatics tools, RNA-Seq and qRT-PCR validation.

Revue : BMC Cancer | Tier : NA | IF estime : NA

Date de publication : 2026-08-17

Resultats principaux : This study combined bioinformatics analysis of RNA-Seq data from the GSE133624 dataset with qRT-PCR validation in peripheral blood from 27 bladder cancer patients and 25 healthy controls to characterise the expression of immune markers CD22 and CD79A, with hypertension included as a reported comorbidity in the clinical subgroup analyses. Bioinformatics revealed significant downregulation of CD22 (log2FoldChange: -3.30; adjusted p=2.49×10−) and CD79A (log2FoldChange: -2.54; adjusted p=1.13×10−) in tumour tissues, implicating these markers in immune signalling pathway disruption within the tumour microenvironment. However, blood-based qRT-PCR showed no significant expression differences between patients and controls overall (p=0.236 for CD22; p=0.531 for CD79A), and AUC values were poor (0.597 and 0.522 respectively), indicating limited diagnostic utility in blood. Notably, significant CD22 and CD79A expression alterations in blood were observed in subgroups defined by comorbidities including hypertension, diabetes, ischemic heart disease, and risk exposures including smoking and alcohol (p<0.05), suggesting that hypertension-associated biological alterations may modulate immune gene expression in cancer patients. The study is limited by its small sample, single-centre design, cross-sectional nature, reliance on peripheral blood rather than tumour tissue for validation, and the absence of longitudinal follow-up to link expression changes to outcomes.

Implications Medical Affairs : While primarily an oncology-focused molecular study, the documented interaction between hypertension as a comorbidity and immune biomarker expression in bladder cancer patients is an emerging signal that may be relevant to Medical Affairs teams tracking the intersection of cardiovascular comorbidity and oncological outcomes, particularly as immuno-oncology therapies become more prevalent in patients with cardiovascular risk profiles. This study adds to growing evidence that hypertension is not merely a passive comorbidity in cancer patients but may actively modulate the tumour immune microenvironment, an insight that could inform clinical risk stratification discussions with oncology KOLs and support the development of comorbidity-sensitive treatment protocols. Medical Affairs should monitor this area for confirmatory evidence, as a validated link between antihypertensive therapy and immune biomarker normalisation in cancer patients could open cross-disciplinary publication opportunities and strengthen the holistic disease management narrative for cardiovascular products used in oncology co-morbidity settings.
