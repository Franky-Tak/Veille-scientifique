Association between meteorological factors and blood pressure variability in northern Guizhou, China.

Revue : Frontiers in Public Health | Tier : NA | IF estime : NA

Date de publication : 2026-08-07

Resultats principaux : This hospital-based ecological study enrolled 4,355 hypertensive patients undergoing 24-hour ambulatory blood pressure monitoring (ABPM) in northern Guizhou, China, with primary endpoints being systolic and diastolic blood pressure variability (BPV) indices. Daily meteorological exposures including relative humidity, shortwave solar radiation, and ambient temperature were assessed over a maximum lag period of 14 days using distributed lag non-linear models (DLNMs). Results demonstrated that higher relative humidity and lower shortwave solar radiation were both significantly and positively associated with increased BPV across the full 14-day lag window, with effect estimates persisting after accounting for lagged relationships. Exploratory subgroup analyses yielded potentially larger effect estimates when data were stratified by age, sex, smoking status, and alcohol consumption, though these findings were derived from aggregated ecological data subject to multiple comparison limitations and cannot confirm individual susceptibility. The authors appropriately caution that these associations represent ecological-level observations rather than evidence of causal effects, and call for validation through multicenter prospective studies incorporating individual-level meteorological exposure assessment and larger, more diverse patient populations.

Implications Medical Affairs : This study provides evidence that environmental meteorological factors independently modulate blood pressure variability in hypertensive patients, reinforcing the clinical importance of 24-hour BP control across varying seasonal and environmental conditions and supporting the value proposition of antihypertensives with proven BPV reduction. From a Medical Affairs standpoint, these findings support the development of real-world evidence dossiers that contextualize antihypertensive efficacy across climatic environments, particularly for products with ambulatory BP monitoring data demonstrating superiority in BPV reduction. KOL engagement should prioritize environmental cardiologists and hypertension specialists in climatically variable regions, and future collaborative research should integrate individual-level meteorological data into multicenter ABPM-based hypertension trials to generate causal evidence.

Phenotype-Dependent Effect of Bedtime Antihypertensive Dosing on Nocturnal Blood Pressure: A Meta-Analysis Guided by Non-Dipper Prevalence.

Revue : Journal of Clinical Hypertension | Tier : Tier 3 | IF estime : 3-5

Date de publication : 2026-08

Resultats principaux : This meta-analysis of 66 randomized controlled trials encompassing 29,654 hypertensive participants compared bedtime versus morning antihypertensive administration, with nighttime ambulatory systolic BP (SBP) and diastolic BP (DBP) as primary outcomes, drawing on searches of PubMed, EMBASE, and the Cochrane Library. In the overall hypertensive population, bedtime dosing significantly reduced nighttime SBP (WMD: 4.87 mmHg; 95% CI: 3.75-6.00) and nighttime DBP (WMD: 2.80 mmHg; 95% CI: 2.23-3.37), with modest additional reductions in 24-hour SBP (WMD: 2.06 mmHg) and daytime SBP (WMD: 0.99 mmHg). In trials restricted exclusively to non-dipper phenotypes, nocturnal BP reductions were markedly more pronounced (SBP WMD: 7.57 mmHg; DBP WMD: 3.77 mmHg), and study populations with a higher baseline non-dipper prevalence of 60% or more experienced approximately two-fold greater nighttime SBP reductions compared to those with lower prevalence (WMD: ~9.7 vs. 4.4 mmHg; p<0.01). The consistent pattern across SBP and DBP subgroup analyses (DBP: 5.1 vs. 2.6 mmHg; between-group p<0.05) underscores that chronotherapy effects are heterogeneous across trial populations and phenotype-dependent rather than uniformly applicable to all hypertensive individuals. These findings establish that patient phenotyping via ABPM for non-dipper identification is critical for maximizing the nocturnal BP-lowering benefit of bedtime antihypertensive dosing strategies.

Implications Medical Affairs : This meta-analysis substantiates chronotherapy as a precision medicine strategy in hypertension management, particularly for non-dipper phenotypes, and supports positioning of bedtime antihypertensive dosing as a differentiating therapeutic approach with two-fold greater nocturnal efficacy in appropriately selected patients. From a Medical Affairs perspective, these findings provide compelling evidence to support label-driven educational initiatives around non-dipper phenotype identification through ABPM, potentially strengthening the value proposition of antihypertensive agents with flexible dosing schedules and demonstrated nocturnal efficacy profiles. KOL strategies should prioritize engagement with hypertension specialists and sleep medicine experts to co-develop ABPM-guided phenotyping algorithms and consensus statements on bedtime dosing protocols, including collaborative real-world evidence studies to confirm long-term cardiovascular outcome benefits in non-dipper populations.

Temporal Trends in Multimorbidity Patterns in Patients With Atrial Fibrillation — A Nationwide Cohort Study.

Revue : European Journal of Clinical Investigation | Tier : NA | IF estime : NA

Date de publication : 2026-08

Resultats principaux : The nationwide Finnish FinACAF cohort study analyzed 196,536 patients with incident atrial fibrillation (AF) between 2009 and 2018 using national healthcare, reimbursement, and prescription registers, applying a validated comorbidity categorization with a fixed 5-year look-back assessment period to characterize multimorbidity burden and its temporal evolution. The primary endpoint was the prevalence and temporal trend in multimorbidity as measured by distinct comorbidity group counts at incident AF diagnosis. Overall, 85% of patients had diagnoses from at least 2 comorbidity groups and 28% from 6 or more, with the mean number of distinct comorbidity groups increasing significantly from 3.9 in 2009 to 4.2 in 2018, remaining statistically significant after adjustment for age and sex, confirming an independent temporal trend beyond population aging. Hypertensive diseases were among the most prevalent comorbidities alongside obesity, other metabolic diseases, and diabetes, while ischemic heart disease and other heart and pulmonary circulatory diseases showed declining prevalence over the study period. Women demonstrated higher multimorbidity burden than men (mean 4.2 vs. 3.9), and multimorbidity increased progressively with age, highlighting the intersection of demographic aging, AF, and hypertension as a growing clinical challenge requiring holistic integrated management strategies.

Implications Medical Affairs : This landmark nationwide cohort study confirms that hypertension is a central and increasing driver of multimorbidity in AF patients, providing robust epidemiological justification for incorporating aggressive hypertension management as a core pillar of integrated AF care pathways and cardiovascular risk reduction strategies. From a Medical Affairs standpoint, these real-world population data support development of educational materials and disease-awareness campaigns targeting cardiologists on comprehensive hypertension-AF co-management frameworks, potentially positioning antihypertensive therapies within multimorbidity bundle treatment protocols that also address metabolic comorbidities. Future research priorities should focus on prospective interventional studies evaluating whether optimized hypertension control in high-risk AF-prone populations reduces multimorbidity burden and incident AF, generating KOL-actionable evidence to guide multidisciplinary treatment protocols in this complex patient population.

Ambient air pollution exposure and cognitive aging; mediators and possible pathways: a systematic review on the epidemiological studies.

Revue : Ageing Research Reviews | Tier : NA | IF estime : NA

Date de publication : 2026-08-22

Resultats principaux : This systematic review synthesized evidence from 16 epidemiological studies examining 72 unique exposure-mediator-outcome associations between ambient air pollution and cognitive aging, covering six pollutants (PM2.5, PM10, NO2, NOX, black carbon, and PM1 components), with PM2.5 representing 65% of all analyses. Cognitive outcomes ranged from memory performance and cognitive processing speed to clinically diagnosed conditions including dementia incidence, while potential mediators included mental health outcomes (depression, anxiety, stress), lung function indices (FEV1, FVC, PEF), vascular diseases (stroke and hypertension), inflammatory markers (CRP), metabolic factors (type 2 diabetes), sleep disruption, and neuroanatomical changes. Cardio- and cerebro-vascular diseases, prominently including hypertension, emerged as a key mechanistic mediator in the air pollution-to-cognitive-aging pathway, with mental health outcomes, sleep, and lung function also demonstrating mediating potential that requires further confirmatory research. Quality of mediation analysis reporting was generally adequate, but most studies had notable risk of bias particularly in outcome assessment and confounding adjustment, limiting the strength of causal inference. The review highlights the need for rigorous causal mediation analyses with standardized reporting frameworks to characterize the complex multi-pathway mechanisms linking ambient air pollution to cognitive aging and identify targeted preventive interventions.

Implications Medical Affairs : This systematic review positions hypertension as a critical pathophysiological intermediary between ambient air pollution and cognitive aging, providing mechanistic justification for aggressive hypertension control as a preventive strategy against air pollution-related cognitive decline and expanding the therapeutic indications rationale for antihypertensive agents. From a Medical Affairs standpoint, these findings support development of cross-disciplinary disease awareness initiatives linking cardiovascular risk factor management, particularly hypertension, to cognitive health preservation in high air pollution exposure populations, potentially positioning antihypertensive treatments within broader neuroprotective prevention strategies. KOL engagement should target environmental cardiologists, neurologists, and public health policymakers to build consensus on integrated cardiovascular-cognitive prevention protocols and to advocate for prospective intervention studies evaluating whether intensive BP control attenuates air pollution-associated cognitive decline.

Machine learning for precision obstetrics: A comprehensive review of risk prediction models for hypertensive disorders and pregnancy-related syndromes.

Revue : Pregnancy Hypertension | Tier : NA | IF estime : NA

Date de publication : 2026-08-22

Resultats principaux : This comprehensive review evaluated the transformative role of machine learning (ML) in predicting major obstetric complications including hypertensive disorders of pregnancy (HDP), gestational diabetes mellitus (GDM), preterm birth, and intrauterine growth restriction, which collectively constitute leading sources of global maternal and neonatal morbidity. Ensemble methods including Random Forest and XGBoost, alongside deep learning architectures, consistently outperformed traditional logistic regression models when integrating multimodal data sources comprising clinical records, biochemical markers, multi-omics profiles, medical imaging, and lifestyle factors, achieving area under the curve (AUC) values exceeding 0.90 in selected cohorts for HDP prediction. Critical methodological advances highlighted included preprocessing techniques for class imbalance correction (SMOTE), model interpretability via Explainable AI frameworks (SHAP), and privacy-preserving federated learning strategies that enable multi-institutional model training without data sharing. However, technical and ethical challenges including algorithmic bias, lack of external prospective validation, and data heterogeneity across multicenter populations remain significant unresolved barriers to routine clinical implementation. The review concludes that prospective, multicenter validation studies are an essential prerequisite before ML-based HDP risk stratification tools can be responsibly translated into clinical obstetric practice.

Implications Medical Affairs : The demonstrated capacity of ML models to achieve AUC greater than 0.90 for hypertensive disorders of pregnancy prediction signals an imminent paradigm shift toward precision obstetrics, with direct implications for earlier antihypertensive intervention strategies and prevention of preeclampsia and related maternal-fetal complications. From a Medical Affairs perspective, these findings support strategic investment in digital biomarker research programs and AI-powered risk stratification tool development that could identify high-risk pregnancies amenable to earlier pharmacological antihypertensive intervention, potentially expanding the therapeutic target population for existing antihypertensive agents. Organophosphate flame retardants and incident type 2 diabetes: Integrating population evidence, mechanistic insights, and effect modification by healthy lifestyle.

Revue : Journal of Hazardous Materials | Tier : NA | IF estime : NA

Date de publication : 2026-08-20

Resultats principaux : This nested case-control study within the Henan Rural Cohort assessed associations between urinary organophosphate flame retardant (OPFR) exposures and incident type 2 diabetes mellitus (T2DM) using multiple complementary analytical approaches including Cox proportional hazards models, Weighted Quantile Sum regression, Quantile-based g-Computation, Bayesian Kernel Machine Regression, and an adaptive elastic-net environmental risk score. Higher urinary OPFRs concentrations, particularly triphenyl phosphate (TPHP), were significantly associated with increased T2DM risk, with OPFR mixtures demonstrating a linear dose-response relationship with incident T2DM. Combined high OPFR exposure with unhealthy or deteriorating lifestyle trajectories conferred the greatest T2DM risk, with OPFR and lifestyle factors jointly explaining 55.36% of total model variance in the signed Wald decomposition analysis. In vitro mechanistic experiments in HepG2 cells demonstrated that TPHP impairs glucose consumption and downregulates EGFR expression, with EGFR overexpression reversing TPHP-induced abnormal glucose consumption; molecular docking confirmed potential TPHP-EGFR binding affinity of -8.5 kcal/mol and GEO re-analyses provided external supportive evidence that EGFR expression is higher in healthier lifestyle-associated conditions. While focused primarily on T2DM, the cardiometabolic risk framework examined is directly relevant to hypertension as a core metabolic syndrome component, and the lifestyle-OPFR interaction highlights prevention strategies applicable to hypertensive-diabetic comorbid populations.

Implications Medical Affairs : This study's identification of TPHP-EGFR-mediated cardiometabolic dysregulation expands the mechanistic understanding of environmental chemical contributions to the metabolic syndrome complex, within which hypertension is a central and closely interrelated component, reinforcing the scientific basis for comprehensive cardiometabolic risk factor management. From a Medical Affairs standpoint, these environmental exposure findings support development of population-level messaging that frames antihypertensive treatment within a broader cardiometabolic risk mitigation framework encompassing both lifestyle optimization and environmental chemical minimization, strengthening holistic cardiovascular prevention narratives. Collaborative engagement with environmental health researchers, toxicologists, and public health policymakers to develop integrated cardiometabolic prevention guidelines that account for both modifiable lifestyle and environmental OPFR exposures represents a high-impact KOL opportunity for positioning antihypertensive therapies in prevention-oriented market segments.

Early-pregnancy fasting blood glucose and subsequent GDM define four distinct risk groups for adverse pregnancy outcomes: a cohort study of 15,245 women.

Revue : Frontiers in Endocrinology | Tier : NA | IF estime : NA

Date de publication : 2026-08-07

Resultats principaux : This retrospective cohort study of 15,245 women with singleton pregnancies established a four-group classification based on early-pregnancy fasting blood glucose (FBG) levels (<5.1 vs. ≥5.1 mmol/L) and subsequent gestational diabetes mellitus (GDM) diagnosis: Normoglycemia (normal FBG without GDM), GDM-Regression (elevated FBG without subsequent GDM), Late-onset GDM (normal FBG with GDM), and GDM-Maintained (elevated FBG with GDM). The primary endpoint was hypertensive disorders of pregnancy (HDP) incidence, which increased progressively across groups: 4.6% (Normoglycemia), 6.9% (GDM-Regression), 8.1% (Late-onset GDM), and 11.0% (GDM-Maintained; p for trend <0.001). Compared with Normoglycemia, adjusted odds ratios for HDP were 1.32 (95% CI: 1.03-1.68) for GDM-Regression, 1.53 (95% CI: 1.28-1.83) for Late-onset GDM, and 1.65 (95% CI: 1.23-2.18) for GDM-Maintained after multivariable logistic regression adjustment. Restricted cubic spline analysis revealed a nonlinear dose-response relationship between FBG and HDP risk, with a statistical inflection point at 4.52 mmol/L, and stratified analyses confirmed consistent associations across age, BMI, ethnicity, and parity subgroups with no significant interaction effects. Sensitivity analyses using alternative FBG cutoffs of 5.3 and 5.6 mmol/L confirmed the robustness of these findings and the four-group classification framework for HDP risk stratification.

Implications Medical Affairs : This large cohort study establishes high-quality epidemiological evidence that early-pregnancy glycemic status is independently and dose-dependently associated with hypertensive disorders of pregnancy risk, identifying a novel FBG-based risk stratification framework that could enable earlier identification of women at elevated HDP risk and earlier antihypertensive intervention before clinical hypertension onset. From a Medical Affairs perspective, the identified FBG inflection point at 4.52 mmol/L represents a potential early biomarker for HDP risk that could be incorporated into obstetric risk-stratification tools and prophylactic antihypertensive protocols, supporting label expansion considerations for antihypertensive agents in obstetric prevention contexts. Strategic collaboration with maternal-fetal medicine KOLs and obstetric guidelines committees to develop prospective trials evaluating early glycemic-based antihypertensive intervention for HDP prevention, and to incorporate FBG-based HDP risk stratification into clinical guidelines, represents a high-priority Medical Affairs opportunity with direct therapeutic positioning implications.
