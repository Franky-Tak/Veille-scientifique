Association of postpartum glycemic control with serum MMP-9, ET-1, hypertension, and dyslipidemia in women with gestational diabetes mellitus.

Revue : Hypertension in Pregnancy | Tier : NA | IF estime : NA

Date de publication : 2026-Nov-20 (Electronic)

Resultats principaux : This prospective cross-sectional follow-up study enrolled 300 women with prior gestational diabetes mellitus (GDM) at 12-month postpartum, stratified into good glycemic control (HbA1c < 5.7%, n=141) and poor glycemic control (HbA1c ≥ 5.7%, n=159). The primary endpoint was the comparison of serum matrix metalloproteinase-9 (MMP-9) and endothelin-1 (ET-1) levels, blood pressure, and lipid profiles between groups at the follow-up visit. The poor glycemic control group had significantly higher levels of both MMP-9 and ET-1, with HbA1c positively correlated with these vascular biomarkers. The prevalence of hypertension in the poor control group was 28.9% versus a significantly lower rate in the good control group, while dyslipidemia was present in 37.1% of the poor control group. Exploratory regression-based indirect-effect analyses estimated that MMP-9 and ET-1 each mediated approximately 22-26% of the HbA1c-hypertension association; however, the authors note that contemporaneous cross-sectional design precludes inference of temporal or causal mediation, limiting conclusions about mechanism.

Implications Medical Affairs : These findings suggest that glycemic management after GDM may have direct implications for vascular health metrics, reinforcing the clinical rationale for extended postpartum metabolic follow-up — a potential area for guideline expansion. From a product positioning standpoint, compounds that jointly address hyperglycemia and vascular biomarkers (MMP-9, ET-1) in this high-risk postpartum population could differentiate in the growing GDM-to-hypertension prevention segment. Medical Affairs teams should consider supporting KOL engagement around postpartum cardiometabolic surveillance programs and biomarker-guided risk stratification research in this population.

High blood pressure among children and adolescents with type 1 diabetes mellitus in Uganda: A multicenter cross-sectional study.

Revue : PLoS One | Tier : NA | IF estime : NA

Date de publication : 2026-Nov-20 (Electronic)

Resultats principaux : This multicenter health facility-based cross-sectional study recruited 150 children and adolescents aged 6 months to 18 years with type 1 diabetes mellitus (T1DM) across three regional referral hospitals in Uganda (Jinja, Mbale, and Lira). The primary endpoint was prevalence of high blood pressure, defined using age-sex-height percentiles (≥90th percentile for <13 years; SBP ≥120 or DBP ≥80 mmHg for ≥13 years). Overall, 61 of 150 participants (40.7%; 95% CI 33-49%) had high BP, and 86.9% of those hypertensive had poor glycemic control (HbA1c ≥7%). Multivariable logistic regression identified disease duration of 4-8 years (aOR=2.23, 95% CI 1.05-4.75, p=0.037) and 9-12 years (aOR=4.545, 95% CI 1.075-19.221, p=0.040) as independently associated with high BP, as was poor glycemic control (aOR=2.905, 95% CI 1.149-7.342, p=0.024). Key limitations include the relatively small sample size (n=150), convenience sampling across only three hospitals, and the cross-sectional design which prevents causal inference about the temporal relationship between glycemic control and hypertension development.

Implications Medical Affairs : The strikingly high prevalence of hypertension (40.7%) in pediatric T1DM patients in sub-Saharan Africa highlights a major under-recognized cardiovascular risk burden in a low-resource setting, with implications for global hypertension guidelines that may inadequately address this population. For Medical Affairs, this study underscores the need to position antihypertensive agents with favorable safety profiles in pediatric populations and those with comorbid diabetes as priority research and advocacy areas. Engaging KOLs in pediatric diabetology and global health to advance screening protocols and integrated care pathways in Africa could yield both scientific output and health system impact.

Addressing Disparities in Hypertension Control: The Potential Role of New Drugs and Devices in 2026 and Beyond.

Revue : Current Hypertension Reports | Tier : NA | IF estime : NA

Date de publication : 2026-Nov-20

Resultats principaux : This narrative review from academic centers in the United States examines newly approved and investigational pharmacologic and device-based interventions for hypertension control, with specific focus on racially and ethnically diverse populations who experience a disproportionate burden of hypertension. The review highlights baxdrostat, a recently approved aldosterone synthase inhibitor (ASI), which has demonstrated clinically meaningful blood pressure reductions, and lorundrostat, an investigational ASI designed with intentional enrollment of Black and Hispanic/Latino participants showing consistent efficacy across racial subgroups. Finerenone, a mineralocorticoid receptor antagonist, demonstrated improved cardiac and renal outcomes in pivotal trials but showed limited representation of Black adults, undermining conclusions about generalizability. Aprocitentan, targeting the endothelin pathway, and renal denervation were also approved but have unknown effectiveness in diverse populations. Therapies targeting natriuretic peptide signaling remain investigational and could further expand the therapeutic landscape beyond the 2025 AHA/ACC High Blood Pressure Guideline framework. A critical limitation across reviewed trials is the persistent underrepresentation of racially diverse populations, which limits the generalizability of efficacy and safety data to those who bear the highest burden of disease.

Implications Medical Affairs : This review directly informs product positioning strategy by framing the unmet need around racial equity in hypertension outcomes, which increasingly influences regulatory expectations, payer policies, and guideline committee priorities. Medical Affairs should prioritize advocacy for racially representative trial designs (following the lorundrostat model) in product development programs targeting resistant hypertension, as this differentiates from competitors with limited diversity data. Supporting KOL advisory boards and health equity task forces focused on the 2025 AHA/ACC guideline implementation in underserved populations represents a high-impact actionable strategy.

Association of Serum Complement C3 With Incident Albuminuria: The Danyang Study.

Revue : Journal of Clinical Hypertension | Tier : Tier 3 | IF estime : 3-10

Date de publication : 2026-Nov

Resultats principaux : This prospective community-based cohort study from the Danyang Study enrolled 1,268 participants without albuminuria at baseline and followed them for a median of 3.41 years to identify incident albuminuria (urine albumin-to-creatinine ratio ≥30 mg/g) as the primary endpoint. Multivariable Cox proportional hazards regression, adjusted for conventional cardiovascular, metabolic, and renal risk factors, showed that each 0.1 g/L increment in baseline serum complement C3 was associated with a 19% higher risk of incident albuminuria (HR=1.19, 95% CI 1.02-1.39, p=0.028), with no significant non-linearity. Participants with a persistent high C3 pattern (High-High) had significantly elevated risk compared to persistent low C3 (Low-Low) (adjusted HR=1.73, 95% CI 1.03-2.92, p=0.039). Notably, hypertension status significantly modified the C3-albuminuria association (p for interaction=0.008): C3 was robustly predictive in non-hypertensive individuals (HR=1.50, 95% CI 1.21-1.86, p<0.001) but showed no significant association in those with established hypertension (HR=0.94, p=0.623). Serum C4 showed no significant association. Limitations include the Chinese community-based sample limiting generalizability and the need for validation in independent cohorts before clinical application.

Implications Medical Affairs : The finding that complement C3 predicts early renal injury (albuminuria) independently in non-hypertensive individuals suggests it could serve as a novel biomarker for cardiovascular-renal risk stratification prior to hypertension development, which may be relevant to guideline discussions around primary prevention thresholds. For Medical Affairs, this opens an opportunity to explore complement pathway involvement in cardiorenal continuum research, particularly for agents that influence inflammatory or complement-mediated pathways. Collaboration with KOLs in nephrology and hypertension to prospectively validate C3 as a clinical stratification tool could strengthen the evidence base for precision medicine approaches in hypertension management.

Revue : BMC Public Health | Tier : NA | IF estime : NA

Date de publication : 2026-Aug-26

Resultats principaux : This cross-sectional study enrolled 392 flour mill workers in Goma, Democratic Republic of the Congo, to assess hypertension prevalence and its sociodemographic, lifestyle, and occupational determinants. Blood pressure was measured following WHO-standardized protocols with three consecutive readings, and hypertension was defined as SBP ≥140 and/or DBP ≥90 mmHg or antihypertensive medication use. The overall prevalence of hypertension was 34.9% (95% CI: 30.40-39.79%), a strikingly high figure for an occupational cohort in sub-Saharan Africa. In multivariable logistic regression, independent risk factors included being married (AOR=3.03), family history of hypertension (AOR=2.55), higher education level (AOR=2.54), salt addition at table (AOR=2.08), alcohol consumption (AOR=2.48), occupational noise exposure ≥80 dBA (AOR=2.72), tobacco use (AOR=1.78), and perceived stress (AOR=2.62), with the model demonstrating high discriminative ability (AUC=0.837). Limitations include the cross-sectional design precluding causal inference, potential selection bias in this occupational sample, and self-reported behavioral variables subject to recall bias.

Implications Medical Affairs : This study from sub-Saharan Africa adds to the growing body of evidence linking occupational noise exposure, lifestyle factors, and psychosocial stress to hypertension, reinforcing the case for multi-factorial prevention frameworks that go beyond traditional cardiovascular risk factor management. From a Medical Affairs perspective, this data supports the therapeutic rationale for targeting modifiable lifestyle and occupational factors in hypertension prevention programs in emerging markets, an area of growing interest for global health policy and market access strategies. Partnering with KOLs in occupational medicine and global cardiovascular epidemiology to co-author position papers on workplace-based hypertension screening and prevention in low-resource settings could build scientific credibility in underserved markets.

Association between beta-blocker selectivity and mortality in left heart failure patients with comorbid COPD.

Revue : BMC Pulmonary Medicine | Tier : NA | IF estime : NA

Date de publication : 2026-Aug-27

Resultats principaux : This retrospective observational study used the MIMIC-IV database to evaluate the impact of post-ICU beta-blocker use on clinical outcomes in 3,255 patients with left heart failure (LHF) complicated by chronic obstructive pulmonary disease (COPD). The primary outcomes were 30-day and 365-day all-cause mortality. Among the cohort, 84.18% received beta-blockers post-ICU: 60.31% received cardioselective agents and 23.87% non-cardioselective agents. Kaplan-Meier survival analysis demonstrated that beta-blocker users had significantly lower 30-day mortality (HR=0.291, 95% CI 0.236-0.357, p<0.001) and 365-day mortality (HR=0.468, 95% CI 0.405-0.541, p<0.001) compared to non-users, with this benefit observed consistently across all heart failure subtypes with comorbid COPD. Notably, non-selective beta-blockers demonstrated even lower mortality rates compared to cardioselective agents in both survival analyses, with multivariable Cox regression confirming both cardioselective (30-day HR=0.239; 365-day HR=0.440) and non-selective (30-day HR=0.232; 365-day HR=0.393) agents as independent predictors of reduced mortality. These results were validated after propensity score matching. No significant association was observed between beta-blocker dose and all-cause mortality. A major limitation is the retrospective design using ICU database data, which may have inherent selection bias and lack complete information on COPD severity classification.

Implications Medical Affairs : The finding that non-selective beta-blockers showed superior mortality outcomes versus cardioselective agents in LHF-COPD patients challenges the conventional prescribing preference for cardioselective agents in COPD and may stimulate guideline revision discussions that Medical Affairs teams should actively monitor. This evidence could support positioning of non-selective beta-blockers in the LHF-COPD overlap syndrome, an underserved clinical niche where differentiation based on drug selectivity rather than dosage could be a compelling value proposition. Medical Affairs should prioritize scientific engagement with pulmonology and cardiology KOLs to validate these findings prospectively, as the retrospective database design limits immediate clinical translation.

Effectiveness of a Nurse-led Intervention Targeting Antihypertensive Treatment-Related Adverse Effects in Apparent Resistant Hypertension: A Randomized Controlled Trial.

Revue : Journal of Clinical Hypertension | Tier : Tier 3 | IF estime : 3-10

Date de publication : 2026-Nov

Resultats principaux : This randomized, prospective, open-label trial enrolled 46 patients with ABPM-confirmed apparent resistant hypertension (aRH) and suboptimal medication adherence (assessed by Morisky Medication Adherence Scale-8) at a Spanish university hospital. Participants were randomized 1:1 to a nurse-led intervention (n=23) consisting of 6-8 structured visits targeting underrecognized treatment-related adverse effects, or to usual care (n=23). After 6 months, the primary endpoint of reduction in 24-hour systolic blood pressure was significantly greater in the intervention group (-16.8 mmHg) versus the control group (-4.2 mmHg; unadjusted difference p=0.015). In the baseline-adjusted ANCOVA model, the adjusted between-group difference was -9.4 mmHg (p=0.046), confirming the primary finding. The intervention was also associated with a significant reduction in therapeutic inertia (13.0% vs 56.5%; p=0.040; RR=0.23, 95% CI 0.08-0.70). Limitations include the small sample size (n=46), the open-label design, and the single-center setting, which reduce statistical power and generalizability, though the effect sizes are clinically meaningful.

Implications Medical Affairs : This RCT provides Level II evidence that systematic nurse-led adverse effect management can produce clinically meaningful blood pressure reductions (-9.4 mmHg adjusted) in patients with apparent resistant hypertension, adding to the evidence base for non-pharmacologic optimization strategies before medication escalation and supporting a broader team-based care model that guidelines increasingly recommend. From a Medical Affairs perspective, these findings support the positioning of antihypertensive portfolios within comprehensive care programs that include allied health components, as adherence barriers may be as important as pharmacologic potency in real-world resistance. This study could also serve as a model for investigator-initiated research partnerships with nursing teams to prospectively evaluate adherence-focused interventions as adjuncts to pharmacotherapy, strengthening the KOL network in resistant hypertension management.

Efficacy and Safety Analysis of Lisan Compound Ointment in the Treatment of Bronchiolitis Obliterans Syndrome After Allogeneic Hematopoietic Stem Cell Transplantation.

Revue : Zhongguo Shi Yan Xue Ye Xue Za Zhi | Tier : NA | IF estime : NA

Date de publication : 2026-Oct

Resultats principaux : This randomized controlled trial enrolled 62 hematological patients who developed bronchiolitis obliterans syndrome (BOS) following allogeneic hematopoietic stem cell transplantation (allo-HSCT), randomizing them to receive either a glucocorticoid-based anti-rejection regimen alone (control, n=32) or the same regimen plus Lisan compound ointment (observation, n=30). The study was conducted between October 2016 and October 2020 at Chinese transplant centers. The primary finding was a significantly higher overall response rate in the observation group at 6 months (76.7% vs 40.6%, p=0.026). Importantly for the hypertension context, the incidence of hypertension as a glucocorticoid-related adverse effect was significantly lower in the Lisan group compared to controls (p<0.05), along with lower rates of lung infection and osteoporosis. The 3-year overall survival was significantly better in the observation group (80.0% vs 53.1%, p<0.05), with multivariate analysis confirming Lisan as independently reducing 3-year mortality risk (OR=0.028, 95% CI 0.002-0.355). Limitations include the small sample size, the Chinese herbal medicine nature of the intervention that may limit generalizability, and the absence of detailed blood pressure measurements as a formal secondary endpoint.

Implications Medical Affairs : While primarily a hematology study, the significant reduction in glucocorticoid-induced hypertension with Lisan compound ointment highlights the clinical relevance of steroid-sparing strategies in preventing iatrogenic hypertension following transplantation, a niche area that is often overlooked in hypertension management guidelines. From a Medical Affairs perspective, this study draws attention to hypertension as a preventable adverse event in immunosuppressive therapy, suggesting opportunities for real-world evidence generation on antihypertensive agents specifically in the post-transplant setting. Scientific engagement with KOLs in hematology-oncology who manage post-transplant hypertension may open interdisciplinary collaboration opportunities at the intersection of oncology, nephrology, and cardiovascular pharmacology.

Nutrition, metabolism, and cardiovascular longevity — what is established, what is hypothesis?

Revue : Herz | Tier : NA | IF estime : NA

Date de publication : 2026-Nov-20

Resultats principaux : This narrative review from the Technische Universitat Munchen examined the evidence base for nutritional and metabolic interventions targeting cardiovascular longevity, distinguishing established from hypothetical benefits across RCTs, cohort studies, and mechanistic research. Whole dietary patterns — specifically the Mediterranean diet, Dietary Approaches to Stop Hypertension (DASH), and sodium reduction strategies — were confirmed to reduce hard cardiovascular endpoints, with the causal roles of apolipoprotein B-containing lipoproteins, blood pressure, and adiposity firmly established. GLP-1 receptor agonist-based pharmacologic weight loss was highlighted as superior to lifestyle programs alone in reducing cardiovascular events, shifting the paradigm toward preservation of lean mass and micronutrient adequacy as nutritional goals alongside drug therapy. In contrast, isolated nutrients and supplements, specific geroprotective effects of intermittent fasting, and the clinical utility of epigenetic aging clocks remain unproven, with randomized caloric restriction showing only modest slowing of epigenetic aging pace without demonstrated mortality benefit in humans. The authors conclude that nutrition acts primarily through established cardiovascular risk factors rather than through direct lifespan extension, and that endpoint-driven trials are needed rather than surrogate marker-based research. Limitations of the narrative review format include potential selection bias and the lack of systematic search methodology.

Implications Medical Affairs : This review reinforces the evidence hierarchy for dietary interventions in hypertension — where DASH and sodium reduction have robust trial data — and positions GLP-1 agonists as a pharmacologic adjunct to nutrition in cardiometabolic risk reduction, relevant to guideline updates and therapeutic positioning strategies. From a Medical Affairs standpoint, this supports scientific communication that pairs antihypertensive pharmacotherapy with evidence-based dietary counseling (DASH, Mediterranean) rather than emerging but unproven interventions, strengthening the credibility of comprehensive treatment narratives. Supporting KOL-driven publication programs on the cardiovascular longevity evidence hierarchy could differentiate Medical Affairs scientific leadership and inform payer and guideline stakeholder engagement.

Sleep Irregularity and Risk of Incident Hypertension in the UK Biobank.

Revue : medRxiv (preprint) | Tier : NA | IF estime : NA

Date de publication : 2026-Nov-15

Resultats principaux : This large prospective cohort study from the UK Biobank enrolled 55,572 participants without hypertension or antihypertensive medication use who provided at least five valid days of wrist accelerometry data between 2013-2015 (60% women, mean age 61 years). Sleep regularity was quantified using intra-individual standard deviation of sleep duration and the Sleep Regularity Index (SRI), measuring day-to-day sleep-wake pattern similarity. Cox proportional hazards models adjusted for demographic, socioeconomic, and lifestyle factors revealed that over 7.9 years of follow-up, 5,713 participants developed incident hypertension identified through linked health records. Participants with sleep duration SD greater than 90 minutes had an 18% higher risk of incident hypertension compared to those with SD 30 minutes or less (HR=1.18, 95% CI 1.06-1.31). More strikingly, participants in the lowest SRI quintile (most irregular sleep) had a 40% higher risk than those in the highest quintile (HR=1.40, 95% CI 1.29-1.52). These associations were consistent across demographic, clinical, and sleep-defined subgroups, and sleep irregularity and genetic susceptibility contributed independently to hypertension risk without evidence of interaction. The main limitation is the preprint status (not yet peer-reviewed), and the observational design prevents causal conclusions.

Implications Medical Affairs : This high-powered prospective study from UK Biobank provides compelling evidence that sleep irregularity — independent of sleep duration, quality, and genetic susceptibility — is a novel modifiable risk factor for hypertension, with potential implications for the expansion of hypertension prevention guidelines to include sleep schedule regularity alongside traditional lifestyle recommendations. For Medical Affairs, this study supports the development of a scientific narrative around holistic lifestyle intervention programs that complement antihypertensive pharmacotherapy, particularly in primary prevention settings where patient adherence to behavioral recommendations is a key differentiator. Collaborating with KOLs in sleep medicine, digital health, and preventive cardiology to advance this research from preprint to publication and guideline integration represents a high-visibility scientific engagement opportunity.

Cardiovascular risk in urologists.

Revue : The French Journal of Urology | Tier : NA | IF estime : NA

Date de publication : 2026-Nov

Resultats principaux : This narrative review from French academic centers examined the cardiovascular risk profile specific to urological surgeons, arguing that this occupational group faces a heightened burden of cardiovascular diseases — including coronary artery disease, cardiac arrhythmias, and arterial hypertension — compared to other medical specialties and the general population. Specific occupational risk factors identified include chronic stress, heavy workloads, mandatory night shifts, and a predominantly sedentary lifestyle outside of surgical activity, as well as operating room-specific hazards such as noise exposure and ionizing radiation. The proposed pathophysiological mechanisms linking these exposures to cardiovascular disease encompass chronic sympathetic nervous system activation, low-grade systemic inflammation, endothelial dysfunction, and metabolic disturbances including dysglycemia and dyslipidemia. Preventive strategies discussed include structured physical activity, psychological stress management, optimized shift scheduling, and monitoring of cardiovascular risk factors including blood pressure. Limitations include the narrative (non-systematic) review format, the absence of quantitative risk data specific to urologists, and reliance on evidence largely extrapolated from broader surgical populations.

Implications Medical Affairs : This review highlights an underexplored occupational dimension of cardiovascular risk, specifically arterial hypertension driven by occupational stressors, that is relevant to the broader epidemiology of hypertension in high-stress professions and may stimulate targeted screening program initiatives. From a Medical Affairs perspective, the emphasis on occupational-specific hypertension risk underscores the value of antihypertensive strategies tailored to shift workers and high-stress professional groups, a niche that could inform real-world evidence studies and payer negotiations around preventive cardiovascular screenings. Prevalence of Prehypertension and Its Association With Sedentary Lifestyle Indicators Among Adults: A Cross-Sectional Study.

Revue : Cureus | Tier : NA | IF estime : NA

Date de publication : 2026-Oct

Resultats principaux : This cross-sectional observational study enrolled 100 adults aged 18-40 years in Karad, Maharashtra, India, using convenience sampling, with blood pressure measured by standardized sphygmomanometry and physical activity assessed by the International Physical Activity Questionnaire (IPAQ). The prevalence of prehypertension (SBP 120-139 or DBP 80-89 mmHg) was 48%, a strikingly high rate among young adults, with 37 of 65 males (56.9%) affected versus 11 of 35 females (31.4%; p=0.015). Overweight and obese participants had significantly higher prehypertension prevalence compared to normal-BMI participants (p=0.009), and mean BMI was significantly higher in prehypertensive vs normotensive participants (25.60 vs 23.93 kg/m2, p=0.002). A trend toward higher prehypertension with lower physical activity was observed (68.0% low PA vs 43.1% moderate PA vs 35.3% high PA) but did not reach statistical significance (p=0.059). On multivariable logistic regression, BMI remained the only independent predictor of prehypertension (aOR=1.24, 95% CI 1.05-1.48, p=0.013), while gender and physical activity level were not independently associated after adjustment. Key limitations include the small sample size (n=100) with convenience sampling, limiting generalizability, and the cross-sectional design preventing longitudinal risk inference.

Implications Medical Affairs : The 48% prevalence of prehypertension in young Indian adults confirms a substantial pre-disease burden in a large emerging market population, reinforcing the strategic importance of early intervention programs targeting modifiable factors — particularly BMI — in young adults before progression to established hypertension. This study supports the positioning of antihypertensive or weight management interventions as preventive strategies in the prehypertension continuum, particularly in the rapidly growing young adult segment of South Asian markets where metabolic risk factors converge at earlier ages. Medical Affairs can leverage this epidemiological signal to partner with KOLs in preventive medicine and primary care in India to design lifestyle-pharmacology integration studies and advocacy efforts around early cardiovascular risk screening in this demographic.
