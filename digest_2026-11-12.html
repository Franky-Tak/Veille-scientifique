Horizon-Aware Forecasting of Hypertension Prevalence and Mean Total Cholesterol in Chinese Men: Retrospective Leakage-Controlled Benchmark Study Comparing Statistical, Machine-Learning, and Neural Models.

Revue : JMIR Medical Informatics | Tier : NA | IF estime : NA

Date de publication : 2026-11-11

Resultats principaux : This retrospective benchmark study compared statistical, machine learning, and neural network forecasting models for age-standardized hypertension prevalence (ASPH) in Chinese men aged 30-79 years using 30 annual data points (1990-2019). Rigorous leakage-controlled expanding-window evaluation was applied across 1-, 2-, and 5-year forecast horizons, encompassing 7,242 planned fits. Statistical models—specifically ARIMA—outperformed all alternatives in all 6 target-horizon cells (e.g., RMSE 0.000178 at h=1 for ASPH). Removing attention mechanisms from neural models reduced RMSE in 5 of 6 ablation cells, and compact architectures outperformed larger ones in 5 of 6 cells, demonstrating that model complexity conferred no consistent advantage. Predictive intervals frequently undercovered, highlighting poor calibration despite reasonable point accuracy. A key limitation is the small annual series size (30-39 observations) constraining model complexity assessment, and findings apply only to these two Chinese targets.

Implications Medical Affairs : These findings have direct implications for pharmaceutical market planning in China, as reliable ARIMA-based hypertension prevalence forecasts support sales projections and resource allocation for one of the world's largest hypertension markets. The finding that simple statistical models outperform complex ML/AI approaches for short health time series challenges assumptions about AI superiority in health analytics, which is relevant for Medical Affairs teams deploying predictive tools for strategic planning. This benchmark establishes a reproducible leakage-controlled framework for evaluating forecasting approaches that could inform national public health planning tools and guideline committee projections.

Neuronal CD200 Loss in the Rostral Ventrolateral Medulla Exacerbates Neuroinflammation and Promotes Stress-Induced Hypertension.

Revue : Journal of Neurochemistry | Tier : NA | IF estime : NA

Date de publication : 2026-11

Resultats principaux : This preclinical study in Sprague-Dawley rats used proteomic profiling, lentiviral knockdown, and pharmacological rescue to characterize the role of CD200—a neuroimmune checkpoint protein—in stress-induced hypertension (SIH). RVLM proteomic screening identified significant CD200 downregulation in SIH rats confirmed by Western blot and immunofluorescence. Lentiviral CD200 knockdown in normal rat RVLM induced M1 microglial polarization, neuroinflammation, and elevated blood pressure. Local microinjection of CD200Fc (a CD200 receptor agonist) into the RVLM of SIH rats reduced blood pressure and ameliorated neuroinflammation. The proposed mechanism involves CD200 loss promoting M1 microglial polarization, enhancing RVLM neuronal excitability, and driving excessive sympathetic outflow sustaining hypertension. Key limitations include exclusive use of rodent models without human validation and the invasiveness of RVLM interventions.

Implications Medical Affairs : This study identifies a novel neuroinflammatory mechanism underlying stress-induced hypertension via the CD200/CD200R axis, representing a potential therapeutic target distinct from all current antihypertensive drug classes and particularly relevant for treatment-resistant or stress-related hypertension. From a Medical Affairs perspective, the CD200/CD200R pathway provides a compelling mechanistic narrative for future CNS-targeted antihypertensive pipeline positioning, especially in patients who do not respond adequately to conventional renin-angiotensin or sympathetic blockade. This basic science data supports KOL engagement at the neuroinflammation-cardiovascular interface, stimulating investigator-initiated research into neuroimmune mechanisms of hypertension.

Global inequalities, attributable risks, and future trends in hypertensive heart disease burden: An ecological study based on the Global Burden of Disease Study 2021, 1990-2021.

Revue : Medicine | Tier : NA | IF estime : NA

Date de publication : 2026-10-30

Resultats principaux : This ecological study analyzed Global Burden of Disease 2021 data across 204 countries to characterize hypertensive heart disease (HHD) trends from 1990 to 2021. In 2021, HHD accounted for 12.51 million prevalent cases, 1.33 million deaths, and 25.46 million DALYs globally. Age-standardized prevalence rates increased from 125.44 to 148.32 per 100,000 from 1990 to 2021, while age-standardized death rates decreased from 20.92 to 16.32 per 100,000 over the same period. High BMI emerged as the leading modifiable risk factor beyond systolic blood pressure, with its DALY-attributable fraction increasing dramatically from 35.6% to 48.9%. ARIMA projections forecast ASPR to reach 152.04 per 100,000 by 2031 while ASDR is expected to decline to 12.27 per 100,000. Limitations include the ecological design precluding individual-level causal inference and the retrospective nature of GBD model estimates.

Implications Medical Affairs : The rising global prevalence of HHD despite declining mortality creates an expanding population requiring chronic antihypertensive management, representing a sustained commercial opportunity for antihypertensive portfolios particularly in low- and middle-income countries. The dramatic increase in BMI as an HHD risk factor (attributable fraction rising to 48.9%) supports positioning antihypertensive treatments alongside weight management strategies, including potential synergy with GLP-1 receptor agonists in dual-indication patients. Medical Affairs teams should leverage these epidemiological projections in KOL education programs to reinforce early aggressive blood pressure control and guideline adherence, particularly in disadvantaged regions and older women where the burden remains disproportionately high.

Hysterectomy and gallstone risk in women: A cross-sectional NHANES study on the mediating role of metabolic disorders.

Revue : Medicine | Tier : NA | IF estime : NA

Date de publication : 2026-10-30

Resultats principaux : This cross-sectional study analyzed 5,317 female participants (aged ≥20) from NHANES cycles 2017-2023 to assess the relationship between prior hysterectomy and gallstone disease (GSD). Women with hysterectomy history had 32% higher odds of GSD (fully adjusted OR=1.32, 95% CI: 1.10-1.59). Mediation analysis revealed that BMI accounted for 16.5% of this association, diabetes for 13.9%, and hypertension for 6.5% of the hysterectomy-GSD relationship. The association was more pronounced in women of lower socioeconomic status. Hyperlipidemia was not a significant mediator despite being a known metabolic comorbidity. Limitations include the cross-sectional design preventing causal inference, self-reported hysterectomy status, and potential residual confounding.

Implications Medical Affairs : While primarily gynecological in focus, the finding that hypertension mediates 6.5% of the hysterectomy-gallstone association reinforces hypertension's role as a systemic metabolic driver with consequences extending beyond cardiovascular endpoints. For Medical Affairs, this provides supporting evidence for the broad systemic consequences of uncontrolled hypertension in postmenopausal women, strengthening messaging around optimal blood pressure management as part of holistic women's health strategies. This data may inform KOL engagement around hypertension's multisystem metabolic impact in women's cardiovascular health awareness campaigns.

Revue : Medicine | Tier : NA | IF estime : NA

Date de publication : 2026-10-30

Resultats principaux : This retrospective cross-sectional study reviewed 300 ACS patients admitted to Jimma University Medical Center in Ethiopia over 5 years (November 2017–October 2022). In-hospital mortality was notably high at 35.3%. Hypertension was the most prevalent risk factor, present in 88.3% of ACS patients. Standard therapies were administered at high rates: aspirin 94.7%, clopidogrel 95.3%, beta-blockers 75.7%, statins 94.3%. Implications Medical Affairs : The 35.3% in-hospital ACS mortality in this Ethiopian setting underscores the critical unmet need for cardiovascular disease management in sub-Saharan Africa, where hypertension is the dominant risk factor affecting 88.3% of ACS patients. This data supports Medical Affairs advocacy for hypertension early detection and treatment programs in emerging African markets, positioning antihypertensive therapies as primary prevention tools with significant public health impact. KOL engagement strategies in sub-Saharan Africa should emphasize guideline implementation and formulary access, particularly for beta-blockers and renin-angiotensin system inhibitors to reduce the high ACS mortality burden.

Waist wins: Waist circumference tops newer indices for identifying prevalent elevated BP and hypertension in the Saudi population.

Revue : Medicine | Tier : NA | IF estime : NA

Date de publication : 2026-10-30

Resultats principaux : This cross-sectional study of 1,041 Saudi adults compared waist circumference (WC), body roundness index (BRI), weight-adjusted waist index (WWI), a body shape index (ABSI), and BMI as discriminators for elevated blood pressure and hypertension. In fully adjusted linear models, a 1 SD increase in WC (~17 cm) was associated with +4.80/+3.68 mmHg SBP/DBP (p<0.0001), substantially larger than other indices. ROC analyses confirmed WC achieved the highest area under the curve for discriminating both elevated BP and hypertension. Receiver operating characteristic analyses supported WC superiority for hypertension screening specifically in women. Limitations include the cross-sectional design, restriction to Saudi adults limiting generalizability, and absence of prospective validation of the cutoffs.

Implications Medical Affairs : The finding that waist circumference outperforms newer anthropometric indices for hypertension screening in a Middle Eastern population has direct implications for cardiovascular risk stratification protocols in a region with high and rising hypertension prevalence. For Medical Affairs, this supports messaging around the metabolic-cardiovascular nexus in patients with central obesity, reinforcing combination treatment strategies targeting both blood pressure and abdominal adiposity in the Gulf region. Engaging KOLs around sex-specific screening cutoffs and risk stratification could differentiate positioning of antihypertensive agents in patients identified through WC-based screening programs in Saudi Arabia and neighboring countries.

The association between obesity index and hypertension in type 2 diabetes: a cross-sectional study.

Revue : Frontiers in Endocrinology | Tier : NA | IF estime : NA

Date de publication : 2026-10-27

Resultats principaux : This cross-sectional study enrolled 4,155 T2DM patients from a Chinese hospital, finding that 68.5% had comorbid hypertension. Chinese visceral adiposity index (CVAI) and lipid accumulation product (LAP) were both positively associated with hypertension (p for trend <0.001 for both). The highest CVAI quartile had OR 3.27 (95% CI: 2.66-4.02) for hypertension versus the lowest quartile; LAP Q4 OR was 3.01 (95% CI: 2.43-3.72). Implications Medical Affairs : The identification of an LAP threshold of approximately 44.2 in T2DM patients provides a potential clinical decision tool for identifying the highest-risk subgroup requiring aggressive antihypertensive intervention, which could be integrated into Chinese clinical guidelines for T2DM-hypertension comorbidity management. For Medical Affairs, this finding strengthens the rationale for proactive antihypertensive screening in all T2DM patients, particularly those with elevated visceral adiposity metrics, supporting product positioning in the growing cardiometabolic comorbidity space. This data supports cross-specialty KOL engagement between endocrinology and cardiology to promote integrated management approaches for the overlapping T2DM-hypertension patient population in China.

Effect of discontinuing antihypertensives on perioperative hypotension in older patients before non-cardiac surgery: multicentre, randomised, double blind, controlled trial.

Revue : BMJ | Tier : 1 | IF estime : >105

Date de publication : 2026-11-11

Resultats principaux : This multicenter, randomized, double-blind, placebo-controlled trial enrolled 2,036 older patients (60-80 years) using ACE inhibitors or ARBs for at least 2 weeks before non-cardiac surgery at five Chinese tertiary hospitals (November 2020-December 2023). The primary endpoint was perioperative hypotension (MAP <65 mmHg or ≥30% decrease from baseline requiring treatment). Discontinuing antihypertensives significantly reduced perioperative hypotension: 31.2% vs 45.5% (adjusted OR 0.52, 95% CI 0.43-0.63; ARD -14.4%, 95% CI -18.5% to -10.1%; p<0.001). Duration of hypotension and vasopressor requirements were also significantly reduced in the discontinuation arm. Critically, 30-day complications (neurocognitive disorders, major cardiocerebral events) and mortality did not differ significantly between groups, providing high-certainty safety reassurance for the withholding strategy. Limitations include restriction to Chinese populations and to patients on ACE inhibitors or ARBs only, with applicability to other antihypertensive classes requiring further study.

Implications Medical Affairs : This landmark BMJ RCT provides high-certainty evidence that temporarily withholding ACE inhibitors or ARBs on the morning of surgery safely reduces perioperative hypotension by an absolute 14.4% without increasing 30-day cardiovascular complications, directly addressing a major safety concern for the large population of hypertensive patients undergoing surgery globally. From a Medical Affairs perspective, this evidence is immediately actionable for updating surgical guidelines and perioperative management protocols in anesthesiology and cardiovascular society guidelines, with direct labeling and positioning implications for ACE inhibitors and ARBs. This trial should be a priority for Medical Affairs communications to anesthesiology and cardiology KOLs, as it validates a simple, safe protocol modification—withholding on the morning of surgery—that could reduce intraoperative vasopressor use and improve patient safety outcomes worldwide.

Esketamine-Based Versus Sevoflurane-Based Anesthesia with Dexmedetomidine for Emergence Agitation in Pediatric Corneal Suture Removal: A Randomised Controlled Trial.

Revue : Drug Design, Development and Therapy | Tier : NA | IF estime : NA

Date de publication : 2026-11-06

Resultats principaux : This prospective RCT enrolled 116 pediatric patients undergoing elective corneal suture removal, randomized to esketamine-based (Group E, n=58) or sevoflurane-based (Group S, n=58) anesthesia with dexmedetomidine as baseline sedation. The primary endpoint (emergence agitation) was significantly lower in Group E (6.9% vs 27.6%; RR 0.25, 95% CI 0.09-0.70; p=0.003). Esketamine also reduced respiratory depression (3.4% vs 43.1%, p=0.001) and shortened PACU discharge time (17.00 vs 19.50 min, p=0.02). However, esketamine was associated with a markedly higher incidence of hypertension (22.4% vs 0.0%, p=0.001) and elevated intraocular pressure (16 vs 13 mmHg, p=0.003). Limitations include the single-center design, relatively small sample size, and pediatric-specific context limiting generalizability to adult or other surgical populations.

Implications Medical Affairs : The finding that esketamine causes hypertension in 22.4% of pediatric patients compared to 0% with sevoflurane identifies a clinically significant cardiovascular safety signal that must be weighed against efficacy advantages, particularly in patients with hypertensive tendency or elevated cardiovascular risk. For Medical Affairs teams managing cardiovascular portfolios, this data reinforces the importance of blood pressure monitoring protocols during ketamine-derivative anesthesia and may support positioning of rapidly-acting antihypertensive formulations as rescue therapy in esketamine-exposed surgical patients. KOL engagement with pediatric anesthesiologists should address cardiovascular monitoring requirements and co-management strategies to safely harness esketamine's antiagitation benefits while mitigating hypertensive adverse events.

Date de publication : 2026-11-11

Resultats principaux : This multi-omics study integrated genome-wide association analyses, neuroimaging, and proteomics from 42,039 individuals from the UK Biobank and ADNI cohorts to characterize tract-specific white matter free water (FW-WM) as a marker of extracellular brain water. GWAS identified 27 independent loci and 88 prioritized genes enriched in vascular endothelial cells, pericytes, and astrocytes, with 20 loci (74%) being novel compared to conventional white matter metrics. Imaging-derived FW-WM showed strong associations with stroke (OR=3.14) and dementia (OR=4.15), and higher baseline FW-WM predicted MCI-to-AD progression (HR=2.58). Integrative proteomic analyses nominated PEAR1 and ANGPTL3 as candidate proteins linking hypertension-related vascular processes to FW-WM variation. Limitations include primarily UK Biobank-centric population limiting geographic generalizability and retrospective design.

Implications Medical Affairs : This high-impact PNAS multi-omics study establishes a genetic and vascular basis for white matter disease and identifies hypertension as a probable causal driver of neurological decline via measurable neuroimaging biomarkers, which could transform how hypertension's end-organ effects on the brain are assessed and monitored in clinical trials. The identification of PEAR1 and ANGPTL3 as mechanistic candidates linking hypertension to white matter changes provides potential biomarker targets for demonstrating antihypertensive benefit beyond traditional cardiovascular endpoints, relevant for label expansion strategies. Date de publication : 2026-08-03

Resultats principaux : This longitudinal analysis examined attrition patterns in the AWI-Gen cohort including 12,032 participants from six African sites with median baseline age of 51 years (IQR: 45-57 years). After five years, the cohort experienced 40% overall attrition. Main causes included being unreachable by phone (42.6%), refusal (26.0%), death (13.9%), relocation (10.5%), and being too ill (3.9%). Implications Medical Affairs : The identification of hypertension as a significant predictor of death-related attrition in African longitudinal studies has critical methodological implications for hypertension research design, as attrition biases may systematically underestimate true hypertension-related mortality if the highest-risk patients are disproportionately lost to follow-up.
