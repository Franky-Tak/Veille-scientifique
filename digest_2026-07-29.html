Abdominal fat thickness predicts the risk of pregnancy-associated hypertension: A prospective cohort study.

Revue : Asia Pacific Journal of Clinical Nutrition | Tier : NA | IF estime : NA

Date de publication : 2026-08

Resultats principaux : This prospective cohort study enrolled 1,912 pregnant women between 9 and 13+6 weeks of gestation in China and assessed early-pregnancy abdominal fat thickness by ultrasound as a predictor of pregnancy-associated hypertension (PAH). The overall incidence of PAH (gestational hypertension and pre-eclampsia combined) was 7.1% (136/1,912). High subcutaneous adipose tissue (SAT ≥17.05 mm) and high visceral adipose tissue (VAT ≥28.85 mm) were each independently associated with significantly elevated PAH risk, with adjusted odds ratios of 2.74 (95% CI: 1.89–3.96) and 2.42 (95% CI: 1.67–3.50) respectively, even after controlling for BMI. A predictive model combining these adiposity markers showed an AUC of 0.745 (95% CI: 0.703–0.788), indicating good discrimination for early PAH identification. Notably, no significant associations were observed between daily nutrient intake and PAH risk, and obesity (BMI ≥30) conferred the highest odds (aOR 7.04), highlighting the incremental value of ultrasound fat quantification beyond BMI alone.

Implications Medical Affairs : The ability to identify high-risk pregnancies for PAH in the first trimester using routine ultrasound fat measurements represents a clinically actionable screening opportunity that could be incorporated into antenatal guidelines. From a Medical Affairs standpoint, this finding supports the broader narrative around early cardiovascular risk identification in women, a population segment where antihypertensive therapy decision-making is particularly sensitive. Companies with products indicated in pre-eclampsia prevention or early pregnancy hypertension management should engage KOLs in maternal-fetal medicine to explore how adiposity-based risk stratification could improve treatment initiation timing.

Revue : PloS One | Tier : NA | IF estime : NA

Date de publication : 2026-07-28

Resultats principaux : This qualitative study nested within the SEARCH Sapphire pilot randomized controlled trial examined perceptions of CHW-facilitated clinician-driven telehealth versus clinic-based care for adults ≥40 years with moderate-to-severe hypertension in rural western Kenya and southwestern Uganda. In-depth semi-structured interviews were conducted with 40 participants and 15 healthcare providers between January and April 2023. Implications Medical Affairs : This study demonstrates the feasibility and acceptability of task-sharing models for hypertension care in sub-Saharan Africa, a region with the highest uncontrolled hypertension burden globally, and strengthens the evidence base for digital health integration in resource-limited settings. Association between obstructive sleep apnea syndrome and blood pressure variability: a meta-analysis.

Revue : Frontiers in Medicine | Tier : NA | IF estime : NA

Date de publication : 2026-07-06

Resultats principaux : This systematic review and meta-analysis evaluated the association between obstructive sleep apnea syndrome (OSAS) and blood pressure variability (BPV) as assessed by ambulatory blood pressure monitoring (ABPM), including 11 studies comprising 17 datasets (1,261 OSAS patients and 826 controls). OSAS was associated with significantly elevated BPV across all periods, with nighttime BPV showing the largest differences: systolic BPV MD +1.89 mmHg (95% CI: 1.07–2.70) and diastolic BPV MD +1.93 mmHg (95% CI: 1.17–2.69). Twenty-four-hour systolic BPV was also significantly higher in OSAS patients (MD +1.77 mmHg; 95% CI: 0.80–2.75), and daytime systolic BPV showed a smaller but significant elevation (+1.17 mmHg; 95% CI: 0.35–1.99). Subgroup analyses suggested a trend toward increasing BPV with greater OSAS severity, though this was statistically significant only for nighttime diastolic BPV. The certainty of evidence ranged from low to very low per GRADE criteria, and substantial between-study heterogeneity warrants cautious interpretation.

Implications Medical Affairs : Given that elevated BPV is an independent predictor of cardiovascular events and target organ damage beyond mean blood pressure levels, this meta-analysis reinforces the importance of treating OSAS as a modifiable cardiovascular risk factor in hypertensive patients, and adds impetus to including ABPM-based BPV assessment in routine hypertension monitoring. For Medical Affairs, these findings support the clinical narrative for 24-hour blood pressure control and may differentiate antihypertensive agents with proven BPV-reducing properties (notably calcium channel blockers) in the growing OSAS-hypertension comorbid population. KOL engagement strategies should target sleep medicine specialists and hypertension experts to position ABPM-guided therapy as the standard in patients with suspected or confirmed OSAS.

Theme 2 : ACE Inhibitors (2 articles)

Renin-angiotensin system blockade in diabetic kidney disease: an old pillar that still stands at the core of therapy.

Revue : Kidney Research and Clinical Practice | Tier : NA | IF estime : NA

Date de publication : 2026-07-28

Resultats principaux : This comprehensive review examines the mechanistic basis and clinical evidence for renin-angiotensin system (RAS) blockade—encompassing ACE inhibitors and ARBs—in diabetic kidney disease (DKD), the leading global cause of chronic kidney disease and end-stage kidney disease. Angiotensin II promotes intraglomerular hypertension via efferent arteriolar constriction, while non-hemodynamic effects including TGF-β upregulation, NADPH oxidase-derived oxidative stress, and podocyte apoptosis drive structural kidney injury. Large randomized trials have consistently demonstrated that both ACEi and ARBs reduce albuminuria, slow GFR decline, and delay kidney failure across DKD stages; cardiovascular benefits including reduced heart failure events have also been documented. Recent evidence challenges the practice of routinely discontinuing RAS blockade in advanced CKD, showing that cessation does not improve kidney function and may increase cardiorenal risk. Albuminuria reduction is emphasized as a mechanistic and therapeutic marker closely correlated with long-term kidney outcomes, and RAS inhibition is confirmed as the foundational therapy for DKD even as SGLT2 inhibitors and other agents expand the treatment armamentarium.

Implications Medical Affairs : This review underscores that despite newer therapeutic classes, ACEi and ARBs remain indispensable as the backbone of DKD therapy and should not be deprioritized—a message directly relevant to defending market positioning and guideline alignment of RAS-blocking agents. From a Medical Affairs perspective, the evidence against routine RAS blockade discontinuation in advanced CKD represents an important clinical narrative that should be communicated proactively to nephrologists and diabetologists to prevent inappropriate therapy cessation. KOL engagement focused on the albuminuria-as-surrogate-endpoint narrative can support registration strategy for combination therapies and reinforce value propositions in payer discussions.

Effects of statins on functional capacity and cardiac remodeling in patients with heart failure and preserved ejection fraction: a randomized open-label pilot trial.

Revue : Frontiers in Cardiovascular Medicine | Tier : NA | IF estime : NA

Date de publication : 2026-07-13

Resultats principaux : This randomized open-label pilot trial enrolled 59 HFpEF patients (mean age not specified) with no prior statin use and randomly assigned them to atorvastatin 20–80 mg/day (n=30) or rosuvastatin 10–40 mg/day (n=29) for 6 months, assessing echocardiographic parameters, 6-minute walk distance (6MWD), inflammatory biomarkers (hsCRP, MCP-1), and fibrosis markers (PICP, sST2). Atorvastatin was associated with a significantly greater increase in 6MWD compared with rosuvastatin, though both groups showed comparable NYHA functional class improvement and bicycle exercise duration gains. Both statins produced comparable reductions in E/e' ratio and left atrial volume index, and similar improvements in diastolic and chronotropic reserves on exercise stress testing; atorvastatin was superior only in modifying LA reservoir strain on exercise. All biomarkers decreased significantly in the atorvastatin group but not in the rosuvastatin group, with rosuvastatin showing reductions in hsCRP and MCP-1 only in patients with baseline values above the median. Key limitations include small sample size, open-label design, absence of a placebo arm, and the absence of clinical event data, making this a hypothesis-generating pilot study.

Implications Medical Affairs : This pilot trial provides preliminary evidence that lipophilic statins (atorvastatin) may offer additional anti-inflammatory and functional capacity benefits over hydrophilic statins in HFpEF—a condition frequently comorbid with hypertension—potentially informing future trial design comparing statin types in heart failure populations. From a Medical Affairs perspective, these findings are relevant to positioning strategies for companies in the hypertension-HFpEF continuum, particularly as HFpEF represents an unmet medical need where guideline-directed medical therapy remains limited. KOL engagement with heart failure specialists should focus on the mechanistic rationale for lipophilic versus hydrophilic statin effects in HFpEF, supporting the generation of larger, placebo-controlled confirmatory trials.

High incidence of colorectal cancer in Japanese individuals with hypertension.

Revue : Hypertension Research | Tier : NA | IF estime : NA

Date de publication : 2026-07-28

Resultats principaux : This population-based retrospective cohort study used the Shizuoka Kokuho Database (2012–2022) and propensity score matching to compare colorectal cancer (CRC) incidence between 113,724 normotensive and 85,399 hypertensive individuals not taking antihypertensive medications, with a median follow-up of 4.38 years. After propensity score matching, hypertensive individuals had significantly higher CRC incidence (HR 1.15; 95% CI: 1.02–1.30), with this association observed predominantly in men. Among women taking antihypertensive medications, an increased CRC risk was also observed. To evaluate whether genetically determined hypertension susceptibility mediated this risk, a polygenic risk score (PRS) analysis in the Japan Multi-Institutional Collaborative Cohort found no positive association between blood pressure PRS and CRC incidence, suggesting the epidemiological association may involve shared environmental factors rather than a genetic causal pathway. The discordance between observational epidemiology and Mendelian randomization signals underscores the need for further investigation into shared lifestyle, metabolic, or medication-related mechanisms linking hypertension and CRC.

Implications Medical Affairs : This study adds to accumulating evidence that hypertension patients face elevated CRC risk, an important comorbidity signal that should inform patient monitoring strategies and benefit-risk communications for antihypertensive therapies—particularly as some antihypertensive drug classes have been proposed to have either protective or deleterious cancer-related effects. The Immunopathology of Preeclampsia.

Revue : Biomedicines | Tier : NA | IF estime : NA

Date de publication : 2026-07-16

Resultats principaux : This comprehensive review examines the immune-mediated pathological mechanisms underlying preeclampsia, a hypertensive disorder of pregnancy affecting approximately 5% of all pregnancies and characterized by target organ damage.
