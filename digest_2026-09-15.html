[Expert consensus on initial combination lipid-lowering therapy in patients with type 2 diabetes mellitus and hypertension (2026)]

Revue : Zhonghua Nei Ke Za Zhi | Tier : NA | IF estime : NA

Date de publication : 2026-09-01

Resultats principaux : This expert consensus document, developed collaboratively by Chinese cardiologists and endocrinologists, addresses the initial combination lipid-lowering strategy for patients with type 2 diabetes mellitus (T2DM) and hypertension — a population in which the co-prevalence of hypertension reaches 60 to 76%, substantially amplifying ASCVD risk beyond either condition alone. The consensus synthesizes recent evidence demonstrating that initiating combination lipid-lowering therapy (statin plus ezetimibe) compared with statin monotherapy achieves more rapid and significantly greater reductions in LDL-C while substantially lowering major cardiovascular event rates. PCSK9 inhibitors are recommended as an add-on for patients who fail to reach LDL-C targets on dual oral therapy, establishing a clear escalation algorithm for this high-risk population. For patients with concomitant hypertriglyceridemia, the addition of icosapent ethyl or fibrates is recommended to further reduce residual cardiovascular risk or pancreatitis risk, addressing the residual lipid-related burden not captured by LDL-C alone. The principal limitation is that this is a consensus statement based on expert synthesis of existing evidence rather than a de novo clinical trial, and its applicability to non-Chinese populations may be constrained by ethnic, dietary, and healthcare-system differences.

Implications Medical Affairs : This consensus reinforces the accelerating trend toward earlier and more aggressive lipid management in high-risk patients with dual cardiovascular risk factors, which may influence guideline updates in both cardiology and endocrinology beyond China and create pressure for harmonized targets in international recommendations. For pharmaceutical companies marketing PCSK9 inhibitors or ezetimibe-based combinations, this document represents an important positioning opportunity in a large underserved co-morbid population where monotherapy remains inadequate and combination use is now expert-endorsed. Medical Affairs teams should prioritize dual engagement of cardiologist and endocrinologist KOLs, leverage this consensus in real-world evidence generation programs, and develop educational content that bridges the T2DM-hypertension lipid management gap for clinical practice optimization.

[Early outcomes of controllable pulmonary thromboendarterectomy for chronic thromboembolic pulmonary hypertension: a single-centre experience at the Almazov National Medical Research Centre]

Revue : Khirurgiia (Mosk) | Tier : NA | IF estime : NA

Date de publication : 2026

Resultats principaux : This single-center retrospective-prospective cohort study from the Almazov National Medical Research Centre in Russia evaluated early hemodynamic, clinical, and functional outcomes of a novel controllable pulmonary thromboendarterectomy (PEA) technique in 74 CTEPH patients operated between 2018 and 2024 using deep hypothermic circulatory arrest and a patented intraoperative completeness-assessment method. The primary endpoint — greater than 50% reduction in mean pulmonary artery pressure (mPAP) and pulmonary vascular resistance (PVR) — was broadly achieved: mPAP decreased by a median of 62% at ICU discharge, and PVR dropped dramatically from 11.45 Wood units to 1.95 Wood units (p less than 0.001). Residual pulmonary hypertension (mPAP ≥25 mmHg and PVR ≥2.5 Wood units) was observed in only 12.3% of patients, and 85.7% achieved NYHA functional class I-II, with six-minute walk distance improving from 347 to 420 m (p less than 0.001). Complication rates were notable — reperfusion pulmonary edema in 41%, pneumonia in 22.2%, reintubation in 8.1%, and stroke in 4.1% — while in-hospital mortality was 1.4% (one patient, due to generalized infection with multi-organ failure), consistent with outcomes at international expert centers. The main limitations include the single-center design, the retrospective component of the study, and the relatively small sample size of 74 patients, which limits statistical power for subgroup analyses and generalizability.

Implications Medical Affairs : This study strengthens the evidence base supporting surgical PEA as a highly effective treatment for CTEPH with outcomes comparable to international expert centers, potentially influencing the positioning of surgery versus pharmacological therapy in multidisciplinary treatment algorithms. From a Medical Affairs perspective focused on pulmonary hypertension, these results reinforce the centrality of operability assessment as a critical decision node before initiating targeted PAH pharmacotherapy, with direct implications for positioning riociguat or selexipag in the inoperable-CTEPH segment. KOL engagement should be prioritized with thoracic surgery and pulmonary hypertension centers of excellence to generate multidisciplinary advisory content and support registry-based evidence on the real-world complement between surgical and pharmacological management.

Theme 6 : Clinical Trials and Guidelines (1 article)

Tenecteplase versus standard care for minor ischaemic stroke according to the baseline occlusion status in TEMPO-2 trial

Revue : European Stroke Journal | Tier : NA | IF estime : NA

Date de publication : 2026-09-05

Resultats principaux : This prespecified secondary analysis of the randomized, controlled, blinded TEMPO-2 trial (NCT02398656) enrolled 881 patients with minor ischemic stroke (NIHSS score 5 or less) presenting with intracranial occlusion or focal perfusion abnormality within 12 hours of symptom onset, randomized to intravenous tenecteplase versus non-thrombolytic standard care. Patients were stratified by baseline vessel occlusion status into three subgroups: complete occlusion (218 control vs. 216 tenecteplase), near occlusion (102 vs. 92), and no visible occlusion with focal perfusion abnormality (130 vs. 123). The primary outcome — return to pre-stroke neurological functioning or better at 90 days, measured by modified Rankin Scale — showed no benefit of tenecteplase over standard care in any subgroup: adjusted RR 1.00 (95% CI 0.89-1.12) for complete occlusion, 0.85 (0.71-1.01) for near occlusion, and 1.03 (0.90-1.18) for no visible occlusion. Baseline imbalances were noted, including higher rates of hypertension and older age in the tenecteplase arm with complete occlusion, and longer onset-to-randomisation times in the control arm with near occlusion, which were adjusted for in analyses. The main limitations are the secondary and post-hoc nature of the subgroup analysis, residual confounding from baseline imbalances, and insufficient power for definitive subgroup conclusions given the study was not designed for this stratification.

Implications Medical Affairs : The consistent absence of tenecteplase benefit across all vessel occlusion subgroups in minor ischemic stroke adds high-quality randomized evidence arguing against expanding thrombolysis indications in this low-severity population, reinforcing a conservative approach to guideline development for minor stroke management. From a Medical Affairs perspective, the finding that hypertension was more prevalent in the tenecteplase arm with complete occlusion (without modifying the null result) is relevant for labeling discussions and risk-benefit communications in hypertensive patients considered for thrombolysis. Future KOL strategy and investigator-initiated research should focus on the remaining unmet need in minor stroke — particularly exploring neuroprotective agents, antiplatelet optimization, or patient-selection biomarkers that might identify a responder subgroup within this heterogeneous population.

Theme 7 : Environment and Risk Factors (1 article)

Cardiometabolic Health in P'urépecha Migrant-Sending Communities in Michoacan, Mexico

Revue : American Journal of Human Biology | Tier : NA | IF estime : NA

Date de publication : 2026-09

Resultats principaux : This mixed-methods biocultural cross-sectional study assessed cardiometabolic risk among 193 never-migrant P'urépecha indigenous adults living in migrant-sending communities in Michoacan, Mexico, combining anthropometric measures, cardiometabolic biomarkers, survey data, and ethnographic observations of local food environments, with comparisons to national ENSANUT surveillance benchmarks. The prevalence of cardiometabolic risk was alarmingly high: 75% of participants were overweight or obese, 59% were hypertensive, 34% exhibited dysglycemia (HbA1c 5.7% or higher or medication use), and 77% met criteria for metabolic syndrome — rates broadly comparable to or exceeding national averages. In multivariable analyses, age and sex were consistently the primary determinants of cardiometabolic risk, while income, perceived stress, language proficiency, and cultural or ethnic identification were not independently associated with any cardiometabolic outcome. Notably, bivariate differences by language group (used as a proxy for cultural traditionalism) disappeared entirely after adjustment for age, suggesting that apparent cultural protective effects are largely attributable to age confounding rather than genuine cultural factors. The main limitations are the cross-sectional design preventing causal inference, the non-representative convenience sample from a single geographic region, and the reliance on self-reported stress and cultural measures, which limits generalizability to other indigenous or migrant-sending populations.

Implications Medical Affairs : This study critically challenges the assumption that pre-migration indigenous communities are metabolically protected, revealing that hypertension prevalence of 59% and metabolic syndrome prevalence of 77% are already embedded in these communities before any migration occurs — findings with major implications for dismantling migration-centric narratives in cardiometabolic epidemiology and for designing upstream, community-level interventions. For Medical Affairs, the extreme burden of hypertension in this indigenous population highlights a severely underserved and pharmacologically undertreated market segment in Latin America, where awareness campaigns and access programs for antihypertensive therapy could generate both public health impact and brand presence. KOL engagement with global health researchers, social medicine experts, and indigenous health advocates should be pursued to co-develop real-world evidence on treatment gaps and unmet needs in indigenous cardiometabolic populations across Latin America.
