Hidden hypertension and early cardiac alterations: insights from ambulatory blood pressure monitoring in children and adolescents.

Revue : Italian Journal of Pediatrics | Tier : NA | IF estime : NA

Date de publication : 2026-08-22

Resultats principaux : This cross-sectional study conducted in Egypt enrolled 354 schoolchildren: 234 primary Grade 3 students (aged 8-9 years) and 120 secondary Grade 9 students (aged approximately 15 years), with assessments including medical history, physical examination, and standardized blood pressure measurement. Clinic-based hypertension prevalence was 15.4% in Grade 3 and 12.5% in Grade 9, while obesity was markedly more prevalent in the older group, with 81.5% classified as overweight or obese compared to 22% in Grade 3. Ambulatory Blood Pressure Monitoring (ABPM) was performed in all students with elevated office BP, confirming sustained hypertension beyond white-coat effect and demonstrating significant correlations with BMI Z-scores and lifestyle factors. Notably, echocardiography revealed early cardiac remodeling in asymptomatic children across both groups, with mean interventricular septal thickness of 0.75 cm in Grade 3 compared to 1.07 cm in Grade 9, indicating progressive structural changes associated with age and increasing adiposity. Key limitations include the single-country, school-based sampling frame in Egypt limiting generalizability, the cross-sectional design which prevents causal inference, and the relatively small sample size of the Grade 9 subgroup.

Implications Medical Affairs : These findings reinforce the urgent need to integrate pediatric blood pressure screening with ABPM confirmation into school health programs, as the identification of subclinical cardiac remodeling in asymptomatic children with hypertension represents a substantial long-term cardiovascular burden that could influence guideline recommendations toward earlier and more rigorous diagnostic protocols. From a Medical Affairs perspective, this data strengthens the case for positioning antihypertensive and lifestyle-focused interventions earlier in the patient journey, potentially expanding awareness of pediatric hypertension as an addressable target population for existing lifestyle and pharmacological therapies. Engaging pediatric cardiologists and public health KOLs around school-based screening programs and ABPM standardization in children with elevated office blood pressure would represent a high-impact advocacy and disease awareness strategy.

Theme 5 : Diuretics and Beta-blockers (1 article)

Shoshin beriberi with severe lactic acidosis and haemodynamic collapse due to long-term diuretic therapy.

Revue : BMJ Case Reports | Tier : NA | IF estime : NA

Date de publication : 2026-08-25

Resultats principaux : This case report describes a patient receiving long-term diuretic therapy for pulmonary arterial hypertension who presented with acute hemodynamic instability, cardiovascular collapse, and profound high anion gap metabolic acidosis with severe lactic acidosis refractory to standard resuscitative measures including fluid administration, vasopressor support, and mechanical ventilation. Comprehensive workup systematically excluded common causes of severe lactic acidosis, including sepsis, hypoxia, diabetic ketoacidosis, acute pulmonary embolism, and mesenteric ischemia, leaving thiamine deficiency (Shoshin beriberi) as the primary clinical suspicion given the history of prolonged diuretic use. Empirical intravenous thiamine administration led to rapid, dramatic, and sustained correction of the acid-base disturbance and hemodynamic stabilization within hours, enabling progressive vasopressor tapering. The case highlights that diuretic-induced thiamine depletion, while well-described in heart failure literature, remains clinically under-recognized and potentially fatal if not considered promptly in the differential diagnosis of cardiovascular collapse. Key limitations include the case report design which prevents causal confirmation, the absence of pre-treatment serum thiamine levels limiting biochemical validation of the deficiency, and restriction to a single patient case preventing generalizability of incidence estimates.

Implications Medical Affairs : This report highlights a life-threatening but under-recognized safety signal associated with chronic diuretic therapy, specifically thiamine depletion leading to Shoshin beriberi with cardiovascular collapse, which is directly relevant for Medical Affairs safety monitoring across diuretic-treated populations including hypertension, heart failure, and pulmonary arterial hypertension. From a product safety and Medical Affairs standpoint, this case strengthens the rationale for developing physician education initiatives on thiamine status monitoring and supplementation in patients on long-term diuretics, particularly those with nutritional vulnerability, representing an opportunity to differentiate through patient safety programs and risk minimization materials. Proactive KOL engagement with cardiology, nephrology, and intensive care specialists around thiamine monitoring protocols in diuretic-treated populations, combined with advocacy for incorporation of screening recommendations into clinical practice guidelines, would be both scientifically aligned and strategically valuable.

Metabolic Syndrome: From Mechanisms to Therapeutic Interventions.

Revue : MedComm | Tier : NA | IF estime : NA

Date de publication : 2026-08-24

Resultats principaux : This comprehensive narrative review synthesizes current knowledge on the pathophysiology and therapeutic management of metabolic syndrome (MetS), a cluster of metabolic conditions encompassing obesity, hypertension, dyslipidemia, insulin resistance, chronic kidney disease, and metabolic dysfunction-associated steatotic liver disease that has emerged as a major global health burden. The authors systematically characterize central mechanistic drivers of MetS including insulin resistance, chronic low-grade inflammation, oxidative stress, and epigenetic modifications, with a dedicated focus on the gut-adipose tissue axis involving adipocyte-derived extracellular vesicles, adipokines, gut microbiota, and their secreted metabolites (LPS, short-chain fatty acids, bile acids, endocannabinoids, aryl hydrocarbon receptor ligands, and tryptophan derivatives). Established pharmacological interventions reviewed include GLP-1 receptor agonists, SGLT2 inhibitors, and RAAS inhibitors as foundational therapies, while emerging strategies target the gut-adipose axis through prebiotics, probiotics, synbiotics, fecal microbiota transplantation, bariatric surgery, CB1R antagonists, and novel pipeline agents. Subgroup analyses are not applicable to this review design, though the authors identify gaps in evidence regarding long-term outcomes of gut-targeted therapies in MetS. Key limitations include the narrative rather than systematic methodology and absence of quantitative meta-analytic synthesis, which limits the strength of comparative efficacy conclusions.

Implications Medical Affairs : This review reinforces the growing scientific consensus that hypertension should be managed within an integrated cardiometabolic syndrome framework rather than in isolation, supporting the strategic positioning of RAAS inhibitors as cornerstone therapy alongside SGLT2 inhibitors and GLP-1 receptor agonists in the evolving cardiometabolic treatment landscape. From a Medical Affairs standpoint, the detailed coverage of gut microbiota and gut-adipose axis mechanisms provides a scientifically substantive basis for developing educational programs targeting internists, endocrinologists, and cardiologists on comprehensive metabolic risk management beyond blood pressure control alone. The emphasis on emerging therapies targeting gut-adipose crosstalk signals priority research directions that Medical Affairs teams should monitor closely for early-engagement opportunities with KOLs and potential alignment with investigator-initiated research programs.

Revue : Urolithiasis | Tier : NA | IF estime : NA

Date de publication : 2026-08-25

Resultats principaux : This case-control study from the Swiss Kidney Stone Cohort enrolled 232 patients with recurrent kidney stone disease and 168 healthy controls, applying untargeted urinary volatile organic compound (VOC) profiling by dynamic headspace vacuum in-tube extraction gas chromatography-mass spectrometry (DHS-V-ITEX-GC-MS) to 24-hour urine samples to identify candidate biomarkers. Analyses adjusted for study center, sex, age, BMI, smoking, and diabetes status identified 77 metabolites differing significantly between stone formers and controls, with octane (1,1'-oxybis-) and 6-methyl-5-hepten-2-one present at higher levels in controls and acetic acid and propanoic acid elevated in kidney stone patients. Three discriminative VOCs were associated with fermented food consumption, with 3-nonen-2-one emerging as a potential mediator of the inverse relationship between coffee intake and kidney stone risk. No direct primary endpoints related to hypertension were assessed, as the study focused on nephrolithiasis biomarker discovery and dietary pattern associations. Limitations include the cross-sectional design limiting causal inference, single-country facility-based sampling, and the exploratory nature of untargeted metabolomics requiring external validation.

Implications Medical Affairs : While this study does not directly investigate hypertension, the identification of diet-linked urinary VOC biomarkers in kidney stone patients is indirectly relevant to Medical Affairs teams given the well-established bidirectional association between nephrolithiasis and hypertension risk within the cardiorenal comorbidity spectrum. Association of Adherence to Various Dietary Patterns With Mortality Among Survivors of Myocardial Infarction: A Prospective Cohort Study.

Revue : Circulation | Tier : 1 | IF estime : > 20

Date de publication : 2026-08-25

Resultats principaux : This large prospective cohort study included 3,277 women from the Nurses' Health Study and 2,618 men from the Health Professionals Follow-Up Study who survived a nonfatal myocardial infarction (MI), with a mean age at MI diagnosis of 68.2 years (SD 10.2), aiming to evaluate eight established healthy dietary pattern scores in relation to long-term post-MI mortality. Over 15.7 years of follow-up, 3,858 deaths (65.4%) were documented including 1,639 cardiovascular deaths (42.5%), and higher adherence to all eight dietary quality scores was consistently associated with lower total mortality; hazard ratios for the highest versus lowest quintile ranged from 0.66 (95% CI 0.59-0.75) for the Alternate Mediterranean Diet Score to 0.77 (95% CI 0.69-0.86) for the DASH score, with similar inverse associations for cardiovascular mortality and recurrent nonfatal MI. Improved dietary adherence after MI relative to before the event was independently associated with lower mortality (HR 0.86, 95% CI 0.76-0.97), while worsening adherence post-MI correlated with higher mortality for most patterns. Subgroup consistency was observed across sex and age strata, though the study population was predominantly White health professionals, limiting generalizability to more diverse populations. Key limitations include observational design subject to residual confounding, potential dietary assessment error through self-reported food frequency questionnaires, and lack of randomization preventing definitive causal attribution.

Implications Medical Affairs : This Tier 1 publication in Circulation provides compelling evidence from two major US cohorts that diet quality, including adherence to the DASH pattern specifically designed to reduce hypertension, is a robust and modifiable determinant of post-MI survival, reinforcing dietary counseling as a critical pillar of evidence-based secondary cardiovascular prevention guidelines. From a Medical Affairs perspective, the consistent mortality benefit across all eight dietary patterns strengthens the scientific rationale for positioning antihypertensive drug therapy within a comprehensive lifestyle modification framework, providing high-quality evidence to support educational campaigns for cardiologists, internists, and general practitioners on integrated secondary prevention. The prominence of this publication in Circulation represents a high-priority opportunity to engage cardiovascular and preventive medicine KOLs around combined pharmacological and dietary intervention programs for MI survivors with hypertension, including potential alignment with real-world evidence generation strategies.

Resultats principaux : This facility-based cross-sectional study enrolled 398 HIV-positive patients receiving antiretroviral therapy (ART) at two tertiary hospitals in Addis Ababa, Ethiopia, using systematic random sampling between November and December 2025, with a median participant age of 50 years (IQR 42-56) and 66.8% female representation. Limitations include the cross-sectional design, restriction to tertiary care facilities introducing selection bias toward more complex patients, single-country setting in Ethiopia limiting regional generalizability, and reliance on single-timepoint fasting lipid measurements.

Antihypertensive effect of acupuncture involve reshaping gut microbiota and alleviating gut inflammation in spontaneously hypertensive rats.

Revue : Journal of Traditional Chinese Medicine | Tier : NA | IF estime : NA

Date de publication : 2026-08

Resultats principaux : This preclinical experimental study evaluated the antihypertensive mechanism of acupuncture at Renying (ST9) and Zusanli (ST36) acupoints using 18 male spontaneously hypertensive rats (SHRs) divided into SHR control, acupuncture (SHR+A), sham acupuncture (SHR+SA), and six normotensive Wistar-Kyoto rats as controls, with multiple validated endpoints including 16S rRNA gene sequencing for gut microbiota profiling, ELISA for inflammatory cytokines, hematoxylin-eosin staining for colonic damage assessment, and Western blotting for intestinal inflammation markers. Acupuncture at the two acupoints reduced blood pressure in SHRs, modulated gut microbial beta diversity, and increased the abundance of beneficial gut bacterial genera compared to untreated SHRs, demonstrating a reshaped intestinal microbiota composition. At the molecular level, acupuncture decreased serum inflammatory cytokine levels and inhibited the TLR4/NF-kappaB signaling pathway in intestinal tissue, thereby restoring intestinal barrier integrity that was disrupted by hypertension-induced pathological changes. Sham acupuncture did not replicate these effects, supporting acupoint-specificity of the intervention, though the study does not quantify blood pressure reduction numerically in the abstract. Primary limitations include the very small sample size (n=6 per group), exclusive reliance on an animal model precluding direct clinical translation, use of a single hypertensive rat strain that may not capture the heterogeneity of human hypertension, and absence of pharmacological comparator arms.

Implications Medical Affairs : This preclinical study contributes mechanistic evidence linking gut microbiota modulation to blood pressure reduction via TLR4/NF-kappaB pathway inhibition, advancing the scientific rationale for the gut-heart axis as a potential therapeutic target in hypertension and representing a research space that Medical Affairs teams should monitor for future clinical translation. Date de publication : 2026-07-18

Resultats principaux : This narrative review from the All India Institute of Medical Sciences Bibinagar systematically examines the epidemiological and mechanistic evidence linking bacteria, viruses, and the gut microbiome to cardiovascular disease (CVD) pathogenesis, with CVDs identified as the leading global cause of morbidity and mortality. The review characterizes four primary pathways through which microbial agents contribute to cardiovascular risk: chronic inflammation, molecular mimicry, endothelial dysfunction, and direct microbial invasion of cardiovascular tissues, contextualized within the established CVD risk factor framework that includes hypertension, diabetes, and lifestyle behaviors. Recent study data are synthesized to illustrate specific associations between microbial dysbiosis and cardiovascular conditions including atherosclerosis, heart failure, and arrhythmias, though causal directionality remains challenging to establish. Specific pathogens (including Chlamydia pneumoniae, Helicobacter pylori, herpesviruses) are discussed in relation to endothelial injury and plaque destabilization, representing potential targets for preventive or therapeutic intervention. Limitations include the narrative rather than systematic review methodology, significant heterogeneity across included studies, absence of meta-analytic pooling of effect estimates, and predominantly cross-sectional primary evidence, limiting the ability to draw definitive causal or quantitative conclusions.
