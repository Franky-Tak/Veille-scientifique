Maternal weight and hypertension as risk factors for juvenile idiopathic arthritis: a prospective population-based pregnancy cohort study.

Revue : RMD Open | Tier : NA | IF estime : NA

Date de publication : 2026-07-30

Resultats principaux : This prospective population-based cohort study linked the Norwegian Mother, Father and Child Cohort Study (MoBa, 78,901 eligible children) to the Norwegian Patient Registry, identifying 265 children who developed juvenile idiopathic arthritis (JIA), with all children aged at least 14 years at end of follow-up. Each 1 standard deviation (~6 kg) increase in gestational weight gain (GWG) was independently associated with modestly higher JIA odds (aOR 1.12, 95% CI 1.00-1.26), with stronger effects among women with prepregnancy obesity (aOR 1.40, 95% CI 1.10-1.77). Maternal hypertension during pregnancy was associated with a 43% higher JIA risk in offspring (aOR 1.43, 95% CI 1.02-1.99), and pre-eclampsia was associated with a 61% higher risk (aOR 1.61, 95% CI 1.10-2.37), both statistically significant. Causal mediation analysis showed hypertension did not mediate the GWG-JIA association (p>0.05), indicating these are independent pathways. In the genotyped subsample (n=44,245), among children with a lower JIA polygenic risk score, maternal obesity was paradoxically associated with a lower JIA risk (aOR 0.37, 95% CI 0.17-0.80), suggesting that genetic susceptibility modifies the effect of maternal metabolic exposures on offspring immune development. Key limitations include the observational design with potential residual confounding, registry-based JIA identification that may miss milder cases, and modest absolute case numbers (265 JIA cases across a very large cohort).

Implications Medical Affairs : Hypertensive disorders of pregnancy are now linked to increased offspring risk of immune-mediated disease, substantially expanding the clinical significance of maternal blood pressure management beyond cardiovascular endpoints and elevating the importance of antihypertensive treatment in pregnancy from a Medical Affairs perspective. This finding opens a new narrative for positioning antihypertensive therapies: effective blood pressure control during pregnancy may confer long-term pediatric health benefits, an angle relevant to label expansion discussions and health economic modeling for maternal-fetal outcomes. KOL strategy should actively engage maternal-fetal medicine specialists and pediatric rheumatologists as emerging stakeholders, and companies should support prospective studies examining whether optimal antihypertensive control during pregnancy reduces offspring autoimmune disease risk.

"I left my country and the people I love. It gave me hypertension": a qualitative study of social support and hypertension management in refugees.

Revue : Frontiers in Public Health | Tier : NA | IF estime : NA

Date de publication : 2026-07-15

Resultats principaux : This mixed-methods study recruited 107 refugees from Iraq (n=84) and Syria (n=23) resettled in San Diego, California, all diagnosed with hypertension, from a federally qualified health center in a refugee-majority neighborhood; mean age was 61.61 years (SD 9.81), with 48.6% female participants. In quantitative surveys, 60.7% reported social disconnection and 61.7% reported annual income below $15,000, indicating severe social and economic vulnerability in this cohort. Qualitative in-depth interviews with 54 participants (to thematic saturation) identified three domains of social support impacting hypertension management: informational support (reliance on close networks for health information due to distrust of formal systems), instrumental support (deficits including lack of transportation and language barriers in clinical settings), and emotional support (losses including disruption of social networks and social status, generating chronic psychosocial stress). Participants directly attributed hypertension onset and poor control to the stress of displacement, family separation, and social isolation, highlighting the psychosocial pathway to cardiovascular risk. The study's cross-sectional quantitative component and convenience sampling from a single city limit generalizability, and self-reported hypertension management data introduces recall bias. Authors recommend integrating family reunification support, community-based peer programs, shared medical appointments, and family-centered care into refugee hypertension management strategies.

Implications Medical Affairs : Hypertension disparities in refugee populations are driven by structural social determinants rather than biological factors alone, underscoring the need for Medical Affairs to incorporate health equity frameworks into evidence generation and market access strategies for antihypertensive products. Adherence-support programs targeting refugees and other migrant populations must address language access, transportation, and community integration to be effective, creating opportunities to position antihypertensive products within integrated care models. KOL partnerships with community health center networks and refugee health specialists would strengthen the evidence base for real-world adherence in underserved populations and position the company as a health equity leader with payers and policy makers.

Interpretable machine learning for identifying determinants of high hypertension burden under extreme heat vulnerability: evidence from Maryland, USA.

Revue : Frontiers in Public Health | Tier : NA | IF estime : NA

Date de publication : 2026-07-15

Resultats principaux : This cross-sectional ecological study applied an interpretable machine learning framework (XGBoost with SHAP explanations) to classify 1,385 census tracts in Maryland as high or low hypertension burden in the context of heat vulnerability, using demographic, socioeconomic, built-environment, heat anomaly, and adaptive-capacity variables. The model achieved strong predictive performance with an accuracy of 0.819, balanced accuracy of 0.806, ROC-AUC of 0.821, and PR-AUC of 0.909, validating XGBoost as a suitable classifier for this public health application. SHAP analyses identified African-American population share, older-adult population share, and low educational attainment as the three strongest predictors of high hypertension burden, followed by summer maximum air temperature, non-vegetated land area, and lack of household air conditioning. Dependence plots revealed non-linear, threshold-like relationships: predicted hypertension risk increases sharply beyond certain levels of demographic vulnerability, educational disadvantage, heat exposure, and limited cooling access, rather than increasing linearly. Spatially, high-burden tracts were concentrated in Baltimore City, Prince George's County, southern Maryland, western Maryland, and parts of the Eastern Shore, indicating geographic clustering of cardiovascular risk. The ecological cross-sectional design precludes causal inference at the individual level, and the study is limited to Maryland, potentially limiting national generalizability.

Implications Medical Affairs : Environmental heat exposure, particularly in the absence of cooling infrastructure, is now quantified as a significant and non-linearly acting driver of hypertension burden at the census-tract level, signaling that climate change will progressively increase the hypertensive disease burden in vulnerable communities. Medical Affairs should actively engage with public health authorities, payers, and health systems serving climate-vulnerable populations to position antihypertensive therapies within broader heat health resilience strategies, particularly for elderly Black patients in urban heat islands. Future real-world evidence studies for antihypertensive products should incorporate climate and environmental vulnerability metrics as covariates to demonstrate differential benefit in high-risk geographic areas, strengthening value propositions for formulary inclusion and market access.

A novel nutritional index for hypertension risk and prediction: Cross-sectional, prospective, and trajectory evidence from the English Longitudinal Study of Ageing.

Revue : The Journal of International Medical Research | Tier : NA | IF estime : NA

Date de publication : 2026-07-30

Resultats principaux : This longitudinal cohort study analyzed data from 5,731 middle-aged and older adults in the English Longitudinal Study of Ageing (ELSA), examining the association of a novel triglyceride-total cholesterol-body weight (TCW) index with hypertension through cross-sectional, prospective, and trajectory analyses. In cross-sectional analysis, each one-unit increase in log-transformed TCW index was associated with 43% higher odds of hypertension (adjusted OR 1.43, 95% CI 1.31-1.55), indicating a robust cross-sectional relationship. In the prospective analysis (participants without baseline hypertension followed for a median of 12 years), higher baseline TCW index predicted incident hypertension (adjusted HR 1.16, 95% CI 1.05-1.28), establishing a temporal relationship between this metabolic index and hypertension development. Trajectory analysis identified three long-term TCW index patterns (low, moderate, high), demonstrating a graded increase in hypertension risk: the high-trajectory group had 69% higher odds of hypertension compared to the low-trajectory group (adjusted OR 1.69, 95% CI 1.37-2.09, p<0.001). Propensity score matching and subgroup analyses were conducted to assess robustness of findings, adding analytic rigor to this observational study. Key limitations include potential confounding by unmeasured dietary and lifestyle factors, reliance on self-reported hypertension status in some analyses, and the absence of external validation in independent cohorts.

Implications Medical Affairs : The TCW index, derived from routinely collected clinical measurements (triglycerides, total cholesterol, body weight), offers a simple and pragmatic metabolic risk stratification tool that could identify patients at high hypertension risk before diagnosis, supporting earlier antihypertensive intervention. Medical Affairs can leverage this index as a patient profiling tool in disease awareness campaigns and in value demonstration strategies for antihypertensive agents used in primary prevention, particularly in cardio-metabolic risk management settings. Future research should prospectively validate the TCW index in diverse populations and evaluate whether TCW-guided antihypertensive initiation improves long-term blood pressure control and reduces cardiovascular events, potentially supporting guideline inclusion.

Integrating Multimodal Data and Genomics for a Comprehensive Assessment of Cardiovascular Aging and Its Impact.

Revue : Journal of Cardiovascular Magnetic Resonance | Tier : NA | IF estime : NA

Date de publication : 2026-07-30

Resultats principaux : This large UK Biobank study integrated cardiovascular magnetic resonance, electrocardiogram, arterial stiffness, and carotid ultrasound biomarkers across 22,452 participants with complete multimodal data; 13,694 free of baseline cardiovascular disease (median age 62.6 years, median follow-up 4.8 years) were selected for model development. A CatBoost machine learning model was trained on 57 cardiovascular aging biomarkers and achieved the best predictive performance (Pearson r=0.75, mean absolute error 3.88 years), allowing calculation of an individual cardiovascular age gap (CardioAG) as a measure of biological cardiovascular aging. CardioAG was independently associated with hypertension, stroke, atrial fibrillation, coronary artery disease, and composite major adverse cardiovascular events (MACE, HR 1.08, 95% CI 1.06-1.10), demonstrating that accelerated cardiovascular aging predicts hard clinical outcomes. Whole-genome sequencing analysis identified novel genetic variants associated with inter-individual variability in CardioAG, including RN7SKP155, SVIL, and CBFA2T3, implicating vascular remodeling, electrophysiological regulation, and cardiovascular functional reserve in biological aging. Lifestyle factors including diet, sleep duration, physical activity, smoking, and alcohol intake were all significantly associated with CardioAG, confirming modifiable lifestyle pathways to cardiovascular aging. Limitations include potential selection bias in UK Biobank participants (healthier volunteer bias), the relatively short median follow-up of 4.8 years for hard outcomes, and observational study design precluding causal inference.

Implications Medical Affairs : Hypertension emerges as an independent driver of accelerated cardiovascular aging in one of the largest multimodal imaging-genetics cohorts assembled to date, providing robust evidence to support antihypertensive treatment as an intervention that may slow biological cardiovascular aging beyond blood pressure reduction alone. Medical Affairs can leverage the cardiovascular age gap concept as an emerging composite endpoint of interest to KOLs in precision cardiology, positioning antihypertensive agents within a biological aging framework that resonates with specialist audiences and payers increasingly focused on biomarker-driven outcomes. The identification of genetic modulators of cardiovascular aging creates an opportunity for pharmacogenomic research to identify patient subgroups deriving the greatest cardiovascular protection from antihypertensive therapy, supporting personalized treatment positioning strategies.

Urinary proteomic signature of exercise intolerance in patients at risk of heart failure: evidence from the HOMAGE trial.

Revue : EBioMedicine | Tier : NA | IF estime : NA

Date de publication : 2026-07-30

Resultats principaux : This substudy of the HOMAGE trial enrolled 268 patients at risk of heart failure who underwent the incremental shuttle walk test and urinary proteomic profiling (UPP) by capillary electrophoresis-mass spectrometry at baseline and at the 9-month final visit, enabling both discovery and replication analyses. Sequencing of 1,498 urinary peptides identified 170 non-collagen and 40 collagen-derived proteins; 29 non-collagen and 31 collagen-derived peptides were significantly associated with reduced exercise capacity after correction for multiple testing. Elastic net regression (with bootstrapping over 1,000 runs) retained FXYD2, GAPDH, HBB, KCNB1, MB, MYOCD, SECTM1, SNX9, TACC3, TMSB4X, and TTN as key non-collagen proteins, alongside collagens COL5A1, COL6A1, COL11A2, and COL28A1, in more than 50% of bootstrap runs. Pathway analysis of replicated proteins highlighted enrichment in oxygen homeostasis, oxidative stress regulation, metabolic and developmental processes, and muscle biology pathways, implicating systemic and tissue-level dysfunction in exercise intolerance. These findings suggest that exercise intolerance in patients at risk of heart failure reflects simultaneous dysfunction in oxygenation, vascular and muscle structure and function, circulating volume maintenance, and oxidative stress protection. Limitations include the exploratory hypothesis-generating nature of the study, relatively small sample size (n=268), and the fact that replication occurred within the same trial population rather than an independent external cohort.

Implications Medical Affairs : This study establishes a urinary proteomic signature of exercise intolerance in hypertensive patients at risk of heart failure, identifying candidate biomarkers that could serve as mechanistic endpoints in future trials of antihypertensive therapies in the heart failure prevention setting. Medical Affairs should track the development of proteomic biomarker panels as potential surrogate endpoints in hypertension-to-heart-failure prevention trials, which could accelerate regulatory approval timelines for antihypertensive agents with cardioprotective claims. Collaborative engagement with HOMAGE investigators would position the company at the forefront of biomarker-driven cardiovascular prevention research, supporting differentiated messaging around antihypertensive mechanisms beyond blood pressure lowering.

Chrononutrition, Circadian Rhythms, and Genetic Susceptibility in Noncommunicable Diseases: Sex-Specific Perspective.

Revue : Nutrition Reviews | Tier : NA | IF estime : NA

Date de publication : 2026-07-29

Resultats principaux : This narrative review synthesized 112 studies (randomized trials, observational studies, systematic reviews, meta-analyses, Mendelian randomization, and mechanistic investigations) published between 2006 and 2026 via PubMed, Scopus, and Web of Science, examining how chrononutrition, sleep, circadian rhythms, and genetic susceptibility interact to influence obesity, diabetes, and hypertension. Sleep deprivation, obstructive sleep apnea, and circadian misalignment were consistently associated with insulin resistance, appetite dysregulation, elevated blood pressure, and increased cardiometabolic risk, particularly in shift workers, with hormonal mediators including leptin, ghrelin, cortisol, and melatonin. Sex-specific differences were prominent: women showed greater vulnerability to sleep-related appetite disturbances, while men had higher prevalence of visceral adiposity, obstructive sleep apnea, and hypertension, with evidence of different hormonal and genomic pathways driving these divergent presentations. Chrononutrition interventions including time-restricted eating and Mediterranean dietary patterns demonstrated beneficial effects on sleep quality, glycemic control, systemic inflammation, and overall metabolic health across multiple study designs. Emerging bidirectional interactions between gut microbiota rhythmicity and circadian regulation were highlighted as a novel area with implications for cardiometabolic disease prevention. Key limitations include the narrative review design (susceptible to selection bias), heterogeneity of included studies, and limited ability to draw firm causal conclusions across study types.

Implications Medical Affairs : Circadian disruption and meal timing are now substantiated as modifiable environmental risk factors for hypertension, supporting Medical Affairs positioning of antihypertensive therapies as components of comprehensive circadian-aware cardiometabolic prevention programs rather than standalone blood pressure interventions. The pronounced sex-specific differences in hypertension risk driven by sleep and circadian factors underscore the importance of gender-stratified analyses in antihypertensive clinical trials and real-world evidence studies, a gap that may also represent a regulatory differentiation opportunity. KOL engagement with chronobiology, sleep medicine, and nutrition specialists represents a strategic Medical Affairs opportunity to expand the antihypertensive treatment narrative into circadian medicine, particularly for shift workers and patients with obstructive sleep apnea who carry disproportionate hypertension burden.

Artificial intelligence in cardiology: implications for healthcare outcomes.

Revue : Frontiers in Artificial Intelligence | Tier : NA | IF estime : NA

Date de publication : 2026-07-15

Resultats principaux : This review examined the integration of artificial intelligence and precision medicine in cardiovascular care, focusing on persistent health disparities affecting racial, ethnic, and gender groups across both younger adults (<65 years) and older adults (>=65 years) with cardiovascular disease in the United States. Black patients experience disproportionately higher prevalence of heart failure and hypertension compared to White patients, with transthyretin amyloid cardiomyopathy-related heart failure particularly prevalent and Black women most severely affected due to compounding structural, environmental, and clinical risk factors. Racially and ethnically diverse children with cardiovascular diseases have significantly higher mortality odds than White counterparts, extending disparity findings into pediatric cardiology. The authors systematically demonstrate that AI models and precision medicine tools developed primarily from White population datasets risk embedding historical care disparities into clinical decision-support systems, perpetuating inequitable treatment recommendations at scale. Factors including genetic predisposition differences, limited care access, health literacy gaps, lack of insurance, and suboptimal adherence are identified as major contributors to adverse outcomes that remain underresearched and unresolved even with Medicaid expansion. Limitations include the narrative review format without quantitative synthesis, selective framing of the literature, and absence of a formal quality assessment of included studies.

Implications Medical Affairs : The underrepresentation of racially and ethnically diverse populations in AI cardiovascular training datasets mirrors systemic gaps in antihypertensive clinical trial diversity that are under increasing regulatory scrutiny from the FDA and EMA, making health equity a strategic Medical Affairs priority. Medical Affairs should proactively support and publicize inclusive trial designs for hypertension studies and invest in real-world evidence generation in underrepresented communities to strengthen label claims, close evidence gaps, and fortify market access arguments with equity-conscious payers. Strategic KOL engagement with academic institutions historically serving diverse populations, including historically Black colleges and universities and safety-net hospitals, would position the company as a health equity leader in hypertension and cardiovascular disease management.

Changes in ultra-processed food consumption or MIND diet adherence and cognitive impairment.

Revue : Alzheimer's & Dementia (Amsterdam) | Tier : NA | IF estime : NA

Date de publication : 2026-03-22

Resultats principaux : This longitudinal analysis used data from the REGARDS (Reasons for Geographic and Racial Differences in Stroke) study, enrolling 7,555 Black and White adults aged 45 years or older from 2003 to 2007, with dietary reassessment at a second visit from 2013 to 2016 and subsequent cognitive impairment assessment using annual Six-Item Screeners. A 10% increase in ultra-processed food (UPF) consumption was significantly associated with higher cognitive impairment risk by percentage of grams (HR 1.24, 95% CI 1.12-1.37, p<0.001) and by percentage of calories (HR 1.15, 95% CI 1.06-1.25, p<0.001), establishing a dose-response relationship. Each 1-point increase in the MIND diet score (Mediterranean-Dietary Approaches to Stop Hypertension Intervention for Neurodegenerative Delay, combining Mediterranean and DASH dietary principles) was associated with 10% lower cognitive impairment risk (HR 0.90, 95% CI 0.85-0.96, p=0.002). The study specifically captured dietary change over time rather than single-point dietary status, thereby capturing the impact of dietary trajectory on cognitive health in a racially diverse cohort with high hypertension prevalence. Subgroup analyses supported the robustness of the associations across racial groups, though the study was not powered for formal interaction testing. Limitations include reliance on food frequency questionnaires (subject to recall and social desirability bias), use of a brief cognitive screener rather than neuropsychological assessment, and observational design with residual confounding from unmeasured lifestyle factors.

Implications Medical Affairs : The DASH dietary pattern, central to both hypertension management guidelines and the MIND diet, is confirmed as protective against cognitive decline in a population with high hypertension burden, directly linking antihypertensive lifestyle recommendations to neurological health outcomes. Medical Affairs can use this evidence to support a broader narrative that antihypertensive lifestyle adherence, particularly DASH diet compliance, delivers value beyond blood pressure reduction to include cognitive protection, strengthening health economic arguments for comprehensive hypertension management programs. Future research should prospectively evaluate whether antihypertensive pharmacotherapy combined with structured DASH dietary interventions produces additive or synergistic benefits on cognitive outcomes, providing a compelling combined therapy value proposition for aging hypertensive patient segments.
