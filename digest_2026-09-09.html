Association of the maternal fish and PUFA intake with the risk of hypertensive disorders of pregnancy: a nationwide birth cohort-The Japan Environment and Children's Study (JECS).

Revue : European Journal of Nutrition | Tier : NA | IF estime : NA

Date de publication : 2026-09-08

Resultats principaux : This large prospective birth cohort study enrolled 86,009 women from the Japan Environment and Children's Study (JECS) and assessed dietary fish and omega-3/omega-6 PUFA intake using a validated food-frequency questionnaire at two timepoints: before/early pregnancy (median gestational age 15.0 weeks) and mid-to-late pregnancy (median 27.0 weeks). The primary endpoints were the adjusted odds ratios (aORs) for hypertensive disorders of pregnancy (HDP) overall, early-onset HDP (Eo-HDP, less than 32 weeks), and late-onset HDP (Lo-HDP, at or after 32 weeks), estimated by logistic regression with multiple imputation for missing data. Higher pre-pregnancy fish intake was significantly associated with reduced risk of Eo-HDP in nulliparous women (Q4: aOR 0.57; 95% CI 0.34-0.97; p for trend 0.02), while higher fish intake during pregnancy was associated with reduced risk of Lo-HDP (Q4: aOR 0.79; 95% CI 0.64-0.96) and overall HDP. Subgroup analyses in nulliparous women showed more pronounced protective associations than in the overall population, suggesting that parity modifies the fish-HDP relationship; total n-3 PUFA intake showed associations with HDP only in some analyses, suggesting other fish constituents may contribute. Key limitations include the observational design precluding causal inference, self-reported dietary assessment via questionnaire subject to measurement error, and the Japanese-specific population potentially limiting broader generalizability.

Implications Medical Affairs : This high-quality epidemiological study from one of the world's largest birth cohorts adds robust evidence that sustained fish consumption from preconception through pregnancy may meaningfully reduce HDP risk, offering a foundation for expanding nutritional guidance in maternal hypertension prevention programs. From a Medical Affairs standpoint, these findings could support the positioning of omega-3 supplementation products and reinforce dietary counseling recommendations within preconception care pathways, particularly in Asian markets where HDP represents significant unmet need. KOL engagement should prioritize obstetric and maternal medicine specialists to design future RCTs testing whether targeted n-3 supplementation replicates this observational benefit and to inform product differentiation strategies in maternal health.

Antihypertensive Medications and Risk of Heat-Related Illness During Heatwaves: Self-Controlled Case Series.

Revue : Pharmacoepidemiology and Drug Safety | Tier : NA | IF estime : NA

Date de publication : 2026-09

Resultats principaux : This pharmacoepidemiological study used a self-controlled case series (SCCS) design with administrative claims data from Tsukuba City, Japan (April 2014-March 2019), identifying adults with hypertension to examine whether antihypertensive medications modify risk of heat-related illness during heatwaves. The primary endpoint was the incidence rate ratio (IRR) for heat-related illness during heatwave periods versus baseline within individuals, stratified by prescription status for ACE inhibitors/ARBs, calcium channel blockers (CCBs), or non-loop diuretics. Persons prescribed antihypertensives showed a significantly greater increase in heat-related illness during heatwaves (IRR: 1.64, 95% CI: 1.53-1.75) compared to those not prescribed antihypertensives (IRR: 1.42, 95% CI: 1.27-1.58), with evidence of a significant drug-temperature interaction (p less than 0.01). In subgroup analyses by drug class, ACE inhibitors/ARBs and CCBs were each associated with increased heat-related illness risk during heatwaves, whereas diuretics showed no significant effect. Limitations include ecological temperature exposure assessment, potential residual confounding by disease severity in treated versus untreated patients, and the inability to establish causality from this observational design.

Implications Medical Affairs : This study raises an important safety signal for widely prescribed antihypertensive drug classes—specifically ACE inhibitors, ARBs, and CCBs—regarding increased heat-related illness vulnerability during heatwaves, with potential implications for prescribing guidance and risk communication in climate-vulnerable populations. From a Medical Affairs perspective, these findings should prompt proactive review of product labeling, patient communication materials, and risk mitigation strategies for antihypertensive portfolios as extreme heat events increase in frequency and severity globally. Engagement with regulatory bodies, clinical societies, and pharmacoepidemiology KOLs should be prioritized to assess whether updated safety communications or prescribing cautions during heatwaves are warranted.

Correlation Analysis of Blood Lithium Metabolism Kinetics and Concentration-to-Dose Ratio in Patients With Hypertension and Manic Episodes.

Revue : Human Psychopharmacology | Tier : NA | IF estime : NA

Date de publication : 2026-09

Resultats principaux : This single-center retrospective cohort study enrolled 210 patients with manic episodes stratified into a hypertension group (n=90) and a non-hypertension group (n=120) to evaluate the independent effect of hypertension on steady-state lithium pharmacokinetics and the concentration-to-dose (C/D) ratio. Primary endpoints included steady-state serum lithium concentrations, C/D ratios, rate of achieving therapeutic lithium levels, and incidence of lithium toxicity, analyzed by t-test, chi-square, and multiple linear regression. Hypertensive patients had significantly higher serum lithium concentrations and C/D ratios than the non-hypertension group (p less than 0.001), with markedly lower rates of achieving therapeutic levels (42.22% vs. 65.83%) and higher lithium toxicity incidence (11.11% vs. 2.50%). Multiple regression confirmed hypertension (beta=0.215), age, and daily dose as independent predictors of elevated C/D ratio, with elderly hypertensive patients showing the most pronounced increase; the type of antihypertensive medication did not significantly affect lithium metabolism. Key limitations include the retrospective single-center design with potential selection bias, the relatively small sample size of 210 patients, and insufficient statistical power to detect differential effects of specific antihypertensive drug classes on lithium metabolism.

Implications Medical Affairs : This study identifies a clinically important drug-disease interaction between hypertension and lithium therapy that significantly elevates toxicity risk, with particular relevance to the growing population of elderly patients with comorbid psychiatric and cardiovascular conditions who are at highest risk. From a Medical Affairs perspective, these findings underscore the need for therapeutic drug monitoring guidance and dose adjustment algorithms when hypertension co-exists with lithium treatment, representing an opportunity to develop educational materials for clinicians at the psychiatry-cardiology interface. Collaborative KOL engagement across psychiatry and internal medicine disciplines could support evidence-based prescribing guidance updates incorporating hypertension status as a critical variable in lithium dose decisions.

Colorectal Polyp Occurrence Associated With the ZMIZ1-AS1 rs704017 Variant and Hypertension Among Taiwan Biobank Participants.

Revue : Cancer Medicine | Tier : NA | IF estime : NA

Date de publication : 2026-09

Resultats principaux : This case-control study linked 8,970 participants aged 30-70 years from the Taiwan Biobank (2008-2015) to the National Health Insurance Research Database (1998-2015), examining associations between the ZMIZ1-AS1 rs704017 genetic variant, hypertension status, and colorectal polyp (CP) risk using multiple logistic regression models. The rs704017 AG+GG genotype was independently associated with significantly higher odds of CPs compared to the AA genotype (aOR=1.39, 95% CI: 1.16-1.66), whereas hypertension alone showed no significant independent association with CP risk after multivariable adjustment. A significant gene-environment interaction between hypertension and rs704017 was identified (interaction p=0.029), with the highest relative CP risk among non-hypertensive AG+GG carriers (aOR=1.62, 95% CI: 1.29-2.03), followed by hypertensive AA individuals (aOR=1.45, 95% CI: 1.09-1.95) and hypertensive AG+GG carriers (aOR=1.56, 95% CI: 1.16-2.08) versus non-hypertensive AA referents. Subgroup analyses revealed consistent directional effects across genotype-hypertension strata, though the interaction pattern suggests differential modulation by hypertension across genotypes. Key limitations include the cross-sectional linkage design preventing temporal inferences, potential for population stratification bias, and incomplete data on colorectal cancer risk factors such as diet, family history, or colonoscopy indication.

Implications Medical Affairs : The identification of a gene-environment interaction between ZMIZ1-AS1 rs704017 and hypertension in colorectal polyp risk extends understanding of how hypertension may modify genetic susceptibility to gastrointestinal premalignant lesions, adding comorbidity context relevant to personalized cancer and cardiovascular risk stratification. This finding is of modest direct relevance for antihypertensive product positioning but highlights an emerging precision oncology-cardiovascular comorbidity intersection that could generate KOL interest in integrated screening guidelines for hypertensive patients with ZMIZ1-AS1 risk genotypes. Future mechanistic research investigating the biological pathway linking hypertension to ZMIZ1-AS1-mediated colorectal carcinogenesis could identify actionable biomarkers for early colonoscopic screening in hypertensive patient populations.

Latent profiles of social networks and their association with digital health readiness among rural older adults with hypertension.

Revue : Scientific Reports | Tier : NA | IF estime : NA

Date de publication : 2026-08-14

Resultats principaux : This cross-sectional survey enrolled 576 rural older adults with hypertension in Hunan Province, China, using the Lubben Social Network Scale-6 and the Digital Health Readiness Questionnaire (DHRQ) to identify latent social network profiles and examine their association with digital health readiness (DHR) via latent profile analysis. Three social network profiles were identified: socially restricted (16.5%), moderately connected (65.3%), and well-integrated (18.2%), and all profiles were independently associated with DHRQ scores (F=20.362, p less than 0.001, adjusted R-squared=0.270) after controlling for demographic, socioeconomic, and health-related confounders. The moderately connected profile was associated with a 4.536-point higher DHRQ score (95% CI: 1.346-7.726, p=0.005) and the well-integrated profile with an 8.386-point higher score (95% CI: 4.447-12.326, p less than 0.001) compared to the socially restricted profile. No significant subgroup-specific findings beyond the main profile comparisons were reported. Limitations include the cross-sectional design precluding causal inference, single-province sampling limiting generalizability to rural China, and self-report bias inherent in questionnaire-based social network assessment.

Implications Medical Affairs : These findings reveal that social connectedness is a significant predictor of digital health technology uptake among rural hypertensive elderly, demonstrating that digital health interventions for blood pressure management in this population must account for social isolation as a structural barrier to adoption and adherence. From a Medical Affairs perspective, this evidence supports developing companion digital health solutions and connected care platforms for antihypertensive therapies that incorporate social engagement features, particularly in the rapidly expanding rural Chinese hypertension market. KOL strategy should engage public health experts, gerontologists, and digital health innovators in China to co-develop patient support programs that leverage social networks as a lever to improve medication adherence and remote monitoring uptake in rural elderly hypertensive populations.

Predicting all-cause and cardiovascular mortality in hypertensive patients by combining the CHG index and its obesity indicators: a prospective cohort study with survival prediction model.

Revue : Cardiovascular Diabetology | Tier : NA | IF estime : NA

Date de publication : 2026-08-05

Resultats principaux : This prospective cohort study analyzed 4,661 hypertensive participants from the China Health and Retirement Longitudinal Study (CHARLS) to evaluate the cholesterol-HDL-glucose (CHG) index and its obesity-adjusted variants (CHG-BMI, CHG-WC, CHG-WHtR) as predictors of all-cause and cardiovascular mortality using survival machine learning approaches including Cox regression, Cox-LASSO, and Survival XGBoost. The primary outcome was mortality risk prediction performance assessed by Harrell's C-index and time-dependent AUC (tdAUC) at 3, 5, and 7 years, with incremental value measured via continuous net reclassification index (NRI) and integrated discrimination improvement (IDI). CHG-WHtR showed the strongest association with all-cause mortality (HR=2.060, 95% CI: 1.852-2.292, p less than 0.001) and cardiovascular mortality (HR=2.020, 95% CI: 1.816-2.247, p less than 0.001) per 1-SD increase, with the Cox-LASSO model achieving a test C-index of 0.920 (95% CI: 0.878-0.962) and tdAUCs of 0.851-0.916 across time horizons. Adding CHG indices substantially improved reclassification (continuous NRI 0.349-0.496, IDI 0.024-0.040) and revealed L-shaped non-linear relationships with both all-cause and cardiovascular mortality (non-linear p less than 0.001). Key limitations include the observational CHARLS design subject to residual confounding, single Chinese-cohort sampling limiting international generalizability, and potential anthropometric measurement variability from self-reporting.

Implications Medical Affairs : The CHG-WHtR index demonstrates superior prognostic performance for mortality risk in hypertensive patients compared to conventional biomarkers, offering a simple and actionable insulin resistance and adiposity composite tool for risk stratification that could be incorporated into clinical practice guidelines for hypertension management. From a Medical Affairs perspective, this validated risk stratification approach could support product positioning by identifying the highest-risk patient segments most likely to benefit from intensive antihypertensive treatment and cardiometabolic risk reduction, aligned with growing interest in the metabolic-cardiovascular interface in hypertension. Engaging KOLs in cardiometabolic risk, metabolic syndrome, and cardiovascular epidemiology in China could advance adoption of CHG-based risk tools and inform future clinical trials specifically targeting the insulin resistance-hypertension mortality axis.

Sex-Dependent Epigenomic and Transcriptomic Reprogramming Links Maternal Obesity to Cardiac Remodeling in Adult Offspring.

Revue : American Journal of Physiology. Heart and Circulatory Physiology | Tier : NA | IF estime : NA

Date de publication : 2026-09-08

Resultats principaux : This translational mechanistic study used a mouse model of maternal high-fat diet (HFD)-induced obesity to investigate epigenomic and transcriptomic perturbations in offspring hearts, comparing offspring of HFD-fed mothers (Off-HFD) to offspring of regular diet-fed mothers (Off-RD) across multiple physiological and molecular endpoints. Both male and female Off-HFD mice developed progressive cardiac hypertrophy, but male offspring exhibited a more severe phenotype including hypertension, increased vascular stiffness, cardiac dysfunction, and myocardial fibrosis compared to female offspring. DNA methylation analysis revealed extensive sex-dependent alterations at genes involved in cardiac development, lipid metabolism, hypertrophic growth, and inflammatory signaling, with many epigenetic changes persisting into adulthood, demonstrating a durable molecular memory from maternal obesity exposure. Transcriptome analysis of adult hearts showed activation of heart failure and pathological cardiac remodeling gene programs in male Off-HFD mice, whereas female Off-HFD mice activated cardioprotective pathway signatures, consistent with the sex-specific functional phenotypes. Key limitations include the animal model translational gap to human disease, lack of human cardiac epigenomic validation data, and restriction to a single HFD composition without dose-response characterization.

Implications Medical Affairs : This study provides compelling mechanistic evidence that maternal obesity establishes sex-specific epigenetic and transcriptomic cardiac programming that persists into adulthood and predisposes male offspring to hypertension and pathological cardiac remodeling, expanding the developmental origins of cardiovascular disease framework and identifying potential early-life intervention windows. From a Medical Affairs perspective, these data support contributing to a broader narrative around maternal metabolic health and offspring cardiovascular risk, relevant to positioning cardiometabolic risk reduction strategies within a developmental prevention paradigm. KOL engagement with epigenetics, developmental programming, and maternal-fetal medicine researchers would be valuable to translate these preclinical findings toward human biomarker discovery and early intervention trial design targeting maternal obesity.

Device measured physical activity among women with and without adverse pregnancy outcomes: Data from the HUNT study.

Revue : PLoS One | Tier : NA | IF estime : NA

Date de publication : 2026-09-08

Resultats principaux : This population-based cohort study from the Norwegian HUNT4 study enrolled 3,771 women with accelerometry-measured physical activity (PA) over 3-7 consecutive days, comparing PA levels between 868 women with a history of adverse pregnancy outcomes (APOs, including hypertensive disorders of pregnancy, gestational diabetes, SGA offspring, preterm birth, and placental abruption) and 2,903 women without APO history. Primary outcomes were moderate-vigorous physical activity (MVPA) in MET-minutes/week and total PA in min/day calculated from three walking paces, running and cycling measurements. Unadjusted analyses showed lower PA levels in women with APO history, but these differences were attenuated to non-significance in fully adjusted models after accounting for postpartum general health and modifiable lifestyle factors. Across both groups, 15.9-17.7% of women failed to achieve recommended PA levels, confirming a persistent cardiovascular risk gap regardless of APO history. Key limitations include potential selection bias from HUNT4 participation and birth registry linkage, cross-sectional PA measurement not capturing lifetime patterns, and heterogeneous APO grouping potentially obscuring condition-specific associations between individual APO types and PA.

Implications Medical Affairs : The finding that nearly one in five women with a history of APOs—including hypertensive disorders of pregnancy—fails to meet PA guidelines underscores a persistent prevention gap requiring targeted post-pregnancy cardiovascular risk management programs that integrate lifestyle interventions alongside pharmacological blood pressure control. This evidence supports the case for integrated cardiovascular prevention strategies addressing lifestyle factors in women with prior APO history, an underserved population with elevated lifetime hypertension and CVD risk that represents a growing market opportunity for preventive therapeutics. Engagement with obstetric and cardiovascular disease prevention KOLs would be strategic to build advocacy for post-pregnancy follow-up programs and position relevant antihypertensive products within a lifecycle cardiovascular risk prevention framework for women.

Revue : International Urology and Nephrology | Tier : NA | IF estime : NA

Date de publication : 2026-09-08

Resultats principaux : This SANRA-compliant critical narrative review synthesized PubMed and Scopus evidence from 2010-2026 on APOL1 kidney disease caused by G1 and G2 risk variants, which disproportionately burden individuals of African ancestry and carry approximately 15-20% penetrance toward overt nephropathy, including hypertension-attributed kidney failure. The review assigned qualitative certainty ratings to mechanistic and clinical claims, covering pathogenic gain-of-function ion channel disruption, mitochondrial dysfunction, and inflammatory pathway activation as downstream podocyte injury mechanisms. Phase 2a data for inaxaplin (VX-147) showed a 47.6% reduction in urine protein-to-creatinine ratio (UPCR) at week 13 in a 16-participant open-label study, while MZE829 achieved a 35.6% reduction in urine albumin-to-creatinine ratio in a 12-participant Phase 2 HORIZON study; both results are limited by small sample size, open-label design, and short follow-up without hard kidney endpoints. The ongoing randomized Phase 2/3 AMPLITUDE trial (NCT05312879) for inaxaplin has a pre-specified week 48 interim analysis anticipated in early 2027; the APOL1 association is robust for FSGS, HIV-associated nephropathy, and hypertension-attributed kidney failure but inconsistent for diabetic kidney disease. Limitations include reliance on predominantly overexpression system and observational data for mechanistic claims, absence of long-term RCT hard endpoint data, and clinical heterogeneity complicating unified therapeutic algorithms.

Implications Medical Affairs : The AMPLITUDE trial interim analysis in early 2027 represents a critical near-term data readout that could reshape the therapeutic landscape for APOL1-associated hypertensive nephropathy and create a new precision nephrology treatment paradigm with significant implications for health equity in Black patient populations. From a Medical Affairs perspective, companies with antihypertensive or nephroprotective products should closely monitor AMPLITUDE data and prepare positioning strategies for the post-readout environment, particularly for renin-angiotensin system inhibitors currently the standard of care in APOL1-associated kidney disease. Proactive KOL engagement with nephrology, genetics, and health equity specialists aligned with the 2025 KDIGO Controversies Conference framework is strategically important to prepare for guideline evolution and to support genotype-guided screening conversations in hypertensive Black patients at risk for APOL1-related kidney failure.

Resultats principaux : This community-based cross-sectional study enrolled 2,533 adults aged 65 years and older in Hangzhou, China (February-December 2023), measuring bone mineral density by dual-energy X-ray absorptiometry at lumbar spine, femoral neck, and total hip to determine osteoporosis prevalence (T-score at or below -2.5) and associated risk factors, with medical history including hypertension and diabetes collected via standardized questionnaire. Key limitations include the cross-sectional design preventing causal inference, single-city Hangzhou sampling limiting national generalizability, and absence of nutritional biomarker or hormonal data that could confound osteoporosis risk assessment.

Implications Medical Affairs : Although hypertension showed no independent association with osteoporosis after multivariable adjustment, the very high osteoporosis prevalence (32.8%) in elderly Chinese adults managed predominantly for cardiometabolic conditions warrants clinical attention for integrated bone health screening within hypertensive care pathways and multimorbidity management programs. This finding supports the case for comprehensive cardiometabolic-bone health management, highlighting that specific antihypertensive drug classes—particularly thiazide diuretics with known bone-sparing effects—could offer a product differentiation angle worth developing with KOLs in geriatrics and endocrinology. Future research should specifically examine whether antihypertensive drug class differentially impacts bone mineral density in elderly Chinese hypertensive patients, providing actionable data for guideline development and targeted market positioning in the geriatric cardiovascular market.

Prevalence and Associated Factors of Hypertension Among Diabetic Individuals in the Sylhet Region of Bangladesh.

Revue : International Journal of Hypertension | Tier : NA | IF estime : NA

Date de publication : 2026-09-07

Resultats principaux : This cross-sectional study enrolled 295 adults with diabetes in the Sylhet region of Bangladesh to determine hypertension prevalence and identify associated factors, collecting anthropometric measurements, lifestyle factors, lipid profiles (by standard colorimetric methods), and sociodemographic characteristics via structured questionnaire and medical records review. The overall prevalence of hypertension among diabetic individuals was 61.4% (181/295), rising to 69.4% in participants older than 55 years and 70.0% among those with obesity. Implications Medical Affairs : The very high hypertension prevalence (61.4%) among diabetic patients in this Bangladeshi cohort underscores the critical urgency for integrated cardiometabolic risk management in South Asian diabetic populations, where obesity-hypertension co-occurrence represents a major public health challenge with growing market relevance. From a Medical Affairs perspective, this epidemiological profile supports positioning of combination antihypertensive and metabolic risk reduction therapies in Bangladesh and similar South Asian markets, while emphasizing the need for country-specific prevalence data to inform access and reimbursement strategies for hypertension products in emerging markets. The Silent Vascular Shift: Endothelial Dysfunction and Cardiovascular Risk in Menopausal Women.

Revue : Cardiovascular and Hematological Disorders Drug Targets | Tier : NA | IF estime : NA

Date de publication : 2026-08-29

Resultats principaux : This comprehensive literature review synthesized evidence from PubMed, Scopus, and Web of Science (2015-2025) on the role of endothelial dysfunction in cardiovascular risk among postmenopausal women, focusing on the mechanistic pathways by which hormonal changes during menopause drive atherosclerosis and cardiovascular disease. Estrogen decline was found to drive vascular stiffening, decreased nitric oxide bioavailability, and a heightened inflammatory response, contributing to significantly elevated CVD risk in postmenopausal women relative to premenopausal women and age-matched men. Compared to men, postmenopausal women experience unique lipid profile changes, increased metabolic syndrome risk, and central fat redistribution that amplify CVD susceptibility, while traditional risk factors including hypertension, diabetes, smoking, physical inactivity, and obesity affect women with sex-specific pathophysiological mechanisms. Structured exercise showed the most consistent evidence as a risk-reducing lifestyle intervention; evidence supporting phytoestrogens and nutraceuticals was characterized as limited and heterogeneous without sufficient RCT support. Limitations include the narrative review methodology introducing potential selection bias, the restriction to 2015-2025 publications potentially missing foundational earlier evidence, and the focus primarily on large epidemiological cohorts and RCTs which may not fully capture mechanistic heterogeneity of menopausal cardiovascular risk.

Implications Medical Affairs : This review reinforces that menopause represents a critical vascular inflection point where endothelial dysfunction amplifies hypertension and CVD risk, justifying aggressive proactive cardiometabolic risk screening and antihypertensive management starting at perimenopause as a clinical and public health priority. From a Medical Affairs perspective, the sex-specific pathophysiology of postmenopausal cardiovascular risk supports differentiated messaging around antihypertensive products for women, with significant potential for guideline contribution advocacy around sex-specific hypertension treatment algorithms in postmenopausal care settings. KOL partnerships with cardiovascular specialists in women's health, endocrinology, and menopause medicine would be strategically valuable to advance the evidence base for sex-tailored hypertension management and to position relevant therapeutic products as leaders in women's cardiovascular health initiatives.
