Cardiometabolic multimorbidity and associated clinical, lifestyle and genetic factors among Ghanaian patients.

Revue : PloS one | Tier : NA | IF estime : NA

Date de publication : 2026-11-17

Resultats principaux : This cross-sectional study enrolled 203 adults attending specialized clinics at a tertiary hospital in Cape Coast, Ghana between May 2023 and April 2024, characterizing the burden of cardiometabolic multimorbidity (concurrent hypertension, type 2 diabetes, metabolic syndrome, and CKD) and evaluating clinical, lifestyle, and candidate genetic determinants. Over 80% of participants had at least two cardiometabolic conditions, more than 80% were overweight or obese, and over two-thirds met metabolic syndrome criteria, revealing an exceptionally high multimorbidity burden in this sub-Saharan African population. Despite high antihypertensive and glucose-lowering treatment rates, uncontrolled blood pressure and hyperglycemia remained common, exposing a substantial therapeutic gap. Obesity was the strongest independent predictor of CKD (aOR 7-8 for Obese I/II classes), antihypertensive therapy was associated with lower odds of uncontrolled BP (aOR 0.25, 95% CI 0.07-0.90, p=0.033), and the TCF7L2 rs7903146 CT genotype was significantly associated with higher odds of hypertension (aOR 2.15, 95% CI 1.06-4.37, p=0.034). Combined clinical, lifestyle, and genetic models achieved the highest predictive discrimination (AUCs 0.83-0.89), though genetic-only models performed poorly (AUCs 0.63-0.67), and the small single-center convenience sample with cross-sectional design limits generalizability and precludes causal inference.

Implications Medical Affairs : This study highlights the high and undertreated hypertension burden in sub-Saharan Africa, reinforcing the need for accessible antihypertensive regimens in low- and middle-income countries, particularly fixed-dose combinations that could address treatment inertia in resource-limited settings. From a Medical Affairs standpoint, the obesity-driven cardiometabolic clustering underscores the strategic value of positioning antihypertensive agents with favorable metabolic profiles and advocating for integrated cardiometabolic risk reduction programs at the KOL level in West Africa. Engaging regional cardiovascular and endocrinology KOLs around multimorbidity management guidelines specific to sub-Saharan Africa would align with this evidence and support scientific leadership in an underserved high-burden geography.

Optimal Dose of Ciprofol for Anesthesia Induction in Older Hypertensive Patients with Elevated Baseline Systolic Blood Pressure: A Bayesian Phase 1/2 Trial.

Revue : Drug design, development and therapy | Tier : NA | IF estime : NA

Date de publication : 2026-11-12

Resultats principaux : This single-center, seamless Bayesian Phase 1/2 trial enrolled 51 hypertensive patients aged 65-80 years with baseline invasive systolic blood pressure of 140 mmHg or higher undergoing elective non-cardiac procedures, aiming to identify the optimal ciprofol induction dose for perioperative hemodynamic management. Phase 1 using the Bayesian Optimal Interval design identified the maximum tolerated dose at 0.35 mg/kg with a posterior mean post-induction hypotension incidence of 33% (95% credible interval 11-61%), while Phase 2 selected 0.25 mg/kg as the optimal biological dose with a 91% induction success rate (95% CrI 71-99%) and a 25% hypotension risk (95% CrI 6-52%). Notably, the lower 0.25 mg/kg dose was less effective at attenuating intubation-induced hypertensive surges compared to 0.35 mg/kg, with significantly higher peak MAP after intubation at 0.25 mg/kg (126.1 +/- 20.9 vs 97.8 +/- 14.8 mmHg; Bonferroni-adjusted p=0.002), highlighting a clinical tradeoff between safety and BP suppression. No subgroup analyses beyond the primary dose comparison were reported, and the small sample size with single-center design substantially limits external validity and precludes definitive dosing recommendations. The exploratory nature of this trial requires validation in larger multicenter studies before informing perioperative anesthesia guidelines in hypertensive elderly patients.

Implications Medical Affairs : This trial underscores the perioperative hemodynamic challenge in elderly patients with uncontrolled hypertension, where anesthetic induction carries compound cardiovascular risks, an observation directly relevant for companies positioning antihypertensive therapies targeting perioperative BP stability and cardiovascular event reduction. The tradeoff identified between hypotension risk and attenuation of intubation-induced hypertensive surges provides a data anchor for discussions with anesthesiology and cardiology KOLs about the clinical importance of preoperative BP optimization in elderly patients. Medical Affairs teams should monitor this emerging evidence on perioperative hypertension management, as forthcoming guideline updates on preoperative BP targets may influence prescribing strategies and open positioning opportunities for antihypertensives with stable hemodynamic profiles.

Associations of sleep architecture, sleep fragmentation, and hypoxia-related parameters with cognitive frailty in older patients with hypertension and obstructive sleep apnea: a cross-sectional study.

Revue : BMC geriatrics | Tier : NA | IF estime : NA

Date de publication : 2026-08-19

Resultats principaux : This single-center cross-sectional study enrolled 240 hospitalized patients aged 60 years or older with confirmed hypertension and polysomnography-confirmed obstructive sleep apnea (OSA) in China, investigating the association between polysomnographic parameters and cognitive frailty assessed by the Montreal Cognitive Assessment combined with the Fried Frailty Phenotype. The cognitive frailty group (n=83, 34.6%) showed significantly shorter N3 sleep duration, lower minimum oxygen saturation, and higher arousal index compared to the non-cognitive frailty group (n=157). After multivariable adjustment for sex, age, education, and BMI, N3 sleep duration (OR 0.988, 95% CI 0.981-0.996, p=0.002) and minimum oxygen saturation (OR 0.945, 95% CI 0.914-0.977, p<0.001) were independently negatively associated with cognitive frailty, while arousal index showed a positive independent association (OR 1.016, 95% CI 1.005-1.027, p=0.005). No subgroup analyses stratified by antihypertensive treatment regimen or nocturnal BP control status were presented, limiting mechanistic interpretation of the hypertension contribution. Key limitations include the cross-sectional design precluding causal inference, the single-center hospitalized patient sample, and the absence of antihypertensive drug stratification.

Implications Medical Affairs : The convergence of hypertension, obstructive sleep apnea, and nocturnal hypoxia as independent drivers of cognitive frailty in elderly patients identifies an underappreciated clinical intersection with direct relevance to antihypertensive agents offering 24-hour BP coverage and sleep-compatible hemodynamic profiles. From a Medical Affairs perspective, this evidence supports engagement with geriatric cardiology and sleep medicine KOLs around the value of optimal nocturnal BP management and its potential cognitive protective effects in high-risk elderly populations with concurrent OSA. This dataset could meaningfully inform scientific communication strategies for antihypertensives that demonstrate favorable nocturnal BP profiles, particularly in elderly patients where cognitive frailty prevention represents an emerging endpoint of clinical and regulatory interest.

Theme 6 : Clinical Trials and Guidelines (1 article)

Neoadjuvant anlotinib for locally advanced thyroid cancer: a single-arm phase II study (ALTER-T001).

Revue : Frontiers in endocrinology | Tier : NA | IF estime : NA

Date de publication : 2026-11-02

Resultats principaux : This single-arm Phase II trial (ALTER-T001, ChiCTR2100048077) enrolled 50 patients with pathologically confirmed locally advanced differentiated thyroid cancer (DTC) not amenable to upfront surgery across multiple Chinese oncology centers, evaluating neoadjuvant anlotinib for safety, efficacy, and quality of life. In 50 DTC patients, the objective response rate was 42.0% in the intention-to-treat population (48.8% in the 43 evaluable cases), and the disease control rate was 84.0% (97.7% in evaluable cases), with a median time to response of 2.8 months; surgery was subsequently achieved in 42.0% of patients (21/50), yielding a 57.1% R0 resection rate. Critically, hypertension was the most frequent grade 3 or higher treatment-related adverse event, occurring in 18.0% of patients, with dose reductions required in 16.0% and interruptions in 10.0% of patients; no treatment-related deaths occurred. Median progression-free survival in non-surgical patients reached 29.8 months, though global health status showed generally stable scores with some functional domain deterioration observed during treatment. Key limitations include the small single-arm uncontrolled design without a comparator arm, the limited MTC subgroup (single case described separately), and the absence of head-to-head data against established neoadjuvant regimens.

Implications Medical Affairs : The identification of hypertension as the leading grade 3 or higher adverse event in this VEGFR-inhibitor oncology trial reinforces the critical importance of proactive antihypertensive management protocols in patients receiving anti-angiogenic therapies, representing a Medical Affairs opportunity to position antihypertensive agents specifically for the cardio-oncology setting. This trial provides further clinical evidence supporting rigorous cardiovascular safety monitoring in thyroid cancer patients on tyrosine kinase inhibitors, and Medical Affairs teams should actively track the growing cardio-oncology intersection with hypertension management guidance in oncology treatment protocols. KOL engagement in thyroid oncology and cardio-oncology centers around BP management strategies for patients on VEGFR inhibitors could position antihypertensive expertise at this rapidly expanding clinical frontier.

Twenty-Three Years of Survival Observed in a Diabetic Woman Receiving Extended-Hours Hemodialysis (18-25 Hours per Week) With a Liberalized Diet.

Revue : Journal of medical cases | Tier : NA | IF estime : NA

Date de publication : 2026-11-09

Resultats principaux : This case report describes a 23-year survival observation in a Japanese woman with diabetic nephropathy who initiated renal replacement therapy and subsequently transitioned to extended-hours hemodialysis (18-25 hours per week) with a liberalized diet at a specialized nephrology clinic, specifically to control hypertension and prevent malnutrition. The patient maintained glycemic control using alpha-glucosidase inhibitor monotherapy with an average glycated albumin of 18.0 +/- 1.7%, while BMI and muscle mass improved following transfer to the extended-hours program. Despite overall survival and functional maintenance, two severe vascular complications attributed to pre-existing vascular calcification occurred: ischemic heart disease and below-knee amputation, with BMI temporarily decreasing at each event before recovering. Left ventricular ejection fraction remained within normal range (62-72%) throughout follow-up, and the patient maintained daily life independence with wheelchair use after amputation. As a single case report, this observation cannot establish causality, generalizability is severely limited, and multiple confounders including health literacy, dialysis center quality, and patient-specific metabolic factors cannot be excluded.

Implications Medical Affairs : This case illustrates that extended dialysis with dietary liberalization may play a complementary role in hypertension management in end-stage renal disease patients, a population with complex cardiovascular comorbidities where antihypertensive drug selection must account for renal clearance, volume management, and hemodynamic tolerance during dialysis sessions. From a Medical Affairs standpoint, the emphasis on early-established vascular calcification and ischemic events despite prolonged survival underlines the importance of early and aggressive hypertension and metabolic risk factor control in diabetic nephropathy well before dialysis initiation. Engaging nephrology KOLs around antihypertensive management in pre-dialysis and dialysis populations, particularly agents with established cardiorenal protective profiles, represents a high-value scientific communication opportunity in the growing CKD-hypertension comorbidity space.

Effects of workplace bullying on cardiovascular health: A prospective cohort study of the Gutenberg Health Study with a ten-year follow-up.

Revue : Scandinavian journal of work, environment & health | Tier : NA | IF estime : NA

Date de publication : 2026-11-16

Resultats principaux : This prospective cohort study from the Gutenberg Health Study enrolled 4,137 employees under 65 years at baseline (2007-2012) in Mainz/Mainz-Bingen, Germany, investigating the association between self-reported workplace bullying and 10-year incidence of clinically verified cardiovascular disease (CVD) and hypertension at follow-up (2017-2022). Bullying prevalence was 15.3% by single-item and 19.3% by five-item instrument, and while point estimates for incident CVD showed non-significant trends toward increased risk (HR 1.08, 95% CI 0.74-1.59 for single-item; HR 1.28, 95% CI 0.95-1.72 for five-item), participants reporting persistent bullying at both baseline and 5-year follow-up had a markedly elevated CVD risk (HR 2.44, 95% CI 1.09-5.44). No significant association was observed between workplace bullying and incident hypertension (single-item RR 1.04, 95% CI 0.84-1.28; five-item RR 0.94, 95% CI 0.78-1.13), suggesting that the CVD risk pathway may operate independently of blood pressure elevation. Trajectory analyses showed that only persistent, chronic workplace bullying conferred statistically significant CVD risk elevation, while single time-point exposure did not reach significance. Key limitations include reliance on self-reported workplace bullying, potential unmeasured confounders such as depression and alcohol intake, limited statistical power due to small CVD event numbers, and restricted generalizability beyond Western European employed populations.

Implications Medical Affairs : The finding that chronic, persistent workplace bullying is associated with a 2.4-fold elevated CVD risk—even after adjustment for sex, age, and socioeconomic status—positions occupational psychosocial stress as a clinically meaningful cardiovascular risk factor that complements traditional hypertension risk determinants tracked in prevention guidelines. From a Medical Affairs standpoint, this evidence supports scientific communication messaging around total cardiovascular risk management that integrates psychosocial dimensions, reinforcing the value of comprehensive antihypertensive strategies that address the broader sympathoadrenal and neurohormonal axes activated by chronic workplace stress. KOL engagement at the intersection of occupational medicine, preventive cardiology, and hypertension is strategically warranted, particularly to advocate for psychosocial screening integration in routine cardiovascular risk assessment frameworks as clinical guidelines evolve.

Association and epistasis analyses of obesity-related genes in osteoporosis.

Revue : Frontiers in endocrinology | Tier : NA | IF estime : NA

Date de publication : 2026-11-02

Resultats principaux : This case-control study from Mexico enrolled 365 participants (195 controls and 170 with osteoporosis) classified by WHO densitometric criteria of the lumbar spine and femoral neck, investigating associations and gene-gene and gene-environment interactions among LEP, LEPR, ADIPOQ, and FTO obesity-related gene variants and osteoporosis risk. Age, hypertension, diabetes, and arthritis were identified as independent risk factors for osteoporosis in multivariable analyses, while obesity (BMI 30 kg/m2 or higher) and coffee intake were protective factors. No individual single nucleotide variants showed statistically significant independent associations with osteoporosis under codominant, dominant, or recessive inheritance models. Two multilocus gene-gene interaction models suggested synergistic contributions (ADIPOQ rs1501299 with FTO rs9939609; and LEPR rs1805134, ADIPOQ rs1501299, ADIPOQ rs17300539 with FTO rs9939609), though neither reached statistical significance (p>0.05), and a significant gene-environment interaction involving LEP rs2167270 with BMI and reproductive factors was identified (p=0.002). The exploratory nature of these analyses, the modest sample size underpowered for gene-gene interaction modeling, and the absence of hypertension-stratified analyses that could clarify its independent contribution to bone metabolism are key limitations.

Implications Medical Affairs : The identification of hypertension as an independent risk factor for osteoporosis in this Mexican population highlights a clinically relevant comorbidity intersection, with implications for antihypertensive drug selection in patients at concurrent risk for skeletal fragility, particularly postmenopausal women. From a Medical Affairs perspective, evidence linking hypertension with osteoporosis risk—even in exploratory analyses—supports messaging around antihypertensives with neutral or potentially favorable bone metabolism profiles, as this differentiation could become meaningful in prescribing decisions for multi-morbid patients. Collaboration with endocrinology and rheumatology KOLs on the shared metabolic pathways connecting hypertension and osteoporosis may open cross-specialty scientific communication opportunities, particularly in populations with high coincidence of both conditions.

Lifestyle associations with hypertension and diabetes risk via metabolic function and physical indicators: a cross-sectional serial mediation model among community adults.

Revue : Frontiers in medicine | Tier : NA | IF estime : NA

Date de publication : 2026-11-02

Resultats principaux : This large cross-sectional study enrolled 6,178 community adults in Nanning, southern China, applying structural equation modeling (SEM) with 5,000-resample bias-corrected bootstrap analysis to examine how lifestyle factors influence hypertension and diabetes risk through sequential indirect pathways involving metabolic function and physical indicators. Physical indicators exhibited the strongest direct association with disease risk (beta = 0.763, p<0.001), while metabolic function also showed a positive direct association (beta = 0.048, p=0.010), confirming both as significant mediators on the lifestyle-to-disease pathway. Healthy lifestyle was associated with lower disease risk via indirect pathways through physical indicators alone (beta = -0.360, p=0.001) and sequentially via metabolic function followed by physical indicators (beta = -0.011, p=0.021), while unhealthy lifestyle showed indirect associations with elevated disease risk through metabolic function (beta = 0.003, p=0.036) and physical indicators (beta = 0.092, p=0.014). Direct associations of lifestyle with disease risk were small in magnitude (absolute beta 0.035 or less) and of limited clinical importance, indicating that lifestyle effects are predominantly mediated rather than direct. Key limitations include the cross-sectional design precluding causal inference, potential measurement error in self-reported lifestyle variables, the marginally elevated chi-square/df ratio of 5.19, and the absence of subgroup analyses by sex, age group, or specific lifestyle components that could strengthen clinical translation.

Implications Medical Affairs : This large community-based SEM study reinforces the primacy of physical indicators, likely BMI and waist circumference, as the dominant mediating pathway linking lifestyle to hypertension and diabetes risk, providing a population-level mechanistic rationale for body-weight-focused cardiovascular prevention strategies in Chinese community settings. From a Medical Affairs perspective, the mediation architecture suggests that antihypertensive agents with favorable effects on metabolic and physical body composition indicators could be positioned as particularly congruent with the mechanistic drivers of hypertension development in Asian populations, strengthening product differentiation narratives. Engagement with preventive medicine and community health KOLs in China and similar high-burden settings using this evidence could reinforce scientific leadership in hypertension prevention, particularly in support of real-world evidence programs examining lifestyle interventions combined with pharmacotherapy.
