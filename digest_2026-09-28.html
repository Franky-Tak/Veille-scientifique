Association of the TyG-related indices with hypertension-diabetes comorbidity in middle-aged and older adults: a suburban community-based study.

Revue : BMC Cardiovascular Disorders | Tier : NA | IF estime : NA

Date de publication : 2026-07-25

Resultats principaux : This cross-sectional study enrolled 2,854 middle-aged and older adults (aged 45 years or older) residing in a suburban community in Qinghai, China, to investigate associations between triglyceride-glucose (TyG)-related indices (TyG, TyG-BMI, and TyG-BRI) and hypertension-diabetes comorbidity (HTN-DM). The primary analysis applied multivariable logistic regression to estimate odds ratios with 95% confidence intervals, restricted cubic splines (RCS) to characterize dose-response relationships, and receiver operating characteristic (ROC) curves to evaluate discriminative performance of each index. Each 1-SD increment in TyG, TyG-BMI, and TyG-BRI conferred multivariable-adjusted ORs of 3.16 (95% CI: 2.75-3.64), 1.39 (95% CI: 1.15-1.66), and 1.37 (95% CI: 1.22-1.54), respectively (all P less than 0.001); participants in the highest quartile showed ORs of 30.99, 6.42, and 6.43 versus the lowest quartile (all P for trend less than 0.001). RCS analyses demonstrated a linear dose-response relationship for TyG with HTN-DM, whereas TyG-BMI and TyG-BRI exhibited nonlinear associations; TyG had the highest discriminative capacity (AUC=0.786) compared to TyG-BMI (AUC=0.679) and TyG-BRI (AUC=0.633), suggesting that composite indices incorporating BMI or body roundness index do not substantially improve discrimination beyond TyG alone. Subgroup analyses confirmed positive associations across all strata with a statistically significant interaction by sex (P=0.045), but key limitations include the cross-sectional design precluding causal inference, restriction to a single suburban Chinese community limiting generalizability, and absence of longitudinal follow-up to assess incident HTN-DM.

Implications Medical Affairs : The exceptionally strong association between TyG and HTN-DM prevalence (OR 30.99 for the highest vs. lowest quartile) positions this simple, inexpensive composite index as a clinically actionable screening tool that could influence future international guidelines on cardiovascular-metabolic risk stratification, particularly for resource-limited settings. From a product positioning standpoint, pharmaceutical companies developing or marketing antihypertensive agents that address insulin resistance pathways should consider TyG as a potential surrogate biomarker for patient enrichment strategies in clinical trial design, as it identifies a high-risk comorbid phenotype with significant unmet medical need. Medical Affairs teams should proactively engage KOLs in endocrinology, preventive cardiology, and primary care to explore joint research initiatives on metabolic syndrome-driven hypertension, leveraging TyG as a unifying risk biomarker to strengthen scientific communications, health economics arguments, and real-world evidence strategies.
