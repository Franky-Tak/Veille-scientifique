Blood pressure targets in adults with hypertension.

Revue : The Cochrane Database of Systematic Reviews | Tier : NA | IF estime : NA

Date de publication : 2026-10-12

Resultats principaux : This article is a protocol for a forthcoming Cochrane systematic review and meta-analysis evaluating the benefits and harms of lower blood pressure (BP) targets (defined as BP at or below 135/85 mmHg) compared to standard targets (BP at or below 140-160/90-100 mmHg) in adults aged 18 years or older with elevated BP. The primary endpoint is total mortality and morbidity, while secondary endpoints include achieved systolic and diastolic BP and withdrawals due to adverse events. The protocol explicitly plans for equity-related subgroup analyses examining the differential effect of BP targets by place of residence, race/ethnicity/culture/ancestry, sex, socioeconomic status, and social capital, as well as other context-specific comorbidity factors. The review will focus exclusively on randomized controlled trials to ensure high internal validity. As this is a protocol publication, no quantitative results or statistical outcomes are currently available; the full review remains pending.

Implications Medical Affairs : This Cochrane protocol signals a forthcoming high-quality synthesis that will likely influence international hypertension guidelines on optimal BP targets, making it critical to monitor for its eventual publication and results. The planned equity-focused subgroup analyses may reveal disparities in optimal BP thresholds across demographic and socioeconomic groups, providing Medical Affairs teams with differentiated evidence for targeted patient population strategies and label support. Proactively engaging with investigators and relevant hypertension guideline committees ahead of the review's publication represents a strategic KOL opportunity to position antihypertensive products in alignment with evolving evidence-based BP targets.

Perinatal Factors Associated With Standardized Blood Pressure Percentiles for Children Aged 6 to 12 Years: The BOSHI Study.

Revue : Journal of Clinical Hypertension (Greenwich) | Tier : Tier 3 | IF estime : 3-10

Date de publication : 2026-10

Resultats principaux : This prospective cohort study from Japan (BOSHI study) examined associations between maternal and perinatal factors and standardized blood pressure (BP) percentiles in 175 mother-offspring pairs, with a median child age at follow-up of 7 years. Maternal mean age was 32.4 years, mean birth weight was 3031 g, and hypertensive disorders of pregnancy (HDP) occurred in 43 mothers (approximately 24.6% of the cohort). Children's home BP was measured using standardized protocols and converted to age- and sex-adjusted percentiles. Multivariable analyses revealed that higher birth weight was significantly associated with lower diastolic BP percentiles (-0.87 percentile per 100-g increase; p = 0.03), consistent with the Developmental Origins of Health and Disease (DOHaD) hypothesis. No statistically significant association between HDP and childhood BP percentiles was reported in this sample, though the limited sample size reduces statistical power. The study underscores that perinatal exposures influence early cardiovascular risk markers in children, and that home BP monitoring provides valid data for epidemiological research in pediatric populations.

Implications Medical Affairs : The inverse association between birth weight and childhood diastolic BP extends the hypertension prevention paradigm to the perinatal period, suggesting that obstetric and neonatal care quality has downstream cardiovascular implications that are highly relevant for long-term disease burden modeling. For Medical Affairs, this evidence opens dialogue with maternal-fetal medicine specialists and pediatric cardiologists as emerging KOL segments for hypertension prevention, potentially supporting cross-specialty engagement strategies. Funding longitudinal studies expanding on DOHaD-based research in Asian populations could strengthen scientific leadership positioning while identifying high-risk pediatric cohorts for early intervention.

Theme 6 : Clinical Trials and Guidelines (1 article)

Executive Summary of the Clinical Practice Guideline on Tinnitus Update.

Revue : Otolaryngology — Head and Neck Surgery | Tier : NA | IF estime : NA

Date de publication : 2026-10

Resultats principaux : This article presents the executive summary of an updated American Academy of Otolaryngology-Head and Neck Surgery clinical practice guideline for the evaluation and management of persistent, bothersome tinnitus in adults aged 18 years and older. More than 50 million Americans report tinnitus, with a prevalence estimated at 10-15% in adults. The guideline is directed at all clinicians involved in tinnitus care and provides strong recommendations to distinguish bothersome from non-bothersome tinnitus, screen for self-harm risk, and avoid routine neuroradiologic imaging in the absence of concerning features. Cognitive Behavioral Therapy (CBT) and hearing aid evaluation are positively recommended, while the guideline strongly advises against antidepressants, anticonvulsants, anxiolytics, dietary supplements, and repetitive transcranial magnetic stimulation (rTMS) for tinnitus management. Of particular relevance to arterial hypertension, the guideline identifies intracranial hypertension as a non-auditory cause of tinnitus (specifically pulsatile tinnitus), requiring clinical exclusion during initial evaluation. The document underscores that vascular anomalies and hypertension-related conditions may present with auditory symptoms requiring interdisciplinary management.

Implications Medical Affairs : While primarily an ENT guideline, the inclusion of intracranial hypertension as a recognized cause of tinnitus in a major national guideline represents a cross-specialty touchpoint relevant to Medical Affairs teams managing hypertension portfolios, as it may drive referrals and diagnostic workups in hypertensive patients presenting with auditory symptoms. This highlights the importance of comprehensive blood pressure control as part of preventing secondary conditions beyond classical cardiovascular endpoints, potentially enriching disease education materials. Medical Affairs teams should consider collaborating with otolaryngology KOLs to ensure alignment between ENT and cardiology/nephrology guidelines when managing hypertensive patients who present with tinnitus as a symptom.

Theme 7 : Environment and Risk Factors (4 articles)

A cross-sectional analysis of cardiometabolic and lifestyle-related profiles in men living with HIV in Japan using linked nationwide claims and health checkup data.

Revue : HIV Medicine | Tier : NA | IF estime : NA

Date de publication : 2026-10-11

Resultats principaux : This cross-sectional matched analysis used the JMDC nationwide database linking administrative claims with annual health checkup data in Japan, covering the period 2017-2023. The study included 2,596 men living with HIV (PLWH) receiving antiretroviral therapy (ART) and 12,980 matched men without HIV (1:5 matching by age and calendar year), with a median age of 45.0 years (IQR 37.0-51.0). Despite PLWH being more frequently treated for hypertension, diabetes mellitus, and dyslipidaemia, their objectively measured systolic BP, HbA1c, and LDL-C levels were comparable to or lower than matched controls, suggesting surveillance bias in routine HIV care settings. After conditional logistic regression, HIV infection was not independently associated with hypertension (adjusted OR 0.95, 95% CI 0.82-1.09) or diabetes mellitus (aOR 1.00, 95% CI 0.82-1.22), but was significantly associated with dyslipidaemia (aOR 1.22, 95% CI 1.08-1.39) and slightly higher BMI (mean difference +0.28 kg/m2, 95% CI 0.02-0.55). The use of linked nationwide health checkup and claims data provides a methodological advance by partially mitigating ascertainment bias inherent in HIV clinic-based cohorts. These findings challenge earlier reports suggesting higher hypertension prevalence in PLWH and underscore the importance of contemporary real-world data in cardiometabolic risk assessment.

Implications Medical Affairs : The absence of an independent association between well-managed HIV infection and hypertension in this large Japanese cohort challenges prior assumptions and may reduce the cardiometabolic concern threshold for antihypertensive prescribing in contemporary HIV care settings, with implications for drug positioning in HIV-comorbid populations. For Medical Affairs, these findings support differentiated communications with HIV/infectious disease specialists emphasizing that while dyslipidaemia monitoring remains essential, hypertension management in PLWH on modern ART may not require a distinct protocol. Investment in real-world evidence studies using linked administrative databases in other geographic populations would validate generalizability and strengthen the scientific narrative around cardiometabolic risk stratification in complex comorbid patients.

Therapeutic Inertia in the Management of Hypertension by Moroccan General Practitioners.

Revue : Current Hypertension Reviews | Tier : NA | IF estime : NA

Date de publication : 2026-10-01

Resultats principaux : This descriptive cross-sectional study conducted from October 2021 to January 2022 assessed the prevalence of and factors associated with therapeutic inertia in hypertension management among 130 Moroccan general practitioners (GPs) via an anonymous questionnaire. The study population was predominantly female (62.3%), had a median age of 32 years (IQR 26-50), and had roughly equal representation of GPs with five or fewer versus more than five years of practice. Using a cutoff of two or more inertia-demonstrating clinical scenarios, 78.5% of GPs (95% CI 70.4-85.2%) were classified as exhibiting therapeutic inertia. The two leading reasons for therapeutic abstention were the preference to improve patient compliance and lifestyle measures before escalating treatment (81.5%) and skepticism about whether office BP reflects true blood pressure (43.8%). In multivariable logistic regression, diabetic patients were associated with a four-fold increased therapeutic inertia among GPs (OR 4.42, 95% CI 1.2-7.23), and a history of ischemic stroke was also positively associated (OR 1.53, 95% CI 1.23-4.23). Advanced patient age was inversely associated with therapeutic inertia. The exploratory nature and sampling limitations preclude generalization beyond the study population.

Implications Medical Affairs : A therapeutic inertia rate exceeding 78% among Moroccan GPs represents a critical gap between clinical guidelines and real-world hypertension management in North Africa, identifying a high-impact opportunity for Medical Affairs to deploy GP-targeted educational programs using clinical case simulations and decision-support tools focused on timely treatment escalation. The paradoxically higher inertia in diabetic hypertensive patients — who represent a highest-risk subgroup — signals the need for clear, simplified messaging and patient management algorithms that overcome the perceived complexity of managing dual pathologies simultaneously. Building strategic alliances with primary care medical associations and cardiology societies across the MENA region should be prioritized to close the guideline-practice gap and improve BP control rates in these markets.

Unlocking the Potential of Nutritional Approaches and Bioactive Compounds to Modulate Oxidative Stress and Inflammation in Systemic Arterial Hypertension.

Revue : Molecular Nutrition & Food Research | Tier : NA | IF estime : NA

Date de publication : 2026-10

Resultats principaux : This narrative review from Brazilian investigators synthesizes current evidence on dietary interventions and bioactive compounds as complementary strategies to conventional antihypertensive treatment in systemic arterial hypertension (SAH). The review covers key dietary modifications — reducing sodium, increasing potassium, and adopting plant-rich diets such as the DASH diet — as foundational lifestyle approaches with demonstrated BP-lowering effects. The authors systematically review evidence on bioactive compounds including polyphenols (e.g., flavonoids, resveratrol), curcumin, and other phytochemicals, as well as probiotics, focusing on their ability to enhance endothelial function, increase nitric oxide bioavailability, reduce reactive oxygen species, and attenuate pro-inflammatory pathways. Mechanistic insights suggest these compounds act on NF-kB, Nrf2, and eNOS pathways relevant to hypertension pathophysiology. Limitations include the narrative rather than systematic methodology, significant heterogeneity across included studies, absence of standardized bioactive compound dosing regimens, and a lack of pooled quantitative BP reduction estimates. The review does not include clinical trial data from randomized controlled trials with sufficient power to make definitive efficacy claims.

Implications Medical Affairs : The growing scientific focus on bioactive compounds and dietary patterns as adjunctive antihypertensive strategies positions the nutritional pharmacology space as an emerging area for Medical Affairs to monitor, particularly as patients increasingly seek non-pharmacological approaches that could complement or reduce the dose requirements of conventional antihypertensives. Companies with established antihypertensive portfolios can develop differentiated patient education programs emphasizing synergistic lifestyle-pharmacotherapy approaches, reinforcing adherence messaging while distinguishing products on holistic cardiovascular risk management. Commissioning meta-analyses or funding rigorous RCTs on specific bioactive compound-antihypertensive drug interactions would accelerate evidence generation, strengthen scientific credibility, and open collaborative opportunities with nutritional scientists and preventive cardiology KOLs.

Association Between Aerobic Physical Activity and Hypertension Among U.S. Adults: A Cross-Sectional Analysis of NHANES (National Health and Nutrition Examination Survey) 2021-2023.

Revue : Cureus | Tier : NA | IF estime : NA

Date de publication : 2026-09-11

Resultats principaux : This cross-sectional study analyzed data from 6,123 adult participants in NHANES 2021-2023, representing approximately 245 million U.S. adults after survey weighting. Hypertension was defined as mean systolic BP at or above 130 mmHg or mean diastolic BP at or above 80 mmHg based on up to three standardized oscillometric measurements, with antihypertensive medication use intentionally excluded from the case definition. Aerobic physical activity was categorized as meeting or not meeting Physical Activity Guidelines for Americans, defined as 150 minutes per week or more of moderate-intensity equivalent activity. Of the sample, 54.7% met aerobic activity guidelines and 37.5% met the hypertension criteria. Hypertension prevalence was notably lower among physically active adults (34.4% vs. 41.2% in inactive adults). Survey-weighted multivariable logistic regression, adjusting for age, sex, race/ethnicity, smoking, alcohol, income, and education, demonstrated that meeting aerobic guidelines was independently associated with lower hypertension odds (adjusted OR 0.79, 95% CI 0.62-0.99; p = 0.044). Age was the strongest hypertension predictor (adults 60 years and older: adjusted OR 2.35 vs. 18-39 years, 95% CI 1.44-3.82). No significant subgroup effect modification was identified. Key limitations include the cross-sectional design precluding causal inference, self-reported physical activity data, and the exclusion of antihypertensive medication use from the hypertension definition.

Implications Medical Affairs : This nationally representative analysis provides contemporary population-level confirmation that regular aerobic physical activity, meeting established guideline thresholds, is independently associated with a meaningful 21% lower adjusted odds of hypertension, reinforcing the evidence base for integrating structured lifestyle prescriptions alongside pharmacotherapy in hypertension management programs. For Medical Affairs, this data supports the development of holistic cardiovascular disease prevention messaging that positions antihypertensive therapies within a broader lifestyle optimization framework, potentially differentiating product profiles beyond direct BP lowering to include lifestyle-synergistic benefits. Partnering with preventive cardiology, sports medicine associations, and health system wellness programs to co-develop physical activity adherence tools and patient resources could enhance KOL engagement and strengthen population health positioning.
