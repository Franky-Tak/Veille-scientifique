Cardiac Damage and Clinical Outcomes in Symptomatic Severe Aortic Stenosis Undergoing Transcatheter Aortic Valve Replacement.

Revue : JACC. Cardiovascular Interventions | Tier : Tier 2 | IF estimé : ~15

Date de publication : 2026-11-09

Résultats principaux : This post hoc subanalysis of the LANDMARK randomized controlled trial enrolled 596 of 768 patients with severe aortic stenosis (mean age 80.2 years, mean STS score 3.3%) and applied an updated cardiac damage staging system incorporating echocardiographic probability of pulmonary hypertension. Five damage stages were defined: stage 0 (no damage, n=36), stage 1 (left ventricular damage, n=88), stage 2 (left atrial or mitral valve damage, n=284), stage 3 (pulmonary artery or tricuspid valve damage, n=84), and stage 4 (right ventricular damage, n=104). The primary endpoint of 1-year all-cause mortality increased progressively: 0%, 1.1%, 4.7%, 7.2%, and 20.4% for stages 0 through 4 respectively (log-rank P<0.001), with an AUC of 0.739 for mortality prediction. Cumulative cardiac damage (meeting all stage 1-4 criteria simultaneously) conferred the highest 1-year mortality at 23.6%. In multivariable analysis, stage 4 was independently associated with higher 1-year all-cause mortality; a key limitation is the post hoc design from a single trial without external validation.

Implications Medical Affairs : Integration of pulmonary hypertension probability into cardiac damage staging provides a clinically actionable stratification tool that may inform guideline updates on TAVR patient selection and timing. From a Medical Affairs perspective, this framework aligns with the value proposition of early structural heart disease intervention by demonstrating that advanced cardiac remodeling dramatically amplifies post-procedural mortality risk. This evidence supports KOL engagement around echocardiographic screening protocols and multidisciplinary heart team decision-making to optimize treatment timing.

Serial cross-sectional assessment of glycaemic and blood pressure profiles at a KwaZulu-Natal tertiary hospital: A 5-year analysis (2021-2025).

Revue : South African Family Practice | Tier : NA | IF estimé : NA

Date de publication : 2026-09-04

Résultats principaux : This 5-year serial cross-sectional study conducted annual single-day audits from 2021 to 2025 at a KwaZulu-Natal tertiary hospital in South Africa, enrolling outpatient attendees and volunteering hospital staff (N=411 total records). Known hypertension was present in 149 of 334 assessable individuals (44.6%; 95% CI 39.4-50.0%), with blood pressure control achieved in only 41 of 107 hypertensive participants with reconcilable data (38.3%; 95% CI 29.7-47.8%). Known diabetes was identified in 24.1% of participants, and among those without known diabetes, 46.3% had dysglycaemia including 16.4% with diabetes-range HbA1c. In 2025, retroviral disease (RVD)-positive status was recorded in 28.7% of participants, with significant overlap between HIV, diabetes, and hypertension. Key limitations include convenience sampling, single-day audit methodology, and small annual sample sizes.

Implications Medical Affairs : These findings underscore persistently suboptimal blood pressure control rates (38.3%) in a resource-constrained South African hospital setting, relevant for pharmaceutical strategies targeting emerging cardiometabolic markets. The growing HIV-cardiometabolic multimorbidity overlap represents a critical unmet need that Medical Affairs teams should monitor when evaluating market access for antihypertensive agents in sub-Saharan Africa. KOL engagement should prioritize integrated chronic disease management programs addressing the diagnostic gap highlighted by high rates of undiagnosed dysglycaemia in this population.

Mental health outcomes after severe pregnancy complications: a scoping review.

Revue : BMC Pregnancy and Childbirth | Tier : NA | IF estimé : NA

Date de publication : 2026-08-12

Résultats principaux : This PRISMA-ScR scoping review searched MEDLINE, Embase, PsycInfo, and CINAHL from inception through August 2025, including 79 studies encompassing 68.1 million participants. Meta-analyses demonstrated that hypertensive disorders of pregnancy were associated with significantly increased odds of postpartum depression (OR 1.72, 95% CI 1.27-2.32, up to 6 pooled studies), and obstetric hemorrhage showed a comparable association (OR 1.73, 95% CI 1.27-2.39). Associations between composite severe maternal morbidity and postpartum depression were inconsistent; evidence for PTSD associations was suggestive but heterogeneous. Insufficient data prevented meta-analysis for anxiety disorders, substance abuse, suicidality, and psychosis. The review highlights a major gap in long-term psychiatric follow-up protocols for women experiencing hypertensive pregnancy disorders.

Implications Medical Affairs : The robust 72% increased odds of postpartum depression associated with hypertensive disorders of pregnancy has significant implications for how antihypertensives used in obstetric practice should be positioned in terms of their broader maternal mental health value proposition. Medical Affairs teams should monitor evolving evidence linking preeclampsia to downstream psychiatric outcomes, as this may influence guideline recommendations for postpartum psychiatric screening. This evidence base supports collaboration with obstetric and psychiatric KOLs to explore integrated care pathways and reinforces the need for research into long-term outcomes of antihypertensive treatment in pregnancy.

Patients with hypertension consistently showed earlier onset of diabetes: A UK Biobank survival analysis.

Revue : Molecular Medicine | Tier : NA | IF estimé : NA

Date de publication : 2026-08-05

Résultats principaux : This matched cohort analysis used UK Biobank data with a 6-month lag period to minimize reverse causation, including 76,439 participants (37,951 with hypertension and 38,488 matched controls) free of diabetes at baseline, with 4,315 incident T2D events (3,571 in hypertensive vs. 744 in controls). After full adjustment for adiposity, lipids, statin use, antihypertensive medication class, genetic principal components, and polygenic risk scores, hypertension remained independently associated with T2D onset (HR 1.30, p<0.001). Both a genome-wide polygenic risk score and a targeted multi-variant score independently predicted T2D risk and demonstrated significant interactions with hypertension. XGBoost survival machine learning achieved the highest discrimination (C-index 0.823, 95% CI 0.812-0.833), with consistent associations across multiple sensitivity analyses. Limitations include the observational design and UK-centric population that may limit global generalizability.

Implications Medical Affairs : The demonstration that hypertension independently predicts T2D onset with HR 1.30 regardless of genetic susceptibility strongly positions antihypertensive therapy as a component of diabetes prevention strategy and supports integrated cardiometabolic treatment approaches. This evidence strengthens the scientific rationale for single-pill combination products addressing both hypertension and metabolic risk, and may support expanded labeling discussions around cardiometabolic co-morbidity prevention. KOL strategy should incorporate this evidence to engage endocrinologists and primary care physicians who may not currently view hypertension control as a diabetes prevention tool.

Mineralocorticoid receptor antagonists for VEGF-pathway inhibitor-associated hypertension and proteinuria: biological rationale, clinical evidence, and a hypothesis-generating framework.

Revue : Medical Oncology | Tier : NA | IF estimé : NA

Date de publication : 2026-11-09

Résultats principaux : This narrative review synthesizes mechanistic, clinical, and cardiorenal evidence on mineralocorticoid receptor antagonists (MRAs) in VEGF-pathway inhibitor-associated hypertension and proteinuria. VEGF inhibition causes endothelial dysfunction through altered nitric oxide and endothelin signaling, producing heterogeneous glomerular injury including thrombotic microangiopathy with VEGF-ligand blockade and podocytopathy with VEGFR tyrosine kinase inhibitors. Current management rests on RAAS blockade, dihydropyridine CCBs, blood pressure monitoring, and proteinuria assessment. The biological rationale for MRAs includes blood pressure lowering, proteinuria reduction, and modulation of inflammatory/vascular pathways; however, oncology-specific evidence is limited to small nonrandomized datasets. MRAs should not be positioned as preferred third-line antihypertensives or prophylaxis without prospective oncology trial data, and may be considered only when hypertension co-exists with non-nephrotic proteinuria under strict monitoring.

Implications Medical Affairs : This review identifies a critical evidence gap in managing hypertension and proteinuria in cancer patients on VEGF-pathway inhibitors, representing a potential market expansion opportunity for newer selective MRAs such as finerenone in cardioncology. Medical Affairs teams should design medical education programs addressing cardioncology management guidelines around appropriate patient selection for MRA use during antineoplastic therapy. This framework supports engagement with onco-nephrologists and cardiovascular oncologists as a priority KOL segment for research partnership and investigator-initiated trial development.

Genome-Wide Association Study of Kidney Function Decline in ~100 000 Taiwanese Adults - A Taiwan Biobank Cohort.

Revue : Nephrology (Carlton, Vic.) | Tier : NA | IF estimé : NA

Date de publication : 2026-11

Résultats principaux : This GWAS utilized over 110,000 Taiwanese adults from the Taiwan Biobank community cohort with 28,426 participants having longitudinal follow-up data, examining five kidney function-related traits: BUN, serum creatinine, eGFR, urine microalbumin, and serum uric acid. Sixty-eight genetic loci were significantly associated (p=5x10-8) with at least two kidney-related traits; urine microalbumin was specifically interconnected with hypertension and diabetes pathways in genetic correlation network analysis. Among the 68 loci, 6 were significantly associated with kidney function decline in the follow-up cohort. Using a prospective cohort of 266 early CKD patients (stages I-III) with approximately 6 years of follow-up, the locus rs3925584 near the MPPED2 gene significantly predicted the slope of kidney function decline (p=0.0193). Limitations include the ethnically homogeneous Taiwanese population and the small prospective validation cohort.

Implications Medical Affairs : The identification of hypertension as a pathway-linked phenotype in microalbuminuria genetics reinforces the mechanistic link between blood pressure dysregulation and early kidney injury, supporting the positioning of antihypertensive agents with demonstrated nephroprotective effects within cardiorenal outcome frameworks. The discovery of the rs3925584/MPPED2 locus as a predictor of kidney function decline slope represents a potential biomarker opportunity relevant to cardiorenal outcome trials and companion diagnostic development. These findings support KOL engagement at the intersection of nephrology, hypertension research, and Asian genomic medicine.

Synergistic impact of insulin resistance and physical inactivity on cardiovascular disease: a cohort study.

Revue : BMC Cardiovascular Disorders | Tier : NA | IF estimé : NA

Date de publication : 2026-08-12

Résultats principaux : This prospective cohort analysis used the China Health and Retirement Longitudinal Study (CHARLS), computing the estimated glucose disposal rate (eGDR) from waist circumference, hypertension status, and HbA1c as a marker of insulin resistance. Physical activity was categorized as adequate (>=600 MET-min/week) versus insufficient, with incident cardiovascular disease (CVD) as the primary self-reported outcome. Lower eGDR (HR 0.930, 95% CI 0.886-0.977) and insufficient physical activity (HR 0.818, 95% CI 0.699-0.958) were each independently associated with higher CVD incidence. A synergistic eGDR-PA interaction was observed; combined optimization was associated with OR 0.412 (95% CI 0.186-0.890) for CVD in those without diabetes and OR 0.669 (95% CI 0.502-0.905) in those without CKD. Mediation analysis showed eGDR mediated 25.7% of physical activity's CVD effect, with population-attributable fraction analysis suggesting 19.6% of CVD cases preventable by combined optimization; limitations include self-reported CVD outcomes and baseline-only eGDR measurement.

Implications Medical Affairs : The synergistic relationship between insulin resistance and physical inactivity in driving CVD risk reinforces the importance of holistic cardiometabolic risk assessment in hypertensive patients, who frequently have concurrent insulin resistance captured by eGDR. Medical Affairs teams can leverage this evidence to position antihypertensive agents within broader cardiometabolic risk reduction frameworks, particularly in Asian markets where lifestyle co-intervention strategies are gaining traction. KOL engagement should focus on cardiovascular prevention specialists who can champion integrated insulin sensitivity measurement and physical activity promotion protocols within hypertension management guidelines.

Comparative associations of six dietary pattern indices with mortality among U.S. adults with sarcopenia: a nationally representative cohort study.

Revue : Nutrition and Metabolism | Tier : NA | IF estimé : NA

Date de publication : 2026-08-11

Résultats principaux : This nationally representative cohort study used NHANES data with linked mortality information, including 1,774 U.S. adults meeting FNIH sarcopenia criteria, with 545 all-cause deaths and 183 cardiovascular deaths during follow-up. Among six dietary indices evaluated (aMED, HEI-2020, DASH, PHDI, DII, KDR), the alternate Mediterranean Diet score (aMED) showed the most consistent inverse association with all-cause mortality (Q4 vs. Q1: HR 0.60, 95% CI 0.39-0.92) with evidence of a nonlinear dose-response relationship. DASH adherence showed a weaker but significant inverse association in the highest quartile (HR 0.68, 95% CI 0.47-0.97), while PHDI Q3-Q4 showed suggestive inverse associations (HR 0.70-0.71) that were attenuated after FDR correction. HEI-2020, DII, and KDR showed no robust associations with all-cause mortality. No dietary index showed consistent associations with cardiovascular-specific mortality. Limitations include cross-sectional diet assessment at baseline and the relatively small sarcopenia subsample limiting cardiovascular mortality analysis.

Implications Medical Affairs : The finding that DASH diet adherence is associated with lower all-cause mortality in sarcopenic adults is directly relevant to Medical Affairs communications on antihypertensive lifestyle modifications, as DASH was specifically designed to address hypertension and is embedded in major guidelines. This evidence supports integrating DASH dietary counseling as a complementary strategy alongside antihypertensive pharmacotherapy, reinforcing the value of non-pharmacological components in guideline-based hypertension management. KOL engagement with cardiologists, geriatricians, and clinical nutritionists is recommended to explore the intersection of sarcopenia, dietary quality, and cardiovascular mortality in aging hypertensive populations.
