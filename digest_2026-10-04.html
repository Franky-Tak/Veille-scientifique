Diet patterns are associated with progression of coronary artery calcification and differ across race/ethnicity: The Multi-Ethnic Study of Atherosclerosis.

Revue : American Journal of Preventive Cardiology | Tier : NA | IF estime : NA

Date de publication : 2026-08-09

Resultats principaux : This longitudinal study analyzed 3,182 participants from the Multi-Ethnic Study of Atherosclerosis (MESA) using food frequency questionnaires at two time points (2000-2002 and 2010-2012) to evaluate empirically derived dietary patterns and their association with coronary artery calcification (CAC) progression over approximately ten years. Four major dietary patterns were identified by principal components analysis: High Fat and Processed Meat; Vegetables and Fish; Whole Grains and Fruit; and Beans, Tomatoes, and Refined Grains. In fully adjusted multivariable models controlling for age, sex, race/ethnicity, hypertension, baseline CAC score, and other cardiovascular risk factors, the Whole Grains and Fruit pattern was inversely associated with incident CAC (OR 0.89, 95% CI 0.80-0.99; P=0.04), while the High Fat and Processed Meat pattern was positively associated with CAC progression (OR 1.20, 95% CI 1.01-1.43; P=0.03). In ordinal regression incorporating death as an outcome, the High Fat and Processed Meat pattern retained borderline-to-significant association with higher progression odds (OR=1.12, 95% CI 1.00-1.25; p=0.05), while Whole Grains and Fruit was associated with lower odds (OR=0.86, 95% CI 0.79-0.93). Importantly, race/ethnicity subgroup analysis revealed significant effect modification: associations between dietary patterns and CAC were pronounced among White participants but substantially attenuated among Black participants (P-interaction=0.009 and 0.006 respectively), underscoring the need for racially stratified nutritional guidance. A primary limitation is reliance on self-reported dietary assessment via food frequency questionnaire, which carries recall bias, and the observational design precludes causal inference.

Implications Medical Affairs : These findings reinforce dietary quality as a modifiable cardiovascular risk factor directly relevant to atherosclerosis prevention in the context of hypertension management, providing supporting evidence for integrated lifestyle modification recommendations in current cardiovascular guidelines. The significant race/ethnicity differential effects highlight the need for precision nutrition strategies in Medical Affairs communications, creating opportunities to position cardiovascular risk reduction programs tailored to diverse patient populations. From a KOL strategy perspective, this data supports engagement with preventive cardiologists, nutritional epidemiologists, and community health specialists to promote dietary quality as a cornerstone of comprehensive hypertension risk reduction, complementary to pharmacological therapy.

Association of Mixed Metal Exposures with Cardiovascular Disease: Insights from a Population-Based Study.

Revue : Cardiovascular Toxicology | Tier : NA | IF estime : NA

Date de publication : 2026-10-03

Resultats principaux : This cross-sectional population-based study analyzed 1,967 participants (211 cardiovascular disease cases) from the NHANES 2011-2016 survey to characterize the independent and joint associations of eight blood metals — copper (Cu), zinc (Zn), Cu/Zn ratio, selenium (Se), lead (Pb), cadmium (Cd), total mercury (HgT), and manganese (Mn) — with prevalent cardiovascular disease, including a designated hypertensive subgroup analysis. In fully adjusted quartile-based logistic regression, cadmium showed the strongest and most consistent positive association with CVD, with participants in the highest exposure quartile facing substantially higher odds (OR=2.29, 95% CI 1.37-3.87; P=0.002). Weighted quantile sum regression confirmed a significant positive metal mixture effect (OR=1.70, 95% CI 1.16-2.52; P=0.008) with Cd, Cu/Zn ratio, and Pb carrying the largest positive weights, while quantile-based g-computation yielded comparable results (OR=1.64, 95% CI 1.10-2.45; P=0.014). Restricted cubic spline analyses revealed linear dose-response relationships for Pb, Cd, and Mn with cardiovascular disease prevalence, and manganese contributed inversely to the mixture effect. Subgroup analyses demonstrated that hypertensive individuals constituted a particularly vulnerable population in whom metal exposures exerted amplified cardiovascular effects, alongside younger males, smokers, drinkers, the elderly, and low-BMI subjects. The cross-sectional design is the primary limitation, precluding causal inference, and single blood-draw metal quantification may not adequately represent chronic environmental exposure trajectories.

Implications Medical Affairs : These findings position environmental metal pollution — particularly cadmium and lead — as a clinically relevant co-risk factor that may potentiate cardiovascular risk in hypertensive patients, supporting the integration of environmental risk determinants into updated hypertension guidelines and patient risk stratification tools. For Medical Affairs teams, this study provides a scientific foundation for communications emphasizing the multifactorial nature of hypertension-related cardiovascular risk, enabling KOL engagement strategies that bridge cardiovascular pharmacology with environmental and occupational medicine. An actionable research priority is prospective evaluation of whether antihypertensive drug classes — particularly those with documented organ protection — differentially modify the cardiovascular risk attributable to heavy metal exposure in high-burden populations.

Integrated morphological and transcriptomic analysis of bone disorders in aldosterone-salt hypertension.

Revue : Hypertension Research | Tier : NA | IF estime : NA

Date de publication : 2026-10-02

Resultats principaux : This preclinical mechanistic study used male Sprague-Dawley rats subjected to four weeks of combined aldosterone infusion and 8% sodium chloride diet (Aldo-HS model) to investigate renal and skeletal consequences of salt-sensitive hypertension with mineralocorticoid excess, with or without esaxerenone — a selective nonsteroidal mineralocorticoid receptor (MR) antagonist. Micro-computed tomography, three-dimensional morphometry, and histomorphometry demonstrated marked trabecular bone loss in Aldo-HS rats, with fractional calcium excretion strongly and inversely correlated with trabecular bone volume (r=-0.74, p<0.001), establishing renal calcium wasting as the primary mechanistic driver. RNA sequencing and weighted gene co-expression network analysis identified suppression of a collagen-enriched transcriptomic module (Col1a1, Col1a2, Col11a1) as the dominant bone-specific signature, which was tightly linked to the degree of calcium wasting (r=-0.97, p<0.001) and overlapped with loci associated with bone mineral density in human genome-wide association studies. Esaxerenone treatment prevented trabecular bone loss and partially restored the collagen gene module expression, demonstrating pharmacological reversibility and confirming the bone-kidney axis as a therapeutically targetable pathway. A key limitation is the preclinical single-species experimental design; translation to clinical hypertension populations with aldosterone excess requires validation in prospective human studies incorporating longitudinal dual-energy X-ray absorptiometry monitoring.

Implications Medical Affairs : This study identifies the skeletal system as an underappreciated target organ in salt-sensitive mineralocorticoid hypertension, with the bone-kidney axis providing novel mechanistic rationale for including fracture risk assessment in hypertensive patients on high-sodium diets or with hyperaldosteronism. For Medical Affairs, the demonstrated skeletal protection by MR antagonism provides a compelling organ-protection narrative for nonsteroidal mineralocorticoid receptor antagonists such as esaxerenone and finerenone, supporting differentiated positioning beyond blood pressure and cardiorenal endpoints. Future clinical trial designs should incorporate bone mineral density as a secondary endpoint in MR antagonist studies, an evidence gap that Medical Affairs teams can champion in advisory board discussions and research grant partnerships with osteoporosis and nephrology KOLs.

Lifestyle strategies and nutraceuticals in chronic venous disease: an evidence-weighted synthesis and clinical positioning.

Revue : Clinical Nutrition | Tier : NA | IF estime : NA

Date de publication : 2026-09-18

Resultats principaux : This comprehensive systematic evidence synthesis evaluated the role of lifestyle strategies and venoactive nutraceuticals in chronic venous disease (CVD), including chronic venous insufficiency and venous leg ulcers, drawing on data from epidemiological studies, clinical cohorts, randomized trials, and meta-analyses within a pragmatic phenotype-stratified framework (disease severity C0 through C6). Excess adiposity demonstrated a consistent, modifiable association with higher chronic venous disease prevalence, greater clinical severity scores, and poorer venous ulcer healing outcomes, establishing weight management as the primary foundational conservative strategy with direct relevance to hypertension-associated cardiometabolic risk. Among evaluated nutraceuticals for symptomatic C0-C4 disease, flavonoid-based venoactive compounds — particularly micronized purified flavonoid fraction (MPFF) and Vitis vinifera seed extract (VVSE) — showed the most consistent improvements across outcomes including heaviness, pain, clinical severity scores, and quality of life metrics. In advanced disease with venous leg ulcers (C5-C6), adjunctive MPFF, Centella asiatica, and omega-3 fatty acids demonstrated potential benefit within compression-based therapeutic protocols, though heterogeneity across trials limits the certainty of evidence. The synthesis identifies a significant limitation in the predominantly associative nature of nutritional epidemiology data informing dietary pattern recommendations and substantial heterogeneity in trial designs, patient populations, and outcome measures that reduces precision of individual nutraceutical effect size estimates.

Implications Medical Affairs : This review underscores the shared cardiometabolic risk burden linking chronic venous disease and arterial hypertension — particularly through obesity and sedentary lifestyle — supporting integrated preventive messaging frameworks that Medical Affairs teams can deploy across both therapeutic areas. The evidence profile for flavonoid-based nutraceuticals in venous vasoprotection provides scientific context for exploring analogous mechanisms in hypertensive vasculopathy, opening interdisciplinary KOL engagement opportunities at the intersection of phlebology, nutrition science, and hypertension medicine. An actionable strategic insight is the identified need for harmonized clinical trials with standardized efficacy endpoints in the nutraceutical space, which Medical Affairs could champion through research consortium partnerships to strengthen the evidence base for lifestyle and nutraceutical co-interventions in cardiovascular risk reduction.
