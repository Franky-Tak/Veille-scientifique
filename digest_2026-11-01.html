Causal effects of physical activity and sedentary behaviors on hypertensive disorders of pregnancy: a two-sample Mendelian randomization study.

Revue : Hypertension in Pregnancy | Tier : NA | IF estime : NA

Date de publication : 2026-10-31

Resultats principaux : This two-sample Mendelian randomization study leveraged genetic instruments for 11 physical activity and sedentary behavior traits derived from the UK Biobank, with outcome data on gestational hypertension and pre-eclampsia sourced from the FinnGen biobank, providing robust causal inference beyond observational limitations. The primary endpoints were risk of gestational hypertension and pre-eclampsia as measured by genome-wide association study-derived genetic proxies. Light do-it-yourself (DIY) activities were causally associated with a lower risk of gestational hypertension (beta = -3.56, p = 0.002) and pre-eclampsia (beta = -3.54, p = 0.029), while aerobic exercise was significantly associated with reduced pre-eclampsia risk (beta = -3.56, p = 0.017). No statistically significant associations were found for vigorous physical activity, moderate-to-vigorous activity, or the three sedentary behaviors examined (computer use, television viewing, and driving). Key limitations include the ecological nature of MR inference, potential pleiotropy of genetic instruments, and the restriction of the outcome population to the FinnGen Finnish cohort, which may limit generalizability to other ethnicities.

Implications Medical Affairs : This study provides actionable causal evidence for the inclusion of low-intensity physical activity recommendations in antenatal care guidelines to reduce hypertensive disorders of pregnancy, potentially informing future updates to ESC/ESH and ISSHP guidance. From a Medical Affairs perspective, these findings support the positioning of lifestyle-based interventions as complementary to antihypertensive pharmacotherapy in pregnant populations, an underserved segment with limited drug options. Engagement with maternal-fetal medicine KOLs and obstetricians represents a strategic opportunity to advance awareness of non-pharmacological hypertension prevention in pregnancy.

Nursing Student Led Blood Pressure Education in a Community Mental Health Setting: A Pre Post Test Study.

Revue : International Journal of Mental Health Nursing | Tier : NA | IF estime : NA

Date de publication : 2026-12-01

Resultats principaux : This single-group pre-post test intervention study was conducted during community mental health clinical placements in New South Wales, Australia, involving 21 consumers living with mental illness and 44 nursing students. The primary endpoints were consumer hypertension knowledge (measured via the HELM scale) and student health education competence (measured via the I-CEpSE instrument). Consumer HELM scores increased significantly from a mean of 5.0 (SD 2.65) before the intervention to 6.9 (SD 2.69) after the activity (p < 0.001), demonstrating improved hypertension awareness. Nursing student I-CEpSE total scores rose from 210.95 (SD 28.81) to 257.27 (SD 30.22) (p < 0.001), reflecting enhanced competence in health education delivery. Key limitations include the absence of a control group, the small sample size, and the single-site design, which preclude causal attribution or broad generalizability.

Implications Medical Affairs : This study highlights the disproportionate cardiovascular risk burden in patients with mental illness and underscores the value of community-based hypertension literacy programs as part of a holistic cardiovascular risk management strategy. For Medical Affairs, there is an opportunity to engage with mental health service providers and nursing education programs to integrate hypertension awareness into routine mental health care pathways. This work supports the development of patient education materials targeting health-literacy-limited populations, a population segment often underrepresented in clinical trial recruitment.

Do Adverse Intrauterine Environments Affect Body Mass Index in Infants and Preschool Children?

Revue : American Journal of Human Biology | Tier : NA | IF estime : NA

Date de publication : 2026-11-01

Resultats principaux : This longitudinal cohort study enrolled 342 mother-child dyads from three hospitals in southern Brazil, allocated across five groups: gestational diabetes mellitus (DM), systemic arterial hypertension (SAH), maternal smoking, small for gestational age (SGA), and a control group, with assessments at seven time points from birth to preschool age. The primary endpoint was the BMI-for-age z-score trajectory from birth to preschool. Generalized estimating equations (GEE) identified a significant group-by-time interaction (p < 0.001), suggesting that adverse intrauterine exposures differentially affect BMI trajectories. Among girls, the DM group exhibited higher BMI-for-age z-scores at birth and elevated scores at preschool age compared to controls; SGA girls showed marked catch-up growth at 6 months. Among boys, SGA yielded higher BMI-for-age at preschool age compared to DM and control groups. Important limitations include unequal subgroup sizes, substantial loss to follow-up at preschool age, and the inability to disentangle postnatal confounders from intrauterine programming effects.

Implications Medical Affairs : These findings contribute to the Developmental Origins of Health and Disease (DOHaD) evidence base, supporting the notion that intrauterine hypertensive environments may program long-term cardiometabolic risk, relevant to future pediatric hypertension epidemiology. From a Medical Affairs standpoint, this research reinforces the importance of managing maternal hypertension not only for immediate obstetric outcomes but also for reducing downstream cardiovascular and metabolic disease burden in offspring. Collaboration with pediatric cardiology and neonatology KOLs could help translate these findings into early screening strategies and patient journey mapping for high-risk children.

Malignant Left Ventricular Hypertrophy: a Dynamic High-Risk Pre-Heart Failure Phenotype.

Revue : Current Hypertension Reports | Tier : NA | IF estime : NA

Date de publication : 2026-10-31

Resultats principaux : This narrative review from the University of Maryland synthesizes recent evidence on malignant left ventricular hypertrophy (LVH) as a distinct, high-risk phenotype within the Stage B/pre-heart failure spectrum, characterized by structural remodeling and biomarker elevation beyond conventional concentric LVH. The review's primary focus is on the prognostic significance of malignant LVH and its associations with incident atrial fibrillation, stroke, and cognitive impairment, conditions not typically captured in routine LVH staging. Key data suggest that intensive blood pressure lowering may reduce incident malignant LVH and potentially induce phenotype regression, adding therapeutic rationale for aggressive BP control. Novel management approaches discussed include direct antifibrotic agents, incretin analogues (GLP-1 receptor agonists), and established HF therapies repurposed for pre-HF regression. Limitations include the narrative review design, heterogeneity of biomarker and imaging criteria across cited studies, and the lack of prospective RCT data specifically targeting the malignant LVH phenotype.

Implications Medical Affairs : This review positions malignant LVH as a compelling therapeutic target in the pre-heart failure continuum, relevant to pharmaceutical portfolios that include antihypertensive and cardioprotective agents. Medical Affairs teams should monitor emerging data on GLP-1 receptor agonists and antifibrotic agents as potential differentiators in the hypertensive heart disease space, supporting expanded indications discussions with regulatory bodies. Engaging with heart failure and hypertension KOLs around this evolving phenotype could support targeted clinical development programs and evidence generation strategies for next-generation cardiovascular therapies.

Body mass index modifies the noise-hypertension link: a cross-sectional study in automobile workers.

Revue : PeerJ | Tier : NA | IF estime : NA

Date de publication : 2026-10-27

Resultats principaux : This cross-sectional study examined 21.6% hypertension prevalence among workers at an automobile manufacturing plant in China, using modified Poisson regression to estimate prevalence ratios (PRs) for the association between occupational noise exposure duration and hypertension risk, with BMI as a continuous effect modifier. The primary endpoint was hypertension prevalence, with occupational noise exposure duration as the main predictor; the analysis included demographic, occupational history, and medical examination data. Each additional year of noise exposure increased hypertension risk by approximately 11.9% (PR 1.119, 95% CI 1.047-1.196) at a BMI of 18.5 kg/m2, attenuating to 4.1% (PR 1.041, p significant) at BMI 24 kg/m2, and becoming non-significant above BMI 27.02 kg/m2. A significant negative interaction between continuous BMI and noise exposure duration was found (PR 0.987, 95% CI 0.979-0.995, p = 0.001), confirmed in sensitivity analyses using binary BMI classification. Limitations include the cross-sectional design precluding causal inference, reliance on occupational records for noise exposure (without personal dosimetry), and the single-site sample from one Chinese automobile plant.

Implications Medical Affairs : This study illuminates a novel interaction between occupational noise and body composition in hypertension risk, which has implications for occupational health guidelines and may inform the design of targeted workplace cardiovascular screening programs. From a Medical Affairs perspective, these findings support the case for stratified hypertension risk assessment in occupational settings, potentially opening avenues for partnerships with occupational health stakeholders in industrial sectors. The interaction with BMI also reinforces the mechanistic rationale for anti-obesity interventions as part of multifactorial hypertension prevention strategies.

Central obesity and blood pressure burden among Chinese children and adolescents with normal body mass index: a repeated-measures analysis of the China health and nutrition survey.

Revue : Frontiers in Public Health | Tier : NA | IF estime : NA

Date de publication : 2026-10-16

Resultats principaux : This study analyzed repeated examination data from 4,841 Chinese children and adolescents aged 7-17 years (7,192 complete-case observations) with normal BMI from the China Health and Nutrition Survey (1993-2011), using generalized estimating equations (GEE) with robust standard errors. Central obesity was defined by a waist-to-height ratio (WHtR) >= 0.50 and was present in 4.3% of normal-BMI observations; the primary outcomes were above-normal and high blood pressure classified by Chinese age-, sex-, and height-specific references. In fully adjusted models, central obesity was associated with significantly higher odds of above-normal blood pressure (OR 1.43, 95% CI 1.07-1.90, p = 0.016) and high blood pressure (OR 1.54, 95% CI 1.07-2.21, p = 0.022), with standardized absolute risk differences of 5.1 and 3.5 percentage points, respectively. Associations were generally consistent across sex, urbanicity, and survey periods; exploratory longitudinal models were directionally compatible but limited by sparse exposed observations. Limitations include the ecological span of the dataset (1993-2011), potential measurement variability in serial anthropometry, and limited generalizability beyond Chinese pediatric populations.

Implications Medical Affairs : This study reinforces evidence that BMI alone is insufficient for pediatric hypertension risk stratification and that waist-based measures should be incorporated into routine screening, with potential implications for revising pediatric cardiovascular risk assessment tools. From a Medical Affairs perspective, these data support advocacy for multi-parameter childhood obesity assessments that could identify early intervention opportunities, including lifestyle modification and pharmacological treatment in severe cases. KOL engagement in pediatric cardiology and endocrinology is warranted to promote adoption of central adiposity metrics in clinical practice guidelines.

Association between the triacylglycerol-glucose index, TyG-BMI and hypertension in children and adolescents: a cross-sectional study with machine-learning-based risk stratification.

Revue : Frontiers in Endocrinology | Tier : NA | IF estime : NA

Date de publication : 2026-10-16

Resultats principaux : This large cross-sectional study enrolled 25,886 children and adolescents aged 7-17 years from 18 prefecture-level cities in Henan Province, China, of whom 6.95% (n=1,798) had hypertension defined as BP at or above the 95th percentile for age, sex, and height. Both the TyG index and TyG-BMI were independently associated with hypertension in all four logistic regression models, with TyG-BMI showing stronger associations (Model IV OR 2.109, 95% CI 1.985-2.241) compared to TyG (Model IV OR 1.306, 95% CI 1.145-1.489) per 1-SD increment. Eleven machine-learning models were trained on a 70:30 stratified split, yielding moderate discrimination (best AUC 0.7544 for TyG); adding TyG or TyG-BMI to a baseline model containing BMI increased AUC by only approximately 0.003. Additive interaction analyses showed all six RERI estimates were positive with CIs excluding zero, though no multiplicative interaction survived Bonferroni correction. Limitations include the cross-sectional design, single-province sampling, inability to establish causality, and the absence of fasting glucose in some participants.

Implications Medical Affairs : This study positions TyG-BMI as a potentially superior insulin resistance surrogate over the TyG index for pediatric hypertension risk stratification in large population studies, which has relevance for screening protocol development in high-risk pediatric populations. Medical Affairs teams supporting metabolic and antihypertensive portfolios should note the growing evidence linking insulin resistance markers to early-onset hypertension, which could support broader market access arguments for early therapeutic intervention. Future RCTs or interventional studies targeting TyG-BMI as a composite biomarker endpoint may help define drug efficacy in insulin-resistant hypertensive youth.

Dose-response association and predictive value of plasma aldosterone concentration for cardiometabolic multimorbidity in patients with hypertension: a retrospective cohort study.

Revue : Frontiers in Endocrinology | Tier : NA | IF estime : NA

Date de publication : 2026-10-16

Resultats principaux : This retrospective cohort study included 7,677 hospitalized hypertensive patients without cardiometabolic multimorbidity (CMM) at baseline, recruited between January 2010 and January 2026, with a mean follow-up of 3.52 years. The primary endpoint was incident CMM, defined as the co-occurrence of at least two cardiometabolic conditions; 1,949 incident CMM cases were documented during follow-up. Multivariable Cox regression showed a 2% increase in CMM hazard per 1-ng/dL increment in plasma aldosterone concentration (PAC) (HR 1.02, 95% CI 1.02-1.03), with highest-quartile patients showing 65% greater risk (HR 1.65, 95% CI 1.45-1.87) versus lowest-quartile patients. Restricted cubic spline analyses identified a nonlinear association with an inflection at approximately 15.34 ng/dL, and a significant interaction with serum potassium was observed (p < 0.05). Adding PAC to the baseline model increased time-dependent AUC from 0.678 to 0.735 (p < 0.001), with NRI of 0.372 and IDI of 0.038, and Boruta feature selection confirmed PAC as an important predictor.

Implications Medical Affairs : These findings strengthen the case for routine plasma aldosterone concentration monitoring in hypertensive patients as an early biomarker for cardiometabolic multimorbidity risk stratification, which could reframe how mineralocorticoid receptor antagonists are positioned in multimorbidity prevention strategies. From a Medical Affairs standpoint, this study provides supportive evidence for aldosterone pathway-targeted therapies (including finerenone and newer MRA agents) to be discussed in the context of CMM prevention rather than solely end-organ protection. KOL engagement should focus on cardiologists and endocrinologists specializing in aldosteronism to accelerate translation of PAC-guided risk stratification into clinical practice.

The interconnections between novel adiposity indices and cardiometabolic multimorbidity in Chinese adults: a population-based cross-sectional study.

Revue : BMC Cardiovascular Disorders | Tier : NA | IF estime : NA

Date de publication : 2026-09-01

Resultats principaux : This cross-sectional analysis used data from 10,383 participants in the China Health and Retirement Longitudinal Study (CHARLS), examining the associations of seven novel adiposity indices with CMM, defined as the presence of at least two of the following: hypertension, diabetes, coronary heart disease, or stroke. All seven indices showed significant positive associations with CMM in fully adjusted models; the CTI (CRP-triglyceride-glucose index) showed the strongest per-1-SD increment association (OR 1.57, 95% CI 1.49-1.65), followed by LAP (OR 1.49) and AIP (OR 1.44). Significant non-linear relationships with inflection points were identified for LAP, VAI, BRI, AIP, RC, and CI, while CTI showed a linear pattern; all AUCs were below 0.70, indicating modest standalone screening utility. Variance decomposition analysis suggested CTI accounted for 26.50% to 43.13% of shared statistical variance between other adiposity indices and CMM. Limitations include the cross-sectional design precluding causal inference, possible selection bias from voluntary study participation, and the restriction to Chinese middle-aged and older adults.

Implications Medical Affairs : This study highlights the utility of composite metabolic-inflammatory adiposity indices (particularly CTI and LAP) over simple BMI for CMM risk stratification, which has direct relevance for patient selection and subgroup analyses in cardiovascular outcome trials. Medical Affairs can use these findings to support the value of multi-biomarker risk profiling in hypertensive patients with coexistent metabolic disorders, reinforcing the rationale for combination therapy approaches. There is a strategic opportunity to engage with health economics and outcomes research (HEOR) teams to model the cost-effectiveness of CTI-guided screening algorithms for early cardiometabolic intervention.

Revue : BMC Cardiovascular Disorders | Tier : NA | IF estime : NA

Date de publication : 2026-08-04

Resultats principaux : The SNAP2 trial is a multi-centre, unblinded, randomised controlled trial protocol recruiting at least 628 participants across England who experienced any hypertensive disorder of pregnancy, randomising them within 7 days post-birth to a digitally-supported self-management intervention or usual care. The primary outcome is the difference in mean daytime ambulatory diastolic BP between groups, measured at 22-39 weeks postpartum; secondary outcomes include systolic BP measures, maternal quality-of-life, medication adherence, adverse events, and hospital readmissions up to one year post-birth. The trial incorporates a cross-cutting health equity theme to address the disproportionate burden of postnatal hypertension in women from minoritised ethnic and socioeconomically disadvantaged backgrounds. A qualitative process evaluation and health economic analysis are embedded components, enabling implementation and cost-effectiveness assessment. Limitations inherent to the design include lack of blinding, potential differential dropout, and the challenge of standardizing clinician-guided medication titration across multiple sites.

Implications Medical Affairs : The SNAP2 trial addresses a critical evidence gap in postnatal hypertension management, a condition associated with long-term cardiovascular risk that currently lacks robust guideline-informed care pathways; its results could directly influence national and international postnatal care guidelines. From a Medical Affairs perspective, this trial represents an important research investment in digital health-assisted antihypertensive adherence, aligning with the growing evidence base for remote blood pressure monitoring as a complement to pharmacotherapy. Early engagement with the SNAP2 principal investigators as KOLs could position the company at the forefront of conversations around digital-antihypertensive co-management models in the postpartum setting.

Cardiometabolic risk factors and Mediterranean diet adherence in patients with premature atherosclerotic cardiovascular disease in Iran.

Revue : Journal of Diabetes and Metabolic Disorders | Tier : NA | IF estime : NA

Date de publication : 2026-10-29

Resultats principaux : This cross-sectional study enrolled 425 adults with premature atherosclerotic cardiovascular disease (ASCVD) from a tertiary cardiovascular referral center in Alborz Province, Iran, using consecutive sampling on scheduled data-collection days. Mediterranean diet adherence was assessed with a culturally adapted 13-item Mediterranean Diet Adherence Screener (MEDAS), and cardiometabolic risk factors were defined by established clinical criteria. Among participants (70.1% male, mean age 50.23 +/- 6.54 years), excess weight prevalence was 81.6% and metabolic syndrome ranged from 66.4-82.8% across five diagnostic criteria; women had higher prevalence of low HDL-C and elevated LDL-C, while men had substantially higher smoking rates. In multivariable logistic regression, male sex was the only significant predictor of lower Mediterranean diet adherence (OR 0.57, 95% CI 0.33-0.99, p = 0.050); age, BMI, SES, tobacco exposure, and physical activity were not significantly associated. Sensitivity analysis showed those with extremely premature ASCVD (age < 40) had lower prevalences of elevated triglycerides, low HDL-C, elevated BP, and metabolic syndrome compared to standard premature ASCVD patients.

Implications Medical Affairs : This study reveals high cardiometabolic risk burden and suboptimal dietary adherence in a Middle Eastern premature ASCVD population, highlighting the need for sex-tailored secondary prevention programs that address both dietary behavior and hypertension management in younger patients. Medical Affairs teams should consider this evidence when developing patient support programs and educational tools for secondary prevention in hypertensive patients with early-onset cardiovascular disease, particularly in Middle Eastern and similar populations. Engagement with lipidology and preventive cardiology KOLs in Iran and the broader MENA region could support regional advisory board activities focused on personalized secondary prevention strategies.

Dietary patterns and cardiovascular risk in adult survivors of childhood cancer: findings from the SCCSS-Nutrition Study.

Revue : Journal of Cancer Survivorship | Tier : NA | IF estime : NA

Date de publication : 2026-10-31

Resultats principaux : This study analyzed 802 adult childhood cancer survivors (CCSs) from the Swiss Childhood Cancer Survivor Study (SCCSS), with a median age of 35 years (IQR 29-41), who completed a food frequency questionnaire assessing 52 food groups and underwent factor and hierarchical cluster analyses to identify dietary patterns. Five dietary patterns were identified: Mediterranean-inspired (22%), Western indulgence (38%), Western sweet & fatty (26%), restrained indulgence (9%), and plant-based & alcohol-rich (6%). Cardiovascular disease risk profiles were classified as CVD, CVD risk factors (including obesity, hypertension, diabetes, and high cholesterol), or CVD risk-free; no statistically significant associations were observed between dietary patterns and CVD risk profiles in adjusted analyses. Male sex, younger age, German-speaking region, smoking, and cohabitation were associated with unhealthy dietary patterns, while higher socioeconomic position was associated with the plant-based & alcohol-rich pattern. Limitations include potential recall bias from self-reported dietary data, cross-sectional assessment of diet-disease associations, and possible survivorship bias in the long-term cohort.

Implications Medical Affairs : The absence of a detectable association between dietary patterns and CVD risk profiles in this population suggests that established CVD risk factors, including hypertension, may be driven more by treatment-related late effects than by modifiable lifestyle behaviors in CCSs, which has implications for survivorship guideline development. For Medical Affairs, this finding underscores the distinct cardiovascular risk profile of oncology survivors as a special population that may require disease-specific hypertension management approaches rather than general lifestyle recommendations. KOL partnerships with late-effects specialists in pediatric oncology could identify opportunities to study antihypertensive therapies in treatment-related cardiovascular disease in cancer survivors.

The Association Between Psoriasis and Cardiovascular Comorbidity: A Narrative Review.

Revue : Galen Medical Journal | Tier : NA | IF estime : NA

Date de publication : 2026-08-27

Resultats principaux : This narrative review synthesizes current evidence on the association between psoriasis and cardiovascular comorbidity, focusing on disease severity, systemic inflammation, psoriatic arthritis, pediatric psoriasis, and cardiovascular risk factors including hypertension, diabetes, obesity, and arrhythmia. The review highlights that mild psoriasis may not consistently increase cardiovascular event risk, whereas moderate-to-severe psoriasis is more frequently associated with adverse cardiovascular outcomes including myocardial infarction, stroke, atherosclerotic cardiovascular disease, and cardiovascular death. Pediatric psoriasis has been specifically associated with obesity, hypertension, diabetes, arrhythmia, and other comorbidities, underscoring the early onset of cardiometabolic risk. Shared inflammatory pathways (particularly TNF-alpha, IL-17, and IL-23 axes), endothelial dysfunction, metabolic disturbances, and traditional cardiovascular risk factors including hypertension are proposed as mechanistic contributors. Limitations include the narrative review methodology, selection bias in the literature reviewed, and lack of meta-analytic quantification of effect sizes.

Implications Medical Affairs : This review reinforces the importance of systemic cardiovascular risk assessment, including hypertension screening, in all patients with moderate-to-severe psoriasis, supporting the value of integrated dermatology-cardiology care models that could increase antihypertensive therapy initiation in this high-risk group. Medical Affairs teams with cardiovascular or immunology portfolios should consider the psoriasis-hypertension comorbidity link as a basis for real-world evidence generation, examining BP outcomes in biologics-treated psoriasis populations. Collaborative KOL engagement with both dermatology and preventive cardiology thought leaders could support the development of comorbidity management algorithms and patient registry initiatives.

Scoping Review of Predictors and Risk Factors for Acute Myocardial Infarction in Patients with Chronic Kidney Disease.

Revue : Galen Medical Journal | Tier : NA | IF estime : NA

Date de publication : 2026-02-02

Resultats principaux : This systematic review following PRISMA guidelines synthesized data from 20 studies (ranging from 54 to 911,360 participants) identified through searches in PubMed, Embase, Scopus, Web of Science, and Cochrane Library up to August 14, 2025, examining predictors of acute myocardial infarction (AMI) in adults with chronic kidney disease (CKD). Hypertension was identified as one of the key AMI predictors alongside dyslipidemia, inflammatory biomarkers (CRP, IL-6), cardiac biomarkers (NT-proBNP, hs-cTnT), lower eGFR, albuminuria, advanced CKD stage, prior AMI, and older age. Novel composite risk indices, including the triglyceride-glucose (TyG) index and TyG-BMI, demonstrated high predictive accuracy for AMI in CKD, as did established risk scores (GRACE, PRECISE-DAPT). Diagnostic and therapeutic challenges in CKD were highlighted, including altered biomarker profiles and underutilization of interventions such as percutaneous coronary intervention (PCI). Limitations include narrative synthesis due to heterogeneity, potential publication bias, and variability in CKD staging and AMI definitions across included studies.

Implications Medical Affairs : This review underscores hypertension as a key modifiable AMI predictor in CKD patients, a population with notoriously high cardiovascular mortality, and highlights significant therapeutic underutilization that represents an opportunity for both clinical practice improvement and pharmaceutical market development. From a Medical Affairs perspective, this evidence supports the need for targeted antihypertensive trials in CKD populations, particularly evaluating agents with renoprotective properties (RAAS inhibitors, SGLT2 inhibitors, MRA) that could simultaneously address hypertension and cardiorenal endpoints. KOL engagement with nephrologist and cardiologist networks specializing in cardiorenal syndrome is recommended to advocate for integrated BP management pathways in CKD care guidelines.
