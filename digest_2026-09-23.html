Development and Validation of a Machine Learning-Based Model for Predicting All-Cause Mortality Risk in Patients With Type 2 Diabetes Mellitus Combined With Hypertension: National Cohort Study.

Revue : JMIR Medical Informatics | Tier : NA | IF estime : NA

Date de publication : 2026-09-22

Resultats principaux : This large national cohort study leveraged NHANES data from 1999 to 2018 linked to mortality records through 2019, enrolling 2,428 adults aged 20 years or older with concurrent T2DM and hypertension (mean age 62.05 years, SE 0.33; 50.15% female). The primary endpoint was all-cause mortality; 719 deaths (29.6%) occurred over a median follow-up of 6.75 years (IQR 4.20-8.90 years). Five machine learning models were compared, with the random forest model achieving the highest AUC of 0.873 (95% CI 0.856-0.891), substantially outperforming logistic regression (AUC 0.783), extreme gradient boosting (AUC 0.792), light GBM (AUC 0.785), and decision tree (AUC 0.732). The most important predictive features identified were age, race, chronic kidney disease, BMI, and blood urea nitrogen. A key limitation is the cross-sectional nature of NHANES predictor data and lack of longitudinal covariate updating, which may underestimate risk changes over time.

Implications Medical Affairs : This validated predictive model for T2DM-hypertension comorbidity sets a precedent for integrating AI-based risk stratification into hypertension management guidelines, potentially reshaping how clinicians identify high-risk patients requiring intensified antihypertensive treatment. For Medical Affairs, this highlights an opportunity to position antihypertensives within a precision medicine framework targeting diabetic-hypertensive patients with elevated renal risk markers, particularly those with CKD and elevated BUN. KOL engagement should prioritize endocrinologists, nephrologists, and cardiologists interested in digital health tools and risk-stratified prescribing algorithms.

Theme 6 : Clinical Trials and Guidelines (1 article)

Glabridin content and metabolic effects of liquorice confectionaries: A secondary analysis of a randomized crossover trial in young adults.

Revue : PloS one | Tier : NA | IF estime : NA

Date de publication : 2026-09-22

Resultats principaux : This secondary analysis of a randomized 2x2 crossover trial enrolled 28 healthy young adults who consumed liquorice or a control product in alternating 2-week periods, investigating whether glabridin — a bioactive component associated with anti-inflammatory and glucose-lowering properties — could offset the well-documented hypertensive effects of glycyrrhizic acid present in liquorice. Primary endpoints included plasma glucose, glycated hemoglobin A1c, lipids, calcium ion, parathyroid hormone, and high-sensitivity C-reactive protein (hsCRP); glabridin content was measured at 0.25 ± 0.14 µg per mg via LC-MSMS analysis. The only statistically significant difference was an attenuation of hsCRP elevation: during the liquorice period, hsCRP increased by 32.5% (95% CI -9.7 to 74.6%) compared to 311.5% (95% CI -40.8 to 663.7%) during the control period (P=0.045). No significant effects on dysglycemia, lipids, or calcium metabolism were observed, and all other metabolic endpoints were neutral. The primary limitations are the small sample size (n=28), short intervention duration (2 weeks), and use of whole liquorice rather than purified glabridin, making it difficult to isolate glabridin-specific effects.

Implications Medical Affairs : This trial reinforces the clinical relevance of dietary triggers of blood pressure elevation, including widely consumed food products like liquorice, which supports patient education messaging in hypertension prevention and management programs. From a Medical Affairs perspective, this underscores the complexity of lifestyle management in hypertension and provides evidence to support clinical guidelines recommending avoidance of liquorice-containing products in patients with elevated cardiovascular risk. While direct product positioning implications are modest, this data can support KOL-driven educational initiatives on dietary factors that complicate antihypertensive therapy adherence and may attenuate treatment efficacy.

Causes of death across the life course in the Association of Southeast Asian Nations (ASEAN): a systematic analysis for the Global Burden of Disease Study 2023.

Revue : The Lancet. Public health | Tier : NA | IF estime : NA

Date de publication : 2026-10

Resultats principaux : This systematic analysis, conducted as part of the Global Burden of Diseases, Injuries, and Risk Factors Study (GBD) 2023, examined life expectancy, probability of death from birth to age 70 years (70q0), cause-specific mortality, and risk-attributable mortality across all 10 ASEAN member states from 1990 to 2023. In 2023, regional life expectancy was 70.1 years (95% UI 69.6-70.7) for males and 76.2 years (95% UI 75.7-76.7) for females, with marked heterogeneity between countries (87.5 years for females in Singapore versus 62.1 years for males in Myanmar). Neonatal disorders were the leading cause of infant mortality, road injuries dominated male adolescent and early adult mortality (25.0% of deaths in males aged 15-19 years), and non-communicable diseases including ischaemic heart disease and stroke emerged as the leading causes in mid-adulthood. High blood pressure was identified as the prevailing modifiable risk factor among adult females across the region, while tobacco use was dominant for adult males, highlighting divergent gender-specific cardiovascular risk profiles. A key limitation is the reliance on statistical modeling to estimate cause-of-death data in countries with incomplete vital registration systems.

Implications Medical Affairs : The identification of high blood pressure as the leading attributable risk factor for mortality in adult females across ASEAN — a region representing over 650 million people — establishes a compelling epidemiological foundation for targeted antihypertensive therapy expansion and public health intervention in a major growth market for cardiovascular pharmaceuticals. From a Medical Affairs standpoint, this GBD data directly supports the case for female-focused hypertension screening programs and specific drug positioning for women in Southeast Asia, a strategically important and rapidly growing pharmaceutical market. KOL engagement with regional cardiovascular epidemiologists, public health leaders, and health ministry advisors across ASEAN could be leveraged for market access, reimbursement advocacy, and designing female-specific hypertension outcome trials.

The relationship between plasma long-chain fatty acids and hypertension and its underlying molecular mechanisms.

Revue : Nutrition research (New York, N.Y.) | Tier : NA | IF estime : NA

Date de publication : 2026-09-01

Resultats principaux : This cross-sectional study investigated associations between plasma long-chain fatty acid (LCFA) concentrations and hypertension in 2,092 middle-aged and older Chinese adults using stepwise adjusted logistic regression and Bayesian kernel machine regression (BKMR) for single and mixed-exposure analyses, respectively, with all models adjusted for sex, age, sociodemographic characteristics, lifestyle factors, and BMI. Myristoleic acid, arachidonic acid, erucic acid, and docosahexaenoic acid (DHA) were significantly and inversely associated with hypertension, while eicosapentaenoic acid (EPA) and docosatetraenoic acid showed positive associations. BKMR mixed-exposure analysis demonstrated a modest overall inverse effect of the fatty acid mixture, with the key contributors being these specific fatty acids. Network pharmacology and molecular docking analysis identified MAPK3, PTGS2, and ESR1 as hub targets implicated in inflammation, immune responses, lipid metabolism, and vascular function as potential mechanistic pathways. A notable limitation includes the cross-sectional design precluding causal inferences and reliance on a single time-point plasma measurement, which may not reflect habitual dietary fatty acid intake.

Implications Medical Affairs : The identification of specific plasma fatty acids (particularly DHA and arachidonic acid) as protective against hypertension and the delineation of molecular targets (MAPK3, PTGS2, ESR1) provides mechanistic support for dietary omega-3 supplementation as an adjunct to pharmacological hypertension management, which may enhance the profile of combination lifestyle-pharmacotherapy strategies. From a Medical Affairs perspective, this data can support positioning discussions around combination therapy protocols or structured lifestyle modification programs incorporating dietary fatty acid optimization alongside antihypertensive agents, particularly in Asian populations. KOL strategy should consider engaging researchers at the intersection of nutrition science and cardiovascular pharmacology to design prospective trials exploring the synergistic effects of fatty acid profiles and antihypertensive drug response.

Association Between Estimated 24-Hour Urinary Sodium and Potassium Excretion and Cardiovascular Risk and Events: Findings from the Isfahan Cohort Study.

Revue : International journal of preventive medicine | Tier : NA | IF estime : NA

Date de publication : 2026-08-28

Resultats principaux : This historical cohort study from the Isfahan Cohort Study included 1,953 Iranian adults with random morning urine samples collected in 2013 and followed for two years, with 24-hour sodium and potassium excretion estimated using the Tanaka formula; Cox proportional hazards models were adjusted for demographic, clinical, lifestyle, and medication variables to assess associations with incident cardiovascular events. Higher sodium excretion was significantly associated with male sex, higher BMI, elevated blood pressure, and higher plasma sodium and potassium levels. No statistically significant associations were found between sodium or potassium excretion tertiles and incident CV events in fully adjusted models; however, hazard ratios for higher sodium tertiles were consistently greater than 1.0, suggesting a nonsignificant trend toward increased cardiovascular risk. Potassium excretion exhibited a nonlinear pattern with the middle tertile showing the highest hazard ratio, indicating a complex dose-response relationship. The short two-year follow-up period is a critical limitation that substantially reduces statistical power to detect cardiovascular event associations, and spot urine-based estimation of 24-hour excretion introduces measurement error.

Implications Medical Affairs : This regional cohort study from Iran confirms that estimated urinary sodium excretion is linked to cardiometabolic risk factors including elevated blood pressure, even when short-term CV event outcomes do not reach statistical significance, reinforcing the importance of dietary sodium restriction as a cornerstone of hypertension prevention guidelines. The nonsignificant but directionally consistent trend toward increased cardiovascular risk with higher sodium excretion supports Medical Affairs messaging around sodium reduction strategies as a complementary intervention to pharmacological antihypertensive therapy. This type of regional Middle East and North Africa cohort data creates an opportunity to engage KOLs in Iran and the broader MENA region on sodium intake monitoring as part of a holistic hypertension management framework, supporting market development in an underserved cardiovascular market.
