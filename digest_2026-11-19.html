Urinary Sodium-to-Potassium Ratio in Hypertension: From Epidemiologic Marker to Clinical Tool?

Revue : Current Hypertension Reports | Tier : NA | IF estime : NA

Date de publication : 2026 Nov 18

Resultats principaux : This narrative review evaluates whether the urinary sodium-to-potassium (Na/K) ratio can transition from an epidemiologic biomarker of dietary electrolyte balance to a clinically useful tool in hypertension care, drawing on population-based studies, repeated-measurement designs, and cohorts of treated hypertensive patients. Higher urinary Na/K ratios were consistently associated with higher blood pressure, greater hypertension risk, poorer BP control, and selected adverse cardiovascular-kidney outcomes across the reviewed literature, with effect sizes strengthening when measurements were repeated over time. Data from salt-substitution trials further support the physiological rationale that modifying the sodium-to-potassium balance in the diet meaningfully reduces blood pressure, lending biological plausibility to the biomarker. Emerging consensus recommendations and self-monitoring study results suggest practical implementation pathways, including home BP monitoring coupled with urinary Na/K assessment as an integrated self-management strategy. Critical limitations precluding immediate clinical translation include heterogeneous measurement protocols, absence of universally validated thresholds across diverse ethnic populations, lack of interventional trials using the Na/K ratio as a treatment target, and insufficient safety data in high-risk groups such as those with advanced CKD.

Implications Medical Affairs : This review reinforces the growing evidence base for dietary electrolyte management as a modifiable cornerstone of hypertension control, with direct relevance to positioning potassium-sparing antihypertensive combinations and dietary counseling support programs for patients. For Medical Affairs, the urinary Na/K ratio represents an emerging patient engagement and monitoring tool that could strengthen value propositions for antihypertensive therapies targeting dietary adherence, particularly in patients with resistant or uncontrolled hypertension. Collaboration with KOLs in nephrology and clinical nutrition to design interventional trials testing Na/K ratio-guided blood pressure management would be a high-impact strategic priority for differentiated scientific positioning.

Subclinical Left Ventricular Hypertrophy Among Emergency Department Patients With Elevated Blood Pressure.

Revue : Academic Emergency Medicine | Tier : NA | IF estime : NA

Date de publication : 2026 Nov

Resultats principaux : This prospective cross-sectional study enrolled 155 adults aged 18-65 years presenting to an academic emergency department (ED) with asymptomatic elevated blood pressure (SBP ≥140 or DBP ≥90 mmHg on two sequential measurements), who were assessed for left ventricular hypertrophy (LVH) via focused point-of-care cardiac ultrasound. Nearly one-third of participants (29.7%, 95% CI 22.5-36.9%) met criteria for LVH on indexed left ventricular mass assessment, underscoring a high prevalence of subclinical target organ damage in this underserved ED population. Female sex was independently associated with LVH (OR 2.44, 95% CI 1.14-5.22, p=0.021), as was a prior diagnosis of hypertension (OR 2.47, 95% CI 1.14-5.39, p=0.023), pointing to cumulative BP burden and sex-specific cardiac remodeling as key risk determinants. Critically, 18.3% of patients without a prior hypertension diagnosis demonstrated LVH (p=0.004), confirming substantial undetected target organ damage in individuals who had not previously engaged with the healthcare system. Study limitations include the convenience sample design from a single academic center, cross-sectional methodology precluding causal inference, and the potential for operator-dependent variability in point-of-care ultrasound measurements.

Implications Medical Affairs : These findings underscore the clinical and public health urgency of systematic cardiovascular risk stratification in ED populations with elevated BP, particularly among women and those with limited healthcare access, who harbor disproportionate subclinical organ damage. For Medical Affairs, this evidence supports real-world data programs quantifying the downstream cardiovascular consequences of undiagnosed and untreated hypertension in underserved populations, strengthening the value narrative for timely antihypertensive treatment initiation. Strategic engagement with emergency medicine societies and cardiovascular imaging KOLs could catalyze guideline development integrating point-of-care echocardiography into hypertension care pathways, creating advocacy opportunities aligned with broader unmet need messaging.

Exploring the impact of different definitions of blood pressure control prevalence among adults receiving integrated HIV-hypertension services in Lilongwe.

Revue : Malawi Medical Journal | Tier : NA | IF estime : NA

Date de publication : 2026 Sep

Resultats principaux : This retrospective study at a large HIV clinic in Lilongwe, Malawi, evaluated how the choice of BP control definition affects reported hypertension control rates among 167 adults co-managed for HIV and hypertension with two or more antihypertensive drugs in 79.0% of cases. Using a cross-sectional single-visit BP measurement (approach A), hypertension control was estimated at 65.3%, while applying a longitudinal definition based on two or three BP readings over one year (approach B) yielded a significantly lower estimate of 55.7%, a difference of 9.6 percentage points (95% CI 1.7-17.5%, p=0.017). The cohort was predominantly female (62.3%), with high rates of overweight/obesity (60.4%), near-universal viral suppression (98.7%), and a mean age of 54 years, reflecting an aging PLHIV population with significant cardiometabolic comorbidity. These results demonstrate that reliance on a single-visit BP measurement systematically overestimates hypertension control, with potentially significant implications for quality-of-care metrics and regulatory submissions requiring real-world outcome endpoints. Limitations include the retrospective single-center design, modest sample size, and restricted generalizability to other PLHIV settings with different treatment protocols and healthcare access patterns.

Implications Medical Affairs : Accurate and standardized BP control measurement is essential for benchmarking the effectiveness of antihypertensive strategies and for health authority submissions requiring robust real-world outcome data, particularly in special populations such as PLHIV. For Medical Affairs, this study highlights the need to advocate for longitudinal BP control definitions in clinical programs targeting PLHIV, ensuring that pharmacoepidemiology studies and pivotal trial endpoints are not biased by single-visit BP measurements that inflate apparent efficacy. Engaging KOLs working at the intersection of HIV medicine and cardiovascular disease would strengthen scientific communication platforms addressing hypertension management in immunocompromised patients and support the development of integrated care pathway guidance.

Elevated blood pressure among workforce populations in sub-Saharan Africa: a systematic review and meta-analysis.

Revue : BMC Public Health | Tier : NA | IF estime : NA

Date de publication : 2026 Aug 06

Resultats principaux : This systematic review and random-effects meta-analysis synthesized 12 cross-sectional studies involving 7,652 adult workers from six sub-Saharan African (SSA) countries to quantify the burden of hypertension and elevated blood pressure among occupational populations. The pooled hypertension prevalence was 26% (95% CI 22-31%; I²=93.7%), indicating that roughly one in four working adults in SSA is hypertensive, with pooled mean SBP of 125 mmHg (95% CI 122-128) and DBP of 80 mmHg (95% CI 78-82). A significant sex disparity was documented, with hypertension being 54% more prevalent in men than women (prevalence ratio 1.54, 95% CI 1.12-2.12), emphasizing male workforce populations as a priority target for screening. Sensitivity analyses produced stable estimates and Egger's test detected no publication bias, while between-study heterogeneity remained medium-to-high (I²≈67-94%, all p<0.005), reflecting genuine diversity in occupational settings and study populations across SSA. The analysis was limited to English-language studies published between 2010 and 2021, potentially excluding more recent data and non-English regional literature, and high heterogeneity limits the precision of pooled estimates.

Implications Medical Affairs : A workforce hypertension prevalence of 26% across SSA identifies a large, economically active population that is poorly served by current detection and treatment programs, representing a strategic market development opportunity for companies with established antihypertensive portfolios. For Medical Affairs, these data support the development of occupational health program partnerships with large employers, unions, and public health authorities in SSA to increase hypertension diagnosis and treatment uptake, aligned with global NCD prevention agendas. Engaging global health KOLs and multilateral organizations active in SSA to amplify scientific messaging on the cardiovascular burden in working populations would strengthen the positioning of antihypertensive therapies as essential health commodities in emerging market access strategies.

State and trait anxiety following prenatal moderate- versus light-intensity exercise: a secondary analysis of a randomized trial.

Revue : Archives of Women's Mental Health | Tier : NA | IF estime : NA

Date de publication : 2026 Nov 18

Resultats principaux : This secondary analysis of the Prenatal Heart Smart randomized trial examined state and trait anxiety trajectories across late pregnancy (28, 32, and 37 weeks' gestation) among 76 pregnant individuals with obesity randomized to moderate-intensity walking (n=42) or light-intensity stretching (n=34). No significant differences in state or trait anxiety were observed between the two exercise groups at either follow-up assessment (all p ≥ 0.38), nor were there significant within-group changes in either anxiety measure across the third trimester (all p ≥ 0.19). Both state (mean scores: 30.0, 29.6, 29.6) and trait (mean scores: 31.1, 32.1, 30.4) anxiety remained stable and relatively low throughout the observation period, contrasting with prior evidence suggesting anxiety escalates in late pregnancy. Exploratory analyses identified no significant associations between anxiety change scores and gestational hypertension after covariate adjustment, and the small number of preeclampsia cases precluded meaningful subgroup analysis. Limitations include the absence of a non-exercise control group, relatively low baseline anxiety levels reducing generalizability, insufficient statistical power for gestational hypertension analyses, and the secondary nature of these analyses relative to the primary trial objectives.

Implications Medical Affairs : While primarily addressing prenatal mental health, the exploratory null finding for the association between prenatal anxiety trajectory and gestational hypertension adds useful negative evidence regarding psychological stress pathways to pregnancy-related hypertensive disorders. From a Medical Affairs perspective, the lack of an exercise intensity effect on gestational hypertension in this obese pregnancy cohort reinforces the complexity and limited efficacy of non-pharmacological interventions alone in preventing pregnancy-related hypertension, underscoring the continued need for well-characterized pharmacological options in this population. KOL engagement with maternal-fetal medicine specialists and obstetric cardiologists could identify collaborative research opportunities to better characterize the interaction between prenatal lifestyle, anxiety, and hypertensive disorders of pregnancy, particularly in obesity-enriched cohorts.

[Effect of sequential multi-dose tranexamic acid administration on perioperative blood loss in cervical posterior single-door laminoplasty and its safety].

Revue : Chinese Journal of Reparative and Reconstructive Surgery | Tier : NA | IF estime : NA

Date de publication : 2026 Nov 15

Resultats principaux : This Chinese prospective randomized controlled trial enrolled 114 patients with multi-level cervical spondylotic myelopathy undergoing posterior single-door laminoplasty, comparing placebo (n=39), single-dose tranexamic acid - TXA 1.0 g (n=37), and sequential multi-dose TXA (3×1.0 g at 0, 5, and 10 hours; n=38) for perioperative blood management. Sequential multi-dose TXA produced the lowest total blood loss, postoperative blood loss, drain removal time, and allogeneic transfusion rate among all three groups (all p<0.05), and also showed the greatest reduction in postoperative HCT decline. Sequential multi-dose TXA also demonstrated superior suppression of postoperative inflammatory markers (D-dimer, IL-6, CRP) at all measured time points compared to both single-dose TXA and placebo, suggesting broader anti-inflammatory effects. Hypertension was recorded only as a baseline comorbidity with no between-group difference (p>0.05), confirming it did not influence group allocation, and no thromboembolic events (DVT, PE) or TXA-related adverse events occurred during the 30-day follow-up in any group. Limitations include the single-center design in China, relatively short 30-day follow-up, potential limited generalizability to non-Asian surgical populations, and absence of longer-term outcome data.

Implications Medical Affairs : Although this trial does not directly address antihypertensive drug classes, the confirmed safety of sequential multi-dose TXA in patients with hypertension as a comorbidity provides important perioperative management guidance for hypertensive patients requiring cervical spine surgery. Medical Affairs teams with cardiovascular and antihypertensive portfolios should monitor the growing evidence on perioperative blood management in hypertensive surgical patients, as bleeding and hemodynamic instability represent significant compounding risks in this subpopulation. Collaboration with anesthesiology and spine surgery KOLs may support the development of evidence-based perioperative protocols that integrate antihypertensive therapy management with optimal hemostatic strategies, particularly in Asian healthcare markets.

Clinical impact of high-potassium and low-sodium dash diet on H-type hypertension and cognitive impairment in cerebral small vessel disease.

Revue : Malawi Medical Journal | Tier : NA | IF estime : NA

Date de publication : 2026 Sep

Resultats principaux : This randomized controlled trial enrolled 112 Chinese patients with essential hypertension, comparing 66 with H-type hypertension (Hcy ≥15 µmol/L) and 46 without, with the H-type group randomized to a high-potassium, low-sodium DASH diet (n=33) or standard care (n=33) over approximately one year. H-type hypertension patients had significantly lower Montreal Cognitive Assessment (MoCA) and Mini Mental State Examination (MMSE) scores and higher homocysteine levels at baseline, with Hcy negatively correlated with cognitive function scores, confirming the neurotoxic role of elevated Hcy in hypertensive cerebral small vessel disease. Post-intervention, the DASH diet group showed significantly greater reductions in Hcy and improvements in MoCA and MMSE scores compared to standard care, alongside superior improvements in nutritional biomarkers (albumin, hemoglobin, transferrin). Activity of daily living (ADL) scores improved significantly more in the DASH group, suggesting that the dietary intervention confers broader functional benefit beyond cognitive outcomes. Limitations include the single-center design, modest sample size per arm, observation period confined to one institution from 2022-2023, lack of long-term follow-up data, and potential confounding from unmeasured dietary factors beyond the DASH framework.

Implications Medical Affairs : The demonstration that a high-potassium, low-sodium DASH diet reduces homocysteine and delays cognitive decline in H-type hypertension patients has direct implications for treatment guidelines in regions with high homocysteine prevalence, particularly in East Asian populations. For Medical Affairs, this study strengthens the scientific rationale for combination strategies targeting both blood pressure and homocysteine in hypertensive patients at cognitive risk, potentially supporting positioning of folate-containing antihypertensive combinations as neuroprotective adjuncts in cognitive preservation. Engaging neurologists and cardiologists specializing in hypertension-related cognitive outcomes as KOLs would be strategically valuable for developing scientific platforms addressing H-type hypertension, particularly in Asian and other populations with high baseline homocysteine levels.

Allostatic load is not associated with augmented vasoconstrictive response to cold pressor test or functional sympatholysis in Black emerging adults.

Revue : Experimental Physiology | Tier : NA | IF estime : NA

Date de publication : 2026 Nov 18

Resultats principaux : This study assessed forearm blood flow (FBF) and vascular conductance (FVC) during cold pressor test (CPT) alone and combined with handgrip exercise (EX+CPT) in 35 Black undergraduate students (mean age 20 years, 24 female) to determine whether allostatic load (AL) predicts vasoconstrictive reactivity and functional sympatholysis. AL scores were overall low (AL-CC: 1.6±1.2; AL-z: 0.003±3.9), and no significant correlation was found between AL and CPT-induced reductions in FBF or FVC, nor with the magnitude of functional sympatholysis during combined exercise and cold pressor stress. Functional sympatholysis was confirmed in the cohort, with significantly attenuated reductions in FBF and FVC during EX+CPT compared to CPT alone (p<0.001), indicating intact exercise-induced vasodilatory override of sympathetic vasoconstriction. However, blunted diurnal cortisol slope was significantly correlated with larger CPT-induced reductions in FBF (r=-0.55, p=0.003) and increased FBF (r=0.49, p=0.010) and FVC (r=0.43, p=0.028) lysis, implicating HPA axis dysregulation as a potential vascular risk mediator independent of AL. The study is limited by its young, relatively low-stress cohort with low baseline AL, modest sample size (n=35), cross-sectional design, lack of a non-Black comparison group, and inability to control for all psychosocial stressors experienced outside the laboratory.

Implications Medical Affairs : These findings advance mechanistic understanding of how chronic psychosocial stress pathways, particularly HPA axis dysregulation reflected in blunted diurnal cortisol slope, may contribute to vascular risk and hypertension development in Black populations beyond conventional cardiovascular risk factors. For Medical Affairs, this evidence supports scientific platforms linking racial health disparities in hypertension to stress physiology and endocrine dysregulation, reinforcing the need for race-specific clinical trial designs and outcome reporting to demonstrate antihypertensive efficacy across diverse populations. KOL engagement with exercise physiologists and health disparity researchers could facilitate the development of innovative risk-stratification tools targeting stress-mediated vascular dysfunction in high-risk ethnic groups and support culturally tailored patient education programs.

Enhanced antihypertensive efficacy of selenium-enriched black tea is associated with galloylated catechin enrichment: integrating chemical profiling, molecular modeling, and animal studies.

Revue : Journal of the Science of Food and Agriculture | Tier : NA | IF estime : NA

Date de publication : 2026 Nov 18

Resultats principaux : This preclinical study investigated the relationship between selenium biofortification of tea plants, the resulting phytochemical composition of black tea, and antihypertensive effects using a high-salt diet-induced hypertensive rat model combined with chemical profiling, network pharmacology, and molecular dynamics simulation. Selenium biofortification significantly increased the content of galloylated catechins (EGCG and ECG) in black tea leaves, and selenium-enriched black tea (Se-BT) demonstrated superior antioxidative, anti-inflammatory, and antihypertensive effects compared to conventional black tea in hypertensive rats. Se-BT increased serum endothelial nitric oxide synthase (eNOS) activity and nitric oxide (NO) levels by 39.32% and 12.20%, respectively, compared to conventional BT, suggesting enhanced endothelial function as the primary antihypertensive mechanism. Network pharmacology and molecular docking identified EGCG, ECG, and gallocatechin gallate (GCG) as major bioactive constituents converging on eNOS-mediated NO signaling, with molecular dynamics simulations confirming stable binding of EGCG to the eNOS BH4-binding region throughout the simulation. Limitations are inherent to the preclinical rodent model, including lack of human pharmacokinetic and safety data, absence of dose-response studies, and the need for clinical translation before any dietary recommendations can be derived.

Implications Medical Affairs : The identification of galloylated catechins and eNOS pathway activation as the principal antihypertensive mechanism of selenium-enriched black tea provides a novel dietary prevention angle that may complement pharmacological approaches in patients with mild or borderline hypertension, particularly in Asian populations with high tea consumption. Medical Affairs teams should monitor the progression of this research toward human clinical studies, as dietary polyphenol-based interventions are increasingly examined as evidence-based adjuncts to first-line antihypertensive therapy in prevention guidelines and patient counseling programs. KOL partnerships with nutraceutical researchers and functional food scientists working in the cardiovascular space could support the development of scientifically grounded patient counseling materials on dietary adjuncts for BP management and differentiated disease prevention messaging.

Association of Germanic Cultural Preservation with Cardiovascular Risk and Subclinical Atherosclerosis in Adults from Southern Brazil.

Revue : Arquivos Brasileiros de Cardiologia | Tier : NA | IF estime : NA

Date de publication : 2026

Resultats principaux : This cross-sectional study from the Study of Health in Pomerode cohort in southern Brazil enrolled 727 individuals aged 20-79 years and examined the relationship between Germanic cultural preservation (defined as speaking German at home and participating in German cultural associations) and cardiovascular risk including subclinical atherosclerosis. Germanic cultural preservation was reported by 65.6% of participants and was more prevalent among older adults, White individuals, Protestants, and those of middle socioeconomic status, with this group also exhibiting less sedentary behavior but greater use of antihypertensive medications. In age-stratified adjusted models, Germanic cultural preservation was significantly associated with hypertension (OR 2.79, 95% CI 1.49-5.22) and subclinical atherosclerosis (OR 3.01, 95% CI 1.07-8.40) among individuals aged 50 years or older. The association between cultural preservation and subclinical atherosclerosis was attenuated and no longer statistically significant after adjustment for hypertension, suggesting hypertension as a potential mediating pathway between cultural practices and subclinical vascular disease. Limitations include the cross-sectional design precluding causal inference, single-community setting limiting generalizability, and potential unmeasured confounding by dietary habits, stress patterns, and healthcare access within culturally distinct groups.

Implications Medical Affairs : This study highlights that cultural and lifestyle practices within ethnic communities can be significant independent determinants of hypertension prevalence and cardiovascular risk, with important implications for designing culturally tailored hypertension screening and prevention programs in multiethnic Latin American populations. For Medical Affairs, the finding that hypertension mediates the relationship between cultural practices and subclinical atherosclerosis reinforces the cardiovascular urgency of effective hypertension control in aging ethnic minority populations, supporting the positioning of antihypertensive therapies as atherosclerosis-prevention tools. Engaging KOLs with expertise in social determinants of cardiovascular health and culturally adapted preventive medicine in Latin America would strengthen scientific messaging around patient-centered hypertension management and cross-cultural cardiovascular risk stratification.

Revue : BMJ Public Health | Tier : NA | IF estime : NA

Date de publication : 2026

Resultats principaux : This cross-sectional institution-based study analyzed 24,289 individuals screened at non-communicable disease (NCD) corners of a tertiary care hospital in Mumbai, India, between September 2022 and April 2023, using the Community-Based Assessment Checklist (CBAC) for systematic risk assessment. Among screened individuals, 41.1% tested positive for at least one NCD: 17.2% for diabetes, 17.0% for hypertension, and 6.9% for both; among hypertensive individuals, 68.4% had Grade I and 7.7% had Grade III hypertension, while 65.9% of diabetes-positive individuals were in the prediabetic range. Uncontrolled disease was found in 34.8% of known diabetics and 39.7% of known hypertensives, confirming substantial gaps in treatment effectiveness even among those already diagnosed and presumably receiving care. Major risk factors independently associated with hypertension included age ≥50 years, male sex, obesity, high waist circumference, sedentary lifestyle, family history, elevated CBAC score, and clustering of multiple risk factors (present in 93.5% of hypertensive cases). Limitations include the institution-based design that may not represent community-level NCD burden, potential ascertainment bias from opportunistic rather than systematic screening, use of a single blood pressure reading for hypertension diagnosis, and restriction to one metropolitan tertiary center in India.

Implications Medical Affairs : The high positivity rates for undiagnosed and uncontrolled hypertension identified through NCD corners confirm that opportunistic facility-based screening programs are effective at capturing high-risk individuals who have fallen through primary care gaps, with actionable implications for hypertension treatment uptake strategies in India. For Medical Affairs, these large-scale data from one of the world's largest uncontrolled hypertension markets support the strategic case for increasing antihypertensive market access and health system partnerships in India, particularly targeting treatment-naive patients identified through hospital-based NCD screening programs. Engaging KOLs in preventive cardiology and public health in India to co-develop data-driven screening protocols and guideline-informed treatment pathways would be a high-priority action for portfolio expansion and patient identification strategies in emerging markets.

The Solitary Functioning Kidney: From Pathophysiology to Long-Term Management.

Revue : American Journal of Nephrology | Tier : NA | IF estime : NA

Date de publication : 2026 Oct 10

Resultats principaux : This comprehensive narrative review synthesizes current evidence on the pathophysiology, diagnosis, risk factors, and long-term management of solitary functioning kidney (SFK), covering both congenital and acquired etiologies (including prior nephrectomy) across the life course in a unified framework for the first time. SFK is associated with compensatory renal enlargement and glomerular hyperfiltration that initially preserves kidney function but can become maladaptive, contributing to proteinuria, tubulointerstitial inflammation, and progressive fibrosis over time. Hypertension, persistent proteinuria/albuminuria, and declining glomerular filtration rate are identified as the principal clinical indicators of adverse renal outcomes in SFK, with hypertension control emphasized as a central management priority across all SFK etiologies. The authors propose a novel evidence-informed, two-step clinical framework that first identifies established kidney injury markers and then assesses residual renal reserve and secondary nephrotoxic exposures to guide dynamic risk stratification and individualized follow-up intensity. The review acknowledges that most evidence derives from pediatric congenital SFK, kidney donors, and disease-related nephrectomy cohorts studied separately, limiting synthesis into a unified approach, and that the proposed framework remains conceptual pending prospective validation.

Implications Medical Affairs : The emphasis on hypertension control as a central management pillar in SFK underscores the clinical importance of RAAS-inhibiting antihypertensive agents in preventing CKD progression in this population, supporting their positioning as the standard of care in nephroprotective strategies. For Medical Affairs, this review positions ACE inhibitors and ARBs as cornerstone therapies for nephroprotection in SFK patients, providing a scientific platform development opportunity around renoprotective benefits of RAAS inhibition in patients with reduced functional nephron mass. Partnering with nephrologists and pediatric urologists as KOLs could advance evidence-based guidance on antihypertensive selection and blood pressure targets in this under-studied but clinically relevant population, supporting guideline update advocacy and future clinical trial design.
