Underdetected peripheral artery disease in people with hypertension: A narrative review of risk-stratified case-finding, diagnostic pathways, and equity in care.

Revue : Clinical and Experimental Hypertension | Tier : Tier 3 | IF estime : 3–5

Date de publication : 2026-10-30

Resultats principaux : This structured narrative review of PubMed/MEDLINE from inception through July 2026 examines how hypertension care can support risk-stratified recognition of peripheral artery disease (PAD) in affected adults. Hypertension and PAD share age-related, metabolic, renal, and tobacco-related risk determinants, with pressure-related vascular injury providing biological plausibility for their co-occurrence. The review recommends resting ankle-brachial index (ABI) as the initial physiologic test, with toe-brachial index plus waveforms for noncompressible arteries, exercise ABI for persistent exertional symptoms despite normal resting values, and duplex ultrasound when anatomic definition will change management. Guideline-supported risk-based testing is clearly distinguished from universal screening of asymptomatic adults, for which outcome benefit remains unproven. A critical limitation is that the proposed case-finding pathway itself has not been validated in outcomes trials; the review is explicitly hypothesis-generating and calls for pragmatic randomized trials of the complete integrated care pathway.

Implications Medical Affairs : This review reinforces the need to integrate PAD screening protocols into hypertension management guidelines, where companies with antihypertensive portfolios can contribute to scientific advocacy and guideline development. For Medical Affairs teams, the emphasis on equity in access to physiologic testing and unmet needs in under-resourced settings represent opportunities to position antihypertensive products within a broader cardiovascular risk reduction narrative. The call for pragmatic trials of integrated care pathways provides a concrete avenue for KOL engagement and investigator-initiated research partnerships in vascular medicine.

Clinical and psychosocial factors associated with clinician-driven allocation to hybrid versus in-person care in chronic disease management: a Colombian retrospective cohort.

Revue : Primary Health Care Research & Development | Tier : NA | IF estime : NA

Date de publication : 2026-10-30

Resultats principaux : This retrospective observational cohort study enrolled 638 adults (319 hybrid, 319 in-person) with hypertension, type 2 diabetes, and/or dyslipidemia in a chronic disease management program at Alma Mater Hospital of Antioquia, Colombia (2019–2025). Logistic regression identified higher baseline HbA1c (adjusted OR 2.68 per 1% increase; 95% CI 2.17–3.31) and inadequate medication adherence (aOR 1.96; 95% CI 1.17–3.28) as independently associated with physician-driven hybrid care allocation, while higher educational attainment was inversely associated. Over three years of follow-up, hybrid care was associated with significantly higher therapeutic goal attainment (OR 7.34; 95% CI 4.96–10.86) and lower chronic disease-related emergency department utilization (OR 0.53; 95% CI 0.36–0.79). A strong gradient was observed in secondary complexity analyses favoring hybrid care in more complex patients. The study's key limitations are its observational design and physician-driven allocation, which introduces indication bias and precludes causal inference about outcome differences.

Implications Medical Affairs : The superior three-year therapeutic goal attainment in hybrid care is directly relevant to Medical Affairs strategies promoting digital adherence support programs for antihypertensive therapies, particularly in complex multimorbid patients. For KOL strategy, engaging clinical champions of hybrid management models in hypertension-diabetes comorbidity management in Latin America represents an emerging opportunity with high regional impact. This study supports the design of real-world evidence programs comparing hybrid versus traditional care on antihypertensive treatment outcomes, which could be leveraged for market access and payer communications.

Orthostatic blood pressure fluctuations and risk of falls in community-dwelling older adults: the role of recovery time.

Revue : BMC Geriatrics | Tier : NA | IF estime : NA

Date de publication : 2026-10-28

Resultats principaux : This prospective cohort analysis used data from the ActiFE cohort, including 509 community-dwelling older adults (mean age 79 years, 44% female), with blood pressure measured at rest and at 1, 2, and 3 minutes after standing. Systolic orthostatic hypotension (sOH) was associated with at least one fall (adjusted OR 2.00; 95% CI 1.15–3.47), but overall orthostatic hypotension without recovery time (RT) stratification was not independently associated with falls. When RT was considered, Delayed Recovery (1–3 min) markedly increased fall risk for sOH (OR 4.73; 95% CI 1.71–13.10), as did No Recovery (OR 5.06; 95% CI 1.42–22.27). Prevalences were: 16.5% sOH, 26.9% OH, 9.0% sOHTN, and 15.5% OHTN, with 168 falls recorded over 12 months. Orthostatic hypertension was not associated with falls in any analysis. Key limitations include the single-cohort design, potential underpowering for subgroup analyses, and the prospective but single-site nature of the study.

Implications Medical Affairs : These findings have direct implications for the safety labeling and monitoring of antihypertensive drugs with vasodilatory profiles, as prolonged orthostatic hypotension recovery time — not merely orthostatic hypotension per se — identifies elderly patients at highest fall risk. Medical Affairs teams should incorporate recovery time guidance into prescriber educational materials and patient monitoring tools for geriatric hypertension populations, particularly for drugs commonly associated with orthostatic effects. The unmet clinical need for BP monitoring protocols that assess recovery time in geriatric hypertension represents a differentiation opportunity in product messaging for the elderly patient segment.

Real-world discrimination and incremental value of first-trimester placental growth factor for hypertensive disorders of pregnancy: a retrospective Chinese cohort.

Revue : Frontiers in Endocrinology | Tier : NA | IF estime : NA

Date de publication : 2026-10-15

Resultats principaux : This retrospective, single-center cohort study from Hangzhou, China, analyzed 310 pregnancy episodes with 27 hypertensive disorder of pregnancy (HDP) events (11 preeclampsia, 16 gestational hypertension). PlGF alone at 9+0 to 13+6 gestational weeks had a modest AUC of 0.633 (95% bootstrap CI 0.523–0.738), while a parsimonious maternal-characteristics model (age, pre-pregnancy BMI, nulliparity) achieved an AUC of 0.740 (0.635–0.837). Adding log10(PlGF) to the maternal model yielded an AUC of 0.748 (0.638–0.849), with a marginal ΔAUC of 0.009 (95% CI −0.019 to 0.037) that was not statistically significant. No subgroup analyses were feasible given the small number of HDP events. Key limitations include small sample size, reliance on unvalidated free-text discharge diagnoses, and the absence of a complete combined first-trimester algorithm (NT, PAPP-A, uterine PI). The findings do not support isolated clinical use of first-trimester PlGF for HDP prediction in this routine care setting.

Implications Medical Affairs : The modest real-world performance of first-trimester PlGF in a Chinese clinical setting highlights the gap between algorithm validation in controlled research environments and routine care, informing strategic decisions about companion diagnostic investment for hypertensive pregnancy management programs. From a Medical Affairs standpoint, this study underscores the need for well-powered validation studies of hypertensive pregnancy screening tools in diverse Asian populations before widespread adoption, representing a scientific gap where pharmaceutical research sponsorship could generate high-impact evidence. The limited incremental value of PlGF relative to simple maternal characteristics may redirect focus toward combined first-trimester algorithms as the standard of care in preeclampsia prevention programs relevant to antihypertensive product positioning in pregnancy.

A cross-sectional study on anthropometric indices and hypertension in Chinese adults: comparison of discriminatory power and age-stratified analysis.

Revue : Frontiers in Public Health | Tier : NA | IF estime : NA

Date de publication : 2026-10-15

Resultats principaux : This large cross-sectional analysis of 104,248 adults from a health check-up cohort in Southwest China compared five anthropometric obesity indices — WC, BMI, WHtR, ABSI, and BRI — as discriminators of hypertension risk (defined as BP ≥140/90 mmHg, self-reported diagnosis, or antihypertensive use). Per standard deviation increase, WC and BMI showed the highest adjusted odds ratios (OR 1.61; 95% CI 1.58–1.64 each), while ABSI was weakest (OR 1.09; 95% CI 1.07–1.11). Overall AUC was highest and equal for WHtR and BRI (0.676), followed by WC (0.653) and ABSI (0.612). Critically, discriminatory ability was strongest in the 18–35 age group, where WC achieved an AUC of 0.763 — the highest across all age-index combinations. All anthropometric indices showed significantly attenuated associations with hypertension in older age groups (p for interaction <0.001). Limitations include the cross-sectional design precluding causal inference, potential misclassification from self-reported diagnoses, and the single-region Chinese population limiting external generalizability.

Implications Medical Affairs : The superiority of WHtR and BRI over BMI as hypertension screening tools — especially their advantage in young adults — provides evidence to advocate for guideline revisions beyond BMI-centric approaches, an area where Medical Affairs teams can influence through KOL-driven publications and advisory board discussions. The pronounced age-dependent performance of anthropometric indices reinforces the need for age-stratified risk communication materials and population-specific threshold recommendations in hypertension prevention messaging. Given the scale of hypertension burden in China (104K+ patients studied here), these findings create a basis for partnerships with Chinese health authorities and national cardiovascular prevention programs supporting antihypertensive brand positioning in primary prevention.

Multidimensional dietary patterns and cardiometabolic comorbidity in adults with anxiety disorders: evidence from NHANES and linked dietary databases using interpretable machine learning.

Revue : BMC Public Health | Tier : NA | IF estime : NA

Date de publication : 2026-08-04

Resultats principaux : This study analyzed seven NHANES cycles (2005–2018) in adults with anxiety disorders to examine dietary associations with hypertension-coronary heart disease (CHD) comorbidity, using 24-hour recalls across three complementary dietary domains (micronutrients, food groups, individual foods). Six machine learning models were developed with class imbalance addressed by SMOTE, and results were interpreted using SHAP and LIME methods. Higher intakes of vitamin E, polyunsaturated fatty acids, folate, calcium, protein, and total energy were consistently associated with lower comorbidity odds across analytic approaches, while zinc and iron showed positive associations. At the dietary pattern level, protein foods, poultry, seafood, and vegetables were inversely related to comorbidity, while starchy vegetables and cured meats elevated risk. At the individual food level, bananas, coffee, cream substitutes, and tomato products were associated with lower risk, while yeast breads, margarine, cakes, and pies were associated with higher risk. Limitations include the cross-sectional NHANES design precluding causal inference and the machine-learning models being hypothesis-generating rather than confirmatory.

Implications Medical Affairs : The identification of modifiable dietary risk factors for hypertension-CHD comorbidity in adults with anxiety disorders highlights an underserved patient population where integrated cardiometabolic-psychiatric care models could generate significant medical and commercial value for antihypertensive brands. For product positioning, these findings reinforce the importance of dietary modification as a documented adjunct to pharmacological antihypertensive treatment in patients with comorbid mental health conditions, supporting holistic disease management programs. The machine-learning methodology applied here provides a validated template for real-world data analyses that Medical Affairs teams can commission to characterize patient subpopulations most likely to benefit from combined pharmacological and lifestyle interventions.

What triggers intraoperative blood pressure/heart rate surges and postoperative hypertonia during unilateral biportal endoscopy?

Revue : BMC Anesthesiology | Tier : NA | IF estime : NA

Date de publication : 2026-08-03

Resultats principaux : This case report describes a 49-year-old woman (ASA I) who underwent unilateral biportal endoscopy (UBE) for lumbar disc herniation under total intravenous anesthesia, complicated by an occult dural tear and cumulative irrigation volume of 51,000 mL over 4 hours 23 minutes. Approximately 2.5 hours after incision, progressive treatment-refractory tachycardia (70 to 117 bpm) and severe hypertension (up to 183/102 mmHg) developed; standard antihypertensive agents esmolol and urapidil produced minimal blood pressure reduction despite augmented anesthetic depth. Postoperatively the patient exhibited impaired consciousness, tachypnea (40–50 breaths/min), and generalized hypertonia with decerebrate posturing, attributed to acute intracranial hypertension from irrigation fluid entering the subarachnoid space via the unrecognized dural defect. Management with osmotic diuresis (furosemide, mannitol), corticosteroids, and sedation resulted in full neurological recovery and discharge on day 14. The report proposes a high-risk triad: progressive refractory hypertension and tachycardia, UBE duration over 2 hours, and irrigation volume exceeding 20 L. Limitations are inherent to the single-case design; no quantitative statistical analysis is possible and generalizability is severely limited.

Implications Medical Affairs : This case highlights a specific perioperative scenario in which standard antihypertensive agents including urapidil and esmolol may be ineffective against secondary intracranial hypertension, providing an important context for prescriber labeling communications and anesthesiology KOL education around limitations of antihypertensive agents in complex surgical settings. For Medical Affairs teams marketing antihypertensive drugs used perioperatively, this case is a safety communication opportunity regarding the differentiated mechanisms of blood pressure elevation and the critical need to address underlying causes before escalating antihypertensive doses. The hypothesis-generating nature of this report identifies perioperative blood pressure variability in minimally invasive spine surgery as a niche area for targeted publication strategies bridging anesthesiology and cardiovascular medicine.

Cardiometabolic Abnormalities among Normal-Weight Adults: Prevalence and Associated Sociodemographic and Lifestyle Factors in a Large Spanish Cohort.

Revue : Revista Clinica Espanola | Tier : NA | IF estime : NA

Date de publication : 2026-10-30

Resultats principaux : This large cross-sectional study analyzed 163,254 Spanish workers aged 18–69 years with normal BMI (18.5–24.9 kg/m²) undergoing occupational health examinations between 2021 and 2024; cardiometabolic abnormalities were defined as the presence of hypertension, dyslipidemia, or hyperglycemia. A striking 49.0% of normal-weight adults exhibited at least one cardiometabolic abnormality, with higher prevalence in men (54.3%) versus women (44.5%), revealing a high hidden cardiometabolic burden in the ostensibly healthy population. Multivariate logistic regression identified male sex, older age, smoking, low physical activity, poor Mediterranean diet adherence, and high alcohol consumption as independent risk factors, with risk increasing progressively with the accumulation of unhealthy habits. Lower occupational social class was also independently associated with greater cardiometabolic risk, underscoring socioeconomic determinants in this population. The cross-sectional design precludes causal inference, and occupational screening may underdetect treated or controlled conditions; the worker population may not represent the general population.

Implications Medical Affairs : The high prevalence of hypertension among normal-weight adults (nearly half of all normal-BMI workers) strongly challenges BMI-centric cardiovascular risk screening and supports development of metabolic screening guidelines incorporating lifestyle and sociodemographic factors, an advocacy area for Medical Affairs teams. This study provides compelling evidence to position antihypertensive therapies beyond the overweight or obese patient segment and to develop occupational health screening partnerships as a non-traditional channel for hypertension detection and early treatment. The accumulative lifestyle risk factor burden — poor diet, inactivity, smoking — presents clear opportunities for patient education programs and lifestyle-oriented companion strategies that can be embedded in antihypertensive product communications.

Rationale and Design of a Parallel Randomized Trial of a DASH-Enhanced Portfolio Diet With or Without Ultra-Processed Plant Proteins for Blood Pressure and Other Cardiometabolic Risk Factors in Adults With Hypertension and Obesity: The SWITCH Trial.

Revue : Journal of the American Heart Association | Tier : NA | IF estime : NA

Date de publication : 2026-10-30

Resultats principaux : This protocol paper describes the SWITCH trial (NCT06907862), a parallel randomized 3-arm open-label superiority-noninferiority trial enrolling 300 adults with hypertension and obesity (50% with type 2 diabetes) at St. Michael's Hospital, Toronto, Canada. Participants are randomized to a high-UPF or low-UPF soy protein-containing DASH-enhanced Portfolio diet, or standard of care, over 12 weeks, with systolic blood pressure as the primary endpoint and non-HDL cholesterol and body weight as key secondary endpoints. The trial addresses two questions: whether a DASH-enhanced Portfolio diet reduces blood pressure meaningfully versus standard of care, and whether high-UPF soy protein is non-inferior to low-UPF soy for blood pressure reduction. The inclusion of 50% participants with type 2 diabetes enables important subgroup analyses on the cardiometabolic effects of combined dietary strategies in metabolically complex patients. No efficacy results are yet available as this is a design and rationale publication; the main limitation is the open-label design inherent to dietary intervention trials.

Implications Medical Affairs : The SWITCH trial directly addresses the role of ultra-processed plant proteins in guideline-recommended dietary patterns for hypertension, potentially reshaping non-pharmacological BP management recommendations that complement antihypertensive drug therapy. For Medical Affairs teams, the trial's combined DASH-Portfolio hypothesis provides a scientifically credible framework for patient education materials on dietary adjuncts, supporting comprehensive treatment narratives for antihypertensive drugs in obesity-hypertension comorbidity. Engaging with SWITCH trial investigators represents a high-value KOL development opportunity in the growing cardiometabolic diet-drug interaction space, with potential to leverage trial outcomes for product positioning if dietary effects prove additive with pharmacotherapy.

Sudden Cardiac Death in Firefighters From the United States: A 16-Year Postmortem Analysis.

Revue : Journal of the American Heart Association | Tier : NA | IF estime : NA

Date de publication : 2026-10-30

Resultats principaux : This retrospective postmortem analysis examined 794 available autopsy reports from 2,100 on-duty firefighter deaths in the United States over 16 years (1999–2014), with a mean age of 44 years (range 17–82, 4.0% women). Of 794 reviewed deaths, 312 (39.3%) were adjudicated as sudden cardiac death (SCD) by expert cardiologists and cardiovascular pathologists. Coronary artery disease/ischemic heart disease combined with hypertensive heart disease accounted for 81.8% of all SCD events, highlighting the dominant role of traditional cardiometabolic risk factors. Even among young firefighters under 40 years, coronary artery disease/ischemic heart disease remained the leading SCD cause (48.0% of 50 events). Among exertional SCD cases attributable to coronary artery disease (n=97), 68.1% showed severe fixed coronary stenosis while 31.9% demonstrated acute plaque rupture/thrombosis. A key limitation is that postmortem evaluations were available for only 37.8% of total deaths, potentially introducing selection bias; the retrospective design also precludes adjustment for clinical management differences over the 16-year span.

Implications Medical Affairs : The dominant role of hypertensive heart disease in SCD among high-demand occupational groups demonstrates a significant unmet need for structured cardiovascular screening and targeted antihypertensive management programs in the occupational health sector, a non-traditional but high-impact channel for Medical Affairs engagement. From a product positioning perspective, these data support advocacy for mandatory cardiovascular screening in at-risk professions, where antihypertensive therapy could directly impact SCD prevention and generate compelling real-world effectiveness data. The prevalence of hypertensive cardiomyopathy even in young firefighters reinforces Medical Affairs narratives advocating for earlier initiation of evidence-based antihypertensive regimens before irreversible structural cardiac changes — and SCD — occur.

Date de publication : 2026-10-30

Resultats principaux : This narrative review synthesizes experimental and translational evidence on the glymphatic system's role in Alzheimer's disease (AD), examining how hypertension, ischemic heart disease, sleep disturbances, high-fat diet, diabetes, and gut microbiome dysbiosis collectively impair glymphatic and perivascular clearance of amyloid-beta and tau from the central nervous system. Emerging therapeutic strategies with experimental or early translational support include continuous theta-burst stimulation, positive airway pressure therapy for obstructive sleep apnea, pharmacological AQP4 enhancement, and nutritional interventions. A significant limitation is the narrative review design, which precludes systematic synthesis or quantitative meta-analysis, and most evidence cited is from preclinical or early translational studies with limited clinical validation.

Endothelial BACE1 impairs insulin signaling to drive cerebral small vessel disease and stroke.

Revue : Science Bulletin | Tier : NA | IF estime : NA

Date de publication : 2026-10-16

Resultats principaux : This experimental study identified a non-canonical pathogenic mechanism linking beta-secretase 1 (BACE1), a known Alzheimer's drug target, to endothelial vascular insulin resistance and cerebral small vessel disease (CSVD) in hypertensive conditions. BACE1 was upregulated in the cerebral endothelium of patients with hypertensive arteriopathy and cerebral amyloid angiopathy, confirmed by immunohistochemistry. Mechanistically, upregulated endothelial BACE1 cleaved the beta-subunit of the insulin receptor (INSR), disrupting the INSR-Akt-eNOS signaling axis required for vascular homeostasis and nitric oxide-dependent vasodilation. In a validated stroke-prone spontaneously hypertensive rat model on a high-salt diet, this BACE1-mediated disruption occurred early in disease progression. Treatment with the clinical BACE1 inhibitor elenbecestat (E2609) restored endothelial function, ameliorated cerebrovascular pathology, and significantly reduced stroke incidence, with INSR blockade abolishing these protective effects, confirming an INSR-dependent mechanism. The main limitation is the predominantly preclinical and in vitro evidence base; human data are limited to immunohistochemical observations in arteriopathy patient samples, requiring prospective clinical validation.

Implications Medical Affairs : The discovery of BACE1-driven endothelial insulin resistance as a hypertension-related driver of cerebrovascular disease opens a novel therapeutic intersection, suggesting that BACE1 inhibitors previously abandoned in Alzheimer's trials may have a second-life therapeutic potential in hypertensive CSVD and stroke prevention, representing a paradigm shift warranting Medical Affairs attention. From a strategic perspective, these preclinical findings justify investment in translational research programs exploring BACE1 inhibitor vascular effects in hypertensive patients at high cerebrovascular risk, with potential to establish scientific leadership at the intersection of vascular and neurodegenerative pathology. This work provides high-value content for scientific advisory board discussions on endothelial insulin resistance as a mechanism of hypertensive target organ damage in the brain, potentially reshaping the clinical trial agenda for next-generation antihypertensive combination strategies.

Effects of outdoor air pollution on the cardiovascular health of school-aged children residing near polluted areas and the efficacy of mitigation measures in Dar es Salaam, Tanzania.

Revue : BMC Public Health | Tier : NA | IF estime : NA

Date de publication : 2026-08-04

Resultats principaux : This community-based cross-sectional study enrolled 653 school-aged children living near polluted areas in Dar es Salaam, Tanzania, between December 2024 and July 2025, assessing cardiovascular health via oscillometric blood pressure measurement and structured questionnaires. The prevalence of elevated blood pressure among participants was a strikingly high 19.9%, suggesting a substantial hidden pediatric hypertension burden in this low-income urban setting. Children with a history of respiratory disease had a markedly higher adjusted risk of cardiovascular symptoms (ARR 8.81; 95% CI 6.12–12.67), indicating a clinically important respiratory-to-cardiovascular risk pathway in pollution-exposed youth. Awareness of government mitigation measures was heterogeneous: 71.5% knew of waste management policies but only 44.5% were aware of vehicle emission standards, and only 45% perceived government measures as effective despite 61% of households using natural gas for cooking. Key limitations include single blood pressure measurement without confirmatory assessment, cross-sectional design precluding causal inference, and potential questionnaire response bias; the absence of direct air pollution exposure measurement (PM2.5, NO2) limits dose-response conclusions.

Implications Medical Affairs : The alarmingly high prevalence of elevated blood pressure (nearly 1 in 5) in school-aged children from air-polluted urban environments in sub-Saharan Africa underscores an emerging pediatric hypertension epidemic with environmental etiology, a domain with currently limited pharmacological and regulatory attention where early Medical Affairs engagement could establish thought leadership. For Medical Affairs strategy, this study quantifies a growing global burden of early-onset hypertension linked to air pollution exposure, supporting advocacy for pediatric cardiovascular surveillance programs and regulatory engagement around environmental pollution as a hypertension risk factor in low-income country settings. The disproportionate respiratory-to-cardiovascular risk amplification in pollution-exposed children provides compelling content for KOL education programs on environmental contributors to lifelong hypertension risk, potentially framing early cardiovascular intervention as a global health equity priority.
