Cognitive impairment and associated factors among community-dwelling older adults with hypertension in tropical China

Revue : Frontiers in Public Health | Tier : NA | IF estime : NA

Date de publication : 2026-08-13

Resultats principaux : This community-based cross-sectional study enrolled 735 community-dwelling older adults aged 60 years or older with physician-diagnosed hypertension, recruited from health centers in Haikou, tropical China between 2023 and 2025; the mean age was 72.3 years (SD 7.7) and 57.6% were women. The primary endpoint was prevalence of cognitive impairment (CI) assessed via the Chinese Mini-Mental Status Examination with education-adjusted cutoffs, with secondary analyses identifying associated factors through multivariable logistic regression. The overall prevalence of CI was 30.3% (223/735); in the fully adjusted model, age 80 years or older was associated with significantly higher odds of CI compared to those aged 60-69 years (OR 2.21, 95% CI 1.34-3.64), and depressive symptoms were associated with increased odds (OR 1.87, 95% CI 1.03-3.39). Protective factors included higher educational attainment, medium monthly income, and higher BMI; stratified analyses revealed a significant interaction between sex and marital status on CI risk (P for interaction 0.015), suggesting divergent social risk profiles between men and women with hypertension. Key limitations include the cross-sectional design precluding causal inferences, reliance on self-reported physician diagnoses rather than measured blood pressure, and recruitment restricted to a single tropical region limiting generalizability to other populations.

Implications Medical Affairs : The high CI prevalence (30.3%) in hypertensive older adults supports the need for integrated neurological screening in hypertension management guidelines, potentially expanding the therapeutic goals of antihypertensive treatment beyond blood pressure control to include cognitive preservation. From a Medical Affairs perspective, these findings provide epidemiological rationale for positioning antihypertensive therapies with demonstrated neuroprotective properties as preferred treatment options in the elderly. KOL engagement should leverage these data to initiate evidence-generation programs assessing whether optimized blood pressure control in tropical or subtropical Asian populations attenuates cognitive decline, a gap highlighted by the regional scarcity of evidence noted by the authors.

Modeling chronic kidney disease progression using a continuous-time multistate Markov model: Transition dynamics and covariate effects

Revue : The Journal of International Medical Research | Tier : NA | IF estime : NA

Date de publication : 2026-08-28

Resultats principaux : This retrospective cohort study analyzed 194 patients with chronic kidney disease (CKD) across 1,506 clinic visits at Jimma Medical Center in Ethiopia between February 2019 and February 2024, using a continuous-time multistate Markov model to characterize stage-specific disease progression dynamics. Primary endpoints were transition intensities, mean sojourn times, and next-state probabilities; secondary analyses evaluated the effects of hypertension, diabetes, and cardiovascular disease on transition rates between CKD stages. Hypertension was identified as a major driver of advanced CKD progression: patients with hypertension were significantly more likely to transition from CKD stage 4 to stage 5 (end-stage renal disease) compared to those without (HR 2.62, 95% CI 1.79-3.84). Mean sojourn times were 4.33, 4.44, 5.79, and 4.57 months for CKD stages 1-4 respectively, with longest dwell time at stage 3 and rapid progression once stage 4 is reached; diabetes and cardiovascular disease were also significantly associated with CKD progression. The study is limited by its single-center retrospective design in a resource-limited East African setting, and stage classifications depended on eGFR estimates from available laboratory data.

Implications Medical Affairs : The quantification that hypertension more than doubles the risk of CKD stage 4-to-5 transition (HR 2.62) provides compelling evidence of the high unmet medical need for integrated cardiorenal protection in hypertensive patients, reinforcing the strategic importance of renoprotective antihypertensive regimens. These findings are directly actionable for Medical Affairs positioning of agents with combined antihypertensive and renoprotective profiles, supporting expanded value propositions in CKD comorbidity settings. Collaboration with nephrology and cardiology KOLs in sub-Saharan Africa would strengthen advocacy for earlier and more intensive blood pressure management protocols in CKD guidelines applicable to low- and middle-income countries.

Revue : The American Journal of Managed Care | Tier : NA | IF estime : NA

Date de publication : 2026-08-01

Resultats principaux : This quasi-experimental difference-in-differences study used the Virginia All-Payer Claims Database to examine NSAID prescription trends among patients with CKD, heart failure, and/or hypertension during 2019-2021, comparing pre- and peri-COVID-19 pandemic periods with Poisson regression to estimate incidence rate ratios (IRRs). The primary outcome was NSAID prescription dispensing rates; subgroup analyses stratified by rurality, sex, and age were conducted. Extrapolated to the state population, approximately 3.8 million NSAID prescriptions were dispensed to high-risk Virginians over three years; overall utilization declined 23% during the first two pandemic years. Rural residents had significantly higher NSAID utilization than urban counterparts (IRR 1.594, 95% CI 1.408-1.803); patients aged 40-64, 65-79, and 80+ years had 6.4-, 7.0-, and 5.2-fold higher rates respectively compared to those aged 18-39; women had higher utilization than men (IRR for men 0.779, p significant). Implications Medical Affairs : Persistent high NSAID use in hypertensive patients despite guideline contraindications represents a significant pharmacovigilance concern, highlighting the need for Medical Affairs teams to actively engage prescribers on deprescribing programs and safer analgesic alternatives. The pronounced rural-urban disparity identifies rural communities as a priority target for pharmacovigilance outreach and patient education campaigns addressing analgesic choice in high-risk hypertensive populations. For companies marketing antihypertensive products, these data support the development of real-world evidence programs documenting the blood pressure consequences of concomitant NSAID use, strengthening label differentiation for agents with robust BP-protective profiles.

Impact of hypertension and antihypertensive medications on early implant failure following sinus augmentation

Revue : Dental and Medical Problems | Tier : NA | IF estime : NA

Date de publication : 2026-01-01

Resultats principaux : This retrospective cohort study at a tertiary referral center analyzed data from 425 dental implants in 152 patients undergoing sinus augmentation procedures between 2013 and 2021, examining the impact of hypertension and individual antihypertensive drug classes on early implant failure (EIF), defined as implant loss before prosthetic loading. Univariate and multivariable logistic regression analyses were performed at both patient and implant levels. Hypertension per se was not a significant risk factor for EIF; however, beta-blocker therapy emerged as an independent risk factor at both the patient level (OR 4.05, p=0.018) and implant level (OR 3.13, p=0.045). Tobacco smoking was the strongest independent predictor of EIF (OR 8.52, p<0.001), and a higher number of implants per patient also increased risk (OR 1.49, p=0.039); other antihypertensive classes including ACEIs, ARBs, CCBs, and diuretics were not significantly associated with EIF. Study limitations include the retrospective single-center design, heterogeneity in surgical techniques and graft materials over the 8-year study period, and limited statistical power for subgroup analyses by individual antihypertensive drug class.

Implications Medical Affairs : The class-specific finding that beta-blocker therapy but not other antihypertensive drug classes significantly increases early dental implant failure risk has important cross-specialty positioning implications, potentially informing clinical decision-making for hypertensive patients requiring dental implant procedures. From a Medical Affairs perspective, these data could strengthen messaging to dentists and oral surgeons when positioning antihypertensive products without beta-blockade mechanisms, supporting evidence-based cross-specialty differentiation. While the absolute number of patients is modest, this signal warrants attention in guideline updates addressing perioperative management of hypertensive patients and should prompt real-world evidence generation in larger multicenter datasets to confirm the association.

Decompressive craniectomy versus craniotomy for patients undergoing surgical evacuation of an acute subdural hematoma: RESCUE-ASDH RCT and cost effectiveness

Revue : Health Technology Assessment | Tier : NA | IF estime : NA

Date de publication : 2026-08-01

Resultats principaux : The RESCUE-ASDH trial was an international multicentre pragmatic parallel-group randomized controlled trial comparing decompressive craniectomy versus craniotomy for surgical evacuation of traumatic acute subdural hematomas; 450 patients (228 craniotomy, 222 craniectomy) were enrolled across UK and international neurosurgical centers, with an additional observational arm for non-randomizable patients. The primary endpoint was the extended Glasgow Outcome Scale (eGOS) at 12 months post-injury; a health economic evaluation was conducted from the UK NHS perspective. The primary analysis showed no statistically significant difference between surgical approaches: common OR for eGOS differences 0.85 (95% CI 0.60-1.18, p=0.324); 12-month mortality was 30.2% in the craniotomy group versus 32.2% in craniectomy, while good recovery occurred in 25.6% versus 19.9% respectively. Additional surgery was required more frequently in the craniotomy group, while wound complications occurred more commonly after craniectomy; observational cohort patients who received craniectomy had worse outcomes, though their baseline characteristics differed substantially. Key limitations include non-blinding of clinicians to allocation, crossover in both arms (8.8% in craniotomy, 5.4% in craniectomy), and the trial's focus on intracranial hypertension rather than arterial hypertension.

Implications Medical Affairs : Although this RCT addresses neurosurgical management of intracranial hypertension in the context of subdural hematoma rather than arterial hypertension, the pragmatic trial design exemplifies robust RCT methodology that Medical Affairs teams can reference when evaluating clinical evidence quality standards in the hypertension field. The robust NIHR-funded multicentre design, including intent-to-treat analysis with observational arm, demonstrates methodological approaches applicable to future antihypertensive outcome trial design, particularly for pragmatic real-world evidence generation. For arterial hypertension Medical Affairs, this publication has limited direct product relevance but highlights the importance of pre-specifying subgroup analyses and maintaining methodological rigor in complex multisite RCTs used to support guideline development.

Revue : Health Science Reports | Tier : NA | IF estime : NA

Date de publication : 2026-08-26

Resultats principaux : This comprehensive narrative review synthesized approximately 156 publications (85% from 2020-2025) retrieved from PubMed, ScienceDirect, and Google Scholar to provide an updated overview of CVD epidemiology, pathophysiology, risk factors, and emerging therapeutic approaches including phytotherapy. The review found a near-doubling of global CVD prevalence between 1990 and 2019 with mortality continuing to rise worldwide; atherosclerosis was characterized as the primary pathological basis of ischemic cardiovascular disorders, mediated by endothelial dysfunction, oxidative stress, inflammatory cytokines, and vascular remodeling. Association Between the Non-High-Density Lipoprotein/High-Density Lipoprotein Cholesterol Ratio and Sarcopenia in Older Chinese Men

Revue : Zhongguo Yi Xue Ke Xue Yuan Xue Bao (Acta Academiae Medicinae Sinicae) | Tier : NA | IF estime : NA

Date de publication : 2026-08-30

Resultats principaux : This cross-sectional study used baseline data from the China Health and Retirement Longitudinal Study, analyzing 1,849 men aged 60 years or older to investigate the relationship between the non-HDL/HDL cholesterol ratio (NHHR) and sarcopenia risk, with hypertension examined as a stratification covariate in subgroup analyses. Multiple adjusted logistic regression models estimated odds ratios with stepwise adjustment for demographic factors, lifestyle variables, chronic diseases including hypertension, and BMI; restricted cubic spline analysis characterized dose-response relationships. NHHR was inversely associated with sarcopenia: the highest NHHR quartile (Q4) showed an OR of 0.199 (95% CI 0.112-0.338, p<0.001) compared to Q1 in models without BMI adjustment; after BMI adjustment, the association remained significant but was attenuated (OR 0.403, 95% CI 0.220-0.709, p=0.002). The inverse NHHR-sarcopenia association was directionally consistent across hypertension, age, smoking, and drinking subgroups, suggesting hypertension does not substantially modify this metabolic-musculoskeletal relationship; restricted cubic spline analysis confirmed a linear dose-response pattern (p<0.001). Limitations include the cross-sectional design precluding causal inference, use of grip strength and walking speed rather than DXA/BIA-based direct muscle mass measurements, and restriction to male participants limiting generalizability.

Implications Medical Affairs : The consistent inverse NHHR-sarcopenia association across hypertension subgroups highlights complex metabolic interactions between lipid profiles, body composition, and musculoskeletal health in hypertensive older adults, an emerging comorbidity domain relevant to comprehensive cardiovascular risk management strategies. For Medical Affairs, these findings signal a growing evidence base linking cardiometabolic risk factors to functional decline and sarcopenia, providing rationale for positioning antihypertensive regimens within broader metabolic health frameworks that address body composition alongside blood pressure control. Longitudinal studies incorporating direct muscle mass measurements in hypertensive cohorts would fill a critical evidence gap and represent a strategic research investment to establish the musculoskeletal safety and benefit profiles of long-term antihypertensive treatment.

Pre-eclampsia

Revue : Lancet (London, England) | Tier : Tier 1 | IF estime : ~168

Date de publication : 2026-08-29

Resultats principaux : This comprehensive Lancet seminar reviews current understanding of pre-eclampsia, a multisystem disorder affecting at least 2-4% of pregnancies worldwide and characterized by hypertension and new-onset organ dysfunction; incidence and maternal/perinatal mortality are disproportionately higher in low- and middle-income countries (LMICs). The pathophysiology is multifactorial, primarily involving a mismatch between fetoplacental demands and uteroplacental supply compounded by maternal predisposition to cardiometabolic disease; key risk factors include chronic hypertension, diabetes, obesity, and prior pre-eclampsia. Preventive evidence includes structured behavioral interventions focused on diet quality and moderate-intensity physical activity for women with metabolic risk factors, and low-dose aspirin for high-risk women; risk-stratified timed delivery at term is recommended to reduce severe complications. Significant disparities in care exist between high-income countries and LMICs: while screening and prediction tools have advanced, implementation gaps persist in resource-limited settings where pre-eclampsia incidence is higher and mortality from the condition remains elevated. Delivery of the placenta remains the only definitive treatment, highlighting a profound unmet need for disease-modifying pharmacological interventions, and ongoing efforts focus on improved biomarker-based prediction and novel therapeutic targets.

Implications Medical Affairs : Publication in The Lancet of a comprehensive pre-eclampsia seminar reinforces hypertensive disorders of pregnancy as a global medical affairs priority, particularly in LMICs where maternal mortality from these conditions is disproportionately high, creating market and access program opportunities for antihypertensive portfolio companies. The emphasis on low-dose aspirin as the primary preventive intervention and absence of disease-modifying pharmacotherapy beyond delivery represents a substantial opportunity for Medical Affairs to position research investment in novel antihypertensive or vasoprotective agents targeting the pre-eclampsia pathway. KOL engagement with obstetric hypertension specialists referencing this landmark Lancet review strengthens advocacy for integrated maternal-cardiovascular risk management programs that bridge pregnancy hypertension and long-term cardiovascular risk in women.

Resultats principaux : This cross-sectional study enrolled 158 adult patients (mean age 60.79 years, SD 6.56; 86.7% female) referred for dual-energy X-ray absorptiometry (DXA) at the National Hospital in Damascus, Syria, to determine the prevalence of osteoporosis and identify associated factors including hypertension as a cardiometabolic comorbidity. Bone mineral density was classified per WHO criteria using the lowest T-score from lumbar spine or femoral neck; data on comorbidities including hypertension, diabetes, and medication use were collected through structured clinical evaluation forms, with chi-square tests and Spearman's correlation used for statistical analysis. Osteoporosis was identified in 53.2% of participants, osteopenia in 36.7%, and normal BMD in 10.1%; female sex was significantly associated with osteoporosis (chi-square 11.65, p=0.003) and hyperthyroidism was also a significant predictor (p=0.025). Notably, in this cohort hypertension, diabetes mellitus, smoking, and steroid use were not significantly associated with osteoporosis; however, BMI showed a significant negative correlation with femoral T-score (r=-0.257, p=0.001). Major limitations include the small sample size (n=158), selection bias due to restriction to a DXA-referred clinical population, single-center design in a conflict-affected healthcare setting, and cross-sectional design precluding causal inference.

Implications Medical Affairs : The absence of a significant association between hypertension and osteoporosis in this Syrian cohort provides population-specific evidence warranting caution in generalizing associations observed in Western high-income settings to conflict-affected, resource-limited environments where nutritional status and healthcare access differ profoundly. For Medical Affairs, this article highlights the importance of monitoring bone health outcomes in hypertensive patients as a residual cardiovascular risk domain; product safety profiles should include bone density-related data in relevant RCT subgroups, particularly for antihypertensive agents with known effects on mineral metabolism such as loop diuretics and thiazides. The high osteoporosis prevalence (53.2%) in this DXA-referred population presents an opportunity for cross-specialty Medical Affairs engagement with rheumatology and endocrinology KOLs to explore shared care models for hypertensive patients with osteoporosis, supporting comprehensive skeletal and cardiovascular risk management programs.
