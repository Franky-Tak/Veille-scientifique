Improving performance of polygenic risk scores for hypertension across two ancestry groups.

Revue : PloS one | Tier : NA | IF estime : NA

Date de publication : 2026-07-24

Resultats principaux : This cross-ancestry genetic study evaluated the performance of polygenic risk scores (PRS) for hypertension in two cohorts: REGARDS (1,533 European Americans [EA] with 58% HTN; 8,603 African Americans [AA] with 71% HTN) for optimization, and HyperGEN (1,270 EA with 55% HTN; 1,896 AA with 69% HTN) for validation. Two Bayesian PRS methods were compared: PRS-CS using the standard HapMap 3 (HM3) panel, PRS-CS with a multi-ancestry TagIt panel, and LDpred-funct incorporating functional annotations. In European Americans, advanced methods far outperformed the standard approach: PRS-CS-TagIt achieved R2=7.3% (OR per SD=2.17, 95% CI 1.65-2.85, p=3.0x10^-8) and LDpred-funct R2=6.0% (OR=2.14, 95% CI 1.61-2.85), versus R2=1.4% (OR=1.40) for PRS-CS-HM3. In African Americans, improvements were more modest (PRS-CS-TagIt R2=2.9% vs. R2=0.7% for HM3), revealing persistent inequities in PRS performance across ancestries despite method improvements. Key limitations include cross-sectional design, relatively small validation cohort sizes—especially for AA participants—and a predominantly US-based population, which constrains broader global applicability.

Implications Medical Affairs : The demonstrated superiority of multi-ancestry LD reference panels over standard approaches highlights a critical gap in current genetic risk stratification tools for hypertension that could directly influence future guideline recommendations on genotype-based screening. From a Medical Affairs perspective, this underscores the urgent need for ethnically diverse genetic datasets in developing precision medicine approaches for hypertension management, supporting dual positioning of advanced PRS tools alongside antihypertensive therapies in genetically stratified populations. Pharmaceutical companies developing antihypertensives could leverage improved PRS tools to identify high-risk minority populations for targeted clinical trials, providing a compelling framework for KOL engagement around precision medicine and health equity in cardiovascular risk reduction.

Impact of Hypertensive Disorders of Pregnancy on Maternal and Offspring Cardiovascular Health.

Revue : Current cardiology reports | Tier : NA | IF estime : NA

Date de publication : 2026-07-24

Resultats principaux : This narrative review synthesizes current evidence on the long-term cardiovascular (CV) sequelae of hypertensive disorders of pregnancy (HDP)—encompassing preeclampsia, gestational hypertension, and chronic hypertension—affecting 5-10% of pregnancies globally and representing a leading cause of maternal and fetal morbidity and mortality. In affected mothers, HDP confers a 7.29-fold increased risk of developing new chronic hypertension within 24 months postpartum, with documented long-term risks of heart failure, coronary heart disease, and stroke. Offspring exposed to HDP in utero carry a 1.5-fold increased risk for adult hypertension and elevated risks for ischemic heart disease and stroke, demonstrating intergenerational transmission of cardiovascular vulnerability. Proposed pathophysiological mechanisms include shared genetic predispositions, epigenetic programming changes, and persistent immune dysregulation, though the relative contribution of each mechanism remains poorly characterized. The review recommends integrated maternal-offspring CV surveillance beginning immediately postpartum through lifelong follow-up, while acknowledging that women-specific risk prediction tools and equity-focused research remain insufficiently developed.

Implications Medical Affairs : The evidence that HDP confers a 7.29-fold risk of post-partum hypertension supports a strong case for early antihypertensive intervention in HDP survivors, which could expand the eligible treatment population and influence future guideline updates on post-pregnancy cardiovascular management. For Medical Affairs, this creates a compelling positioning opportunity for antihypertensive agents in young women with a history of HDP—a currently underserved and rapidly growing patient segment whose long-term CV risk is increasingly recognized. The intergenerational risk data strengthens the case for KOL-led educational initiatives and research partnerships targeting integrated cardiobstetrics care pathways that demonstrate the long-term value of early blood pressure control in this population.

Prevalence and Impact of Pulmonary Hypertension Associated with Arteriovenous Fistulas and Grafts in End-Stage Renal Disease: A Systematic Review and Meta-Analysis.

Revue : Advances in respiratory medicine | Tier : NA | IF estime : NA

Date de publication : 2026-07-06

Resultats principaux : This systematic review and meta-analysis synthesized data from 11 observational studies (1,299 dialysis patients) examining the association between arteriovenous fistula (AVF) or graft (AVG) vascular access and pulmonary hypertension (PH) in end-stage renal disease (ESRD) patients. The pooled analysis of 10 studies (1,224 patients) demonstrated a statistically significant association between AVF/AVG use and PH (OR 2.06, 95% CI 1.69-2.52), with reassuringly low statistical heterogeneity (I2=0%). However, leave-one-out sensitivity analyses revealed that the pooled estimate lost statistical significance upon removal of the single most influential study, indicating the association is not uniformly robust across the contributing evidence base. A secondary analysis of mean pulmonary artery pressures was precluded by extreme heterogeneity (I2=99.4%) across the five reporting studies, preventing any pooled quantitative comparison of continuous hemodynamic parameters. The overall evidence base consists of predominantly small, cross-sectional, moderate-quality observational studies assessed by the Newcastle-Ottawa Scale, precluding causal inference and highlighting an urgent need for prospective hemodynamic studies.

Implications Medical Affairs : While the findings suggest that AVF/AVG-related cardiac shunting may promote pulmonary vascular remodeling contributing to the cardiovascular comorbidity burden in ESRD, the observational design and fragile pooled estimate preclude clinical recommendations and underscore the need for prospective studies before practice guidelines can be modified. From a Medical Affairs standpoint, this evidence supports targeted messaging to nephrologists and cardiovascular specialists about the unmet diagnostic need for structured PH screening protocols in high-risk ESRD patients, potentially creating positioning opportunities for pulmonary arterial hypertension therapies in this population. A strategic insight for KOL engagement is to highlight existing methodological limitations and advocate for company-sponsored or investigator-initiated prospective studies to establish causality and generate the regulatory-grade evidence needed to drive clinical practice change.

Hyperandrogenism Synergizes With Obesity to Increase the Risk of Hypertensive Disorders of Pregnancy After Frozen Embryo Transfer.

Revue : Journal of clinical hypertension (Greenwich, Conn.) | Tier : Tier 3 | IF estime : 3–10

Date de publication : 2026-07

Resultats principaux : This retrospective cohort study analyzed 1,556 infertile women undergoing frozen embryo transfer (FET) at Ruijin Hospital (Shanghai) between 2015-2022, stratified into four groups: controls (n=1,201), non-PCOS hyperandrogenism only (n=116), overweight/obesity only (n=209), and combined hyperandrogenism plus overweight/obesity (n=30). Multivariable logistic regression demonstrated that overweight/obesity alone was independently associated with gestational hypertension (aOR 3.45) and gestational diabetes mellitus (aOR 1.62). The combination of hyperandrogenism and overweight/obesity identified a dramatically high-risk phenotype: preeclampsia rate of 13.3% (aOR 7.53), preterm birth 23.3% (aOR 3.67), and cervical length shortening 6.7%, suggesting a synergistic rather than additive interaction between endocrine and metabolic disturbances. Non-PCOS hyperandrogenism alone did not significantly increase HDP risk, supporting the specific role of the combined endocrine-metabolic phenotype in amplifying preeclampsia susceptibility through vascular dysregulation. Key limitations include the retrospective single-center design, predominantly Chinese Han population limiting generalizability, and critically small sample size in the combined group (n=30) which limits the statistical power of the high-risk phenotype characterization.

Implications Medical Affairs : This study identifies a novel high-risk preeclampsia phenotype—non-PCOS hyperandrogenism combined with obesity in ART patients—which could catalyze the development of preconception cardiovascular risk stratification tools and influence obstetric guidelines recommending early blood pressure monitoring and preventive antihypertensive strategies in this population. From a Medical Affairs perspective, this represents a clinically significant patient subgroup within the growing global ART population, where company-sponsored real-world evidence initiatives could support antihypertensive prophylaxis and closer surveillance protocol development. Engaging KOLs in reproductive endocrinology and maternal-fetal medicine around this synergistic risk data would be strategically valuable for positioning antihypertensive interventions in at-risk preconception and early pregnancy populations.

Revue : The Pan African medical journal | Tier : NA | IF estime : NA

Date de publication : 2026-03-13

Resultats principaux : This cross-sectional study enrolled 318 hypertensive patients (289 with complete data) attending Kamuli General Hospital in East-Central Uganda using systematic sampling and WHO STEPS-adapted instruments for blood pressure and anthropometric measurement. The study population was predominantly female (79.6%) and older (58.2% aged >51 years), with alarming mean blood pressure values of SBP 162.4±22.8 mmHg and DBP 97.5±15.7 mmHg—substantially above the control threshold. The overall prevalence of uncontrolled hypertension (UCH, defined as SBP ≥140 mmHg and/or DBP ≥90 mmHg) was 85.8% (95% CI 81.3%-89.4%), representing a massive treatment gap in this Sub-Saharan African community health setting. Multivariable robust Poisson regression identified poor adherence to antihypertensive treatment and male gender as the two factors strongly and independently associated with UCH, while education level, physical activity level, and herbal medication use showed no significant independent associations. Notable limitations include the cross-sectional design precluding causal inference, single-hospital recruitment limiting generalizability to community settings, and reliance on self-reported adherence without objective pharmacy dispensing record validation.

Implications Medical Affairs : The 85.8% prevalence of uncontrolled hypertension in this Ugandan cohort underscores the massive treatment gap in Sub-Saharan Africa and reinforces adherence-enhancing strategies as the primary lever for improving population-level blood pressure control—directly relevant to guideline recommendations on structured medication adherence interventions in LMICs. Prehypertension, Hypertension, and Their Association With Weight-Adjusted Waist Index in Normoglycemic Japanese Adults: A Cross-Sectional Study.

Revue : Journal of diabetes research | Tier : NA | IF estime : NA

Date de publication : 2026

Resultats principaux : This secondary analysis of a hospital medical screening program dataset included 15,453 normoglycemic Japanese adults and examined the relationship between the weight-adjusted waist index (WWI—calculated as waist circumference divided by the square root of body weight) and prehypertension/hypertension outcomes. Multivariable logistic regression adjusted for key confounders showed WWI was independently and significantly associated with both prehypertension (OR=1.50, 95% CI 1.39-1.61, p<0.001) and hypertension (OR=1.74, 95% CI 1.51-2.00, p<0.001). Restricted cubic spline models confirmed a strictly linear, monotonic positive dose-response relationship between WWI and both blood pressure outcomes, with no evidence of non-linearity (all p-values for non-linearity >0.05). Subgroup analyses revealed a stronger WWI-prehypertension association in females, individuals without fatty liver disease, and current smokers, suggesting sex- and comorbidity-specific modifying effects on this relationship. Key limitations include the cross-sectional design precluding causal inference, single-center data from one Japanese hospital, and restriction to normoglycemic participants, which limits generalizability to patients with diabetes or impaired glucose tolerance.

Implications Medical Affairs : The robust linear association between WWI and hypertension risk in non-diabetic individuals supports WWI as a simple, non-invasive anthropometric metric that could complement BMI-based risk stratification and inform primary prevention guidelines for hypertension in Asian populations where body fat distribution patterns differ systematically from Western reference populations. From a Medical Affairs perspective, this evidence supports weight-inclusive cardiovascular risk messaging for hypertension in East Asian markets, potentially enabling dual positioning strategies for antihypertensives within the metabolic risk continuum addressing obesity-driven hypertension. Engaging KOLs in preventive cardiology and metabolic medicine in Japan with this data could catalyze interest in prospective weight-intervention-linked hypertension outcome studies that would generate evidence supporting integrated cardiometabolic therapeutic approaches.

Treatment with dapagliflozin and empagliflozin reduces concentrations of N4-acetylcytidine in plasma, a biomarker associated with vascular damage.

Revue : Cardiovascular diabetology | Tier : NA | IF estime : NA

Date de publication : 2026-07-24

Resultats principaux : This mechanistic metabolomics investigation utilized plasma and urine samples from three prospective randomized double-blind placebo-controlled crossover/parallel trials (total n=72 diabetic patients) treated with dapagliflozin, empagliflozin, or non-SGLT2 inhibiting glucose-lowering regimens over 6-12 weeks. Untargeted high-resolution mass spectrometry identified reduction of N4-acetylcytidine (ac4C)—a modified nucleoside and proposed biomarker for NAT10 acetyltransferase enzyme activity—as a novel finding specifically associated with SGLT2 inhibitor treatment (log fold-change: -0.32, adjusted p<0.001), not replicated with non-SGLT2 inhibiting regimens. As expected, SGLT2 inhibitors also reduced glucose (logfc -0.23, adj. p<0.01) and uric acid while increasing ketone bodies (3-hydroxybutyric acid logfc +0.84; 3-hydroxybutyrylcarnitine logfc +0.57 adj. p<0.001). Critically, retinal arteriole wall thickness and central systolic blood pressure were both significantly correlated with plasma ac4C concentrations, linking NAT10 activity inhibition to vascular structural and pressure endpoints relevant to hypertension. Key limitations include the small exploratory sample size (n=72 across three trials), short treatment duration (6-12 weeks), and the hypothesis-generating nature of untargeted metabolomics, which requires independent prospective validation in larger cardiovascular outcome trials.

Implications Medical Affairs : The identification of ac4C as a novel vascular damage biomarker selectively reduced by SGLT2 inhibitors adds mechanistic depth to the cardiovascular protective profile of dapagliflozin and empagliflozin, with direct relevance to reinforcing guideline recommendations for SGLT2i use in hypertensive patients with vascular risk factors beyond glycaemic control. This finding has meaningful Medical Affairs implications for positioning these agents as vascular-protective drugs through the NAT10/RNA acetylation pathway, enabling differentiated scientific messaging to cardiologists and nephrologists who manage high cardiovascular risk patients with hypertension and metabolic comorbidities. The demonstrated correlation of plasma ac4C with central systolic blood pressure represents an actionable biomarker-based insight for KOL-led investigator-initiated studies exploring NAT10 inhibition as a mechanistic endpoint in future hypertension trials incorporating SGLT2 inhibitors.

Effect of aspirin on healthspan in community-dwelling older adults: the ASPirin in reducing events in the elderly study.

Revue : Age and ageing | Tier : NA | IF estime : NA

Date de publication : 2026-07-02

Resultats principaux : The ASPREE (ASPirin in Reducing Events in the Elderly) trial was a double-blind, placebo-controlled, randomized multicenter trial enrolling 19,114 community-dwelling adults aged ≥70 years (≥65 years for US minority ethnic groups) from Australia and the US, followed for a median of 4.7 years. This pre-specified analysis evaluated the effect of daily low-dose aspirin (100 mg) on loss of healthspan, defined as the composite first occurrence of cardiovascular disease, diabetes, dementia, cancer, or death. A total of 5,835 participants (30.5%) experienced healthspan loss, with event rates of 82.1 vs. 82.7 per 1,000 person-years in aspirin and placebo groups respectively, demonstrating no clinically meaningful difference. The primary analysis showed no statistically significant benefit of aspirin (hazard ratio 0.98; 95% CI 0.92-1.05), confirming that low-dose aspirin does not extend healthspan in generally healthy older adults. Participants with hypertension were identified as significantly more likely to experience healthspan loss during the trial period, though aspirin conferred no protective benefit in this subgroup, and the 4.7-year median follow-up may be insufficient to detect differential effects on slower-developing conditions.

Implications Medical Affairs : This large, well-powered null RCT further consolidates the evidence base against aspirin for primary cardiovascular prevention in older adults, reinforcing recent guideline revisions that have deprioritized aspirin in favor of targeted cardiovascular risk reduction strategies including optimal blood pressure control. From a Medical Affairs standpoint, these findings strengthen the scientific positioning of modern antihypertensive agents, statins, and contemporary cardiovascular risk reduction portfolios as the preferred primary prevention approach over aspirin, particularly relevant for the rapidly growing global population of older adults with hypertension. The observation that hypertension was a significant predictor of healthspan loss in this trial represents an actionable key message for KOL engagement around comprehensive cardiometabolic risk factor management—with optimal blood pressure control as the cornerstone of healthy aging strategies.

The ubiquitin system in heart and vessel pathobiology: From mechanisms to therapeutic strategies.

Revue : Genes & diseases | Tier : NA | IF estime : NA

Date de publication : 2026-01-02

Resultats principaux : This comprehensive review synthesizes current evidence on ubiquitination—a key post-translational protein modification—in the pathogenesis of cardiovascular diseases including atherosclerosis, myocardial ischemia/reperfusion injury, cardiac remodeling, arrhythmia, and hypertension, focusing on E3 ubiquitin ligases as regulatory hubs targeting specific substrate proteins. In the context of hypertension, the review details how ubiquitination-driven NF-κB-mediated inflammatory signaling contributes to vascular remodeling, while proteasomal degradation pathways regulate cardiomyocyte hypertrophy—a critical end-organ damage manifestation of sustained hypertension. Specific E3 ligases are described as master regulators of macrophage inflammation, smooth muscle cell phenotypic switching (contributing to arterial stiffness), and fibroblast activation (driving cardiac fibrosis), all mechanistically relevant to hypertensive heart and vascular disease. Deubiquitinases are identified as counterbalancing regulators and emerging therapeutic targets, with the ubiquitin-proteasome system (UPS) positioned as a central node linking multiple cardiovascular pathophysiological processes. The review's primary limitations include its narrative (non-systematic) methodology without formal evidence grading, predominance of preclinical data, and the early-stage nature of clinical translation for ubiquitin-targeting therapeutic strategies in cardiovascular disease.

Implications Medical Affairs : The elucidation of E3 ubiquitin ligase-driven pathways in hypertensive cardiac remodeling and vascular dysfunction identifies novel molecular targets—specific E3 ligases and deubiquitinases—that could inform next-generation antihypertensive drug development programs, particularly addressing the unmet need of hypertension-associated cardiac fibrosis, left ventricular hypertrophy, and vascular stiffness. Resultats principaux : This educational review summarizes the key innovations of the 2024 European Society of Cardiology (ESC) hypertension guidelines, centered on the new clinically actionable category of 'elevated blood pressure' defined as 120-139/70-89 mmHg—a threshold that substantially expands the population eligible for structured clinical management. The 2024 ESC guidelines establish blood pressure as a continuous cardiovascular risk factor and recommend considering early antihypertensive pharmacotherapy below the traditional hypertension threshold (BP >130/80 mmHg) when patients have established cardiovascular disease, heart failure, or moderate-to-high cardiovascular risk, following three months of failed lifestyle modification. The unified cardiometabolic disease continuum: mechanistic stages of a single pathophysiological process.

Revue : Frontiers in endocrinology | Tier : NA | IF estime : NA

Date de publication : 2026-07-09

Resultats principaux : This structured narrative review proposes a unified cardiometabolic disease (UCD) continuum framework in which type 2 diabetes, atherosclerotic cardiovascular disease, HFpEF, MASLD, hypertension, and CKD represent interconnected stages of a single pathophysiological process, based on analysis of translational studies, major outcome trials, and consensus statements published 2017-2026. The proposed mechanistic framework identifies visceral adipose tissue dysfunction and ectopic lipid accumulation as the initiating event, progressing through ceramide-mediated lipotoxicity, NF-κB/NLRP3-driven inflammation, gut microbiome-derived endotoxemia (TMAO), adipokine dysregulation, endothelial dysfunction, epicardial fat-mediated cardiac remodeling, and impaired metabolic flexibility culminating in a cardiorenal amplification loop. Stage-specific biomarkers—ceramides, NLRP3, TMAO, leptin-adiponectin ratio—are proposed as potential tools for risk stratification and therapeutic monitoring across the UCD continuum. The benefits of GLP-1 receptor agonists, SGLT2 inhibitors, and finerenone across T2DM, ASCVD, HFpEF, MASLD, and CKD are interpreted as pharmacological validation of the continuum model, with each drug class modulating multiple mechanistic stages simultaneously. The review's limitations include the narrative methodology without formal systematic review protocol, predominance of preclinical mechanistic data over RCT-level evidence for the continuum framework itself, and the inherently hypothesis-generating nature of the proposed staging system.

Implications Medical Affairs : The UCD continuum framework has significant implications for hypertension guideline evolution, potentially supporting the integration of multi-disease cardiometabolic risk scoring—encompassing T2DM, CKD, MASLD, and ASCVD—alongside traditional BP-based stratification as a more comprehensive guide to treatment initiation and intensity. From a Medical Affairs standpoint, this conceptual framework offers a powerful scientific narrative for companies with broad cardiovascular-metabolic portfolios (combining SGLT2 inhibitors, GLP-1 agonists, and antihypertensives), enabling cross-therapeutic area positioning and reinforcing the mechanistic rationale for multi-drug combination strategies targeting the entire cardiometabolic continuum. Engaging cardiologists, endocrinologists, nephrologists, and hepatologists as cross-disciplinary KOLs around the UCD model through consensus publications and specialty symposia could establish scientific thought leadership and strengthen the evidence base for integrated cardiometabolic management programs.
