Longitudinal Trajectories of Body Roundness Index and Risk of Incident Hypertension in Non-Diabetic Elderly Adults: A Prospective Nationwide Cohort Study Involving Two Cohorts.

Revue : Global Heart | Tier : NA | IF estime : NA

Date de publication : 2026-09-25

Resultats principaux : This prospective nationwide cohort study analyzed non-diabetic middle-aged and older adults from the China Health and Retirement Longitudinal Study (CHARLS) with external validation in the English Longitudinal Study of Ageing (ELSA). BRI measurements from three waves were used to construct trajectories, with incident hypertension assessed at a fourth wave. Three distinct trajectories were identified: low-level inverted U-shaped, moderate-level inverted U-shaped, and high-level increasing. Compared to the low trajectory, adjusted ORs for incident hypertension were 1.40 (95% CI: 1.13-1.73) for the moderate trajectory and 1.76 (95% CI: 1.21-2.56) for the high-level increasing trajectory in CHARLS. Directionally consistent findings were observed in ELSA, and machine learning stacking ensemble models demonstrated BRI trajectory provides complementary predictive information beyond conventional clinical variables. Key limitations include observational design, potential residual confounding, and reliance on self-reported diagnoses across waves.

Implications Medical Affairs : This study supports monitoring longitudinal adiposity trajectories as complementary hypertension risk tools, informing medical affairs strategies around early detection platforms and preventive care positioning. The findings provide a basis for KOL discussions on adiposity trajectory monitoring in antihypertensive prevention trials, potentially supporting novel trial design proposals. Future product positioning for antihypertensives could incorporate BRI trajectory monitoring as a patient identification and stratification tool for high-risk non-diabetic populations.

Association of childhood loneliness with incident hypertension, heart disease, and stroke among low-educated Chinese women: A 9-year cohort study.

Revue : Medicine | Tier : NA | IF estime : NA

Date de publication : 2026-09-25

Resultats principaux : This prospective cohort study used CHARLS data, enrolling 4,341 women aged ≥45 years with low educational attainment at baseline (2011), followed through 2020 with a median 9.0-year follow-up. Childhood loneliness, defined as frequent loneliness without a close friend, was reported by 5.25% of participants. Over follow-up, 52.44% developed hypertension, 31.37% heart disease, and 10.92% had a stroke. In fully adjusted Cox models, childhood loneliness was associated with a 31% higher hazard of hypertension (HR 1.31, 95% CI: 1.01-1.70) and a 41% higher hazard of heart disease (HR 1.41, 95% CI: 1.06-1.89). The stroke association was elevated but not statistically significant (HR 1.47, 95% CI: 0.97-2.21). Limitations include retrospective loneliness assessment with potential recall bias, restriction to low-educated women limiting generalizability, and unmeasured psychosocial confounders.

Implications Medical Affairs : The demonstration that early psychosocial adversity is independently associated with adult hypertension risk expands the pathophysiological narrative beyond conventional risk factors, supporting Medical Affairs content on holistic cardiovascular risk assessment. For KOL engagement, this study provides a discussion point around psychosocial determinants of cardiovascular health and the unmet need in underrepresented female populations in China and similar settings. A product positioned for broad hypertension management should consider incorporating psychosocial co-morbidity messaging in its cardiovascular risk communication strategy.

Association between serum zinc levels and new-onset hypertension: A 5-year retrospective cohort study.

Revue : Medicine | Tier : NA | IF estime : NA

Date de publication : 2026-09-25

Resultats principaux : This retrospective cohort study used the TriNetX Research Network to analyze adults with serum zinc measurements between January 2010 and December 2023, comparing zinc-deficient (<0.7 µg/mL) to normal (0.70-1.20 µg/mL) patients after propensity score matching of 47,843 pairs over 5 years. Zinc deficiency was significantly associated with primary hypertension (HR 1.20, 95% CI: 1.14-1.25, P<.001), secondary hypertension (HR 1.82, 95% CI: 1.45-2.29, P<.001), hypertension-related cardiac and renal complications (HR 1.69, 95% CI: 1.49-1.92, P<.001), and hypertensive crisis (HR 1.09, 95% CI: 1.01-1.18, P=.028). Dose-response analyses revealed a J-shaped association for primary hypertension, with elevated hazards at both low and high zinc concentrations. The association was significantly stronger in men (HR 1.28) than women (HR 1.13, P for interaction = .021). Limitations include retrospective design, potential selection bias inherent to TriNetX data, and inability to establish causality.

Implications Medical Affairs : The J-shaped zinc-hypertension association introduces a novel micronutrient dimension to cardiovascular risk assessment, providing Medical Affairs with emerging evidence for nuanced micronutrient monitoring content in hypertensive patient management. This data opens opportunities for KOL-led discussions on micronutrient status as part of comprehensive cardiovascular risk profiling, particularly given the sex-specific findings. If confirmatory supplementation trials demonstrate modifiable hypertension risk through zinc correction, this could inform adjunctive prevention strategies for pharmaceutical portfolios targeting early hypertension.

Association between hypertension and frozen shoulder: A bidirectional two-sample Mendelian randomization study.

Revue : Medicine | Tier : NA | IF estime : NA

Date de publication : 2026-09-25

Resultats principaux : This bidirectional two-sample Mendelian randomization study analyzed GWAS data from 484,598 individuals for hypertension and 627,998 for frozen shoulder from the IEU Open GWAS database. The primary IVW analysis was complemented by weighted median, MR-Egger, and simple/weighted models. Hypertension was found to significantly increase the risk of frozen shoulder (IVW: OR = 1.749, 95% CI: 1.275-2.399, P = 5.239×10⁻⁴). The reverse direction showed no significant association (IVW: OR = 1.006, 95% CI: 0.991-1.021, P = .426). Heterogeneity and pleiotropy tests confirmed robustness of findings; a leave-one-out analysis assessed stability. The study establishes a unidirectional causal relationship from hypertension to frozen shoulder, highlighting musculoskeletal morbidity as an under-recognized hypertension complication. Limitations include reliance on GWAS summary statistics, potential residual pleiotropy, and predominantly European-ancestry populations.

Implications Medical Affairs : Establishing a causal link from hypertension to frozen shoulder through Mendelian randomization strengthens the evidence that hypertensive patients face greater musculoskeletal morbidity, which can expand the patient-centered complication narrative for antihypertensive treatment positioning. Medical Affairs teams can leverage this finding in KOL discussions on the broader systemic consequences of uncontrolled hypertension beyond conventional cardiovascular endpoints. This supports enriched messaging on comprehensive complication prevention for antihypertensive product portfolios, particularly in rheumatology-cardiology crossover settings.

Enhanced Facility-Based Hypertension Screening: A Quality Improvement Initiative at Primary Health Care Facilities in Ethiopia.

Revue : Global Heart | Tier : NA | IF estime : NA

Date de publication : 2026-09-23

Resultats principaux : This quality improvement initiative was implemented across six primary health care facilities in Ethiopia (April 2024 to February 2025) to increase facility-based blood pressure screening among adults without a prior hypertension diagnosis. Interventions included establishing BP screening desks at key service points, ensuring validated device availability, staff training in accurate measurement, standardized documentation, and reviewing screening indicators in performance meetings. Searching for Metabolic Markers for Hypertension in Human Urine.

Revue : Molecules | Tier : NA | IF estime : NA

Date de publication : 2026-09-21

Resultats principaux : This cross-sectional study used untargeted NMR metabolomics to analyze urinary profiles of normotensive and hypertensive participants without cardiovascular or metabolic comorbidities, in a population with high hypertension prevalence. Hypertensive participants showed significantly higher urinary sarcosine and lower levels of acetone, citrate, 4-hydroxyphenylacetate, 3-methylglutaconate, N-isovaleroylglycine, oxaloacetate, phenylalanine, and pyridoxate compared to normotensive subjects. These metabolites span energy metabolism, amino acid metabolism, gut microbiota-derived pathways, and vitamin B6 metabolism. Notably, none of the identified metabolites were significantly influenced by antihypertensive treatments or comorbidities such as dyslipidemia or diabetes, suggesting specificity for the hypertensive state. Citrate, methylglutaconate, N-isovaleroylglycine, oxaloacetate, phenylalanine, and sarcosine were identified as the most reliable biomarker candidates. Limitations include cross-sectional design, single-center recruitment, and relatively limited sample size without prospective validation.

Implications Medical Affairs : Urinary metabolomic profiling offers a non-invasive, treatment-independent diagnostic approach to hypertension that could serve as a companion screening tool alongside BP-based methods, informing medical affairs strategies in precision diagnostics. Pharmaceutical companies can engage metabolomics and precision medicine KOLs with this data to position biomarker-driven hypertension diagnostics as future differentiators in patient selection and monitoring. The independence of these metabolites from antihypertensive treatments enhances their potential utility as surveillance biomarkers for uncontrolled or emerging hypertension.

The Cerebroplacental-Renal Ratio as a Novel Doppler-Based Marker of Adverse Perinatal Outcome in Pregnancy-Induced Hypertension.

Revue : Medicina (Kaunas) | Tier : NA | IF estime : NA

Date de publication : 2026-09-19

Resultats principaux : This prospective single-center cohort study included 105 women with pregnancy-induced hypertension (PIH): preeclampsia (n=63) and gestational hypertension (n=42). Doppler assessment at ≥34 weeks evaluated the cerebroplacental ratio (CPR), cerebroplacental-uterine ratio (CPUR), and the newly defined cerebroplacental-renal ratio (CPRR = CPR/right renal artery pulsatility index). Adverse perinatal outcome (APO) occurred in 37 pregnancies (35.2%). CPRR showed a higher apparent AUC for APO (0.786, 95% CI: 0.698-0.875) compared to CPR and CPUR, with significantly higher AUCs on pairwise DeLong comparisons. After multivariable logistic regression adjustment, only CPRR was independently associated with APO (adjusted OR 1.423 per 0.1-unit decrease, 95% CI: 1.162-1.743, P=0.001). CPR alone did not differ significantly between APO and non-APO groups. Limitations include single-center design, small sample size, and requirement for external multicenter validation before clinical application.

Implications Medical Affairs : The CPRR introduces a novel composite Doppler marker that may improve perinatal risk stratification in PIH and preeclampsia, addressing a significant clinical unmet need in antihypertensive obstetric management. For Medical Affairs in antihypertensive or maternal health spaces, this evidence supports KOL discussions on optimizing monitoring strategies for high-risk pregnancies managed with antihypertensives. Prospective validation of CPRR in multicenter studies could become a key monitoring endpoint in future trials of interventions in pregnancy-related hypertension.

A Molecular Diagnostic Approach for Hypertension Through Establishment of a Metabolite Risk Score Using a Multi-Metabolite Panel Identified via UHPLC-MS/MS.

Revue : International Journal of Molecular Sciences | Tier : NA | IF estime : NA

Date de publication : 2026-09-12

Resultats principaux : This study used the Korea Biobank Network to conduct untargeted UHPLC-MS/MS plasma metabolomics in healthy individuals and hypertension patients, selecting 8 key metabolites via LASSO regression to construct a Metabolite Risk Score (MRS). In the replication set, MRS demonstrated strong discriminative performance for hypertension (AUC = 0.926, 95% CI: 0.876-0.976) and remained significantly associated with prevalent hypertension after adjusting for age and BMI (OR = 1.747, 95% CI: 1.317-2.318, P<0.001). Classification accuracy at the MRS cut-off was approximately 84%, with 83.7% sensitivity and 84% specificity in the replication set. Weak-to-moderate positive correlations with systolic BP were observed in both discovery (r=0.320) and replication (r=0.335) sets but became non-significant after adjustment. Integration with other omics platforms is proposed for future development. Limitations include cross-sectional design, limited sample sizes in discovery and replication sets, and lack of prospective validation.

Implications Medical Affairs : A plasma metabolite risk score with AUC exceeding 0.90 represents a significant advance in molecular hypertension diagnostics with potential companion diagnostic applications for antihypertensive products. Medical Affairs teams can position this as evidence supporting a paradigm shift toward molecular-based hypertension risk stratification, engaging precision medicine and biomarker KOLs in innovative research discussions. Future clinical trial designs for hypertension prevention or early intervention could incorporate this biomarker panel as a screening or stratification tool, representing a collaboration opportunity.

Gut, Oral, and Fungal Microbiota in Hypertension: A Multi-Compartment Systematic Review.

Revue : International Journal of Molecular Sciences | Tier : NA | IF estime : NA

Date de publication : 2026-09-09

Resultats principaux : This systematic review (PRISMA 2020 and SWiM guidelines) searched PubMed/MEDLINE, Embase, Scopus, and Web of Science from inception to June 2026, covering gut bacteriome, oral bacteriome, and fungal mycobiome in hypertension across observational studies, Mendelian randomization studies, and experimental studies. The most convergent gut finding was increased Ruminococcus gnavus abundance, replicated in three independent populations (OR 1.07, 95% CI: 1.01-1.14 for incident hypertension). Oral compartment depletion of nitrate-reducing Neisseria subflava was convergent across a US prospective cohort and Italian case-control study, with salivary nitric oxide approximately three-fold lower in hypertensive subjects. Mycobiome evidence was contradictory across two studies. A recurring finding across compartments was the collapse of microbial co-correlation networks and significant beta diversity differences despite null alpha diversity. GRADE certainty was very low for all taxon-level findings and low for salivary nitric oxide. Limitations include high study heterogeneity, predominance of cross-sectional designs, and absence of large-scale interventional data.

Implications Medical Affairs : Despite low GRADE certainty, the convergent signals around Ruminococcus gnavus and oral Neisseria subflava depletion warrant targeted investigation, informing Medical Affairs content on emerging microbiome-targeted therapeutic approaches in hypertension. KOLs in gut-cardiovascular research can be engaged on the nitrate-nitric oxide pathway as a modifiable hypertension target and potential adjunctive intervention for antihypertensive combination therapies. The systematic identification of evidence gaps provides a scientific roadmap for future clinical trial investment in microbiome-hypertension research.

Type 2 Diabetes, Visceral Adiposity, and Carotid Intima-Media Thickness in Hospitalized Adults: A Retrospective Cross-Sectional Analysis.

Revue : Medicina (Kaunas) | Tier : NA | IF estime : NA

Date de publication : 2026-09-03

Resultats principaux : This retrospective cross-sectional study included 147 adults admitted to Clinical CF Hospital Timisoara between July 2025 and June 2026, with bilateral cIMT available in 141 patients. T2DM was present in 26.5%, hypertension in 85.0%, and obesity in 81.6%. T2DM patients showed higher HOMA-IR (4.11 vs. 3.08, P=0.005) and fasting glucose (6.60 vs. 5.06 mmol/L, P<0.001) compared to non-diabetic patients. No secondary analysis survived multiple testing correction. Limitations include small sample size, single-center retrospective design, and inability to establish temporal causality.

Implications Medical Affairs : The independent association of hypertension with cIMT, persisting after adjustment for T2DM and visceral adiposity, reinforces the primacy of blood pressure control in preventing subclinical atherosclerosis in cardiometabolic patients. For Medical Affairs, this finding strengthens the argument for early and intensive antihypertensive intervention in hospitalized patients with diabetes and obesity as a cardiovascular risk reduction strategy. KOL engagement can focus on cIMT as an accessible subclinical atherosclerosis surrogate endpoint for cardiovascular protection discussions in antihypertensive research.

Alcohol and Hypertension - A Fresh Look on the Mechanistic Aspects of a Fatal Epidemiologic Relationship.

Revue : Biomolecules | Tier : NA | IF estime : NA

Date de publication : 2026-09-02

Resultats principaux : This narrative review synthesizes mechanistic evidence for the alcohol-hypertension relationship, noting that for heavy drinkers (≥72 g/day), reducing alcohol intake by half yields clinically meaningful BP reductions of 5.50 mmHg systolic and 3.97 mmHg diastolic. Ethanol's effects span neurons, vascular smooth muscle cells, endothelial cells, and immune cells, which express diverse ethanol-sensitive target structures. Membrane ion channels, particularly TRP channels and potassium channels, are highlighted as critical ethanol targets, with novel mechanistic emphasis on lipid-dependent modulation of plasma membrane signaling processes. The review covers autonomic nervous system dysregulation, renin-angiotensin-aldosterone system activation, oxidative stress, endothelial dysfunction, and altered vasomotor tone as pathogenic mechanisms. Evidence for dose-dependent cardiovascular effects and the complex non-linear relationship between alcohol and BP is discussed. Limitations include narrative review methodology with inherent selection bias and lack of systematic search documentation.

Implications Medical Affairs : The mechanistic update on alcohol-hypertension interactions highlights potential molecular targets (membrane ion channels, lipid-dependent signaling) for novel antihypertensive interventions, relevant for Medical Affairs discussions on pipeline innovation. KOLs in vascular biology and molecular pharmacology can be engaged on the translational potential of targeting ethanol-sensitive ion channels, opening discussions on precision antihypertensive strategies for alcohol-associated hypertension. This review supports Medical Affairs narratives positioning lifestyle modification, specifically alcohol reduction, as a clinically meaningful BP-reducing strategy complementary to pharmacotherapy.

Prognostic value of RDW and its derivatives in ICU patients with hypertensive chronic kidney disease.

Revue : BMC Cardiovascular Disorders | Tier : NA | IF estime : NA

Date de publication : 2026-08-08

Resultats principaux : This retrospective cohort study enrolled 6,731 ICU patients with hypertensive CKD from the MIMIC-IV database, analyzing red blood cell distribution width (RDW), hemoglobin-to-RDW ratio (HRR), and RDW-to-platelet ratio (RPR) as prognostic biomarkers. After multivariate adjustment, elevated RDW was associated with higher one-year all-cause mortality (HR = 1.19, 95% CI: 1.16-1.21, P<0.001), and higher HRR with reduced mortality (HR = 0.89, 95% CI: 0.87-0.91, P<0.001); RPR showed a U-shaped relationship. AUCs were 0.668 for RDW, 0.600 for HRR, and 0.515 for RPR. Among 6,731 patients, 2,505 (37.2%) died within one year, underscoring the severity of this comorbid condition. Both RDW and HRR significantly improved conventional risk model performance through NRI and IDI. Subgroup analyses identified significant interactions of RDW with CRRT use and CKD stage. Limitations include retrospective single-database design, potential coding inconsistencies in MIMIC-IV, and lack of external validation.

Implications Medical Affairs : RDW and its derivatives serve as readily available, low-cost prognostic tools in hypertensive CKD ICU patients, which Medical Affairs can incorporate into KOL discussions on cost-effective risk stratification in vulnerable comorbid populations. The high one-year mortality rate (37.2%) in hypertensive CKD ICU patients underscores a critical unmet medical need for more effective treatment strategies in this population. This data can support positioning arguments for renoprotective antihypertensive agents in high-risk CKD patients.

Comparative real-world safety profiles of selective beta1-adrenergic receptor blockers: A multi-database pharmacovigilance disproportionality study.

Revue : Medicine | Tier : NA | IF estime : NA

Date de publication : 2026-09-25

Resultats principaux : This pharmacovigilance study extracted adverse drug events for metoprolol, bisoprolol, and atenolol from 2004 to Q2 2025 from the FDA Adverse Event Reporting System (FAERS) and Canadian Vigilance Adverse Reaction Online Database. Disproportionality analyses, sex-stratified comparisons, and time-to-onset modeling were conducted. All three agents showed disproportionate reporting for bradycardia, conduction abnormalities, heart failure, and blood pressure instability. Metoprolol showed specific signals for BRASH syndrome, neuropsychiatric events, and suicide-related events. Bisoprolol showed signals for bradyarrhythmia, hyperkalemia, and acute kidney injury. Atenolol showed blood pressure perturbations, electrolyte imbalance, and interaction-related events. Sex-stratified analyses identified differences in preferred term reporting distributions for metoprolol and atenolol, with bisoprolol showing a more balanced distribution. All three agents displayed early-failure time-to-onset patterns suggesting need for closer monitoring after treatment initiation. Limitations include spontaneous reporting system limitations: underreporting, reporting bias, and inability to establish incidence or causality.

Implications Medical Affairs : This pharmacovigilance analysis provides Medical Affairs with real-world safety signal differentiation data across the three main selective beta1-blockers, enabling more nuanced safety communication to KOLs and prescribers about individualized drug selection. Drug-specific signals (BRASH syndrome for metoprolol, AKI for bisoprolol, electrolyte disturbances for atenolol) can inform individualized prescribing recommendations and serve as the foundation for KOL-led educational programs on safe beta-blocker use. For product positioning of beta-blocker formulations, these spontaneous reporting patterns can highlight safety differentiation and justify targeted post-marketing surveillance investments.

Mineralocorticoid Receptor Blockade Mitigates Loop Diuretic-Exacerbated Plasma Volume Contraction and Target-Organ Fibrosis in Angiotensin II-Induced Hypertension.

Revue : International Journal of Molecular Sciences | Tier : NA | IF estime : NA

Date de publication : 2026-09-16

Resultats principaux : This preclinical study assigned male Sprague-Dawley rats to six groups (n=6/group): sham or angiotensin II-induced hypertension with or without furosemide and/or spironolactone. Plasma volume (PV) was measured by indocyanine green dilution along with BP, histological, and molecular assessments. Angiotensin II reduced PV, and furosemide caused further PV decline without additional BP reduction, accompanied by aggravated fibrosis, endothelial impairment, and dysregulation of fibrosis-related proteins. Spironolactone significantly attenuated PV contraction, lowered BP, preserved endothelial integrity, alleviated fibrosis, and partially normalized molecular alterations. The study demonstrates that MR blockade protects against loop diuretic-exacerbated volume depletion and target organ injury in RAAS-driven hypertension. Data were analyzed by two-way repeated-measures ANOVA for longitudinal endpoints and nested ANOVA for histological measurements. Limitations include small group sizes (n=6 per group), male-only animal cohort, single-dose drug evaluations, and inherent limitations of extrapolating preclinical findings to human RAAS-driven hypertension.

Implications Medical Affairs : This preclinical evidence provides mechanistic support for combining mineralocorticoid receptor antagonists with loop diuretics in high RAAS-activation hypertension, offering Medical Affairs a data point for KOL discussions on combination regimen rationale. The volume-independent organ protection by spironolactone positions MRA-diuretic combinations as potentially superior to loop diuretic monotherapy in resistant hypertension with RAAS activation, supporting clinical trial design concepts. For MRA-based product positioning, this study reinforces target organ protection as a key narrative beyond blood pressure reduction alone.

Dietary Fiber Supplementation Reduces Systolic Blood Pressure in High-Risk Patients with Type 2 Diabetes and Hypertension: A Secondary Analysis of a Randomized Trial.

Revue : Nutrients | Tier : NA | IF estime : NA

Date de publication : 2026-09-08

Resultats principaux : This secondary analysis was based on a 12-week randomized, double-blind, parallel-group clinical trial enrolling 35 adults with type 2 diabetes and hypertension (mean age 50.7 ± 5.7 years, 77% female). Participants received either 5 g/day of oat beta-glucan (soluble dietary fiber, SDF, n=19) or microcrystalline cellulose (insoluble dietary fiber, IDF, n=16). Both groups showed comparable reductions in systolic BP: SDF -11.2 ± 20.9 mmHg and IDF -11.6 ± 17.0 mmHg (P=0.965 between groups). In the SDF group, significant positive correlations were observed between specific gut bacteria abundance changes and diastolic BP changes. Inflammatory biomarkers were assessed alongside gut microbiota by qPCR. The absence of a true placebo arm limits causal interpretation of the BP reductions. Limitations include absence of a no-fiber placebo control, very small sample size (n=35), 12-week duration limiting assessment of long-term effects, and exploratory nature of microbiota-BP correlations.

Implications Medical Affairs : The comparable and clinically meaningful systolic BP reductions exceeding 11 mmHg with both fiber types in T2D/hypertension patients suggest antihypertensive potential of dietary fiber supplementation, providing Medical Affairs with a compelling lifestyle evidence base for integration into patient education. KOL discussions can be stimulated around microbiome-mediated BP regulation as a complementary mechanism to pharmacological antihypertensive strategies, particularly in high-risk metabolic populations. A placebo-controlled confirmatory trial would substantially strengthen evidence for fiber supplementation as a guideline-recommended adjunctive therapy, representing a high-value research collaboration opportunity.

Risk factors of primary angle-closure glaucoma: An exposome-wide association study in the China Kadoorie Biobank.

Revue : Asia-Pacific Journal of Ophthalmology | Tier : NA | IF estime : NA

Date de publication : 2026-09-26

Resultats principaux : This exposome-wide association study used China Kadoorie Biobank data (499,102 participants aged 30-79 years, median 15.7-year follow-up) and explored 117 exposures in relation to incident probable primary angle-closure glaucoma (PACG), with 3,266 incident cases. After multiple testing correction using elastic-net, XGBoost, and Cox regression, 26 factors were independently associated with PACG. Strongest positive associations were with female sex, postmenopausal status, and age. Metabolic and vascular aging markers including diabetes, higher random plasma glucose, larger waist circumference, and elevated pulse pressure were independently associated with higher PACG risk, while BMI was inversely associated. Notably, hypertension per se, systolic BP, and diastolic BP showed no significant independent associations with PACG. Daily intake of dairy products and preserved vegetables, as well as peptic ulcer, were associated with higher risk, while physical activity and tea consumption were associated with lower risk. Limitations include use of probable PACG without electrodiagnostic adjudication, potential outcome misclassification, and unavailability of intraocular pressure measurements at baseline.

Implications Medical Affairs : The finding that systemic metabolic markers rather than hypertension per se drive PACG risk reshapes understanding of vascular and metabolic contributions to glaucoma pathogenesis, opening new angles for Medical Affairs KOL discussions linking metabolic syndrome to ocular complications. For antihypertensive Medical Affairs, this data clarifies that BP-lowering therapy should not be expected to independently reduce PACG risk, supporting accurate and balanced risk communication to clinicians. Future collaborative research opportunities between ophthalmic and cardiometabolic KOLs can be facilitated using this large-scale prospective evidence base from the CKB.
