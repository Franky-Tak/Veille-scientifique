Time Trends in Mortality from Cardiovascular Diseases: An Ecological Study of Brazil's Regions and States, 2010-2023.

Revue : Arquivos brasileiros de cardiologia | Tier : NA | IF estime : NA

Date de publication : 2026-09-25

Resultats principaux : This ecological time-series study analyzed age-standardized mortality rates for cardiovascular diseases (CVD), hypertensive diseases (HD), ischemic heart disease (IHD), and cerebrovascular diseases (CEVD) by sex across Brazil's 27 Federative Units from 2010 to 2023, using publicly available DATASUS data and Joinpoint regression to compute annual percent change (APC) and average annual percent change (AAPC). The study population encompassed the entire Brazilian population over the 14-year period, with 4,991,937 CVD deaths recorded, of which 52.50% occurred in men. While CVD mortality declined significantly in several regions — Federal District (men AAPC -3.58%, p<0.001) and the South region (men AAPC -2.23%, p<0.001) — it increased in the Northern states of Amazonas (AAPC +1.04%, p<0.01) and Amapá (+1.54%, p<0.01), revealing profound regional disparities. Hypertensive disease mortality specifically increased in Bahia (men: +1.38%, p=0.03; women: +1.50%, p=0.04) and Rio Grande do Sul (men: +1.93%, p<0.001; women: +1.86%, p<0.01), while premature HD mortality rose among men in the South (+1.61%, p<0.001). A key limitation of this ecological study is that individual-level data on access to antihypertensive treatment, medication adherence, or socioeconomic determinants were unavailable, making it impossible to attribute mortality trends directly to specific healthcare interventions or treatment gaps.

Implications Medical Affairs : These findings demonstrate that hypertensive disease mortality is rising in specific Brazilian states and demographic subgroups despite overall cardiovascular improvements, identifying high-unmet-need geographies with direct relevance for pharmaceutical market access strategy, health technology assessment arguments, and targeted real-world evidence generation in Brazil. For Medical Affairs, the divergent mortality trajectories between Southern and Northern Brazil present a compelling case for differentiated KOL engagement strategies and health-system partnership initiatives focused on hypertension screening and treatment adherence programs in vulnerable populations, particularly in Amazonian states. These epidemiological data provide a strong scientific foundation for value dossiers and payer engagement, supporting the argument that expanding antihypertensive therapy access in high-mortality regions is not only clinically imperative but also economically justifiable from a public health perspective.

Effects of SGLT-2 Inhibitors on Plasma Concentrations and Renal Clearance of the Risk Marker Trimethylamine-N-Oxide in Patients With Type-2 Diabetes.

Revue : Endocrinology, diabetes & metabolism | Tier : NA | IF estime : NA

Date de publication : 2026-11

Resultats principaux : This study reports secondary analyses from two prospective, randomized, placebo-controlled, double-blind, crossover trials in patients with type 2 diabetes (T2D), investigating the effects of SGLT-2 inhibitors empagliflozin (N=69, NCT02471963) and dapagliflozin (N=54, NCT02383238) on plasma trimethylamine-N-oxide (TMAO) concentrations and renal TMAO clearance (Cl), measured by LC-MS/MS in plasma and 24-hour urine samples at baseline and after each crossover period. Across the combined 123 T2D patients, SGLT-2 inhibitor treatment was associated with an 11.5% higher TMAO plasma level compared to placebo (p=0.006), accompanied by a 10.3% reduction in renal TMAO clearance (p=0.048), without a significant change in urinary TMAO excretion (p=0.521). The effect was primarily driven by dapagliflozin, showing a 23.4% higher TMAO plasma concentration (p=0.021) and a 22.6% lower renal clearance (p=0.025) versus placebo; empagliflozin showed a similar non-significant trend. Given that elevated TMAO levels have been previously associated with adverse cardiovascular outcomes and increased mortality in observational studies, this unexpected pharmacological signal may have implications for interpreting the net cardiovascular risk-benefit profile of SGLT-2 inhibitors, particularly in patients with comorbid hypertension and T2D. The main limitations include the secondary analysis design — plasma and urine samples were not collected primarily for TMAO measurement — the relatively modest sample sizes per drug, and the absence of long-term cardiovascular outcome data linked to the observed TMAO elevations.

Implications Medical Affairs : The observation that SGLT-2 inhibitors may modestly increase TMAO, a putative gut microbiome-derived cardiovascular risk biomarker, introduces a nuanced pharmacological consideration for the positioning of antihypertensive agents with favorable metabolic profiles in high-risk cardiometabolic patients. For Medical Affairs in the antihypertension space, this finding creates an opportunity to strengthen differentiated messaging for agents lacking metabolic TMAO interactions, particularly in the growing population of patients with both hypertension and T2D, where cardiovascular risk reduction is paramount. Strategically, this study calls for KOL engagement with cardiometabolic specialists to contextualize the clinical significance of TMAO changes relative to the established cardiovascular benefits of SGLT-2 inhibitors, and supports the development of prospective mechanistic studies evaluating TMAO dynamics in hypertensive patients on combined antidiabetic and antihypertensive regimens.

Effects of high-dose supplemental oxygen therapy on pulmonary haemodynamics at 3250 m in high-altitude residents at risk for pulmonary hypertension.

Revue : BMJ open respiratory research | Tier : NA | IF estime : NA

Date de publication : 2026-10-01

Resultats principaux : This open-label, non-randomized, sequential interventional trial enrolled 48 Kyrgyz highlanders living at altitudes above 2500 m with tricuspid regurgitation velocity >2.8 m/s, a marker of elevated systolic pulmonary artery pressure (sPAP) suggestive of pulmonary hypertension risk, to assess the hemodynamic effects of high-dose supplemental oxygen therapy (SOT; FiO2 approximately 0.95, 10 L/min, >15 min) compared to ambient air at 3250 m altitude (NCT06489756). The primary endpoint was sPAP change assessed by echocardiography; secondary endpoints included cardiac output (CO) and total pulmonary resistance (TPR). SOT significantly reduced mean sPAP from 45±11 to 31±5 mmHg (p<0.001), CO from 5.1±1.2 to 4.3±1.4 L/min (p<0.001), and TPR from 9.2±2.7 to 7.5±1.5 WU (p<0.001). An exploratory subanalysis demonstrated that participants with higher baseline sPAP and younger age had larger sPAP reductions with SOT, suggesting that the acute hypoxic vasoconstrictive component is more prominent in earlier stages of high-altitude pulmonary hypertension. Key limitations include the open-label, non-randomized design without a concurrent control group, the small sample size (n=48), and the focus on acute rather than sustained hemodynamic effects, which limits conclusions about long-term therapeutic strategies.

Implications Medical Affairs : This trial provides mechanistic evidence that reversible hypoxic vasoconstriction, rather than fixed vascular remodeling, is the dominant driver of elevated pulmonary artery pressure in high-altitude residents at risk for pulmonary hypertension, supporting the therapeutic rationale for vasodilatory agents — including calcium channel blockers — as first-line treatments in this specific form of pulmonary hypertension. For Medical Affairs, these findings highlight high-altitude pulmonary hypertension as an underrepresented indication with growing scientific interest, presenting opportunities to position antihypertensive compounds with pulmonary vasodilatory properties in emerging market populations in Central Asia and the Andean region. This study also identifies high-altitude Kyrgyz populations as a novel scientific cohort for academic collaborations and KOL development in the field of hypoxic pulmonary hypertension, supporting congress presence and disease awareness initiatives in a historically neglected geographic segment.

Carbon nanoparticle-Fe (II) complex for ferroptosis therapy in refractory solid tumors: a phase I trial.

Revue : Signal transduction and targeted therapy | Tier : NA | IF estime : NA

Date de publication : 2026-10-01

Resultats principaux : This first-in-human, single-arm, open-label, dose-escalation Phase I trial (NCT06048367) evaluated the safety, tolerability, pharmacokinetics, and preliminary efficacy of CNSI-Fe — a novel carbon nanoparticle-Fe(II) complex delivered by intratumoral injection to induce ferroptosis — in 19 patients with histologically confirmed refractory solid tumors across five dose levels (30-150 mg) using a 3+3 design. The primary objectives were dose-limiting toxicity (DLT) and maximum tolerated dose (MTD) determination; the MTD was not reached, with only one DLT event observed in the 90 mg cohort. Hypertension was the second most common adverse event, occurring in 52.6% of patients, alongside injection site pain (89.5%), elevated serum iron (47.4%), and anemia (47.4%), highlighting clinically meaningful blood pressure elevations as a treatment-emergent toxicity of iron-based ferroptosis inducers. Pharmacokinetic analysis revealed rapid iron absorption (peak time 0.15-0.41h) and clearance (half-life 1.96-5.06h), with peak serum concentrations of 3,520-13,962 ng/mL; the disease control rate was 84.2% (overall response rate 10.5%), with 100% disease control in lymph node and sarcoma lesions. The principal limitations include the very small heterogeneous sample (n=19), absence of a control arm, and inability to disentangle systemic from local iron effects as contributors to the observed hypertension adverse events.

Implications Medical Affairs : The high rate of treatment-emergent hypertension (52.6%) in this ferroptosis-based oncology trial adds to the evidence base that novel anticancer agents targeting iron metabolism and oxidative stress pathways can induce clinically significant blood pressure elevations, reinforcing the importance of proactive antihypertensive co-management in oncology settings. For Medical Affairs in the antihypertensive space, this highlights an expanding cardio-oncology opportunity: patients receiving novel targeted therapies increasingly require antihypertensive co-medications, creating a positioning case for well-tolerated antihypertensive agents with favorable safety profiles in cancer populations. This study supports the development of medical education programs and scientific communication targeting oncology-cardiology interfaces, as well as collaborations with oncology KOLs to establish evidence-based protocols for treatment-emergent hypertension management in patients receiving iron-based ferroptosis inducers and other novel cancer therapies.

Baseline characteristics of participants recruited into the COLO-COHORT study: a UK-wide resource for colorectal cancer prevention.

Revue : BMJ open gastroenterology | Tier : NA | IF estime : NA

Date de publication : 2026-10-01

Resultats principaux : The COLO-COHORT is a prospective, multicentre observational study recruiting adults undergoing colonoscopy through National Health Service colorectal cancer screening and routine referral pathways across 27 sites in the United Kingdom, with baseline data now available from the first 4,754 participants. The cohort has a median age of 64 years (IQR 57-70), is 54.3% male, with high prevalence of overweight (38.0%) and obesity (30.5%), reflecting the cardiometabolic risk profile of a colonoscopy-referred UK population. Hypertension was recorded in 39.4% of participants and type 2 diabetes in 11.8%, confirming that this cancer screening cohort overlaps substantially with established cardiovascular risk populations. Colorectal neoplasia was identified at colonoscopy in 56.1% of participants, with detailed polyp characterization including type, size, morphology, and location; core demographic and clinical variables were available for >95% of participants, and linked blood and stool microbiome biospecimens for approximately 80% and 40% respectively. Limitations include the cross-sectional nature of these baseline data, potential selection bias from a colonoscopy-referred population enriched for neoplasia risk, and the absence of dietary or physical activity data in the current report.

Implications Medical Affairs : The 39.4% prevalence of hypertension in this colonoscopy-based colorectal cancer screening cohort underscores that cancer screening programs serve as an important touchpoint for identifying and engaging uncontrolled hypertensive patients, presenting a public health opportunity for integrated cardiometabolic and oncological screening initiatives. For Medical Affairs, these data support the development of cross-specialty educational programs linking gastroenterologists and primary care physicians in the identification and referral of hypertensive patients at cancer screening visits, potentially positioning antihypertensive therapy in the context of comprehensive preventive health. The COLO-COHORT biorepository — with linked microbiome and cardiovascular risk data — represents a scientifically valuable resource for translational research on shared pathophysiological pathways between hypertension and colorectal cancer, including gut microbiome-mediated mechanisms, which could be leveraged for innovative KOL partnerships and hypothesis-generating grant proposals.

Cross-Sectional and Longitudinal Associations Between Dietary Intake and Depressive Symptoms in Young South African Adults: The African-PREDICT Study.

Revue : Depression and anxiety | Tier : NA | IF estime : NA

Date de publication : 2026-09-29

Resultats principaux : This 5-year prospective cohort study, embedded within the African-PREDICT program and the Global Burden of Disease Lifestyle And mental Disorder (GLAD) project, examined cross-sectional and longitudinal associations between dietary intake and depressive symptoms in 1,039 young South African adults aged 20-30 years (mean age 24.55 years; 51.4% female; 48.6% Black) in the North West Province. Dietary exposures were assessed using three non-consecutive 24-hour dietary recalls at baseline, and depression was measured at baseline (n=1,039) and 5-year follow-up (n=551) using the Patient Health Questionnaire (PHQ-9, cut-off ≥10). Baseline depression prevalence was high at 29.45%; in longitudinal analyses adjusted for total energy intake, higher milk intake was associated with lower incident depression risk (RR=0.94, 95% CI 0.91-0.98), and cross-sectionally, higher calcium (OR=0.48, 95% CI: 0.31-0.76) and vegetable intake (OR=0.74, 95% CI: 0.61-0.91) were associated with lower depression odds, while higher sugar-sweetened beverage (SSB) consumption was associated with higher odds. After full sociodemographic adjustment, most dietary associations attenuated substantially, suggesting socioeconomic factors are important confounders of the diet-depression relationship in this young African urban-rural population. Key limitations include potential measurement error from 24-hour dietary recalls, substantial attrition from baseline to follow-up (47%), and inability to establish causal directionality given residual confounding.

Implications Medical Affairs : Although the primary focus of this study is depression rather than hypertension, it originates from the African-PREDICT cardiovascular cohort and its findings on sugar-sweetened beverage consumption and poor dietary quality as risk factors for depression have direct Medical Affairs relevance, given the well-documented bidirectional links between depression, poor dietary adherence, uncontrolled blood pressure, and worse hypertension treatment outcomes. For Medical Affairs in hypertension, these data reinforce the strategic narrative of holistic cardiovascular risk management — emphasizing dietary and lifestyle interventions alongside pharmacotherapy — particularly in young African adults who represent an emerging, rapidly growing hypertensive population in sub-Saharan Africa. The African-PREDICT cohort is an underutilized but high-value scientific resource for KOL partnerships and longitudinal data generation on cardiometabolic comorbidities — including the hypertension-depression-dietary quality nexus — in low- and middle-income countries, presenting opportunities for academic collaborations, grant applications, and data-sharing initiatives that could strengthen a company's scientific leadership in African cardiovascular health.
