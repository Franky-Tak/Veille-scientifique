[Arterial hypertension and bronchial asthma: phenotype, cardiovascular complications, and independent predictors - an analysis of 57,396 patients from a hypertension registry].

Revue : Terapevticheskii arkhiv | Tier : NA | IF estime : NA

Date de publication : 2026-08-18

Resultats principaux : This large-scale retrospective analysis included 57,396 patients from a national hypertension registry, comparing 56,784 AH patients without bronchial asthma (BA) and 612 with comorbid AH+BA. The AH+BA phenotype was predominantly female (71.4% vs 59.4%), older (mean 65.0 vs 62.7 years), with higher BMI and greater rates of abdominal obesity (53.7% vs 39.0%). Coronary artery disease was significantly more prevalent in AH+BA patients (56.2% vs 41.2%), as were chronic heart failure (68.8% vs 48.6%) and atrial fibrillation (10.1% vs 4.3%), all p<0.001. After adjustment for age and sex, BA remained an independent predictor of CAD (OR 2.02, 95%CI 1.71-2.40), CHF (OR 2.47, 95%CI 2.07-2.96), and AF (OR 2.49, 95%CI 1.88-3.23), but showed no significant association with stroke. A key limitation is the retrospective, registry-based design, which precludes causal inference and may be subject to selection bias and incomplete covariate adjustment.

Implications Medical Affairs : The identification of BA as an independent predictor of major cardiovascular complications in hypertensive patients calls for an update of risk stratification tools in hypertension guidelines to formally incorporate BA comorbidity. From a Medical Affairs perspective, this phenotyping data reinforces the positioning of antihypertensive agents with proven cardioprotective benefits in the AH+BA subpopulation, particularly agents not contraindicated in obstructive airway disease. Strategic KOL engagement and real-world evidence generation targeting this high-risk comorbidity phenotype would strengthen the scientific narrative around comprehensive cardiovascular protection.

Renin-Angiotensin System Inhibitor Use and Mortality in Older Hypertensive Patients With and Without Dementia: A Nationwide Registry-Based Cohort Study.

Revue : Journal of Clinical Hypertension (Greenwich) | Tier : 3 | IF estime : ~3

Date de publication : 2026-08

Resultats principaux : This nationwide Swedish register-based cohort study examined 158,176 older hypertensive patients (2007-2018), including 21,152 RASI users and 137,024 non-users, with propensity score matching to balance confounders. In the full cohort, RASI use was associated with significantly lower all-cause mortality (HR 0.93, 95%CI 0.91-0.95, p<0.001), with a more pronounced effect among patients with comorbid dementia (HR 0.88, 95%CI 0.85-0.92). Comparing ACE inhibitors versus ARBs within RASI users, ARBs were associated with lower mortality in the full cohort (HR 0.90, 95%CI 0.85-0.96), though this difference did not reach significance in the dementia subgroup. Analyses of individual agents (enalapril, ramipril, losartan, candesartan) were broadly consistent with class-level findings. As an observational study, residual confounding from unmeasured factors remains a key limitation, and causal conclusions should be drawn with caution.

Implications Medical Affairs : These findings provide robust real-world evidence supporting continued use of RASI therapies in elderly hypertensive patients including those with dementia, a population often undertreated due to polypharmacy concerns, and may influence guideline recommendations for this vulnerable group. The differential mortality benefit favoring ARBs over ACEIs in the general cohort has direct product-positioning implications for Medical Affairs teams promoting ARB-class agents, underscoring a differentiation argument in competitive landscapes. This dataset opens opportunities to initiate KOL-partnered retrospective analyses in other national registries and to design prospective studies addressing hypertension management specifically in patients with cognitive impairment.

Twenty-Four-Hour Blood Pressure Variability Combined With Circadian Rhythm Patterns in Predicting Cardiovascular Events in Hypertensive Patients.

Revue : Journal of Clinical Hypertension (Greenwich) | Tier : 3 | IF estime : ~3

Date de publication : 2026-08

Resultats principaux : This retrospective cohort study enrolled 500 adults with essential hypertension who underwent 24-hour ambulatory blood pressure monitoring (ABPM), with a median follow-up of 4.2 years and MACE (myocardial infarction, stroke, heart failure hospitalization, or cardiovascular death) as the primary composite outcome. Among 128 patients (25.6%) who experienced MACE, high short-term BPV defined by elevated standard deviation of 24-hour systolic BP was associated with significantly higher MACE incidence (34.2% vs 17.1%, p<0.001) and remained an independent predictor after adjustment (HR 1.89, 95%CI 1.32-2.71, p<0.001). Reverse dipping circadian patterns were independently associated with increased cardiovascular risk compared with dipper patterns. Patients combining elevated BPV with abnormal circadian patterns represented the highest-risk phenotype in the study population. Limitations include the retrospective single-center design, modest sample size of 500 patients, and reliance on a single ABPM measurement without serial monitoring.

Implications Medical Affairs : The independent and additive predictive value of BPV and circadian patterns for MACE reinforces the clinical utility of ABPM beyond mean blood pressure measurement, creating an opportunity for Medical Affairs to advocate for broader ABPM integration in hypertension management guidelines. Antihypertensive agents with documented effects on BPV reduction and dipping pattern normalization may leverage this evidence for enhanced positioning in treatment algorithms, particularly in patients with documented circadian BP abnormalities. Engaging cardiologists and hypertension specialists around ABPM-based risk stratification represents a high-value KOL strategy and could anchor company-sponsored education initiatives and investigator-led studies on BPV-targeted therapy.

Cardiovascular events with esaxerenone versus spironolactone in hypertension.

Revue : Journal of Human Hypertension | Tier : 3 | IF estime : ~3

Date de publication : 2026-08-20

Resultats principaux : This real-world pharmacoepidemiological study using a Japanese nationwide claims database enrolled 5,960 hypertensive patients newly initiating either esaxerenone (ESAX, n=3,776), a nonsteroidal mineralocorticoid receptor antagonist, or spironolactone (SPL, n=2,184), a steroidal MRA, with a median follow-up of approximately 15 months. The composite cardiovascular outcome (heart failure, atrial fibrillation, myocardial infarction, or stroke) occurred in 1,060 patients; in propensity score overlap-weighted Cox regression, ESAX was associated with significantly lower CVD risk compared to SPL (HR 0.80, 95%CI 0.70-0.91). The benefit was driven primarily by reductions in heart failure (HR 0.80, 95%CI 0.70-0.92) and atrial fibrillation (HR 0.63, 95%CI 0.44-0.91), while differences for MI (HR 0.97) and stroke (HR 0.88) were not statistically significant. The study population was older (median 73 years) with 20% having diabetes, reflecting a real-world hypertensive cohort with high cardiovascular burden. Limitations include the observational design, residual confounding from unmeasured variables such as dietary intake and treatment adherence, and the relatively short median follow-up of 451 days.

Implications Medical Affairs : These data provide compelling real-world evidence for MRA class differentiation, suggesting esaxerenone may offer superior cardiovascular protection over spironolactone in hypertension particularly for heart failure and atrial fibrillation prevention, outcomes of high importance to payers and formulary decision-makers. From a Medical Affairs standpoint, this study strengthens the scientific rationale for positioning esaxerenone in treatment algorithms for hypertensive patients at high cardiovascular risk, especially those with concomitant risk of AF or HF. Sponsoring or co-authoring confirmatory prospective registry studies and engaging key hypertension and cardiology opinion leaders with these findings would constitute a high-priority scientific platform strategy.

Sociodemographic determinants and prevalence of metabolic risk factors and the metabolic syndrome among women aged 20-49 in Kirinyaga County, Kenya.

Revue : Frontiers in Epidemiology | Tier : NA | IF estime : NA

Date de publication : 2026-08-06

Resultats principaux : This cross-sectional study enrolled 425 women aged 20-49 years in Kirinyaga County, Kenya, using multi-stage sampling to assess the prevalence and sociodemographic determinants of metabolic syndrome (MetS) and related risk factors. The prevalence of MetS was strikingly high at 46.4%, with elevated blood pressure recorded in 22.6%, central obesity in 79.3% by waist circumference and 51.1% by waist-hip ratio, and diabetes in 8.56% of participants. Elevated triglycerides (17.2%) and low HDL-C (68.6%) were prominent lipid abnormalities. Sociodemographic analysis identified wealth quintile and increasing age as primary determinants; women in the highest wealth quintile had significantly increased odds of obesity (AOR 1.83, p=0.036), elevated triglycerides (AOR 2.22, p=0.035), and MetS (AOR 1.79, p=0.040). Notably, hormonal contraceptive use was independently associated with reduced odds of hypertension (AOR 0.32, p<0.001) but was a significant risk factor for low HDL-C (AOR 2.08, p=0.011). A major limitation is the cross-sectional design precluding causal inference, and a single-county sample limiting generalizability.

Implications Medical Affairs : The high burden of metabolic syndrome affecting nearly half of women in this reproductive-age Kenyan cohort signals an urgent unmet need for cardiovascular and metabolic risk management programs in sub-Saharan Africa, a region where many antihypertensive and cardiometabolic products remain underutilized. For Medical Affairs, this evidence supports advocacy for expanded hypertension screening and treatment access in low- and middle-income countries, and underscores the business case for market development activities targeting East African healthcare systems. The unexpected protective association of hormonal contraceptives with hypertension deserves follow-up through prospective research, offering a potential scientific platform for Medical Affairs to engage reproductive health and cardiology KOLs at the intersection of women's cardiovascular health.

Redefining atherosclerotic cardiovascular disease patients with no standard modifiable risk factors "SMuRF-less": six rather than four risk factors. Analysis from the Jordan SMuRF-less Study.

Revue : Frontiers in Cardiovascular Medicine | Tier : NA | IF estime : NA

Date de publication : 2026-08-06

Resultats principaux : This analysis from the Jordan SMuRF-less Study examined 5,540 ASCVD patients (mean age 57.5 years, 24.1% women) to compare the prevalence of SMuRF-less patients under the traditional 4-RF definition (hypertension, T2DM, dyslipidemia, smoking) versus an expanded 6-RF definition adding obesity and physical inactivity. Using the traditional 4-RF definition, 3.9% of the cohort were classified as SMuRF-less; expanding to include obesity and physical inactivity reduced this proportion by 74.4% to only 1.0% in the subgroup with complete data (n=1,121). Patients classified as SMuRF-less under the 6-RF definition were younger and showed more favorable lipid profiles including higher HDL-C and lower triglycerides compared to those with at least one of the 6 RFs. These findings highlight that the widely cited SMuRF-less category substantially overestimates the proportion of ASCVD patients with truly no modifiable risk factors when lifestyle-related factors are omitted. A limitation is that physical inactivity and BMI data were available in only a subgroup of 1,121 out of 5,540 patients, and the single-country sample may limit global generalizability.

Implications Medical Affairs : The dramatic reduction in SMuRF-less prevalence when incorporating lifestyle-derived risk factors reinforces the case for comprehensive risk factor assessment including obesity and sedentary behavior in ASCVD prevention strategies, with potential implications for guideline revisions on risk stratification. Medical Affairs should leverage these findings to advocate for broader lifestyle intervention programs and to position products addressing the obesity-hypertension-cardiovascular continuum in a comprehensive manner across patient risk profiles. This study offers a compelling KOL engagement platform in the Middle Eastern cardiovascular community and supports investigator-initiated research exploring the contribution of lifestyle risk factors to residual cardiovascular risk.

Importance of Preconception Care for Optimizing Fetal Growth in Pregnancies Complicated by Psychiatric Disorders.

Revue : The Journal of Obstetrics and Gynaecology Research | Tier : NA | IF estime : NA

Date de publication : 2026-08

Resultats principaux : This retrospective single-center cohort study analyzed 400 singleton pregnancies complicated by maternal psychiatric disorders from 2019 to 2023, examining factors associated with fetal growth abnormalities including large for gestational age (LGA) and small for gestational age (SGA) outcomes. Of 400 pregnancies, 40 infants were LGA and 40 were SGA; multivariable logistic regression found that psychotropic medication use including polypharmacy and metabolically high-risk agents was not independently associated with either growth aberration. High prepregnancy BMI was the only independent risk factor for LGA, while SGA was independently associated with hypertensive disorders of pregnancy, maternal alcohol consumption, and lower prepregnancy BMI. Hypertensive disorders of pregnancy thus emerged as a significant independent risk factor for SGA, reinforcing the importance of blood pressure control in the antenatal period for women with psychiatric comorbidities. Limitations include the small single-center sample, retrospective design, and limited statistical power to detect associations with rare outcomes.

Implications Medical Affairs : The identification of hypertensive disorders of pregnancy as an independent SGA risk factor in psychiatrically complex pregnancies highlights the need for robust blood pressure management protocols specifically tailored to this high-risk obstetric population. Medical Affairs teams may find value in supporting educational initiatives for obstetricians and psychiatrists on preconception cardiovascular risk optimization, including hypertension screening, as a gateway to improving perinatal outcomes in women with mental health comorbidities. This study opens an opportunity for KOL engagement at the intersection of obstetric hypertension and reproductive psychiatry, and for generating further data on antihypertensive treatment safety and efficacy in this underrepresented patient group.

Combined effects of diet and genetic predisposition on kidney function outcomes.

Revue : Clinical Kidney Journal | Tier : NA | IF estime : NA

Date de publication : 2026-06-19

Resultats principaux : This cross-sectional analysis from the Cooperative Health Research in South Tyrol (CHRIS) study included 5,717 generally healthy adults and examined interactions between a polygenic score (PGS, derived from 131 SNPs) for kidney function and dietary exposures in relation to eGFR estimated from serum creatinine. Dietary intake was assessed via the GALEN food frequency questionnaire with derivation of Mediterranean diet, AHEI, and DASH diet scores, plus measurement of protein, potassium, and phosphorus intake; multivariable generalized additive models incorporating diet-by-PGS interaction terms were used. The PGS was linearly associated with eGFRcrea (p<0.0001) independent of diet; while dietary quality indices showed no significant linear associations, all three nutrients (protein, potassium, phosphorus) showed negative linear associations with eGFRcrea. Nominally significant nonlinear interactions were found between the PGS and both protein (p=0.0298) and phosphorus intake (p=0.0283), suggesting dietary effects on kidney function may be stronger in genetically high-risk individuals. Key limitations include the cross-sectional design, a volunteer-based healthy population limiting generalizability, and the exploratory nature of the gene-diet interaction analyses requiring independent replication.

Implications Medical Affairs : These preliminary gene-diet interaction findings in kidney function have indirect relevance for hypertension Medical Affairs in the context of cardiorenal syndrome, as hypertensive patients with CKD risk may benefit from personalized dietary management strategies tailored to genetic predisposition. For Medical Affairs strategy, this work underscores the emerging importance of precision medicine approaches integrating genetic and dietary profiling in managing hypertension-associated renal decline, and could inform KOL engagement with nephrologists and cardiologists focused on cardiorenal protection. Further research confirming these interactions in larger, more diverse populations would be needed before clinical recommendations on DASH diet adherence stratified by genetic renal risk could be established.
