How Do Sleep Difficulties Interact With Anxiety, Depression and Health-Related Quality of Life in Pulmonary Hypertension?

Revue : The Clinical Respiratory Journal | Tier : NA | IF estime : NA

Date de publication : August 2026

Resultats principaux : This cross-sectional survey enrolled 111 adults with pulmonary hypertension (PH) globally, predominantly female (82%) and White (91%), evaluating multiple dimensions of sleep disturbance and their relationship with psychological distress and health-related quality of life (HRQoL) using validated instruments including the Pittsburgh Sleep Quality Index, Insomnia Severity Scale, Epworth Sleepiness Scale, PHQ-9, GAD-7, and emPHasis-10. A striking 79.8% of participants were classified as poor sleepers, with sleep disturbance and prolonged sleep latency being the most prevalent difficulties. Insomnia affected 35% and daytime sleepiness 33.6% of participants, with frequent co-occurrence, while 40% showed clinical-level depression and 29% clinical-level anxiety. Parallel mediation analyses demonstrated that all sleep difficulty types were negatively associated with HRQoL, with this relationship partially mediated by depressive symptoms. Limitations include the cross-sectional design preventing causal inference, reliance on self-report measures, and a sample skewed toward White females, limiting generalizability across PH subgroups.

Implications Medical Affairs : This study highlights a severely underaddressed comorbidity burden in pulmonary hypertension patients, signaling a clinical need for integrated screening protocols that pair systematic sleep assessment with mood evaluation. From a Medical Affairs standpoint, therapies that improve cardiopulmonary hemodynamics may be positioned as also conferring downstream benefits on patient-reported outcomes including sleep quality and HRQoL, an area requiring dedicated real-world evidence generation. The data offer a strategic pathway for KOL engagement around patient-centered care metrics in PH, with publications focused on sleep-mood-HRQoL interrelationships supporting cross-specialty educational initiatives.

Hypertension Control in Bangladesh: Changes, Sociodemographic Variation, and Socioeconomic Inequality from the 2017-18 to 2022 Bangladesh Demographic and Health Surveys

Revue : Global Heart | Tier : NA | IF estime : NA

Date de publication : July 27, 2026

Resultats principaux : This study analyzed nationally representative data from 13,131 (2017-18) and 14,296 (2022) participants from the Bangladesh Demographic and Health Surveys using a cross-sectional pooled design and survey-weighted multivariable logistic regression with marginal standardization. Hypertension prevalence showed a notable decrease from 27.5% to 20.5%, while awareness rose from 42.4% to 54.3%, treatment from 37.0% to 44.6%, and blood pressure control improved from 12.5% to 19.3% over the study period. Control gains were observed across nearly all sociodemographic subgroups, with the greatest improvements among older adults (+6.5%), urban residents (+6.8%), individuals with higher education (+7.3%), and residents of Sylhet and Chattogram divisions (+7.9% and +7.2% respectively). Socioeconomic inequality in hypertension control persisted and widened, with the concentration index rising from 0.11 to 0.13 and the slope index of inequality increasing from 8.5% to 15.1%. Limitations include differences in survey implementation between rounds and the cross-sectional design precluding causal attribution.

Implications Medical Affairs : These findings reveal meaningful but inequitable progress in Bangladesh's hypertension care cascade, underscoring the critical gap in antihypertensive medication access among lower socioeconomic strata that represents both a public health and market opportunity. Medical Affairs teams can leverage this evidence to support access arguments for affordable antihypertensive fixed-dose combinations in low- and middle-income countries, particularly framing equitable access as a driver of guideline-concordant outcomes. KOL collaboration focused on improving hypertension care continuum metrics in South Asian populations is a high-priority area for companies with antihypertensive portfolios targeting emerging markets.

Endometrial Thickness is Not a Risk Factor for Hypertensive Disorders of Pregnancy After IVF Treatment in Women With Male-Factor Infertility

Revue : Frontiers in Endocrinology | Tier : NA | IF estime : NA

Date de publication : July 17, 2026

Resultats principaux : This single-center retrospective cohort study enrolled 2,536 women aged 20-40 with male-factor infertility undergoing their first frozen-thawed embryo transfer (FET) at a Chinese center to evaluate whether endometrial thickness (EMT) is independently associated with hypertensive disorders of pregnancy (HDP). EMT was measured via transvaginal ultrasound at embryo transfer and categorized into three groups (≤8 mm, 8-14 mm, ≥14 mm). The overall HDP incidence was 3.67% and did not differ significantly across EMT groups (3.97%, 3.74%, and 3.03%; p=0.760). Multivariable logistic regression identified advanced maternal age (OR 1.10, 95% CI 1.05-1.16, p<0.001) and BMI ≥24.0 kg/m² (OR 2.40, 95% CI 1.10-5.25, p=0.028) as the only significant independent HDP risk factors. Limitations include the single-center retrospective design, restriction to male-factor infertility cases, and exclusion of women with prior HDP or multiple gestations, limiting generalizability.

Implications Medical Affairs : By excluding EMT as an independent HDP risk factor in ART-conceived pregnancies, these findings help clinicians focus counseling on modifiable risk factors such as BMI and maternal age in the IVF population. From a Medical Affairs perspective, this supports messaging around metabolic optimization and weight management as pre-conception hypertension prevention priorities, complementary to antihypertensive strategies in high-risk pregnancies. These data may interest KOLs in reproductive medicine and obstetrics seeking evidence-based risk stratification tools for hypertension prevention in assisted reproduction populations.

Using Linked Administrative Health Data to Examine Maternal Preconception Diabetes and Hypertension and Child Growth in Ontario, Canada: A Longitudinal Cohort Study

Revue : International Journal of Population Data Science | Tier : NA | IF estime : NA

Date de publication : July 6, 2026

Resultats principaux : This longitudinal cohort study linked administrative health data from ICES Ontario with the TARGet Kids! practice-based cohort to examine whether maternal preconception diabetes and hypertension influence child body mass index (zBMI) in 9,087 children (0-12 years) contributing 28,861 zBMI measurements across 7,351 mothers. Among mothers, 4.6% had preconception diabetes or hypertension, with preconception hypertension present in 2.9%. Linear mixed effects models adjusted for relevant confounders revealed that maternal preconception hypertension was significantly associated with a 0.29 unit increase in child zBMI (95% CI: 0.03-0.55), a clinically meaningful effect size in childhood growth trajectories. No statistically significant effect of preconception diabetes on child zBMI was detected after full adjustment. Limitations include the small proportion of hypertensive mothers (2.9%), reliance on administrative diagnostic codes potentially underestimating true prevalence, and longitudinal attrition inherent in long-duration cohort designs.

Implications Medical Affairs : These findings introduce maternal preconception hypertension as a potentially modifiable upstream determinant of child adiposity, expanding the evidence base for blood pressure management in reproductive-age women beyond maternal-fetal outcomes to intergenerational health consequences. Medical Affairs teams can leverage this data to reinforce messaging around early and sustained BP control in women of reproductive age as a dual-benefit intervention protecting both maternal and child long-term health. This opens novel cross-specialty KOL engagement opportunities in maternal-fetal medicine, pediatrics, and preventive cardiology, supporting integrated educational initiatives on preconception hypertension management as a public health priority.

Ability of the DHAM Score to Predict 5-Year All-Cause Mortality in Patients With Diabetes and Comorbid Hypertension: Validation and Risk Stratification in Two Prospective Cohorts

Revue : Journal of Diabetes Research | Tier : NA | IF estime : NA

Date de publication : 2026

Resultats principaux : This prospective validation study tested the eight-predictor DHAM score (comprising age, sex, education, marital status, smoking, cardiovascular disease, serum albumin, and urea nitrogen) in two independent cohorts: 3,291 patients from NHANES (1999-2014) and 2,478 from the Chinese Kailuan cohort (2008-2015) with co-occurring diabetes and hypertension. The C-index was 0.758 (95% CI: 0.743-0.833) in NHANES and 0.741 (95% CI: 0.731-0.790) in Kailuan, demonstrating good discriminative capacity across both cohorts. A DHAM score exceeding 148 independently predicted 5-year all-cause mortality in NHANES (adjusted-HR 10.03, 95% CI: 3.37-18.83, p<0.001), with recalibration yielding a threshold of 152 for Kailuan (HR 9.24, 95% CI: 4.71-16.84, p<0.001). Integration of DHAM score with renal function status (eGFR <60 mL/min/1.73 m²) produced a three-tier risk stratification model with log-rank p<0.001 across all strata. Limitations include methodological differences between NHANES and Kailuan data collection, residual confounding potential, and the older age profile of both cohorts limiting applicability to younger patients with dual diagnoses.

Implications Medical Affairs : The validated DHAM score offers a clinically accessible and externally validated tool for risk stratification in one of the most prevalent and high-risk cardiometabolic comorbidity clusters, potentially guiding more intensive therapeutic decision-making in dual-diagnosis patients. Medical Affairs can utilize these findings to support companion strategies whereby antihypertensive and metabolic therapies are positioned as components of an integrated risk reduction approach for patients with diabetes and hypertension. This prognostic model provides a compelling framework for KOL-led educational programs on personalized cardiometabolic risk assessment, particularly in endocrinology and internal medicine settings.

Polygenic Risk Scores Predict Blood Pressure Changes Independent of Dietary Intervention: A Secondary Analysis of the NUPRESS Trial

Revue : European Journal of Nutrition | Tier : NA | IF estime : NA

Date de publication : August 1, 2026

Resultats principaux : This secondary analysis of the Brazilian multicentre NUPRESS randomized controlled trial compared a DASH diet (control group) with a multicomponent dietary intervention over 180 days in adults with systemic arterial hypertension (SAH), using genomic microarray genotyping to construct polygenic risk scores (PRSs). A total of 114 SNPs were associated with systolic BP changes (ΔSBP) and 101 with diastolic BP changes (ΔDBP), yielding PRSs that showed strong correlations with observed BP reductions (r=0.85 for ΔSBP and r=0.84 for ΔDBP; p<0.0001 for both). No statistically significant effect of dietary intervention type on BP change was detected between arms, and no significant interaction between PRSs and dietary approach was found for either ΔSBP (p=0.98) or ΔDBP (p=0.13). These findings demonstrate that genetic architecture may be a stronger independent predictor of individual BP response to dietary modification than the specific dietary composition employed. Key limitations include the secondary analysis design with potential loss of statistical power, modest genotyped sample size, and the Brazilian-specific genomic context limiting global generalizability.

Implications Medical Affairs : This trial provides early pharmacogenomic evidence suggesting that genetic profiling may identify patients less likely to respond to dietary lifestyle interventions alone, strengthening the clinical rationale for early pharmacological intervention in genetically high-risk hypertensive individuals. From a Medical Affairs perspective, these findings support the concept that precision medicine approaches — potentially extending to companion diagnostics or genetic risk stratification tools — could inform first-line management decisions for hypertension beyond traditional guidelines. This study provides a compelling basis for engaging KOLs in precision medicine and nutrigenomics, potentially supporting future research collaborations around pharmacogenomic biomarkers as predictive tools in antihypertensive therapy trials.

Swimming-Induced Pulmonary Oedema in Triathletes: A Narrative Review of Epidemiology, Risk Factors and Prevention

Revue : Sports Medicine and Health Science | Tier : NA | IF estime : NA

Date de publication : August 21, 2025 (indexed August 2026)

Resultats principaux : This narrative review synthesized 48 publications from PubMed and Scopus databases to characterize swimming-induced pulmonary oedema (SIPO) specifically in triathletes, evaluating epidemiology, risk factors, and prevention strategies in competitive endurance sport. Most reported cases involved middle-aged women (30-60 years) participating in IRONMAN 70.3 and IRONMAN events, with SIPO prevalence in triathletes reported at under 1.5%. Key identified risk factors include hypertension, female sex, age over 50 years, fish oil consumption, wetsuit compression, highly trained athletic status, competitive context, longer race distances, and cold water environments. Symptomatology and clinical outcomes in triathletes were comparable to those in other aquatic athletes, confirming a consistent SIPO phenotype across disciplines. The review is limited by reliance predominantly on case reports and small case series introducing publication bias, absence of randomized evidence, and the lack of standardized diagnostic criteria across included studies complicating cross-study comparison.

Implications Medical Affairs : By identifying hypertension as a key risk factor for SIPO in endurance athletes, this review underscores the clinical relevance of optimal blood pressure management even in highly active populations traditionally considered at lower cardiovascular risk. Medical Affairs teams can leverage this data to highlight the importance of pre-participation hypertension screening in competitive athletics, supporting educational campaigns targeting sports medicine practitioners around cardiovascular safety in endurance sports. KOL engagement in sports cardiology and exercise medicine is warranted, with these findings supporting antihypertensive product positioning in active middle-aged patient segments where BP management may have acute safety implications beyond chronic cardiovascular outcomes.

Sex Differences in Blood Pressure and Hypertension Through Adulthood

Revue : Clinical Kidney Journal | Tier : NA | IF estime : NA

Date de publication : June 4, 2026

Resultats principaux : This cross-sectional analysis from the Hamburg City Health Study included 10,000 participants (51% women; mean age 62±8.5 years) with hypertension defined per 2023 European Society of Hypertension guidelines, stratified into age groups of 45-54, 55-64, and 65-74 years and assessed using modified Poisson regression adjusted for sociodemographic, comorbidity, and lifestyle factors. Overall hypertension prevalence was significantly higher in men (71%) versus women (57%), with the disparity most pronounced at 45-54 years (50% vs. 32%; adjusted PR 1.44, 95% CI 1.27-1.62). Sex differences progressively attenuated at 55-64 years (PR 1.23, 95% CI 1.15-1.32) and converged at 65-74 years (PR 1.04, 95% CI 0.99-1.08), concordant with menopausal age, and consistent findings were confirmed in menopausal status-based subgroup analyses. Hypertension awareness and treatment rates were similar between sexes across all age strata. BP control remained suboptimal for both sexes (~40%) and declined specifically in women aged 65-74, coinciding with the post-menopausal transition. Limitations include the cross-sectional design preventing temporal inference, potential health study participation bias, and restriction of age range (45-74 years).

Implications Medical Affairs : These robust European epidemiological data confirm sex-differentiated hypertension trajectories across adulthood, establishing the post-menopausal period as a critical clinical window for intensified BP screening and treatment intensification in women. Medical Affairs can leverage these findings to support educational initiatives and product messaging that accounts for sex-specific cardiovascular risk trajectories, particularly for antihypertensives with data in post-menopausal women or those with specific tolerability profiles relevant to this population. Engaging KOLs in gender-specific cardiovascular medicine and menopause research with these findings could strengthen positioning in the growing women's heart health market and inform gender-stratified trial design for future studies.

Comparing the Effectiveness of Home-Based and Center-Based Cardiac Rehabilitation: A Randomized Clinical Trial

Revue : Future Cardiology | Tier : NA | IF estime : NA

Date de publication : August 1, 2026

Resultats principaux : This Iranian randomized controlled trial enrolled 236 patients with cardiovascular disease, randomizing them to 8-week home-based (n=107) or center-based (n=129) cardiac rehabilitation (CR), with primary and secondary outcomes assessed at baseline and 6 months. ANCOVA adjusted for baseline values revealed that center-based CR was significantly superior in improving exercise capacity (13.65±0.19 vs 11.49±0.22; p<0.001), systolic BP (116.58±1.17 vs 122.08±1.33 mmHg; p=0.005), and diastolic BP (74.35±0.74 vs 79.83±0.84 mmHg; p<0.001) compared to home-based CR. No statistically significant between-group differences were observed for lipid profile, fasting blood glucose, BMI, or waist circumference at 6 months. Both interventions improved exercise capacity from baseline, establishing home-based CR as a viable though inferior alternative when center access is limited. Limitations include the single-center design in an Iranian population with limited ethnic generalizability, the 8-week intervention potentially being insufficient to detect metabolic changes, and potential differential adherence between settings.

Implications Medical Affairs : This RCT provides head-to-head quantitative evidence that supervised center-based cardiac rehabilitation delivers superior blood pressure outcomes over home-based alternatives, data directly relevant for healthcare providers and payers evaluating CR program structures and reimbursement policies. From a Medical Affairs perspective, these findings reinforce the positioning of structured exercise rehabilitation as an evidence-based adjunct to pharmacological antihypertensive therapy in post-cardiac event management, supporting integrated care model advocacy. KOL partnerships with cardiac rehabilitation specialists could leverage these trial data to develop hybrid CR protocols optimizing patient access while maintaining blood pressure management benefits.

Hypertension and Chronic Kidney Disease: A Meta-Analysis

Revue : Cureus | Tier : NA | IF estime : NA

Date de publication : July 30, 2026

Resultats principaux : This systematic review and meta-analysis synthesized data from 13 case-control studies examining 17 risk factors for chronic kidney disease (CKD) in hypertensive patients, using a random-effects model with Cochran's Q test for heterogeneity. Renal impairment demonstrated the strongest association with CKD development in the context of hypertension (OR 3.846, 95% CI: 2.817-5.251, p<0.001), establishing it as the primary independent risk factor. Additional independently confirmed risk factors were male sex (OR 1.701, 95% CI: 1.082-2.674, p<0.001) and smoking (OR 1.732, 95% CI: 1.208-2.482, p<0.001), both reaching conventional significance thresholds. Geographical variation in risk factor magnitudes was observed across studies, highlighting the role of environmental and lifestyle contextual factors in modulating the hypertension-CKD association. Limitations include restriction to case-control designs limiting causal inference, moderate methodological quality of included studies, potential publication bias, and heterogeneous definitions of hypertension and CKD across studies.

Implications Medical Affairs : This meta-analysis confirms the robust epidemiological and clinical link between hypertension and CKD progression, reinforcing the clinical urgency of sustained optimal blood pressure control as a renoprotective strategy in at-risk patients. Medical Affairs teams can leverage this evidence to strengthen the positioning of antihypertensives with established nephroprotective profiles, particularly in patients with early-stage kidney disease, male sex, or smoking history — a well-defined high-risk subgroup. Strategic KOL engagement in nephrology and internal medicine is warranted to translate these meta-analytic data into clinical practice guidelines supporting integrated cardiorenal risk management pathways.

Circulating Proteins Altered in Response to the Dietary Approaches to Stop Hypertension (DASH) Diet Suggest Underlying Molecular Mechanisms and Long-Term Health Benefits

Revue : medRxiv (preprint) | Tier : NA | IF estime : NA

Date de publication : July 24, 2026

Resultats principaux : This Mendelian randomization study analyzed 71 serum proteins previously identified as perturbed by the DASH diet in randomized controlled feeding studies to elucidate the molecular mechanisms underlying DASH diet-mediated cardiovascular protection against hypertension, coronary artery disease (CAD), and type 2 diabetes (T2D). Analyses identified four proteins — ANGPTL3, INHBC, PCOLCE, and PLXNB2 — with causal evidence supporting beneficial effects on cardiometabolic risk factors directionally concordant with DASH diet-induced protein level changes. Missense variants in INHBC were causally associated with reduced CAD risk, and PLXNB2 variants with reduced T2D risk, with directionality concordant with DASH diet-induced protein changes, providing evidence these proteins mediate long-term cardioprotective effects. The molecular phenotyping approach integrated with human genetics provides mechanistic insights into DASH diet effects without requiring prohibitively long randomized outcome trials. As a preprint awaiting peer review, limitations include the inherent assumptions of Mendelian randomization, potential horizontal pleiotropy in genetic instruments, and the absence of replication in independent dietary intervention cohorts.

Implications Medical Affairs : The identification of INHBC and PLXNB2 as potential molecular mediators of DASH diet cardioprotection opens new avenues for drug target validation in cardiometabolic disease prevention, with implications for future pipeline development. For Medical Affairs, these novel protein targets represent emerging biomarker opportunities that could position dietary counseling within a precision medicine framework, potentially complementing antihypertensive pharmacotherapy strategies. Engagement with KOLs in nutritional genomics and proteomics could position a pharmaceutical company at the forefront of translational cardiometabolic research, supporting future research collaborations and reinforcing scientific leadership in hypertension prevention.

Cardiovascular Risk Factors and Health Behaviors of Women Veterans

Revue : Women's Health Reports | Tier : NA | IF estime : NA

Date de publication : June 8, 2026

Resultats principaux : This cross-sectional survey enrolled 380 women veterans (WVs) (20% response rate; mean age 53 years; 58% non-Hispanic White, 20% non-Hispanic Black, 14% Hispanic) receiving care at a VA Medical Center in California, characterizing CVD risk factors, mental health conditions, and health behaviors using validated self-report instruments. The predominant CVD risk factors were hyperlipidemia (38%), obesity (36%), hypertension (33%), smoking (13%), and diabetes (10%), with nearly 70% of all respondents reporting at least one CVD risk factor. Health behaviors were suboptimal, with low vigorous physical activity (1.9 days/week), poor diet quality (score 6.1/10), and hazardous alcohol use in 45.5% of respondents. Mean PHQ-8 and PC-PTSD-5 scores indicated moderate depression (8.7) and mild PTSD (2.9), with risk factors and mental health burden disproportionately concentrated among younger WVs aged 18-44. CVD prevalence peaked among WVs aged 65+, suggesting a compressed cardiovascular risk trajectory compared to the general female population. Limitations include the single-VA-center design, low 20% response rate introducing selection bias, and cross-sectional design precluding causal interpretation.

Implications Medical Affairs : The high prevalence of hypertension (33%) in a relatively young and active female veteran population, combined with elevated mental health burden, underscores the urgent need for gender-sensitive and trauma-informed cardiovascular care models in military health systems. Medical Affairs teams can leverage these findings to highlight the intersection of mental health comorbidities and hypertension as a co-management priority, supporting dual-indication educational programs and reinforcing the relevance of antihypertensives with favorable tolerability profiles in populations with concomitant psychiatric conditions. KOL engagement in women's cardiovascular health, military medicine, and VA system leadership could facilitate evidence-based program development addressing both psychological and cardiovascular risk factors in this underserved patient population.
