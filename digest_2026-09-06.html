Association between non-high-density lipoprotein cholesterol to high-density lipoprotein cholesterol ratio and hypertension in Chinese elderly: A cross-sectional study based on CHARLS.

Revue : Medicine (Baltimore) | Tier : NA | IF estime : NA

Date de publication : 2026-09-04

Resultats principaux : This cross-sectional study used data from the 2015 CHARLS database, enrolling 8342 middle-aged and older Chinese adults (median age 67 years; 49.6% male) to evaluate the association between the non-high-density lipoprotein cholesterol to high-density lipoprotein cholesterol ratio (NHHR) and prevalent hypertension. Multivariable logistic regression showed that each unit increment in NHHR was associated with a significantly higher odds of hypertension across all adjusted models, while the categorical analysis showed a trend with P=0.097 in the fully adjusted model. A penalized spline analysis demonstrated a statistically significant nonlinear relationship between NHHR and hypertension (P < 0.001), suggesting that the risk of hypertension accelerates beyond a certain NHHR threshold. Subgroup and sensitivity analyses confirmed the robustness of the primary finding across demographic and clinical strata. Major limitations include the cross-sectional design which precludes causal inference, reliance on self-reported data from participants, and the absence of longitudinal follow-up to establish temporal directionality.

Implications Medical Affairs : These findings reinforce the growing body of evidence linking dyslipidemia and hypertension, suggesting that simple lipid ratios like NHHR could serve as accessible biomarkers for hypertension screening in elderly populations, potentially informing guideline updates on cardiovascular risk stratification. From a product positioning perspective, companies developing antihypertensive therapies may leverage the NHHR nonlinear relationship with hypertension to identify target populations where concomitant lipid-modifying strategies could provide added cardiovascular benefit. For KOL engagement, this study opens dialogue around comprehensive cardiometabolic risk management and the potential for combination pharmacotherapy protocols in elderly Asian populations, a growing and underrepresented segment in clinical trials.

Combined Pulmonary Fibrosis and Emphysema: Clinicoradiological Parameters Associated with Fibrosis Patterns.

Revue : Journal of the College of Physicians and Surgeons--Pakistan | Tier : NA | IF estime : NA

Date de publication : 2026-09

Resultats principaux : This cross-sectional observational study from Tokat Gaziosmanpasa University, Turkey, included 94 patients with combined pulmonary fibrosis and emphysema (CPFE), divided into UIP (n=48) and NSIP (n=46) pattern groups based on high-resolution CT imaging collected between January 2023 and January 2025. No significant differences were found between the two fibrosis-pattern groups regarding age, gender, smoking history, Charlson Comorbidity Index, or high-risk occupational exposure. Among CPFE patients evaluated for pulmonary hypertension using echocardiography (sPAP threshold of 25 mmHg), those with elevated sPAP were significantly older, had higher CCI scores, lower hemoglobin and hematocrit levels, and significantly reduced DLCO values compared to those without elevated pulmonary artery pressure. Notably, more than half of CPFE patients had elevated systolic pulmonary artery pressure, indicating that pulmonary hypertension is a highly prevalent comorbidity in this disease setting. Limitations include the single-center retrospective design, small sample size, use of echocardiography rather than right heart catheterization as the diagnostic gold standard, and the cross-sectional methodology that precludes causal interpretations.

Implications Medical Affairs : While this study focuses on pulmonary hypertension complicating combined pulmonary fibrosis and emphysema rather than systemic arterial hypertension, the high prevalence of elevated sPAP exceeding 50% in this patient group underscores the need for systematic screening protocols for pulmonary vascular involvement in fibrotic lung disease, which may influence multidisciplinary management guidelines. From a Medical Affairs standpoint, companies with pulmonary hypertension or interstitial lung disease portfolios should monitor this emerging evidence, as the clear overlap between fibrotic patterns and hemodynamic compromise may create new positioning opportunities for combination therapies. This study provides a basis for KOL engagement around the under-recognized burden of pulmonary hypertension in CPFE patients, supporting initiatives to enhance diagnostic workflows and explore targeted therapies in this complex patient population.

Theme 4 : Calcium Channel Blockers (1 article)

Loss of blood pressure control after rifampin initiation: a clinically significant antihypertensive drug-drug interaction.

Revue : BMJ Case Reports | Tier : NA | IF estime : NA

Date de publication : 2026-09-04

Resultats principaux : This case report describes a man in his early 60s with previously well-controlled hypertension on an angiotensin receptor blocker and a calcium channel blocker who experienced sudden loss of blood pressure control two weeks after initiating isoniazid-rifampin therapy for latent tuberculosis infection. The temporal association strongly implicated rifampicin-induced cytochrome P450 3A4 enzyme induction as the mechanism, which is known to reduce plasma concentrations of calcium channel blockers such as amlodipine by up to approximately 80%, significantly diminishing antihypertensive efficacy. The patient presented with headache and newly uncontrolled hypertension, and blood pressure improved after escalation of antihypertensive therapy while antituberculosis treatment was continued without interruption. This case illustrates a clinically significant and potentially under-recognized drug-drug interaction at the intersection of infectious disease and cardiovascular medicine, particularly in populations where latent tuberculosis treatment is common. Major limitations include the inherent constraints of case report methodology, absence of pharmacokinetic measurements to directly confirm reduced amlodipine plasma levels, and limited generalizability from a single patient presentation.

Implications Medical Affairs : This case report highlights a critical drug-drug interaction relevant to the pharmaceutical industry, particularly for companies with calcium channel blocker portfolios, as it underscores the need for structured medication review and potential labeling updates or prescriber education initiatives regarding rifampin co-administration. From a product positioning perspective, this interaction creates differentiation opportunities for antihypertensive agents with lower susceptibility to CYP3A4-mediated interactions, and motivates real-world data collection efforts to quantify the prevalence and clinical impact of this interaction across diverse patient populations. For KOL strategy, this case presents a compelling opportunity to engage cardiologists, infectious disease specialists, and primary care physicians in multidisciplinary discussions on pharmacokinetic drug interactions in complex patients requiring concurrent antihypertensive and antimicrobial therapy, supporting cross-specialty educational initiatives.

Theme 6 : Clinical Trials and Guidelines (1 article)

Hypertension Prevalence and Control at Trial Entry in the REPRIEVE Study Population: Results From a Secondary Analysis.

Revue : Open Forum Infectious Diseases | Tier : NA | IF estime : NA

Date de publication : 2026-08-20

Resultats principaux : This pre-specified secondary analysis of the REPRIEVE randomized controlled trial evaluated hypertension prevalence and control at baseline among 7769 people living with HIV enrolled across 5 global regions, representing a low to moderate traditional cardiovascular disease risk population with a median age of 50 years and 31% women. The overall prevalence of hypertension at baseline was 36%, with 31% of hypertensive cases being previously undiagnosed, revealing a substantial gap in screening and clinical awareness in this population. Among participants with a preexisting hypertension diagnosis, 40% had uncontrolled blood pressure at enrollment, with significant geographic variation observed across the 5 regions. Obesity carried the highest adjusted prevalence ratio for hypertension (1.80; 95% CI, 1.67-1.95), followed by overweight BMI (1.38; 95% CI, 1.28-1.49), underscoring the central role of metabolic comorbidities in driving hypertension burden among people living with HIV. Hypertension at baseline was independently associated with a twofold increased hazard of major adverse cardiovascular events (HR 2.00; 95% CI, 1.57-2.55), including myocardial infarction and stroke, and this secondary analysis is limited by selection of a low-to-moderate CVD risk cohort that may underestimate the true burden in higher-risk HIV populations.

Implications Medical Affairs : These findings from the REPRIEVE trial population have important guideline implications, establishing that hypertension screening and optimized management should be integrated as a priority component of comprehensive HIV care protocols, and that cardiovascular risk stratification tools require adaptation for the HIV-positive population given their substantially elevated MACE risk profile. From a product positioning perspective, pharmaceutical companies with antihypertensive portfolios should note the high rates of undiagnosed and uncontrolled hypertension in people living with HIV as a significant unmet medical need and market opportunity, particularly for agents with favorable metabolic profiles and minimal drug-drug interaction potential with antiretroviral therapies. For KOL strategy, this study provides strong evidence to support targeted engagement with HIV-specialist cardiologists and infectious disease physicians, promoting integrated cardiovascular monitoring programs and evidence-based treatment algorithms designed specifically for hypertension management in people living with HIV.
