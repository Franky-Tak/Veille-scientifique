Drug-Specific Associations of Angiotensin Receptor Blockers With Parkinson's Disease Risk in a Multicenter Electronic Health Record Cohort.

Revue : Fundamental & Clinical Pharmacology | Tier : NA | IF estime : NA

Date de publication : November 2026

Resultats principaux : This multicenter cohort study used large-scale electronic health records from the University of California health system, encompassing 890,712 hypertensive patients across six academic medical centers from 2012 to 2025, making it one of the largest real-world analyses of drug-specific ARB effects to date. Using a new-user, active-comparator design, researchers applied Cox proportional hazards models to estimate hazard ratios for individual ARB associations with incident Parkinson's disease (PD), adjusting for demographics, nine preindex comorbidities, and index date. Drug-specific analysis identified irbesartan (HR = 0.588, 95% CI 0.434-0.796, p = 0.0006) and olmesartan (HR = 0.797, 95% CI 0.665-0.956, p = 0.015) as significantly associated with lower PD risk compared to other ARBs within the class. Irbesartan survived Bonferroni correction for multiple comparisons, and propensity score matching supported both associations, strengthening the robustness of these findings. Notably, not all ARBs showed equivalent neuroprotective effects, suggesting that drug-specific pharmacological properties — particularly differential central nervous system penetration — may mediate neuroprotection within this drug class. Key limitations include the retrospective observational design relying on EHR data, potential residual confounding, and the academic medical center setting which may limit generalizability to broader populations.

Implications Medical Affairs : These findings have significant implications for ARB positioning beyond hypertension management, suggesting that irbesartan and olmesartan may offer added clinical value through neuroprotection — an attribute that could inform scientific exchange discussions with regulators and payers around differentiated benefit-risk profiles. From a Medical Affairs perspective, these data support proactive KOL engagement around the renin-angiotensin system's role in neurodegenerative disease prevention, leveraging the large EHR dataset to build scientific credibility. The study highlights a compelling opportunity to design prospective randomized trials evaluating irbesartan as a neuroprotective agent in hypertensive patients at risk for Parkinson's disease, which could meaningfully differentiate these ARBs from class competitors in a market largely focused on cardiovascular endpoints.

HeatRisk and air quality risk recommendations: A narrative review and action algorithm for the care of people at risk for and living with cardiovascular disease.

Revue : American Journal of Preventive Cardiology | Tier : NA | IF estime : NA

Date de publication : June 15, 2026

Resultats principaux : This narrative review synthesized literature published between 2000 and 2025 on the cardiovascular effects of non-optimal temperature and poor air quality, with a specific focus on patients with preexisting cardiovascular disease including those with hypertension, heart failure, ischemic heart disease, stroke, and myocardial infarction. The review found that non-optimal temperature and air pollution exposure is consistently associated with increased mortality and morbidity across all these cardiovascular conditions, and also worsens cardiometabolic risk factors including hypertension, insulin resistance, and atherosclerosis. High-risk subgroups were identified as women, older adults, residents of temperate climates, individuals taking certain cardiovascular medications, pregnant individuals, and those with socioeconomic or occupational exposure risks, representing a heterogeneous high-priority population. The review proposed a structured risk-stratification algorithm recommending that high-risk individuals (composite risk score greater than or equal to 3) take precautions at HeatRisk Level 1 (Yellow) and/or Air Quality Index (AQI) greater than or equal to 50, while moderate-risk individuals should consider precautions at HeatRisk Level 2 (Orange) and/or AQI greater than or equal to 100. No primary endpoint analysis was performed given the narrative review design; the central contribution is the practical clinical algorithm synthesized from existing CDC and EPA guidance. Limitations include the narrative design without formal systematic meta-analysis, reliance on publicly available guideline tools, and the lack of prospective validation of the proposed risk-stratification algorithm in clinical practice.

Implications Medical Affairs : This review reinforces the growing recognition that climate-related exposures represent modifiable cardiovascular risk factors, supporting Medical Affairs messaging around comprehensive hypertension patient risk management that extends beyond standard pharmacological treatment. The proposed risk-stratification algorithm provides a practical framework for KOL-led patient education initiatives and could be incorporated into guideline updates or disease management programs targeting hypertensive patients in high environmental risk regions. From a product positioning perspective, pharmaceutical companies marketing antihypertensives should consider integrating environmental risk counseling tools into disease management partnerships to demonstrate holistic cardiovascular risk reduction value beyond blood pressure lowering.

Trends in cardiovascular disease and risk factors between 2004 and 2021: Findings from the U.S. Millennium Cohort Study.

Revue : Preventive Medicine | Tier : NA | IF estime : NA

Date de publication : October 18, 2026

Resultats principaux : This longitudinal cohort study from the U.S. Millennium Cohort Study prospectively followed 43,769 post-9/11 military service members and veterans (average baseline age 37.2 years) from 2004 to 2021, examining cross-sectional trends in cardiovascular disease and risk factors at multiple survey timepoints post-military separation. The analysis found that current smoking declined substantially and encouragingly from 23.7 to 8.2 per 100 participants over the study period, representing the only major favorable trend. In stark contrast, all other major CVD risk factors increased significantly: obesity prevalence nearly doubled from 22.2 to 47.7 per 100, hypertension prevalence increased more than 2.5-fold from 9.0 to 23.5 per 100, and diabetes prevalence increased more than fourfold from 1.6 to 7.0 per 100. CVD outcomes, while still relatively uncommon in this relatively younger cohort, also increased from fewer than 0.8 to fewer than 3.0 per 100 participants across the study period. No formal subgroup analyses by deployment history, combat exposure, or specific treatment were reported, limiting insight into drivers of these trends. Limitations include the self-reported nature of CVD and risk factor data susceptible to ascertainment bias, the focus on a specific military veteran population which limits generalizability to the broader civilian population, and the use of cross-sectional survey snapshots rather than true longitudinal within-individual tracking.

Implications Medical Affairs : The near tripling of hypertension prevalence over 17 years in this large veteran cohort underscores an expanding treatment burden and reinforces the significant commercial and public health opportunity for antihypertensive therapies in a growing, younger, and often multi-morbid patient population. Medical Affairs teams should recognize veterans as a distinct high-priority population for hypertension management advocacy, given the simultaneous surge in obesity and diabetes that creates complex polypharmacy and treatment adherence challenges requiring innovative management solutions. This data supports targeted KOL engagement with VA health system clinicians and researchers to develop integrated cardiometabolic management protocols that combine antihypertensive pharmacotherapy with lifestyle intervention programs, potentially differentiating products that demonstrate benefits across the cardiometabolic spectrum.

Not one enclave: Disaggregation and cardiometabolic health in Asian ethnic enclaves.

Revue : Journal of Migration and Health | Tier : NA | IF estime : NA

Date de publication : September 25, 2026

Resultats principaux : This cross-sectional study used six years of survey data from New York City (N = 6,686 Asian Americans) linked with multiple community data sources including neighborhood socioeconomic indicators, built environment measures, and social cohesion data to examine associations between ethnic enclave residence and cardiometabolic risks. Using a combined concentration-based and spatial clustering analysis, the study identified five distinct East Asian enclaves and six South Asian enclaves with no geographic overlap between them, enabling truly ethnicity-specific analysis. Multilevel logistic regression analyses revealed that East Asian enclave residence was associated with lower odds of obesity (OR=0.60; 95% CI 0.38-0.95), while South Asian enclave residence was associated with significantly higher odds of diabetes (OR=1.36; 95% CI 1.02-1.82) and hypertension (OR=1.44; 95% CI 1.07-1.95), with all associations present exclusively among foreign-born individuals. After adjustment for neighborhood characteristics including socioeconomic conditions, built environment, social cohesion, and institutional support, the East Asian enclave protective effect on obesity persisted while South Asian enclave associations with diabetes and hypertension were only partly attenuated, suggesting unmeasured cultural and food environment factors play an important role. No secondary endpoints or intervention data were examined, as this was a purely observational epidemiological study. Limitations include the cross-sectional observational design which precludes causal inference, reliance on self-reported health outcomes, geographic restriction to New York City limiting generalizability, and inability to account for unmeasured cultural, dietary, and acculturation factors within enclaves.

Implications Medical Affairs : These findings challenge the common clinical practice of treating Asian Americans as a monolithic group in cardiovascular risk stratification, revealing that South Asian enclave residents — particularly foreign-born individuals — face a 44% higher odds of hypertension, representing a significantly underserved and high-risk population warranting targeted medical attention. Medical Affairs teams should advocate for disaggregated clinical data collection in hypertension research and support development of culturally tailored patient education materials specific to South Asian communities, where risk profiles differ substantially from East Asian populations. This study identifies a clear opportunity for targeted community-based hypertension screening and treatment programs in urban South Asian enclaves, potentially in partnership with community organizations, representing both a public health priority and a niche market opportunity for culturally aware patient support programs.
