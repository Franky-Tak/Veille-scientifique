Comorbidities increase the severity of COVID-19: an analysis and epidemiological study conducted in Algeria.

Revue : The Libyan Journal of Medicine | Tier : NA | IF estime : NA

Date de publication : 2026-09-28

Resultats principaux : This retrospective epidemiological study analyzed 12,932 COVID-19 patients in Setif, Algeria, to evaluate the impact of comorbidities including hypertension, cardiovascular disease, and diabetes on disease severity and mortality. Patients with heart disease, hypertension, and diabetes demonstrated significantly higher rates of severe COVID-19, with computed odds ratios of 1.50, 1.34, and 2.89, respectively. Among comorbidities, diabetes emerged as the strongest predictor of disease severity in this cohort, while hypertension conferred a moderate but statistically significant increase in risk. The study also examined geographical disparities in comorbidity prevalence across the Setif region, providing locally relevant insights for patient care planning. A key limitation is the observational, retrospective design, which limits causal inference and is subject to selection and information bias.

Implications Medical Affairs : This study reinforces the clinical importance of pre-existing hypertension as a major risk factor for severe COVID-19 outcomes, directly relevant to cardiovascular risk stratification tools used in clinical practice. From a Medical Affairs perspective, these findings underscore the need for proactive identification and management of hypertensive patients during infectious disease outbreaks, presenting cross-therapeutic area collaboration opportunities. KOL engagement strategies should leverage these data to promote integrated cardiometabolic risk management programs addressing both primary hypertension control and infectious disease preparedness in high-risk populations.

Arterial-Ventricular Coupling Strain Index as an Independent Predictor of 10-Year ASCVD Risk in Diabetic-Hypertensive Patients.

Revue : Echocardiography (Mount Kisco, N.Y.) | Tier : NA | IF estime : NA

Date de publication : 2026-10

Resultats principaux : This cross-sectional observational study enrolled 340 participants with both diabetes mellitus and hypertension to evaluate the association between arterial-ventricular coupling and 10-year atherosclerotic cardiovascular disease risk (10Y-ASCVDR) using the China-PAR model. The arterial-ventricular coupling strain index (AVCSI) was calculated as the ratio of arterial velocity pulse index (AVI) to left ventricular global longitudinal strain (LVGLS). Multivariable logistic regression demonstrated AVCSI as an independent predictor of high 10Y-ASCVDR (OR=0.991, 95% CI: 0.984-0.999, p=0.019), and ROC analysis showed a sensitivity of 80.56% for diagnosing 10Y-ASCVDR greater than 10%. No significant subgroup analyses were reported, though the China-PAR model may limit applicability outside of Chinese populations. Key limitations include the cross-sectional design preventing causal inference and the single-center recruitment from one geographic region.

Implications Medical Affairs : The identification of AVCSI as an independent predictor of ASCVD risk in the diabetic-hypertensive population provides a novel imaging biomarker with potential relevance for early cardiac risk stratification beyond standard clinical assessments. From a Medical Affairs perspective, this tool could complement established risk calculators and support product positioning for antihypertensive therapies demonstrably improving ventricular-arterial coupling and end-organ protection. KOL collaboration opportunities exist to validate AVCSI in multicenter trials and potentially include it in hypertension management guidelines as a supplementary imaging endpoint.

Molecular and Cellular Underpinnings of Primary Hypertension: An Integrative Mechanistic Framework.

Revue : Journal of Cellular Physiology | Tier : NA | IF estime : NA

Date de publication : 2026-10

Resultats principaux : This comprehensive review reframes primary hypertension as a systemic multiorgan disorder and synthesizes updated evidence on its complex pathophysiology. Three core interrelated mechanisms are identified: dysregulation of the renin-angiotensin-aldosterone system (RAAS), central nervous system dysfunction, and activation of inflammatory and oxidative stress pathways that operate in a self-reinforcing cycle. Additional contributors including suboptimal dietary patterns, salt sensitivity, gut microbiota dysbiosis, circadian rhythm disruption, and insulin resistance further modulate these central drivers, perpetuating blood pressure elevation. The review elaborates on how these mechanisms span multiple organ systems, driving vascular, renal, neurohumoral, and immune dysfunction, and proposes an integrated, upstream-targeted therapeutic framework. Limitations include the reliance on heterogeneous preclinical and clinical data and the absence of systematic review methodology, which may introduce selection bias in the evidence synthesis.

Implications Medical Affairs : This integrative mechanistic framework directly supports the rationale for combination antihypertensive therapy simultaneously targeting RAAS dysregulation, sympathetic overactivation, and inflammatory pathways, with critical relevance for product portfolio positioning. Medical Affairs teams can leverage these insights to develop educational content positioning novel antihypertensives within the context of upstream systemic disease modulation rather than isolated blood pressure control. Future research directions highlighted — including gut microbiome-targeted interventions and circadian rhythm modulation — represent emerging areas for KOL collaboration and investigator-initiated research grants.

Prevalence and risk factors of cardiometabolic disease among informal sector workers in Cape Coast, Ghana: Insights from the CarMeR study.

Revue : PloS ONE | Tier : NA | IF estime : NA

Date de publication : 2026-09-29

Resultats principaux : This cross-sectional study analyzed the cardiometabolic disease burden in 948 informal sector workers including traders, fisherfolk, artisans, farmers, and drivers in Cape Coast, Ghana. The prevalence of obesity, hypertension, prediabetes, and diabetes was 45.46%, 40.19%, 29.54%, and 13.40%, respectively, with women and middle-aged adults aged 45 to 59 disproportionately affected. Independent risk factors for hypertension included older age (aOR=4.82, 95% CI: 3.06-7.68 for age 60 or above), low physical activity (aOR=1.73, 95% CI: 1.16-2.62), and elevated triglycerides (aOR=1.84, 95% CI: 1.13-3.04). Significant heterogeneity in diagnostic criteria was observed, with only 9.28% of obese and 2.36% of diabetic participants meeting all diagnostic criteria simultaneously, suggesting context-specific tools are needed. Limitations include the cross-sectional design, self-reported lifestyle data, and recruitment from a single urban metropolitan area, limiting generalizability to rural Ghanaian populations.

Implications Medical Affairs : These data highlight the substantial and underdiagnosed burden of hypertension among Africa's informal working population, representing a critical unmet need for cardiovascular screening and treatment services in low- and middle-income countries. From a Medical Affairs perspective, the findings support the case for expanding antihypertensive treatment access programs in Sub-Saharan Africa, with a focus on sex-sensitive and occupation-adapted health interventions. KOL engagement in African cardiology and public health should be prioritized to advocate for context-specific hypertension guidelines that account for diagnostic heterogeneity and workforce-specific risk profiles.

Revue : Revista de la Facultad de Ciencias Medicas (Cordoba, Argentina) | Tier : NA | IF estime : NA

Date de publication : 2026-09-28

Resultats principaux : This descriptive cross-sectional study investigated hypertension risk factors, lifestyle habits, and knowledge levels among 133 fifth-grade students at a public school in La Plata, Argentina, using surveys and blood pressure measurements by medical students. None of the students knew their blood pressure values, and over 60% believed blood pressure measurement was only necessary from age 14 years, demonstrating a critical awareness gap. Only 5% of students consumed fruits and vegetables regularly, while 88% reported household members adding salt to prepared meals, indicating high-risk dietary patterns from an early age. Forty percent of students reported at least one hypertensive parent, yet 74% did not identify cardiovascular disease as the leading cause of death. Limitations include the small, single-school sample, self-reported data from preadolescents prone to response bias, and the descriptive design without control or comparison groups.

Implications Medical Affairs : The profound lack of hypertension awareness among school-age children and families in Argentina underscores an urgent public health gap with direct implications for primary prevention programs targeting salt reduction and cardiovascular health literacy. From a Medical Affairs perspective, school-based hypertension education initiatives represent a cost-effective population-level intervention that pharmaceutical companies can support through health literacy programs and partnerships with public health organizations. KOL advocacy to integrate cardiovascular risk education into national school health curricula would align with broader strategies to improve long-term hypertension control rates in Latin America.

Clinical Profiles and Maternal-Neonatal Outcomes in Pregnancies Complicated by Hypertensive Disorders in Puerto Rico (2020-2023).

Revue : Puerto Rico Health Sciences Journal | Tier : NA | IF estime : NA

Date de publication : 2026-09

Resultats principaux : This retrospective study reviewed clinical profiles and maternal-neonatal outcomes in 278 pregnant patients at the high-risk clinic of the University District Hospital in Puerto Rico from 2020 to 2023, of whom 40.3% had a hypertensive disorder including chronic hypertension (40%) and preeclampsia (22%). Chronic hypertension was strongly associated with superimposed preeclampsia, conferring a 4.8-fold increased odds (95% CI: 2.36-9.75), and higher maternal BMI was significantly associated with all hypertensive subtypes. Hypertensive disorders were linked to earlier gestational age at delivery and lower neonatal Apgar scores, though no significant difference in cesarean delivery rates was observed between groups. Analyses were performed using Chi-square, Fisher's exact test, and Wilcoxon-Mann-Whitney methods with significance at p less than or equal to 0.05. Limitations include the retrospective single-center design, potential for incomplete medical record data, and the relatively small sample size from a specialized high-risk clinic, limiting broader representativeness.

Implications Medical Affairs : These findings reinforce the critical need for early identification and intensive management of chronic hypertension in pregnancy, particularly in Hispanic populations with elevated BMI, with implications for guidelines on hypertensive disorders of pregnancy. From a Medical Affairs perspective, the strong association between chronic hypertension and preeclampsia risk supports positioning of antihypertensives safe in pregnancy as essential tools in obstetric cardiovascular risk management. Collaboration with KOLs in maternal-fetal medicine and cardiology could drive registry studies and prospective trials to optimize antihypertensive treatment algorithms for pregnant hypertensive patients in underrepresented Hispanic populations.

Revue : Asian Pacific Journal of Cancer Prevention | Tier : NA | IF estime : NA

Date de publication : 2026-09-01

Resultats principaux : This prospective cohort study enrolled 250 adult cancer patients receiving chemotherapy at a tertiary care center in Bangladesh to evaluate the combined impact of diabetes, hypertension, and gut microbial diversity on treatment response and survival. Among participants, 32.8% had diabetes and 38.4% had hypertension; objective response rates were significantly lower in hypertensive patients (45.8% versus 55.8% in normotensive patients, p less than 0.05) and diabetic patients (41.5% versus 57.1%, p less than 0.05). Median progression-free survival and overall survival were shorter in patients with hypertension (9.1 and 20.2 months) compared with those without comorbidities (13.4 and 27.9 months). Multivariable analysis confirmed hypertension (adjusted hazard ratio 1.41) and diabetes (aHR 1.58) as independent predictors of worse survival alongside low gut microbial diversity (aHR 1.76) and advanced tumor stage (aHR 2.34). Limitations include the single-center design, relatively small sample, and use of 16S rRNA sequencing without metagenomics, limiting microbiome characterization depth.

Implications Medical Affairs : This study highlights hypertension as an independent adverse prognostic factor in oncology patients receiving chemotherapy, adding a cardiovascular comorbidity dimension to oncological outcomes research with relevance to cardio-oncology. From a Medical Affairs perspective, these findings provide an emerging rationale for integrated cardiometabolic management protocols in oncology settings, positioning antihypertensive therapy as potentially beneficial not only for cardiovascular but also oncological outcomes. Collaboration with oncology KOLs to design prospective trials assessing the impact of optimal blood pressure control on chemotherapy response and survival represents a high-impact research investment for cardio-oncology positioning.

Assessing a multicomponent quality improvement strategy for chronic care of cardiovascular diseases in India: The C-QIP randomized feasibility trial.

Revue : PLoS Medicine | Tier : NA | IF estime : NA

Date de publication : 2026-09-29

Resultats principaux : This pragmatic feasibility randomized controlled trial enrolled 410 adults with ischemic heart disease, ischemic stroke, or heart failure across four public and private hospitals in India to assess a multicomponent collaborative quality improvement (C-QIP) strategy combining electronic decision support, non-physician care coordination, patient education, text-message reminders, and audit-feedback. Retention was high at 93.2% in the C-QIP arm versus 91.7% in usual care at a median follow-up of 15 months, with 94.4% fidelity to lifestyle counseling delivery in C-QIP participants. C-QIP significantly increased guideline-directed medical therapy use for ischemic heart disease (58.3% vs. 32.4%, RR 1.45, 95% CI: 1.18-1.78) and ischemic stroke (76.7% vs. 31.8%, RR 2.41, 95% CI: 1.52-3.81) but not heart failure. Self-reported adherence was higher in C-QIP for medications (RR 1.08), diet (RR 1.07), and physical activity (RR 1.23). Limitations include the feasibility design, individual randomization within shared clinics creating contamination risk, and limited statistical power for clinical outcomes and subgroup analyses.

Implications Medical Affairs : The C-QIP trial provides compelling proof-of-concept that low-technology, high-fidelity quality improvement strategies can substantially close the guideline-directed medical therapy gap in secondary CVD prevention in India, directly relevant to hypertension management in resource-limited settings. From a Medical Affairs perspective, this study supports advocacy for digital adherence tools and non-physician care coordinators as scalable adjuncts to antihypertensive pharmacotherapy in emerging markets. The publication in PLoS Medicine and affiliation with prominent academic centers creates a strong KOL network for future large-scale confirmatory hybrid trials that could reshape CVD care guidelines in South Asia.

Evidence from reviewed literature indicates that adequate levels of 25-hydroxyvitamin D (25(OH)D) are inversely associated with the incidence of cardiovascular diseases and hypertension in subjects with significant vitamin D deficiencies. Prenatal factors shaping adolescent cardiovascular function and cardiometabolic risk trajectories.

Revue : Frontiers in Pediatrics | Tier : NA | IF estime : NA

Date de publication : 2026-09-14

Resultats principaux : This systematic narrative review searched PubMed from database inception through March 14, 2026 to synthesize evidence linking prenatal exposures to cardiovascular outcomes including hypertension in adolescent populations. Growth-related factors, especially low birth weight and preterm birth, emerged as the most consistent predictors of elevated blood pressure and adverse cardiac structural phenotypes in adolescence. Maternal preeclampsia and gestational hypertension were associated with altered offspring cardiac structure and endocrine dysregulation, while maternal obesity and diabetes influenced cardiometabolic outcomes largely mediated by offspring adiposity. Environmental prenatal exposures including maternal smoking and chemical pollutants were associated with increased cardiovascular risk through oxidative stress and disrupted organogenesis. Common mechanistic pathways across exposure categories included impaired nephrogenesis, hypothalamic-pituitary-adrenal axis dysregulation, and disrupted autonomic regulation, with significant knowledge gaps remaining regarding the full contribution of prenatal factors to adolescent cardiovascular health.

Implications Medical Affairs : This review highlights the importance of prenatal exposures in programming lifelong cardiovascular risk including hypertension, offering a developmental epidemiology perspective that could influence early primary prevention strategies. From a Medical Affairs perspective, these findings reinforce the value of maternal hypertension management as a preventive investment not only for the mother but also for offspring cardiovascular health, supporting broader indications and long-term outcome data for antihypertensives used in pregnancy. KOL collaboration with developmental pediatricians and epidemiologists in the DOHaD field could open new research avenues for investigator-initiated studies on early biomarkers of hypertension risk in adolescents born to hypertensive mothers.

Impact of immunosuppressive therapy and clinical risk factors on the prevalence of low bone mineral density in rheumatic diseases.

Revue : Frontiers in Medicine | Tier : NA | IF estime : NA

Date de publication : 2026-09-14

Resultats principaux : This cross-sectional study of 704 adult patients at the Hannover Medical School Rheumatology Outpatient Clinic evaluated the prevalence of low bone mineral density (BMD) and associated risk factors in inflammatory rheumatic diseases including conditions frequently comorbid with hypertension. The overall prevalence of low BMD (T-score of -1.0 or below) was 56%, with rheumatoid arthritis (65.8%) and systemic lupus erythematosus (60.2%) showing the highest rates. Beyond established risk factors such as age, female sex, and low BMI, alcohol consumption was significantly associated with low BMD across multiple disease entities. Conventional synthetic DMARD use was associated with higher low BMD prevalence in RA, likely reflecting greater disease severity rather than direct drug effect. Limitations include the cross-sectional design precluding temporal inference, self-reported lifestyle data, and potential confounding by disease severity.

Implications Medical Affairs : While not primarily focused on arterial hypertension, this study is relevant to Medical Affairs as it highlights the complex comorbidity burden in rheumatic disease patients, many of whom also have hypertension, underscoring the need for comprehensive comorbidity management strategies. From a Medical Affairs perspective, these data support advocacy for integrated multidisciplinary care protocols in rheumatology that address cardiovascular and metabolic comorbidities including hypertension alongside musculoskeletal disease management. KOL engagement at the rheumatology-cardiology interface could facilitate joint care pathways addressing the full cardiometabolic burden of inflammatory rheumatic diseases, including hypertension management guidance for these patients.

Maternal calcium intake during pregnancy and its impact on offspring blood pressure.

Revue : Revista de la Facultad de Ciencias Medicas (Cordoba, Argentina) | Tier : NA | IF estime : NA

Date de publication : 2026-08-24

Resultats principaux : This narrative review evaluated evidence linking maternal calcium intake during pregnancy to blood pressure outcomes in offspring, utilizing animal studies involving Wistar, Sprague Dawley, and Wistar Kyoto rat strains as representative models. An inverse association between calcium intake and high blood pressure has been widely described in the literature, with maternal calcium deficiency during pregnancy potentially programming increased blood pressure in offspring through fetal programming and epigenetic mechanisms. All three rat strains reviewed were identified as appropriate models for studying the relationship between calcium intake and blood pressure in normotensive animals. Evidence specifically addressing the effects of maternal calcium intake during pregnancy on adult offspring blood pressure remains limited, highlighting significant gaps in the mechanistic understanding of this association. Limitations include the predominantly animal model evidence base, narrative review methodology without systematic synthesis, and limited translational evidence from human pregnancy cohorts that could directly inform clinical practice recommendations.

Implications Medical Affairs : The evidence linking maternal calcium nutrition to offspring blood pressure programming aligns with the broader developmental origins of hypertension framework and has implications for nutritional interventions during pregnancy as a primary prevention strategy for hypertension. From a Medical Affairs perspective, these data support educational initiatives on the importance of adequate calcium supplementation during pregnancy for long-term cardiovascular health outcomes in offspring, potentially informing guidelines for pregnancy-related hypertension prevention. Future research in this area, particularly well-powered human cohort studies examining maternal calcium intake and offspring blood pressure trajectories, represents a high-priority area for investigator-initiated research support by Medical Affairs teams.
