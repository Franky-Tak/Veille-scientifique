Prevalence and associated factors of chronic kidney disease among hypertensive patients attending cardiac clinic at a tertiary hospital in Tanzania: a hospital-based cross-sectional study.

Revue : BMJ open | Tier : NA | IF estime : NA

Date de publication : 2026-10-06

Resultats principaux : This hospital-based cross-sectional study enrolled 162 adult hypertensive patients attending a tertiary cardiac outpatient clinic in Dar es Salaam, Tanzania, using simple random sampling. The primary outcome was study-defined chronic kidney disease (CKD), classified as eGFR < 60 mL/min/1.73 m2 and/or urine dipstick proteinuria ≥ 1+. The prevalence of CKD was 21.0% (95% CI 15.4%–27.9%), with 17.9% of participants having an eGFR below 60 mL/min/1.73 m2; median eGFR was 77 mL/min/1.73 m2 (IQR 62–92). In multivariable logistic regression, diabetes was the only independent factor significantly associated with CKD (adjusted OR 3.26, 95% CI 1.17–9.09, p = 0.024); uncontrolled hypertension was not independently significant after adjustment (adjusted OR 1.33, p = 0.531). Key limitations include the single-centre design, absence of UACR for all participants, and lack of repeated eGFR measurements required for full KDIGO CKD confirmation, which may have resulted in underestimation or overestimation of true CKD prevalence.

Implications Medical Affairs : The 21% CKD burden in hypertensive patients at an African tertiary clinic underscores the need for integrated cardiorenal screening protocols in hypertension management guidelines, particularly in sub-Saharan Africa. For pharmaceutical companies marketing RAAS inhibitors, diuretics, or novel agents such as SGLT2 inhibitors in African markets, this data provides a compelling argument for cardiorenal protection messaging and potential co-positioning with diabetes care pathways. Engaging KOLs in African nephrology and cardiology to design multicentre studies using full KDIGO criteria would strengthen the evidence base and create platform opportunities for targeted educational initiatives.

Occupational disparities in chronic disease prevalence and metabolic covariation: a cross-sectional study of six occupational groups in Lanzhou.

Revue : Frontiers in public health | Tier : NA | IF estime : NA

Date de publication : 2026-09-21

Resultats principaux : This retrospective cross-sectional study enrolled 1,473 working-age individuals (18–64 years) from six occupational groups at the Physical Examination Center of the First Hospital of Lanzhou University, China, between August 2024 and August 2025. Age-standardized prevalence (ASR) was calculated for hypertension, diabetes, hyperlipidemia, hyperuricemia, and obesity across occupational categories including police officers, firefighters, software engineers, and healthcare workers. The most prominent occupational disparities were in hyperlipidemia and hyperuricemia: police officers (ASR = 0.435) and software engineers (ASR = 0.418) showed significantly higher hyperlipidemia prevalence than healthcare workers (ASR = 0.218), while firefighters (ASR = 0.381) and police officers (ASR = 0.332) had higher hyperuricemia rates. No statistically significant occupational disparities were observed for hypertension, diabetes mellitus, or obesity. Correlation analyses identified BMI as widely correlated with multiple metabolic indicators (r = 0.39–0.51), and uric acid was strongly correlated with creatinine (r = 0.64), pointing to clustering of cardiometabolic risk; retrospective design and self-report questionnaire data represent notable limitations.

Implications Medical Affairs : The absence of significant occupational differences in hypertension prevalence, despite disparities in dyslipidemia and hyperuricemia, suggests that occupational risk stratification for cardiovascular prevention should incorporate a broader cardiometabolic panel rather than blood pressure alone. For Medical Affairs, this evidence supports occupational health partnerships as a channel for early hypertension detection programs, with BMI and lipid management as co-entry points, potentially expanding patient identification pipelines for antihypertensive and lipid-lowering co-prescribing strategies. KOL engagement with occupational medicine specialists and corporate health program directors in China represents an actionable insight to position preventive cardiovascular risk tools in workplace health settings.

Theme 6 : Clinical Trials and Guidelines (1 article)

Effectiveness of a Sodium Intake Monitoring Smartphone Application (NamakBan) on Clinical Outcomes of Patients Diagnosed With Hypertension: A Randomized Clinical Trial.

Revue : Nursing open | Tier : NA | IF estime : NA

Date de publication : 2026-10

Resultats principaux : This parallel randomized clinical trial enrolled 88 adults with physician-diagnosed primary hypertension at the specialized hypertension clinic of Shahid Modarres Hospital in Tehran, Iran (April 2024 – September 2025), randomized 1:1 to standard care or standard care plus the NamakBan Android smartphone application. Primary outcomes were systolic and diastolic blood pressure; secondary outcomes included self-reported dietary sodium intake, urinary sodium-to-potassium (Na/K) ratio, and hospital readmissions; assessments were performed at baseline, 1 month, and 2 months. The intervention group showed statistically significant greater reductions in both systolic and diastolic blood pressure compared with the control group, along with a greater decline in self-reported daily sodium intake. The urinary Na/K ratio decreased within the experimental group over time, but the between-group difference was not statistically significant; no hypertension-related hospital readmissions were recorded in the intervention arm versus four in the control arm, though this difference was also not significant. Important limitations include an unblinded self-report method for dietary sodium, short follow-up of only 2 months, single-centre design, and Android-only availability of the application.

Implications Medical Affairs : This RCT provides initial evidence that mobile health applications targeting dietary sodium can produce statistically significant blood pressure reductions as an adjunct to standard antihypertensive care, supporting guideline-level interest in digital therapeutics for hypertension management. From a Medical Affairs perspective, digital health tools that reinforce medication adherence and lifestyle modification align with integrated care value propositions, providing an opportunity to position antihypertensive products within broader digital patient management ecosystems. Engaging KOLs in clinical nursing, digital health innovation, and hypertension management to design larger, multicenter RCTs with objective sodium biomarker validation would strengthen the evidence and could support labeling or companion tool development discussions with regulatory bodies.

Early-onset atherosclerotic cardiovascular disease in young adults: Lifestyle, environmental, and psychosocial risk factors.

Revue : International journal of cardiology. Cardiovascular risk and prevention | Tier : NA | IF estime : NA

Date de publication : 2026-09-24

Resultats principaux : This narrative review systematically examined lifestyle, environmental, metabolic, psychosocial, and inherited determinants of early-onset atherosclerotic cardiovascular disease (ASCVD) across adolescents (10–19 years), young adults (20–39 years), and selected adults aged 40–54 years. The review critically distinguishes between established causal drivers—cumulative apolipoprotein B-containing lipoprotein exposure and cigarette smoking—and predominantly associative evidence for sleep disruption, psychosocial stress, e-cigarette use, and air pollution. Epidemiological evidence confirms a substantial burden of CVD among people aged 15–39 years, though evidence for rising clinically manifest ASCVD is less consistent than evidence of increasing obesity, hypertension, dyslipidemia, diabetes, nicotine exposure, and subclinical vascular injury. The review recommends lipid screening and lipoprotein(a) measurement at least once in adulthood, targeted evaluation for familial hypercholesterolemia, and a family history review; coronary artery calcium scoring and carotid imaging are not recommended as universal screening tools in asymptomatic young people. Limitations inherent to narrative reviews include potential selection bias in literature search and the inability to quantify effect sizes or generate pooled estimates for the reviewed risk factors.

Implications Medical Affairs : This review reinforces the importance of early-life exposure to conventional risk factors—including hypertension—in ASCVD pathogenesis, supporting the Medical Affairs narrative that earlier antihypertensive intervention and risk factor modification carry lifetime cardiovascular benefit. For product positioning, the emphasis on hypertension and dyslipidemia as independently underestimated risks in young adults, especially in women, provides a scientific basis for campaigns targeting younger patient segments and primary care physicians in preventive cardiology. A collaborative KOL strategy with academic cardiologists and lipidologists focusing on lipoprotein(a) testing and early cardiovascular risk stratification in hypertensive young adults could help generate real-world evidence supporting earlier treatment initiation.

Apolipoprotein(a) Isoform Size and Lipoprotein(a) Concentration and Acute Myocardial Infarction Risk in China: the INTERHEART China Study.

Revue : The American journal of cardiology | Tier : NA | IF estime : NA

Date de publication : 2026-10-06

Resultats principaux : This case-control study, nested within the INTERHEART China study, enrolled 1,908 participants including 963 patients with first-time acute myocardial infarction (AMI) and 945 age- and sex-matched controls, with lifestyle, metabolic, and psychosocial risk factors collected via standardized questionnaires. Lp(a) concentrations were measured using an isoform-insensitive immunoassay, and apo(a) isoform sizes were quantified via Western blotting; multivariate logistic regression and restricted cubic splines were used to evaluate associations with AMI risk. High Lp(a) concentrations were significantly associated with increased AMI risk, and a consistent negative correlation between apo(a) isoform size and Lp(a) concentration was observed across the cohort. In the single-isoform subgroup, smaller apo(a) size was associated with higher AMI risk in unadjusted models but became non-significant after adjusting for Lp(a) levels; in the dual-isoform subgroup, carrying smaller isoforms (< 23 kringle IV repeats) was independently associated with higher AMI risk (OR 1.83, 95% CI 1.12–3.04), while larger isoform sizes (> 29) showed a trend toward higher MI risk. Limitations include the case-control design (prone to recall bias), a Chinese-specific population limiting generalizability, and the need for validation of isoform-specific thresholds in prospective cohort studies.

Implications Medical Affairs : This study from the INTERHEART China cohort supports the integration of Lp(a) mass and apo(a) isoform genotyping into cardiovascular risk stratification, potentially enabling more precise identification of high-risk hypertensive patients who may benefit from intensified treatment. For Medical Affairs, the dual-isoform finding reinforces the growing scientific rationale for Lp(a)-lowering therapeutics as an adjunct to antihypertensive and lipid-lowering treatment strategies, particularly in Asian populations where Lp(a) epidemiology and isoform distributions may differ from Western cohorts. Engaging KOLs in Chinese cardiovascular research and lipid genomics to develop prospective validation studies would strengthen the evidence base for Lp(a) genotyping panels as standard-of-care tools in hypertensive patients with elevated cardiovascular risk.

Quality of life in hyperkalemia: baseline analysis of a cohort study of management of hyperkalemia in patients with chronic kidney disease or heart failure in Japan.

Revue : Clinical and experimental nephrology | Tier : NA | IF estime : NA

Date de publication : 2026-10-06

Resultats principaux : This prospective cohort study analyzed baseline data from 347 patients with chronic kidney disease (CKD stage G3 or higher) or chronic heart failure (NYHA class II–IV) enrolled at Japanese nephrology and cardiology outpatient clinics, with a median age of 75 years, 74% male, and 93% with CKD. The study assessed treatment patterns and quality of life (QOL) using generic patient-reported outcomes and treatment-specific instruments at enrollment; 59% of patients were prescribed a RAAS inhibitor and 29% received dietary potassium restriction. Physical QOL scores were below population norms, while mental scores were comparable; treatment-specific QOL scores suggested that potassium binders had a relatively small perceived impact on QOL compared with dietary potassium restriction. Adherence to potassium binders was high, and concurrent use of RAAS inhibitors and potassium binders was common, suggesting a role for binders in enabling RAAS inhibitor continuation in hyperkalemic patients. Baseline-only data from a single time point limits causal inference; the study is ongoing, and longitudinal outcomes on QOL, RAAS continuation, and serum potassium trajectories remain to be reported.

Implications Medical Affairs : The finding that potassium binders have a minimal perceived QOL burden and support RAAS inhibitor continuation strengthens the Medical Affairs narrative that guideline-directed cardiorenal therapy can be maintained without compromising patient-reported outcomes. This evidence directly supports positioning of novel potassium-binding agents as enablers of optimal antihypertensive and heart failure therapy, with a patient-centered QOL argument relevant for HTA submissions and payer dialogues in Japan and other Asian markets. Collaborating with nephrology and cardiology KOLs in Japan to publish the longitudinal phase of this cohort, including RAAS inhibitor dose optimization outcomes, would provide compelling real-world evidence for formulary inclusion and prescriber education programs.

From prematurity and low birth weight to CKD: opportunities for intervention.

Revue : Nephrology, dialysis, transplantation : official publication of the European Dialysis and Transplant Association - European Renal Association | Tier : NA | IF estime : NA

Date de publication : 2026-10-06

Resultats principaux : This narrative review synthesizes evidence on the pathway from preterm birth (< 37 gestational weeks), low birth weight (< 2,500 g), and small for gestational age (SGA, < 10th percentile for gestational age)—collectively termed small vulnerable newborns (SVNs)—to lifelong chronic kidney disease (CKD) risk. Globally, approximately 35 million SVNs are born annually; being born as an SVN ranks fifth among risk factors for early mortality and triples CKD risk in early adulthood when extreme prematurity is combined with SGA status. Pathophysiological mechanisms include reduced nephron endowment leading to glomerular hyperfiltration, hypertension, and sclerosis, compounded by neonatal acute kidney injury (AKI) from renal hypoperfusion and nephrotoxic medications in the NICU. The review proposes preventive strategies including optimizing maternal health, reducing NICU-related nephrotoxicity, systematic AKI documentation at discharge, and stratified long-term follow-up by gestational age, birth weight, AKI history, and comorbidities. As a narrative review, the study is limited by potential publication bias and the heterogeneity of underlying studies; most evidence on nephron endowment reduction derives from animal models, with limited longitudinal human data.

Implications Medical Affairs : The global scale of SVN births and their documented lifelong hypertension and CKD risk establishes a large preventable cardiorenal burden, creating an evidence-based rationale for early nephroprotective and antihypertensive interventions in patients with perinatal risk histories. From a Medical Affairs perspective, this review supports the development of patient journey mapping tools for hypertensive adults with perinatal CKD risk factors, enabling more targeted risk stratification messaging in nephrology and maternal-fetal medicine. Partnerships with pediatric nephrologists, neonatologists, and maternal health KOLs to design prospective registries tracking SVNs through adulthood would provide crucial long-term evidence and position pharmaceutical companies as committed partners in the continuum of cardiorenal care.

Risk factors of hypertension in South Africa: A systematic review and meta-analysis to inform the development of a clinical prediction model.

Revue : Health SA = SA Gesondheid | Tier : NA | IF estime : NA

Date de publication : 2026-09-25

Resultats principaux : This systematic review and meta-analysis included 54 studies (cross-sectional, case-control, and cohort designs) from South Africa, retrieved from PubMed, Scopus, MEDLINE, and African databases, covering the period from 1990 to March 2025 across multiple provinces. Random-effects meta-analysis was used to pool odds ratios for eligible hypertension risk factors; certainty of evidence was assessed using GRADE methodology. Strong pooled associations were observed for dyslipidemia (OR 3.79, 95% CI 1.84–7.80), age > 50 years (OR 3.49, 95% CI 1.82–6.70), diabetes (OR 2.57, 95% CI 1.86–3.53), obesity (OR 2.35, 95% CI 1.89–2.91), and gold mine dust exposure (OR 2.35, 95% CI 1.70–3.25). Findings were translated into a clinical prediction model intended to support early hypertension screening in South African primary care settings, with environmental and metabolic factors as the most predictive variables. Limitations include heterogeneity across included studies in terms of design, measurement tools, and populations; external validation of the derived prediction model in independent cohorts is required before clinical deployment.

Implications Medical Affairs : This meta-analysis identifies a high-priority hypertension risk profile—older age, dyslipidemia, diabetes, obesity, and occupational environmental exposures—that is directly relevant to positioning antihypertensive therapies within integrated cardiometabolic care strategies for African low-to-middle-income country markets. The development of a South Africa-specific clinical prediction model is significant for Medical Affairs because it provides an evidence base to support targeted screening programs in partnership with primary care networks, health ministries, and private sector employers in mining industries. Investing in KOL collaborations with South African public health researchers to externally validate and digitize this prediction model could establish a strong regional presence and support market access narratives built on early detection and prevention.

Coronary heart disease in young women: risk factors, mechanistic phenotypes, and implications for primary care.

Revue : Frontiers in cardiovascular medicine | Tier : NA | IF estime : NA

Date de publication : 2026-09-21

Resultats principaux : This clinically oriented narrative review synthesized evidence from PubMed, CNKI, and Chinese General Practice through May 2026 on coronary heart disease (CHD) in young women, integrating conventional and female-specific cardiovascular risk factors with coronary phenotypes and primary care management strategies. The review demonstrates that hypertension, dysglycemia, smoking, dyslipidemia, family history, and psychosocial stress in young women are frequently underestimated clinically, especially when amplified by hypertensive disorders of pregnancy, polycystic ovary syndrome, autoimmune disease, or antiphospholipid syndrome. Beyond obstructive atherosclerosis, the review highlights female-predominant coronary phenotypes including plaque erosion, coronary microvascular dysfunction, vasospasm, myocardial infarction with non-obstructive coronary arteries (MINOCA), and spontaneous coronary artery dissection (SCAD). Standard cardiovascular risk scores are found to be insufficiently calibrated for young women due to heavy age-weighting and lack of female-specific risk-enhancing factors; the review advocates for differentiated screening protocols based on prevention vs. symptomatic vs. established disease contexts. As a narrative review, the study has inherent limitations including risk of selection bias in literature coverage, lack of systematic search methodology, and absence of pooled quantitative risk estimates.

Implications Medical Affairs : The systematic underrecognition of hypertension and other cardiovascular risks in young women, amplified by sex-specific conditions like hypertensive disorders of pregnancy, creates a compelling Medical Affairs argument for expanded hypertension screening protocols targeting women of reproductive age through obstetric and gynecology care pathways. For product positioning, the evidence that standard risk tools miss young women supports advocacy for guideline updates incorporating female-specific risk enhancers, presenting an opportunity to align antihypertensive products with sex-specific cardiovascular prevention campaigns. Engaging women's cardiovascular health KOLs, cardiologists, and obstetricians in multicenter prospective studies on hypertension management across female-specific cardiovascular phenotypes would generate differentiated evidence supporting individualized treatment strategies and real-world effectiveness data.
