Hypertensive disorders during pregnancy and cognitive outcomes in children: the ALSPAC study.

Revue : Hypertension in Pregnancy | Tier : NA | IF estime : NA

Date de publication : 2026-08-31

Resultats principaux : This population-based longitudinal cohort study (ALSPAC) examined associations between hypertensive disorders of pregnancy (HDP) and offspring IQ at ages 8 and 16 years, enrolling over 4,900 mother-child pairs at age 8 and over 3,300 at age 16. Validated IQ assessments were used (WISC-III at age 8, WASI at age 16), and binary and multinomial logistic regression models were applied to estimate odds ratios and 95% CIs. The primary finding was that HDP were not associated with lower offspring IQ at either age 8 or 16, refuting a widely held concern about maternal hypertension impairing fetal neurocognitive development. A secondary finding showed that gestational hypertension (but not pre-eclampsia) was associated with higher odds of above-average IQ at age 16 in multinomial regression; however, this association was not replicated at age 8 and was not confirmed in sensitivity analyses using age-specific IQ tertiles, suggesting it may be a chance finding. Key limitations include the observational longitudinal design with potential selection bias from study attrition, residual confounding by unmeasured socioeconomic and genetic factors, and the restriction of the cohort to a single UK region (Bristol, Avon).

Implications Medical Affairs : These findings challenge the assumption that HDP universally impair fetal neurocognitive development, which could influence how Medical Affairs teams contextualise HDP-related risk information in guidelines and product communications for antihypertensive agents used during or after pregnancy. For companies with antihypertensives indicated in the pregnancy or postpartum setting, this evidence reduces the urgency of neurocognitive risk messaging as a primary driver for early pharmacological intervention, though it does not diminish the cardiovascular rationale for treatment. KOL engagement should prioritise the need for replication studies and exploration of potential mechanisms behind the gestational hypertension-IQ association, identifying collaborative research opportunities in perinatal pharmacoepidemiology.

Stroke incidence trends and the role of chronic disease management in a universal health coverage system: a retrospective observational study in Macao, 2019-2024.

Revue : Family Medicine and Community Health | Tier : NA | IF estime : NA

Date de publication : 2026-08-31

Resultats principaux : This retrospective observational study used electronic health records from Macao's universal health coverage primary care system, covering 8,265 residents aged 15 years or older with confirmed ischaemic or haemorrhagic stroke diagnoses from 2019 to 2024 within a publicly funded, free-of-charge community health service. Age-standardised stroke incidence declined from 250.03 per 100,000 in 2019 to 219.95 per 100,000 in 2022, followed by a rebound in 2023-2024 that may partially reflect pandemic-related data entry delays. Over 88% of patients with pre-existing hypertension or type 2 diabetes had documented healthcare follow-up in the year preceding their stroke event. Among high-risk patients with hypertension or T2DM, stroke incidence declined and the interval from diagnosis to stroke onset lengthened significantly, suggesting that high healthcare engagement is associated with improved risk monitoring and delayed stroke onset. Despite these gains, a substantial stroke burden persisted, suggesting that achieving high coverage alone is insufficient without ongoing quality improvement in chronic disease management; the study's limitations include its retrospective administrative design with no individual-level lifestyle or medication adherence data.

Implications Medical Affairs : The persistent stroke burden despite high healthcare engagement represents a critical unmet need and underscores the value proposition for newer antihypertensive agents offering cardiovascular protection beyond blood pressure lowering alone in integrated chronic disease management pathways. Medical Affairs teams should leverage these real-world data from a UHC setting to support outcome-based messaging that positions innovative therapies as quality-improvement tools capable of translating high healthcare engagement into demonstrably better patient outcomes. KOL strategy could target primary care and public health specialists in Asia-Pacific markets, using this Macao dataset to advocate for intensified pharmacological management protocols in hypertension and T2DM co-managed care models.

Disease-Related Etiologies of Nocturia: A Focused Literature Review.

Revue : Current Urology Reports | Tier : NA | IF estime : NA

Date de publication : 2026-08-31

Resultats principaux : This structured narrative review, guided by expert opinion, identified six common medical conditions contributing to nocturia: obesity, diabetes mellitus, hypertension, obstructive sleep apnea, benign prostatic hyperplasia, and lifestyle habits, with a formal literature review of 49 articles spanning randomised controlled trials, observational studies, systematic reviews, and meta-analyses. Hypertension was characterised as contributing to nocturia through pressure natriuresis, impaired renal tubular reabsorption, and altered circadian blood pressure patterns that increase nocturnal diuresis. The review highlights that nocturia is frequently underrecognised and undertreated, with both genitourinary and systemic consequences including reduced sleep quality, daytime fatigue, falls risk, and impaired quality of life. Each of the six pathologies was shown to have unique pathophysiological pathways driving nocturia, and the review underscores the multifactorial nature of the condition as requiring tailored assessment and treatment strategies. Key limitations include reliance on expert opinion for condition selection and the heterogeneity of included study designs, which limits the strength of causal conclusions about any individual pathology's contribution.

Implications Medical Affairs : This review positions hypertension as a mechanistically plausible and clinically relevant contributor to nocturia, which is important for Medical Affairs teams promoting antihypertensive agents with favourable nocturnal BP-lowering profiles — such as long-acting once-daily agents or those with a chronotherapy advantage — where improved nocturia could be framed as a patient-reported outcome benefit. For product differentiation, highlighting the dual benefit of blood pressure control and potential nocturia reduction could strengthen the value proposition of certain antihypertensives in competitive landscapes emphasising adherence and quality-of-life endpoints. KOL engagement should focus on the need for prospective clinical trials specifically evaluating the impact of optimised antihypertensive regimens on nocturia frequency, an endpoint that could drive cross-specialty KOL development between cardiology and urology.

Periodic limb movements of sleep and hypertension in patients with obstructive sleep apnea: a hypothesis-generating analysis from a polysomnographic cohort.

Revue : Sleep and Breathing | Tier : NA | IF estime : NA

Date de publication : 2026-08-31

Resultats principaux : This retrospective cohort study enrolled 60 polysomnography-confirmed OSA patients and stratified them by Periodic Limb Movement of Sleep Index (PLMS-I) at a threshold of 15 events per hour, comparing hypertension prevalence and cardiovascular risk indices between the two groups. Patients with PLMS-I 15 events per hour or greater had a significantly higher hypertension prevalence than those below the threshold (76.7% vs. 23.3% of all hypertensive patients in the cohort; p=0.026), and demonstrated a significantly elevated arousal index (p=0.001). Binary logistic regression adjusting for age and gender showed high PLMS-I was associated with a 2.4-fold increased odds of hypertension (OR=2.421, 95% CI: 0.640-9.161, p=0.193), which did not reach statistical significance after adjustment, with age emerging as the primary predictor (OR=1.068 per year, 95% CI: 1.020-1.119, p=0.005). Unadjusted correlations between PLMS-I and Framingham Risk Score (r=0.302, p=0.023) and Charlson Comorbidity Index (r=0.346, p=0.007) were substantially attenuated after partial correlation controlling for age, indicating significant confounding. The primary limitations are the small sample size (n=60) providing insufficient statistical power for multivariate analysis, the retrospective design with attendant selection bias, and the hypothesis-generating intent, requiring larger prospective studies for confirmatory evidence.

Implications Medical Affairs : These findings identify PLMS-driven sympathetic activation as a potentially undercharacterised contributor to hypertension in OSA patients, reinforcing the clinical utility of 24-hour ambulatory blood pressure monitoring and comprehensive sleep phenotyping as part of the workup in treatment-resistant or difficult-to-control hypertension. For Medical Affairs teams with antihypertensives demonstrating favourable nocturnal blood pressure reduction profiles, this hypothesis-generating evidence provides a scientific narrative supporting expanded cardiovascular risk assessment in patients with OSA and concurrent PLMS. KOL strategy could promote prospective research linking PLMS with nocturnal hypertension and evaluating whether specific antihypertensive regimens — particularly those with chronotherapy advantages or sympatholytic mechanisms — attenuate the cardiovascular risk associated with PLMS in OSA patients.

Hypertension Incidence and Blood Pressure Control in Hispanic or Latino Adults in the HCHS/SOL Study.

Revue : JAMA Network Open | Tier : NA | IF estime : NA

Date de publication : 2026-08-03

Resultats principaux : This population-based prospective cohort study used data from the Hispanic Community Health Study/Study of Latinos (HCHS/SOL), enrolling 16,415 Hispanic or Latino adults from 4 urban US sites examined at three time points from 2008 to 2024, with 9,061 completing all three study visits. Among 5,082 participants without hypertension at baseline (defined as SBP 130 mmHg or higher, DBP 80 mmHg or higher, or antihypertensive use), 50.5% (95% CI: 47.6-53.4%) developed hypertension over a mean follow-up of 12.4 years, corresponding to an age-adjusted incidence rate of 36.4 (95% CI: 34.2-38.8) per 1,000 person-years. Marked heritage-group disparities were observed: compared with South American adults, Cuban (IRR 1.72, 95% CI: 1.36-2.17), Central American (IRR 1.47, 95% CI: 1.13-1.91), and Dominican (IRR 1.43, 95% CI: 1.07-1.91) adults had significantly higher hypertension incidence rates. Among individuals already treated for hypertension, blood pressure control rates declined from 40.2% (95% CI: 32.7-48.3%) at visit 1 to 34.7% (95% CI: 30.9-38.8%) at visit 3, representing a clinically significant and worsening gap in treatment effectiveness over time. Limitations include differential loss to follow-up across heritage groups, potential self-report bias for antihypertensive medication use, and limited generalisability to non-urban or recently immigrated Hispanic or Latino populations.

Implications Medical Affairs : The finding that approximately half of US Hispanic or Latino adults develop hypertension over 12 years, combined with declining blood pressure control rates in those already treated, represents a major and growing unmet medical need and a substantial commercial opportunity for pharmaceutical companies with antihypertensive portfolios targeting this demographic. Medical Affairs teams should prioritise generation of real-world evidence on antihypertensive efficacy and medication adherence across distinct Hispanic or Latino subpopulations, given the heritage-group disparities in hypertension incidence that suggest heterogeneous cardiovascular risk profiles requiring tailored treatment approaches. KOL engagement with Hispanic cardiology, primary care, and health equity specialists is strategically important to develop culturally and linguistically tailored treatment algorithms, position innovative therapies for high-incidence subgroups such as Cuban and Dominican adults, and ensure representation of Hispanic or Latino participants in future clinical trials.

Gender-based Assessment of Cardiovascular Risk Factors among Young Hypertensives in a Tertiary Hospital in Southwestern Nigeria: A Cross-sectional Study.

Revue : Nigerian Journal of Clinical Practice | Tier : NA | IF estime : NA

Date de publication : 2026-08-26

Resultats principaux : This cross-sectional study consecutively enrolled 157 participants aged 18 to 45 years with systemic hypertension from a tertiary hospital in Southwestern Nigeria, with a median age of 30 years and females representing 48% of the cohort, characterising gender-based differences in cardiovascular risk factor clustering among young hypertensives in a sub-Saharan African context. Males showed a significantly higher prevalence of significant alcohol use (34.2% vs. 5.3%, p less than 0.001) and cigarette smoking (14.6% vs. 1.3%, p less than 0.001). Females demonstrated a significantly higher prevalence of elevated lipoprotein(a) (13.3% vs. 2.2%, p=0.036) and moderate-to-severe obesity (p=0.036). No significant gender differences were observed in the prevalence of hyperuricemia, diabetes mellitus, dyslipidaemia, reduced estimated glomerular filtration rate, or blood pressure control rates. The study is limited by its single-centre cross-sectional design precluding causal inference, a relatively small sample size of 157 participants, and restriction to a tertiary hospital population that may not reflect broader community-level hypertension epidemiology in Southwest Nigeria.

Implications Medical Affairs : The identification of gender-specific cardiovascular risk factor profiles among young African hypertensives supports Medical Affairs investment in sex-differentiated epidemiological research in low- and middle-income country settings, where hypertension in young adults is a rapidly growing public health burden with distinct risk drivers compared to high-income countries. Elevated lipoprotein(a) as a predominant female-specific risk factor in this population creates a scientific rationale for exploring the combination of emerging Lp(a)-lowering therapies with antihypertensive management, particularly for women with early-onset hypertension in Sub-Saharan Africa where guideline-driven treatment remains limited. KOL development in West African academic cardiology centres could support regional guideline adaptation for young hypertensives, enhance clinical trial recruitment in underrepresented African populations, and position the company as a committed partner in global cardiovascular health equity.

Revue : Neurology | Tier : NA | IF estime : NA

Date de publication : 2026-08-31

Resultats principaux : This American Academy of Neurology (AAN) practice guideline, developed through systematic review of studies through June 2024 by a multidisciplinary expert panel including neurologists, headache specialists, and patient representatives, provides updated evidence-based recommendations for pharmacological migraine prevention in adults. The guideline addresses when to initiate preventive pharmacotherapy and which agents to select, covering both newer CGRP-pathway agents and established preventive medications. Of direct relevance to hypertension management, the guideline provides specific recommendations for patients with comorbid hypertension, identifying beta-blockers (propranolol, metoprolol) and certain calcium channel blockers as preferred preventive medications because they simultaneously address both migraine and hypertension, representing a dual-indication clinical opportunity. Implications Medical Affairs : The guideline's explicit recommendation of beta-blockers and calcium channel blockers as first-line preventive treatments in hypertensive migraineurs creates a direct and guideline-endorsed clinical rationale for antihypertensive products with dual migraine-prevention indications, strengthening their value proposition in a comorbidity-driven treatment model and justifying cross-indication positioning strategies. Medical Affairs teams should update their product communication materials and scientific exchange decks to highlight these AAN-endorsed dual-indication benefits, targeting the substantial patient population — estimated at 20-30% of migraineurs — who also carry a diagnosis of hypertension. Prognostic performance of endoscopic ultrasound-guided portal pressure gradient: preliminary results from the EVADIPP study.

Revue : Endoscopy | Tier : NA | IF estime : NA

Date de publication : 2026-08-31

Resultats principaux : This preliminary exploratory analysis from the ongoing prospective EVADIPP study evaluated 90 patients with suspected clinically significant portal hypertension (CSPH) who underwent paired hepatic venous pressure gradient (HVPG) and endoscopic ultrasound-guided portal pressure gradient (EUS-PPG) measurements, with prospective follow-up for hepatic decompensation events. Mean EUS-PPG and HVPG values were 13.8 +/- 5.8 mmHg and 8.9 +/- 4.8 mmHg respectively, with poor overall agreement (ICC 0.08, 95% CI: -0.08 to 0.24) that varied substantially by liver disease aetiology. During follow-up, 28 patients (31%) developed decompensation; no decompensation events occurred among patients with EUS-PPG below 10 mmHg, whereas 60.7% of decompensation events arose in patients with HVPG below 10 mmHg — highlighting the superior discriminative ability of direct portal pressure measurement. In multivariable analysis, EUS-PPG (HR 1.19, 95% CI: 1.10-1.30, p less than 0.001) and albumin were independently associated with decompensation, and EUS-PPG demonstrated superior prognostic discrimination compared to HVPG (C-index 0.78 vs. 0.56) with an optimal threshold of 12 mmHg. Note: this article pertains to portal hypertension in the context of liver disease, not arterial hypertension; limitations include the exploratory preliminary design, small cohort size (n=90), ongoing study status, and limited applicability to antihypertensive pharmacotherapy.

Implications Medical Affairs : While this study addresses portal rather than arterial hypertension, it underscores the broader Medical Affairs principle that accurate haemodynamic monitoring is essential to guide treatment thresholds and predict organ damage across hypertension phenotypes, a concept directly transferable to ambulatory blood pressure monitoring advocacy in arterial hypertension management. For product lines that include non-selective beta-blockers — the only antihypertensive drug class with proven mortality benefit in portal hypertension — Medical Affairs teams should monitor this evolving EUS-PPG literature to determine whether improved portal pressure quantification could redefine treatment initiation or intensification thresholds. Future KOL collaborations with hepatologists could explore whether EUS-PPG-guided treatment optimisation for non-selective beta-blockers in portal hypertension produces outcomes data transferable to broader antihypertensive management models emphasising direct pressure-guided therapy.

Revue : HIV Research and Clinical Practice | Tier : NA | IF estime : NA

Date de publication : 2026-08-31

Resultats principaux : This qualitative descriptive study recruited 20 people with HIV aged 35 years or older, each living with at least one cardiometabolic condition including hypertension, from three HIV clinics in the Southern United States, using photo-elicitation combined with semi-structured interviews to document participants' experiences with cardiovascular preventive health behaviours. The study population had a median age of 57 years, was predominantly African American/Black, and most participants had been living with HIV for more than 10 years. Thematic analysis organised around five social determinant domains — economic stability, neighbourhood and environment, social and community context, education, and healthcare access and quality — identified three key intersecting and modifiable barriers: the high cost of healthy food, unsafe or poorly designed neighbourhood environments limiting physical activity, and family and peer norms reinforcing unhealthy dietary and sedentary behaviours. The photo-elicitation methodology allowed participants to visually document and interpret their own health environments, providing contextually rich qualitative data beyond what traditional survey instruments capture, and highlighting the need to embed food insecurity screening, context-tailored activity counselling, and structural supports within HIV care. Limitations include the small qualitative sample limiting generalisability, restriction to three Southern US clinics, and the cross-sectional methodology which cannot capture changes in behaviour or barriers over time.

Implications Medical Affairs : The structural determinants (food insecurity, built environment, social norms) identified as primary barriers to cardiovascular prevention in people with HIV highlight a critical need for antihypertensive treatment programmes that are embedded within HIV care models and simultaneously address social determinants, as pharmacotherapy alone will be insufficient in this population without structural support. Medical Affairs investment in patient support programmes and real-world studies evaluating antihypertensive adherence in people living with HIV is strategically important, given polypharmacy complexity, drug-drug interactions with antiretroviral therapy, and the cardiometabolic burden now matching or exceeding HIV-related mortality in this ageing population. Cross-specialty KOL development bridging HIV medicine specialists, cardiologists, and social medicine researchers could position a pharmaceutical company as a leader in integrating cardiovascular and HIV care — an increasingly high-profile intersection as UNAIDS 95-95-95 success creates a growing chronic cardiometabolic burden in long-term HIV survivors.

Key limitations include dietary assessment relying on food frequency questionnaires in a predominantly healthcare professional population with high baseline health literacy, limiting generalisability, alongside potential residual confounding by correlated healthy lifestyle behaviours.

Implications Medical Affairs : The consistent mortality benefits of healthy dietary patterns including DASH in T2D patients reinforce the clinical rationale for positioning antihypertensive pharmacotherapy as a complement to dietary lifestyle modification rather than an alternative, supporting Medical Affairs messaging that frames RAAS inhibitors or other antihypertensives as synergistic with guideline-recommended dietary approaches in comprehensive cardiometabolic risk reduction. The strong CVD mortality reductions across dietary patterns provide epidemiological context for medical communication strategies emphasising that hypertension management in T2D patients must extend beyond glycaemic control to cardiovascular risk factor optimisation as a whole, justifying combination therapy approaches and integrated disease management programmes. KOL strategy should leverage this data for scientific exchange supporting combined pharmacological and dietary approaches to hypertension in T2D, targeting academic endocrinologists and clinical nutritionists as cross-specialty partners who could enrich clinical practice guidelines with diet-drug interaction evidence.

Resultats principaux : This retrospective cohort study linked data from 10,368 young parous women in the Australian Longitudinal Study on Women's Health (ALSWH) 1973-1978 cohort to medical records over a median follow-up of 16.5 years, examining associations between hypertensive disorders of pregnancy (HDP), modifiable lifestyle factors, and premature cardiovascular events defined as cardiovascular events occurring before age 50. Primary limitations include the retrospective observational design with potential record linkage bias, heterogeneous composite cardiovascular endpoint grouping events of differing severity, and restricted generalisability to Australian-born women in this cohort.

Implications Medical Affairs : HDP's independent 74% increase in premature cardiovascular event risk, robust even after lifestyle factor adjustment, strongly supports Medical Affairs advocacy for standardised long-term cardiovascular surveillance protocols for women with HDP history — a population that currently receives inconsistent post-delivery cardiovascular follow-up and represents a growing indication opportunity for antihypertensive therapies in the post-pregnancy setting. Revue : Cardiology in Review | Tier : NA | IF estime : NA

Date de publication : 2026-08-31

Resultats principaux : This focused narrative review synthesised contemporary evidence on the mechanistic, subclinical, and clinical links between polycystic ovary syndrome (PCOS) — affecting an estimated 11-13% of reproductive-age women worldwide — and cardiovascular disease, with particular attention to hypertension as a mechanistically central feature. Hyperandrogenism in PCOS contributes to insulin resistance, dyslipidaemia, hypertension, metabolic dysfunction-associated steatotic liver disease, and endothelial dysfunction; chronic low-grade inflammation and sympathetic overactivation were identified as additional BMI-independent cardiovascular risk amplifiers, meaning lean women with PCOS carry a substantial and underappreciated cardiometabolic burden. Subclinical vascular dysfunction markers — including increased carotid intima-media thickness, elevated coronary artery calcium, and elevated biomarkers such as asymmetric dimethylarginine and plasminogen activator inhibitor-1 — are consistently elevated in PCOS women relative to controls, predating overt cardiovascular events. A 2024 meta-analysis of over 300,000 women with PCOS demonstrated pooled odds ratios of 2.50 for myocardial infarction and 1.71 for stroke, with current cardiovascular risk stratification tools and cardiology guidelines not formally designating PCOS as an atherosclerotic cardiovascular disease risk-enhancing condition. Guideline-based cardiovascular interventions reviewed include lifestyle modification, metformin, statins, and GLP-1 receptor agonists, while the review identifies as a priority gap the formal inclusion of PCOS in cardiovascular risk stratification frameworks; limitations include the narrative methodology susceptible to selection bias and the absence of a systematic search protocol.

Implications Medical Affairs : The absence of PCOS from formal cardiovascular risk stratification frameworks represents an immediately actionable advocacy opportunity for Medical Affairs: engaging professional cardiology and endocrinology societies to formally designate PCOS as a cardiovascular risk-enhancing condition would expand the clinical justification for preventive pharmacotherapy including antihypertensives and statins in this population of millions of reproductive-age women. The documented hypertension pathway in PCOS — driven by hyperandrogenism, insulin resistance, and sympathetic overactivation — creates a robust mechanistic rationale for evaluating whether specific antihypertensive drug classes with favourable metabolic profiles (particularly RAAS inhibitors with insulin-sensitising properties) provide preferential cardiovascular protection in PCOS patients. KOL strategy should engage reproductive endocrinologists, women's health cardiologists, and preventive medicine specialists to co-develop and disseminate educational programmes on cardiovascular risk management in PCOS, a systematically undertreated population where Medical Affairs leadership could meaningfully advance the standard of care.

Toxic Metal-Essential Trace Element Imbalance in Cardiometabolic Disease: Ferroptosis as a Mechanistic Link Between Environmental Exposure and Vascular Injury.

Revue : Cardiovascular Toxicology | Tier : NA | IF estime : NA

Date de publication : 2026-08-31

Resultats principaux : This narrative review examined the mechanistic links between toxic metal exposure (lead, cadmium, arsenic, mercury) and cardiometabolic diseases including hypertension, cardiovascular disease, type 2 diabetes mellitus, and obesity-related metabolic dysfunction, proposing ferroptosis as the central mechanistic bridge between environmental exposure and vascular injury. Ferroptosis is characterised as an iron-dependent form of regulated cell death driven by lipid peroxidation and failure of protective systems including the selenium-GPX4-glutathione axis; toxic metals disrupt homeostasis of essential trace elements (iron, zinc, copper, selenium), creating a pro-ferroptotic cellular environment that promotes endothelial dysfunction, nitric oxide impairment, vascular stiffness, atherosclerotic plaque instability, and myocardial ischaemic vulnerability — all directly relevant to hypertension pathophysiology. The review characterises how this toxic metal-essential trace element dyshomeostasis contributes to insulin resistance and beta-cell dysfunction in T2DM, as well as cardiometabolic clustering, reframing heavy metal-associated cardiometabolic disease as a consequence of trace element dyshomeostasis rather than direct toxicity alone. Clinical implications include targeted environmental exposure history, careful biomarker interpretation, correction of documented trace element deficiencies, and avoidance of indiscriminate supplementation, with future research priorities identified as exposomics, metallomics, ferroptosis biomarker panels, and longitudinal cardiometabolic outcome studies. The review is limited by its narrative methodology with no systematic search, susceptibility to publication bias, and the predominantly mechanistic or observational nature of underlying evidence with no randomised clinical trial data directly linking metal exposure reduction to improved hypertension or cardiovascular outcomes.

Implications Medical Affairs : The ferroptosis-hypertension mechanistic axis represents an emerging area for Medical Affairs long-horizon monitoring, particularly as selenium-GPX4 pathway modulation and targeted antioxidant strategies gain traction in cardiovascular drug development pipelines, potentially opening new therapeutic categories that complement existing antihypertensive pharmacotherapy. For current antihypertensive portfolios, this review provides scientific context for incorporating environmental exposure assessment as part of comprehensive hypertension management discussions, especially in industrial or mining communities with documented toxic metal burden where treatment-resistant hypertension may have environmental aetiologies requiring additional management strategies. KOL engagement with environmental cardiologists and exposomics researchers could identify collaborative opportunities for longitudinal cohort studies examining whether antihypertensive therapy modifies ferroptosis-related biomarkers and vascular injury endpoints in chronically metal-exposed populations.

The Relationship Between Weekend Catch-Up Sleep and Hypertension: Insights From the National Health and Nutrition Examination Survey, 2017-2023.

Revue : International Journal of Hypertension | Tier : NA | IF estime : NA

Date de publication : 2026-08-30

Resultats principaux : This cross-sectional study analysed data from 16,268 US adults aged 20 years or older from the National Health and Nutrition Examination Survey (NHANES 2017-2023), examining whether weekend catch-up sleep (WCS, defined as weekend sleep duration minus weekday sleep duration) was associated with hypertension using multivariate logistic regression adjusted for sociodemographic, lifestyle, and metabolic covariates. In fully adjusted models, neither binary (more than 0 vs. 0 hours or less) nor continuous WCS duration was significantly associated with hypertension prevalence (both p greater than 0.05), indicating no meaningful relationship between compensatory weekend sleep and blood pressure status. Among categorical WCS analyses, only the 0-1 hour WCS group showed marginally lower odds of hypertension compared to the 0 hours or less group (OR=0.814, 95% CI: 0.666-0.995, p=0.033), with no dose-response pattern and all other WCS categories (1-2, 2-3, and 3 hours or more) remaining non-significant. Subgroup analyses suggested potential effect modification by sex and education level, but these results were exploratory and not corrected for multiple comparisons, and therefore must be interpreted with extreme caution. Primary limitations are the cross-sectional design precluding causal inference, reliance on self-reported sleep duration subject to recall bias, and lack of correction for multiple comparisons in subgroup analyses, collectively rendering these findings hypothesis-generating rather than conclusive.

Implications Medical Affairs : The absence of a robust association between weekend catch-up sleep and hypertension suggests that chronic sleep debt cannot be effectively compensated through behavioural sleep extension strategies, reinforcing the primacy of pharmacological blood pressure management rather than reliance on lifestyle sleep optimisation alone and supporting Medical Affairs messaging that antihypertensive treatment should not be deferred pending sleep improvement. This NHANES-based evidence could be incorporated into scientific exchange materials to contextualise the limitations of non-pharmacological approaches for hypertension, particularly for physicians who may be inclined to recommend sleep improvement as a first-line strategy in patients with mild or moderate hypertension combined with sleep insufficiency. KOL collaboration with sleep medicine specialists and clinical chronobiologists could yield future prospective studies examining the interplay between sleep chronobiology, nocturnal blood pressure variability, and optimal antihypertensive dosing timing — a research agenda that could differentiate evening-dosed or chronotherapy antihypertensive products.
