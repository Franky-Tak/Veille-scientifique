Blood pressure targets for hypertension in people with diabetes mellitus.

Revue : The Cochrane database of systematic reviews | Tier : NA | IF estime : NA

Date de publication : 2026-09-25

Resultats principaux : This publication is a protocol for a forthcoming Cochrane systematic review examining whether lower blood pressure (BP) targets (i.e., at or below 130/85 mmHg) compared with standard BP targets (140-160/90-100 mmHg) reduce mortality and morbidity in patients with elevated BP and concurrent diabetes mellitus. The protocol outlines the planned methodology for rigorous synthesis of randomized controlled trials in this specific population, registering primary endpoints of all-cause mortality and cardiovascular events alongside secondary safety outcomes. No efficacy results are available at this stage, as this publication describes only the methodological framework and scope of the planned review. The intervention of interest encompasses intensive versus standard antihypertensive treatment strategies in diabetic patients, a population at markedly elevated cardiovascular risk due to combined risk factor burden. As a protocol registration, critical appraisal of results, meta-analytic estimates, and definitive clinical conclusions await the full review completion.

Implications Medical Affairs : The registration of this Cochrane protocol signals high-level international scientific attention to BP target optimization in the large and commercially significant hypertensive-diabetic patient segment, which is directly relevant to antihypertensive product positioning in combined cardiometabolic indications. Once completed, this systematic review will carry substantial influence over ESC/ESH and AHA/ACC guideline revisions, with major downstream implications for regulatory labeling, formulary positioning, and prescribing behavior across major markets. Medical Affairs teams should proactively monitor this protocol's progress, establish relationships with the principal investigators at the University of British Columbia, and engage KOLs in diabetic hypertension management to anticipate and shape the treatment paradigm shift this review may catalyze.

Platelet indices and incident hypertension: effect modification by body mass index in a Chinese cohort.

Revue : Journal of global health | Tier : NA | IF estime : NA

Date de publication : 2026-09-25

Resultats principaux : This prospective cohort study analyzed 51,191 hypertension-free Chinese adults aged 18 years or older from the 2006 baseline of the Kailuan Study, with follow-up through 31 December 2023 (median 8.2 years). The primary outcomes were associations between routine platelet indices—platelet count (PLT), mean platelet volume (MPV), plateletcrit (PCT), and platelet distribution width (PDW)—with incident hypertension, with 29,491 participants developing hypertension at an incidence rate of 70.67 per 1,000 person-years. Weighted Cox regression revealed higher PLT (HR=1.07; 95%CI 1.05-1.09) and PDW (HR=1.03; 95%CI 1.01-1.04) were independently associated with greater hypertension risk, while MPV showed a modest inverse association (HR=0.98; 95%CI 0.96-1.00) and PCT was not significantly associated (HR=0.99; 95%CI 0.98-1.01). Absolute incidence rates increased markedly across Chinese BMI categories from 33.59 to 105.13 per 1,000 person-years, and BMI-stratified analyses revealed heterogeneous patterns particularly for MPV and PDW, suggesting adiposity-dependent modulation of the platelet-hypertension relationship.

Implications Medical Affairs : This large-scale cohort study from China identifies routine platelet indices as modest but statistically significant markers of incident hypertension risk with BMI-dependent heterogeneity, offering potential utility in early-screening algorithms in high-burden Asian populations where hypertension prevalence is substantial. The interaction between adiposity and platelet biomarkers suggests that combined cardiometabolic risk stratification—incorporating both weight status and haematological markers—may strengthen patient selection for preventive antihypertensive interventions, supporting product differentiation in metabolically complex patient profiles. Medical Affairs teams should engage with epidemiology and primary prevention KOLs in Asia-Pacific markets to explore platelet-based biomarker panels as early hypertension risk stratification tools, particularly in integrated cardiovascular prevention programs.

Sociodemographic factors associated with depressive symptoms and self-care agency capacity in hypertensive older adults from Loja, Ecuador: a cross-sectional study.

Revue : Frontiers in public health | Tier : NA | IF estime : NA

Date de publication : 2026-09-10

Resultats principaux : This cross-sectional study enrolled 222 older adults diagnosed with hypertension residing in urban, rural, and urban-marginal areas of Loja, Ecuador, assessing depressive symptoms via the Beck Depression Inventory-II (BDI-II) and self-care capacity via a validated hypertension-specific scale. The majority of participants (69.4%) demonstrated high self-care agency capacity, while 84.2% exhibited minimal depressive symptoms. After adjustment for sociodemographic variables, urban-marginal residence was independently associated with higher self-care capacity scores compared to rural residence, while depressive symptoms were significantly associated with sex, age, and place of residence. Notably, no statistically significant correlation was detected between depressive symptom severity and self-care capacity (Spearman rho=0.111; p=0.100), a finding that challenges the assumption that depression management automatically translates to improved hypertension self-care. Key limitations include the cross-sectional design precluding causal inference, the relatively small single-city sample limiting generalizability, and the absence of direct behavioral adherence data to supplement self-reported self-care.

Implications Medical Affairs : This study provides epidemiological data from Latin America underscoring the role of social determinants—particularly residential context and educational background—in shaping hypertension self-management capacity, directly informing the design of patient support programs for diverse patient populations. The dissociation between depression and self-care capacity highlights that integrated mental health-hypertension management programs must address barriers beyond mood disorder treatment alone, with implications for the framing of patient education and adherence support tools developed by Medical Affairs. Medical Affairs teams should consider these findings when supporting real-world evidence generation on adherence and self-management in Latin American markets, and when engaging nursing and primary care KOLs in program development for under-served hypertensive populations.

Sleep alterations as a risk factor for cardiometabolic health: emerging evidence and clinical implications.

Revue : Giornale italiano di cardiologia (2006) | Tier : NA | IF estime : NA

Date de publication : 2026-10-01

Resultats principaux : This narrative review from Italian internal medicine and cardiology specialists synthesized evidence on the pathophysiological mechanisms linking sleep deprivation to cardiovascular and cardiometabolic disease, focusing on autonomic dysregulation, endothelial dysfunction, systemic inflammatory activation, and neuroendocrine-metabolic alterations. Reduced sleep duration, quality, and regularity were identified as modifiable determinants of hypertension, insulin resistance, visceral obesity, type 2 diabetes, and atherosclerosis. Circadian misalignment emerged as an independent cardiometabolic risk factor beyond sleep duration alone, with relevance to shift workers and populations with irregular social rhythms. Compensatory strategies such as weekend catch-up sleep and daytime napping were assessed as providing only partial protective effects under specific conditions, not fully reversing the cardiometabolic burden of chronic sleep restriction. The authors conclude that sleep duration, quality, continuity, and circadian synchronization should be formally incorporated into clinical cardiovascular risk assessment and prevention protocols, highlighting the need for prospective interventional research on sleep-improvement as a cardiovascular prevention strategy.

Implications Medical Affairs : This review consolidates the mechanistic and epidemiological evidence base positioning sleep health as a critical, actionable, and modifiable cardiometabolic risk factor, providing Medical Affairs teams with a strong scientific rationale for expanding hypertension prevention messaging to include structured sleep assessment. The independent role of circadian misalignment in hypertension risk opens a novel axis for patient stratification relevant to specific occupational populations (e.g., shift workers) and potentially for pipeline compounds targeting chronobiological pathways. Medical Affairs and KOL engagement strategies should explore sleep medicine—cardiology intersections, fostering collaborations with chronobiology experts to position sleep optimization as an emerging pillar of comprehensive cardiovascular prevention alongside pharmacological management.

Endocrine therapy adherence and 10-year cardiometabolic risk in hormone receptor-positive breast cancer.

Revue : JNCI cancer spectrum | Tier : NA | IF estime : NA

Date de publication : 2026-09-24

Resultats principaux : This prospective cohort study of 8,365 postmenopausal women with stage I-III hormone receptor-positive breast cancer within Kaiser Permanente Northern California evaluated the association between adjuvant endocrine therapy (AET) adherence duration and 10-year cardiometabolic outcomes using sequentially doubly robust estimation, adjusting for demographic, clinical, lifestyle, and tumor characteristics. Compared with nonadherence, 5-year AET adherence was associated with significantly higher 10-year cumulative incidence of hypertension (absolute CI difference=8.81%; 95%CI 4.25-13.4%), dyslipidemia (5.16%; 95%CI 0.31-10.0%), and diabetes (2.58%; 95%CI 0.02-5.14%), but also with substantially lower all-cause mortality (10.7%; 95%CI 7.74-13.7%) and breast cancer-specific mortality (5.37%; 95%CI 3.36-7.38%). Aromatase inhibitor (AI) adherence demonstrated greater dyslipidemia risk versus tamoxifen (TAM) (absolute difference 9.86%; 95%CI 5.09-14.6%), with AI adherence associated with hypertension, dyslipidemia, and diabetes, while TAM adherence was associated only with hypertension. The beneficial survival effect of AET adherence, despite elevated cardiometabolic risk, supports the overall risk-benefit balance of treatment completion in early breast cancer. The observational study design limits causal inference, and unmeasured confounders including treatment switching and lifestyle factors may influence results.

Implications Medical Affairs : These findings quantify the differential cardiometabolic burden—particularly incident hypertension—associated with specific AET types in a large real-world breast cancer survivor cohort, providing essential data to inform cardio-oncology monitoring guidelines and antihypertensive therapeutic decision-making in this growing patient population. The distinct hypertension and dyslipidemia risk profiles between aromatase inhibitors and tamoxifen support precision medicine approaches to AET selection, creating strategic Medical Affairs opportunities to position antihypertensive products within structured cardio-oncology care pathways. Medical Affairs teams should prioritize engagement with cardio-oncology KOL networks and advocate for integration of systematic cardiovascular monitoring protocols in early breast cancer treatment programs, where the antihypertensive management market opportunity is significant.

Association between current heated tobacco product use and incident hypertension: A nationwide real-world cohort study.

Revue : Hypertension research : official journal of the Japanese Society of Hypertension | Tier : NA | IF estime : NA

Date de publication : 2026-09-24

Resultats principaux : This real-world cohort study used the Japanese DeSC database—integrating health check-up records, claims data, lifestyle questionnaires, and mHealth application data—to investigate smoking status (including heated tobacco product [HTP] use) and incident hypertension among 30,979 participants (mean age 46.8±9.8 years; 37.0% female) followed between 2021 and 2025. After inverse probability of treatment weighting (IPTW) to balance baseline characteristics, modified Poisson regression demonstrated significantly increased hypertension risk among current HTP users (RR=1.23; 95%CI 1.01-1.50), current cigarette smokers (RR=1.28; 95%CI 1.05-1.57), dual users (RR=1.47; 95%CI 1.08-2.01), and former smokers (RR=1.17; 95%CI 1.05-1.30) relative to never-smokers. Incident hypertension occurred in 5.1% of never-smokers versus 8.2% of HTP users and 9.3% of cigarette smokers, indicating that HTP use confers hypertension risk broadly comparable to conventional cigarette smoking. Subgroup analysis did not identify specific high-risk demographic profiles within HTP users, and dual use (HTP plus cigarettes) showed the highest incident hypertension risk of all groups. Limitations include the observational design precluding causal claims, potential residual confounding despite IPTW adjustment, and follow-up limited to a 4-year period.

Implications Medical Affairs : This nationwide study provides robust real-world evidence that heated tobacco products carry a quantifiable hypertension risk (RR=1.23) comparable to conventional cigarette smoking, directly challenging the perception of HTPs as substantially reduced-harm alternatives and strengthening the scientific rationale for comprehensive tobacco cessation messaging in cardiovascular prevention programs. For Medical Affairs teams managing antihypertensive portfolios, these data highlight a growing population of HTP-associated hypertensive patients requiring treatment, supporting market expansion strategies and HCP education programs that address tobacco type-specific cardiovascular risk in clinical practice. Medical Affairs should collaborate with preventive cardiology and public health KOLs to advocate for inclusion of HTP history in routine cardiovascular risk assessment protocols, and to support regulatory engagement on tobacco-related cardiovascular labeling.

Hypertension prevention attitudes and propensity to achieve a healthy lifestyle in young and middle-aged adults at increased risk: a cross-sectional study.

Revue : Frontiers in public health | Tier : NA | IF estime : NA

Date de publication : 2026-09-10

Resultats principaux : This descriptive cross-sectional study enrolled 351 adults without diagnosed hypertension but with at least one chronic condition associated with increased hypertension risk, using validated online survey instruments between February and August 2025 (mean age 30.73±11.20 years; 67.8% female). The mean score on the Attitudes Scale towards Prevention of Hypertension was 106.37±12.31 (scale range 26-130), indicating generally positive preventive attitudes, while the mean Propensity to Achieve Healthy Lifestyle score was 18.93±4.96, reflecting only moderate readiness for behavioral change. A statistically significant but low-magnitude positive correlation was observed between preventive attitude and lifestyle propensity (r=0.186; p<0.001). Hierarchical multiple regression identified three independent predictors of preventive attitudes, collectively explaining 17.5% of variance: elementary education level (beta=-0.280), no prior hypertension education (beta=-0.175), and higher lifestyle propensity (beta=0.153). Key limitations include the cross-sectional design, internet-based sampling potentially underrepresenting lower-education groups, and the absence of objective behavioral outcome measurement.

Implications Medical Affairs : This study documents a clinically important attitude-readiness gap in hypertension prevention—individuals can hold positive preventive attitudes while demonstrating only moderate behavioral readiness—a discordance that should inform the design of Medical Affairs patient education programs to prioritize action-oriented behavior change strategies over attitude-focused messaging alone. The strong association between structured hypertension education and preventive attitudes supports investment in disease awareness campaigns and HCP-led educational tools as complementary drivers of adherence to preventive antihypertensive management. Medical Affairs teams should partner with primary care, nursing, and health education KOLs to develop evidence-based tools that bridge the attitude-to-behavior gap, particularly targeting younger at-risk populations and those with lower educational attainment, where the prevention opportunity is greatest.
