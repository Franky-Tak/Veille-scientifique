Health-seeking behaviors of pregnant women with hypertensive disorders of pregnancy in low- and middle-income countries: A scoping review protocol.

Revue : PLoS One | Tier : NA | IF estime : NA

Date de publication : 2026-08-27

Resultats principaux : This is a scoping review protocol using JBI methodology, designed to map and synthesize evidence on health-seeking behaviors of pregnant women with hypertensive disorders of pregnancy (HDP) in low- and middle-income countries (LMICs). The population of interest includes pregnant women diagnosed with any form of HDP, encompassing pre-eclampsia, eclampsia, gestational hypertension, and chronic hypertension, as HDP remain a leading cause of maternal and perinatal mortality worldwide. The initial literature search was completed on May 29 and data screening on June 28, 2025, with data extraction ongoing and results expected by December 2025 followed by planned journal submission in January 2026. Hypertension-related complications remain disproportionately high in LMICs, where health-seeking behaviors are influenced by cultural beliefs, systemic inefficiencies, and economic barriers, with no prior scoping or systematic reviews identified on this specific population. Key limitations include the protocol-only nature of the publication with results pending, potential heterogeneity among included studies, and reliance on available published literature that may underrepresent certain LMIC regions.

Implications Medical Affairs : This scoping review protocol fills a critical evidence gap on HDP management in resource-constrained settings, directly relevant to Medical Affairs strategies targeting global health partnerships and expanding market access in LMICs where hypertension burden is rising fastest. Lipoprotein(a) and Hypertension-Mediated Organ Damage in Patients with Essential Hypertension: Associations with Carotid Plaque and Impaired Nocturnal Blood Pressure Dipping.

Revue : Medicina (Kaunas, Lithuania) | Tier : NA | IF estime : NA

Date de publication : 2026-08-14

Resultats principaux : This retrospective cross-sectional study enrolled 80 nondiabetic patients with essential hypertension at a Turkish cardiology center, categorized into elevated Lp(a) (>=50 mg/dL, n=20) versus lower Lp(a) (<50 mg/dL, n=60) groups, with all participants undergoing carotid ultrasonography, echocardiography, 24-hour ambulatory blood pressure monitoring, and ophthalmologic evaluation. Patients with elevated Lp(a) showed a significantly higher prevalence of carotid plaque (55.0% vs. 23.3%, p=0.008) and non-dipper hypertension pattern (75.0% vs. 46.7%, p=0.028) compared to the lower Lp(a) group. Serum Lp(a) concentrations were inversely correlated with the nocturnal dipping percentage (r=-0.362, p=0.001), indicating worse circadian blood pressure regulation with higher Lp(a). In multivariable logistic regression adjusted for age, sex, LDL cholesterol, and smoking, elevated Lp(a) remained independently associated with carotid plaque presence (OR 3.24, 95% CI 1.06-9.80, p=0.039) and with lower nocturnal dipping percentage (beta=-0.294, p=0.008). No significant associations were observed between Lp(a) and carotid intima-media thickness, left ventricular hypertrophy, hypertensive retinopathy, or renal functional parameters, suggesting Lp(a) may be more specifically linked to focal macrovascular atherosclerosis and circadian dysregulation rather than diffuse hypertensive structural remodeling. Key limitations include the small sample size (n=80), single-center cross-sectional design precluding causal inference, and limited generalizability.

Implications Medical Affairs : These findings underscore Lp(a) as a complementary cardiovascular risk biomarker in hypertensive patients, particularly relevant as novel Lp(a)-lowering therapies enter late-stage clinical development, creating opportunities for combination risk stratification narratives that extend beyond LDL-focused paradigms in hypertension management. This study supports Medical Affairs educational initiatives promoting routine Lp(a) measurement and ambulatory blood pressure monitoring as complementary tools for identifying hypertensive patients at highest atherosclerotic and circadian dysregulation risk, consistent with evolving international guideline directions. KOL engagement at the intersection of lipidology and hypertension should highlight these data as evidence for comprehensive cardiovascular risk profiling beyond traditional biomarkers, supporting differentiated positioning of antihypertensive combinations within multi-risk factor management strategies.

Ultra-Processed Food-Rich Dietary Proxies, Dietary-Pattern Context, and Cardiometabolic Outcomes in Spain: Weighted Evidence Consistent with Age Confounding and Possible Reverse-Causality Bias from the 2023 Spanish Health Survey.

Revue : Nutrients | Tier : NA | IF estime : NA

Date de publication : 2026-08-10

Resultats principaux : This cross-sectional study analyzed the 2023 Spanish National Health Survey (n=21,032 adults) to examine associations between dietary patterns and prevalent cardiometabolic conditions, with all diagnoses based on self-reported medical records and dietary data collected via questionnaire. Prevalence of hypertension reached 23.9% in the overall sample, and weekly fast-food consumption was consistently associated with prevalent hypertension (OR 1.18, 95% CI 1.02-1.37), excess weight (OR 1.20, 95% CI 1.07-1.35), obesity (OR 1.28, 95% CI 1.09-1.50), and prevalent diabetes (OR 1.33, 95% CI 1.10-1.61). Implications Medical Affairs : This national-scale study reinforces the epidemiological link between ultra-processed food consumption — particularly fast food — and hypertension prevalence in European populations, providing compelling evidence to support public health messaging and patient education materials aligned with cardiovascular risk reduction programs. Visceral Adiposity Is Independently Associated with Hypertension in Thai Adults: A Case-Control Study.

Revue : International Journal of Environmental Research and Public Health | Tier : NA | IF estime : NA

Date de publication : 2026-08-14

Resultats principaux : This case-control study enrolled 208 Thai adults (103 normotensive controls, 105 hypertensive cases, all aged >=18 years) from a single Thai university hospital, assessing body composition via bioelectrical impedance analysis (BIA) including visceral fat level, fat mass, body fat percentage, and skeletal muscle mass. Hypertensive participants were significantly older (p<0.001) and more likely to be male (p=0.010) and showed greater adiposity across all measured parameters compared to normotensive controls. In the fully adjusted multivariable logistic regression model, older age (OR 1.06, 95% CI 1.04-1.09), male sex (OR 5.35, 95% CI 1.69-16.95), and higher visceral fat level (OR 1.16, 95% CI 1.06-1.26) were independently associated with hypertension, while skeletal muscle mass was not significant (OR 1.03, 95% CI 0.95-1.12, p=0.438). Each one-unit increase in BIA-derived visceral fat level was associated with a 16% increase in the odds of hypertension after full adjustment, and the final model showed acceptable discrimination (AUC 0.79, 95% CI 0.72-0.85). Key limitations include the case-control design, single-center setting, use of BIA rather than gold-standard imaging for visceral fat assessment, and limited generalizability beyond Thai adults.

Implications Medical Affairs : This study provides regional evidence from Southeast Asia that BIA-derived visceral adiposity is the key body composition variable independently associated with hypertension, reinforcing the need for targeted cardiometabolic interventions addressing visceral fat in Asian populations where conventional BMI thresholds may underestimate obesity-related cardiovascular risk. Medical Affairs educational strategies should emphasize the clinical utility of BIA-based visceral fat quantification as a complementary risk stratification tool in hypertension management, particularly in Asian markets where regional body composition norms differ from Western populations. Collaboration with KOLs in Asia-Pacific hypertension and metabolic medicine to incorporate regionally-specific body composition data into cardiovascular risk assessment protocols could support differentiated scientific positioning and regional guideline contributions.

Overview of the Prevalence of Noncommunicable Chronic Diseases in the Brazilian Population Reporting Physical Inactivity.

Revue : International Journal of Environmental Research and Public Health | Tier : NA | IF estime : NA

Date de publication : 2026-07-23

Resultats principaux : This cross-sectional telephone survey included 323,448 Brazilian men and women aged 18-59 years from 26 state capitals and the Federal District, classifying participants as physically inactive (PI) if they reported no physical activity across home, occupational, commuting, or leisure domains. Physical inactivity was significantly associated (p<0.05) with a 16% higher prevalence of obesity in middle-aged women and 28% in middle-aged men. PI was associated with a 49-273% higher prevalence of combined diabetes and hypertension, 60-113% higher for combined diabetes and obesity, and 20-71% higher for combined hypertension and obesity across age and sex subgroups. Most strikingly, physical inactivity was associated with a 73-106% higher prevalence of the simultaneous coexistence of all three conditions (diabetes, hypertension, and obesity). The population-level design allowed detection of robust associations across demographic subgroups, with sex-stratified analyses revealing differential magnitude of effect. Key limitations include the entirely self-reported nature of all variables, cross-sectional design precluding causality, potential recall bias, and possible exclusion of certain socioeconomic strata through telephone survey methodology.

Implications Medical Affairs : The dramatically elevated multimorbidity prevalence associated with physical inactivity in this large Brazilian national sample provides compelling epidemiological justification for integrating structured exercise counseling into antihypertensive treatment pathways, particularly in Latin American markets where cardiometabolic multimorbidity burden is rapidly escalating. These findings support Medical Affairs educational programs emphasizing physical activity as a critical modifiable risk factor in multimorbid hypertensive patients, providing content for high-impact healthcare provider education materials addressing diabetes-hypertension-obesity comorbidity clusters. Date de publication : 2026-08

Resultats principaux : This retrospective single-center study analyzed 416 patients presenting to a Japanese dental out-of-hours clinic with bleeding conditions, categorized as post-extraction bleeding (PEB, n=216), periodontal disease-related gingival bleeding (PDB, n=119), or other bleeding (n=81), with patients further stratified by age into young (20-39 years), middle (40-59 years), and older (>=60 years) groups. Implications Medical Affairs : This real-world study reveals an underappreciated clinical intersection of uncontrolled hypertension, antithrombotic therapy, and acute oral bleeding in older patients, supporting the development of cross-specialty educational initiatives that highlight systemic cardiovascular consequences of inadequately controlled blood pressure in unexpected clinical settings such as dental emergencies. The Effect of Using Hypotension Prediction Index to Reduce Intraoperative Hypotension in Elective Cesarean Sections: A Randomized Controlled Trial.

Revue : Medicina (Kaunas, Lithuania) | Tier : NA | IF estime : NA

Date de publication : 2026-08-01

Resultats principaux : This single-center randomized controlled trial enrolled 100 parturients (96 analyzed) undergoing elective cesarean sections under spinal anesthesia in Malaysia, randomized to HPI-guided (finger cuff non-invasive continuous monitoring) versus standard non-invasive blood pressure (NIBP) monitoring, with baseline demographic characteristics similar between groups. The primary outcome, time-weighted average mean arterial pressure below 65 mmHg (TWA-MAP <65 mmHg), was significantly lower in the HPI group versus control (median 0.24 [IQR 0-0.70] vs. 1.67 [0.71-2.57] mmHg, p<0.001), demonstrating a clinically meaningful reduction in hypotension burden. The incidence of both intraoperative and postoperative nausea and vomiting was significantly reduced in the HPI group (p<0.01 for both), a clinically important secondary benefit for obstetric patients. No significant differences were observed between groups for intraoperative hypertension incidence, bradycardia, fetal Apgar scores, umbilical cord pH, or length of NICU stay (all p>0.05), suggesting neonatal safety was maintained. Key limitations include the single-center design in a Malaysian hospital limiting generalizability, relatively small sample size (n=96 analyzed), and focus on elective rather than emergency cesarean sections which represent a different hemodynamic risk profile.

Implications Medical Affairs : This RCT demonstrates the clinical utility of predictive hemodynamic monitoring in obstetric settings, providing evidence that algorithmic proactive management can significantly reduce intraoperative hypotension burden without increasing maternal hypertensive events — directly relevant to anesthesiology guideline discussions and the positioning of advanced monitoring technologies in high-risk obstetric care. From a Medical Affairs perspective, this study reinforces the importance of distinguishing between iatrogenic hypotension (spinal anesthesia-induced) and hypertensive disease management in obstetric populations, an educational distinction critical for clinicians managing women with pregnancy-related hypertensive disorders during perioperative care. Collaborating with KOLs in obstetric anesthesiology and maternal-fetal medicine on the clinical application of HPI monitoring in hypertensive obstetric patients could strengthen academic positioning and support educational content around precision perioperative blood pressure management.

Endothelial function in relation to low-level chronic residential air pollution in a general population: a cohort study.

Revue : Blood Pressure | Tier : 3 | IF estime : 3-10

Date de publication : 2026-08-27

Resultats principaux : This prospective cohort study assessed endothelial function via finger photoplethysmography after ischemic provocation in 453 Flemish participants (47.7% women, mean age 52.8 years), measuring the maximal ischemic-to-control ratio (Rmax) and maximal amplitude difference (Dmax) in relation to chronic residential exposure to PM10, PM2.5, NO2, and black carbon (BC), with air pollutant levels estimated by high-resolution spatiotemporal interpolation. From 2010 to 2015, annual average concentrations declined significantly: PM10 from higher levels to 15.9 µg/m3, PM2.5 to 12.8 µg/m3, NO2 to 14.3 µg/m3, and BC to 1.04 µg/m3. Dmax was inversely correlated with PM2.5, with consistent inverse associations for other pollutants, and the strength of these associations weakened over 6 years, paralleling declining pollutant concentrations (p<=0.044). In adjusted proportional hazards analyses, higher Rmax and Dmax were associated with significantly reduced cardiovascular event risk (HR 0.27-0.49, p<=0.043), and geographical analyses confirmed that endothelial dysfunction followed PM2.5 spatial gradients across residential areas. Key limitations include potential residual confounding by unmeasured lifestyle factors, measurement error in residential air pollution estimates, and the observational cohort design precluding definitive causal inference.

Implications Medical Affairs : This study provides important mechanistic evidence that long-term low-level air pollution — even below current regulatory standards — induces subclinical endothelial dysfunction and increases cardiovascular event risk, directly linking environmental exposure to the early pathophysiology of hypertension and reinforcing the case for protective vascular interventions in polluted environments. From a Medical Affairs perspective, these findings support the development of environmental exposure-based cardiovascular risk stratification tools and highlight an unmet need for therapeutic interventions with demonstrated vascular protective mechanisms in populations living in high air pollution environments, potentially relevant to the positioning of vasculo-protective antihypertensive agents. Engaging with KOLs in environmental cardiology and public health to integrate air pollution exposure history into hypertension risk assessments could provide differentiated scientific positioning and create opportunities for novel research collaborations in environmental cardiovascular medicine.

Revue : Diabetes, Obesity & Metabolism | Tier : NA | IF estime : NA

Date de publication : 2026-08-26

Resultats principaux : This secondary analysis of the China Diabetes Prevention Program (CDPP, NCT03441750) — a multicenter unblinded RCT — included 1,678 participants aged 18-70 years with impaired glucose regulation from 43 hospitals across China, randomized 1:1 to metformin plus lifestyle intervention versus lifestyle intervention alone, with stratification by glucose status (impaired fasting glucose vs. impaired glucose tolerance), hypertension status, and antihypertensive medication use. Implications Medical Affairs : This large Chinese RCT confirms that combining metformin with lifestyle intervention meaningfully improves prediabetes remission rates, with hypertension explicitly incorporated as a stratification variable, reinforcing the interconnected cardiometabolic risk reduction narrative that is central to antihypertensive therapy positioning in patients with multimorbidity. From a Medical Affairs standpoint, these findings support educational frameworks that position hypertension management within integrated cardiometabolic risk reduction strategies, emphasizing that lifestyle and pharmacological interventions targeting glucose metabolism can also reduce downstream cardiovascular risk including hypertension-related complications. Vitamin D and Metabolic Syndrome: Molecular Mechanisms and Clinical Implications: A Narrative Review.

Revue : International Journal of Molecular Sciences | Tier : NA | IF estime : NA

Date de publication : 2026-08-07

Resultats principaux : This narrative review synthesizes evidence on the molecular mechanisms through which vitamin D may influence metabolic syndrome (MetS), a multisystem disorder encompassing insulin resistance, central obesity, dyslipidemia, hypertension, and chronic low-grade inflammation, all of which substantially increase type 2 diabetes and cardiovascular disease risk. Preclinical and experimental evidence indicates that vitamin D, through activation of the vitamin D receptor (VDR), modulates key signaling pathways including AMPK, mTOR, NF-kB, and PPAR-gamma, thereby influencing insulin sensitivity, inflammation, oxidative stress, mitochondrial function, and metabolic homeostasis. Clinical evidence on vitamin D supplementation in MetS remains heterogeneous, attributed to differing 25-hydroxyvitamin D thresholds for defining deficiency, variable supplementation regimens, diverse study populations, and methodological design differences that limit direct comparability. The review identifies no single universal threshold for vitamin D sufficiency applicable across all metabolic outcomes and concludes that vitamin D should be considered an adjunct to lifestyle-based interventions rather than a standalone therapeutic approach. Key limitations include the narrative review design with potential for inclusion bias, predominance of preclinical mechanistic evidence for specific pathways, and limited high-quality RCT evidence directly testing vitamin D on individual MetS components including hypertension as a primary endpoint.

Implications Medical Affairs : This review reinforces vitamin D's pleiotropic role in metabolic syndrome pathophysiology, including hypertension, providing a mechanistic rationale for considering vitamin D status assessment as part of comprehensive cardiometabolic risk profiling in hypertensive patients with metabolic comorbidities. Medical Affairs educational programs could leverage these mechanistic insights to frame hypertension management within the broader cardiometabolic syndrome paradigm, positioning vitamin D as a potential adjunct biomarker to guide supplementation in deficient hypertensive patients rather than as a standalone antihypertensive intervention. Biomass Cooking Fuel Use and Screen-Detected Hypertension Among Peruvian Women Without Known Hypertension: A National Cross-Sectional Study.

Revue : Life (Basel, Switzerland) | Tier : NA | IF estime : NA

Date de publication : 2026-08-06

Resultats principaux : This analytical cross-sectional study used pooled DHS 2014-2024 microdata from Peru, including 301,575 nonpregnant women aged 15-49 without known hypertension or current antihypertensive treatment, using Poisson regression with robust variance to estimate prevalence ratios (PRs) of screen-detected hypertension (SBP >=140 or DBP >=90 mmHg) by cooking fuel type. The weighted prevalence of screen-detected hypertension was 8.5% among clean-fuel users and 6.4% among biomass/solid-fuel users. The crude association was inverse (crude PR 0.75, 95% CI 0.71-0.79), but reversed after adjustment for age, altitude, residence area, region, education, wealth quintile, and survey year to a positive association (adjusted PR 1.20, 95% CI 1.10-1.31), indicating that the crude inverse finding was explained by confounding. Wood fuel showed the strongest adjusted association (PR 1.25, 95% CI 1.14-1.36), while coal/charcoal showed a null estimate. The urban-rural interaction was statistically significant (p=0.005), with stronger associations in urban areas (adjusted PR 1.28) versus rural areas (adjusted PR 1.09). Key limitations include the cross-sectional design preventing causal inference, single-visit blood pressure measurements, potential residual confounding by unmeasured variables, and restriction to reproductive-age women.

Implications Medical Affairs : This large nationally representative Peruvian study provides strong epidemiological evidence that household biomass combustion is independently associated with a 20% higher prevalence of undiagnosed hypertension in women, identifying indoor air pollution as a significant but underrecognized modifiable cardiovascular risk factor in Latin American resource-limited settings. From a Medical Affairs perspective, these findings support incorporating environmental exposure assessment — including cooking fuel type — into hypertension screening guidelines for primary care providers in LMIC settings, creating opportunities to expand cardiovascular prevention messaging beyond traditional risk factors to include household environmental exposures. Collaborating with KOLs in environmental health and cardiovascular prevention in Latin America around biomass-related cardiovascular risk could build scientific presence in this rapidly growing market while supporting evidence-based advocacy for clean fuel transitions as a hypertension prevention strategy aligned with global sustainability and health equity narratives.

Thromboxane A2-Driven Vascular Hyperreactivity in Cadmium-Induced Hypertension: Role of Oxidative Stress and Therapeutic Implications.

Revue : Journal of Clinical Medicine | Tier : NA | IF estime : NA

Date de publication : 2026-08-13

Resultats principaux : This experimental preclinical study investigated the role of thromboxane A2 (TXA2) in vascular hyperreactivity in a rat model of cadmium-induced hypertension (HICAD), focusing on changes in oxidative stress markers and vasoconstrictor responses in isolated aortic rings. Aortas from HICAD rats showed a 2-4-fold increase in malondialdehyde (MDA) and 4-hydroxyalkenals (4-HAs) compared to controls, alongside approximately 50% reduction in glutathione reductase (GR) activity, indicating significant oxidative stress and impaired antioxidant defense. The vasoconstrictor response to U46619 (a stable TXA2 analogue) was significantly increased by 35% in HICAD aortas compared to normotensive controls, demonstrating enhanced vascular TXA2 receptor sensitivity. When HICAD aortas were incubated with apocynin (an NADPH oxidase inhibitor), the vasoconstrictor response showed no significant changes (~2%), directly implicating NADPH oxidase-derived reactive oxygen species in mediating the TXA2-driven vascular hyperreactivity. This study identifies a novel mechanistic pathway: environmental cadmium exposure leads to NADPH oxidase-mediated oxidative stress that sensitizes vascular TXA2 receptors, contributing to vascular hyperreactivity and hypertension. Key limitations include the animal model setting that may not directly translate to human hypertension physiology, absence of in vivo antihypertensive intervention experiments, and the single-laboratory preclinical design.

Implications Medical Affairs : This preclinical study reveals a novel mechanistic pathway linking environmental cadmium exposure to vascular TXA2 hyperreactivity via NADPH oxidase-mediated oxidative stress, providing scientific rationale for targeting oxidative stress pathways in environmentally-induced hypertension — an emerging subtype of treatment-resistant hypertension of particular interest in industrially exposed populations. Medical Affairs educational programs could leverage these mechanistic insights to broaden physicians' understanding of hypertension etiology beyond traditional genetic and lifestyle factors, positioning environmental heavy metal exposure as a clinically relevant driver of vascular pathology requiring specific therapeutic considerations. Future translational KOL collaborations in toxicology, vascular biology, and hypertension medicine should explore whether TXA2 receptor antagonists or antioxidant adjuncts provide added blood pressure benefit in cadmium-exposed patient populations, potentially identifying a precision medicine opportunity for differentiated antihypertensive research strategy.

Perirenal Adipose Tissue in Cardiovascular Disease: From Molecular Insights to Therapeutic Perspectives.

Revue : Biomedicines | Tier : NA | IF estime : NA

Date de publication : 2026-08-11

Resultats principaux : This narrative review examines the emerging role of perirenal adipose tissue (PRAT) as a clinically relevant endocrine organ in hypertension and cardiovascular disease pathophysiology, focusing on its unique anatomical proximity to the kidneys and consequent effects on intrarenal haemodynamics and blood pressure. PRAT expansion drives renin-angiotensin-aldosterone system (RAAS) activation through altered hydrostatic pressure and adipokine dysregulation, and overexpression of miRNAs (miR-24-3p, miR-155, miR-146a, miR-21) in PRAT modulates inflammation and oxidative stress, contributing to endothelial dysfunction and atherosclerosis. Therapeutic strategies targeting PRAT include lifestyle modifications, RAAS inhibitors, antidiabetic agents, adipose tissue browning agents, NLRP3 inflammasome inhibitors, PPARgamma agonists, and surgical approaches, with emerging strategies including senotherapeutics, ADMSC bioengineering, gut microbiota modulation, and colchicine. The review highlights PRAT as a metabolically active fat depot that extends beyond energy storage and supports an individualized therapeutic targeting framework. Key limitations include the narrative review design with potential bias, predominance of preclinical mechanistic data for novel therapeutic targets, and limited human intervention studies directly targeting PRAT.

Implications Medical Affairs : This review positions PRAT as an emerging pathophysiological hub linking obesity, RAAS activation, and hypertension, directly reinforcing the mechanistic rationale for RAAS inhibitors in obese hypertensive patients and providing rich educational content for Medical Affairs programs addressing the obesity-hypertension interface. Medical Affairs teams can leverage the multi-modal therapeutic approaches targeting PRAT — including RAAS inhibitors and novel agents — to develop integrated treatment narrative materials that position antihypertensive agents within an evolving understanding of adipose tissue biology, obesity medicine, and cardiovascular risk. Resultats principaux : This narrative review examines the safety and clinical utility of fruits, vegetables, and legumes in adults with hypertensive chronic kidney disease (CKD), particularly nondialysis CKD G3-G5 where hypertension affects approximately 80-85% of patients, focusing on the gut-kidney axis including gut microbiota, short-chain fatty acids (SCFAs), gut-derived uremic toxins, potassium bioavailability, and modifiable clinical risk factors.
