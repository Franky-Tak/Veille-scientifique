The longitudinal care cascade for hypertension in people with and without HIV in South Africa: A clinic-based observational study.

Revue : PLoS medicine | Tier : NA | IF estime : NA

Date de publication : 2026-09-11

Resultats principaux : This longitudinal secondary analysis from the iHEART-SA trial included 23,855 adults attending nine public primary healthcare clinics in Johannesburg, South Africa, between August 2022 and May 2024, with follow-up through May 2025. Among participants, 78.5% were people living with HIV (PLHIV) with a median age of 42 years and 69% female, representing a real-world dual-burden chronic disease population. PLHIV were 3.40 times more likely to remain undiagnosed for hypertension compared to HIV-negative individuals (aRR 3.40; 95% CI 2.45-4.73; p<0.001), indicating a major diagnostic gap in HIV programmes. Among those receiving antihypertensive treatment, PLHIV were significantly less likely to achieve blood pressure control (aRR 0.85; 95% CI 0.75-0.97; p=0.012) and exhibited higher rates of cascade regression, including loss of BP control (aRR 1.10; 95% CI 1.02-1.18; p=0.010). Limitations include reliance on routinely collected clinical data, variable follow-up duration, and treatment ascertainment based solely on documented medication records rather than verified dispensing.

Implications Medical Affairs : These findings underscore a critical unmet need for integrated chronic disease management pathways that simultaneously address HIV and hypertension, with important implications for guideline recommendations around dual-disease management in sub-Saharan Africa. From a Medical Affairs perspective, this creates a positioning opportunity for antihypertensive agents with favorable drug interaction profiles with antiretroviral therapies, particularly in high HIV-burden markets across Africa. KOL engagement should target infectious disease specialists, primary care physicians, and global health leaders to elevate awareness of hypertension underdiagnosis in HIV-positive populations and advocate for integrated care models.

Screen-detected raised blood pressure and gaps in the hypertension care cascade among adults attending a humanitarian medical mission in Ukraine: A cross-sectional study.

Revue : PloS one | Tier : NA | IF estime : NA

Date de publication : 2026-09-11

Resultats principaux : This cross-sectional study enrolled 364 adults (mean age 52.5 years; 63.2% women) attending a MacKay Memorial Hospital humanitarian medical mission across three Ukrainian sites in April 2026, during the ongoing armed conflict. Blood pressure was measured once, with raised BP defined as a single reading at or above 140/90 mmHg or a prior diagnosis of hypertension. The crude prevalence of raised blood pressure was 65.7% (95% CI 60.5-70.5), falling to 54.7% (95% CI 48.1-61.3) after WHO world population age-standardization. Within the hypertension care cascade, 74.6% of those with raised BP were aware, 58.8% were receiving treatment, but only 15.8% had controlled blood pressure, revealing a severe control gap. Key independent predictors of raised blood pressure included older age (adjusted prevalence ratio 1.19 per 10 years; 95% CI 1.11-1.27), male sex (1.26; 95% CI 1.08-1.47), and obesity (1.24; 95% CI 1.09-1.42); however, single-reading BP measurement and a self-selected sample preclude population-level inferences.

Implications Medical Affairs : The critically low hypertension control rate of 15.8% in a conflict setting demonstrates the severe disruption to long-term cardiovascular care under humanitarian conditions, with direct implications for essential medicine access policies and emergency hypertension management guidelines. From a Medical Affairs perspective, this data supports advocacy efforts for inclusion of antihypertensive medications in humanitarian essential medicine kits and simplified treatment protocols, representing both a public health priority and a market access opportunity in post-conflict reconstruction contexts. KOL collaboration with humanitarian medicine organizations and global health NGOs could amplify the evidence base for streamlined hypertension treatment pathways in resource-constrained environments.

Clinical inertia in cardiovascular risk management: prevalence, associated factors, and impact on outcomes of the OPM study.

Revue : Journal of global health | Tier : NA | IF estime : NA

Date de publication : 2026-09-11

Resultats principaux : This pre-specified secondary analysis of the multicentre prospective OPM study was conducted across nine Spanish regions from 2024 to 2025 and included 711 high-risk primary care patients with at least one uncontrolled cardiovascular risk (CVR) factor at baseline (hypertension, diabetes, or dyslipidemia). Clinical inertia, defined as failure to initiate or intensify pharmacological therapy in adherent patients with uncontrolled risk factors, was observed in 49.3% of patients with uncontrolled hypertension, 31.1% for diabetes, and 38.2% for dyslipidemia; overall, 54.3% of all participants exhibited clinical inertia for at least one condition. Clinical inertia significantly increased the risk of persistent uncontrolled CVR factor status (relative risk 1.13; 95% CI 1.03-1.24; absolute risk difference 9.3%; p=0.007). At 90-day follow-up, patients with clinical inertia showed significantly smaller reductions in total cholesterol (between-group difference 19.3 mg/dL; p<0.001) and LDL cholesterol (15.5 mg/dL; p<0.001) compared to those who received treatment intensification. Notably, no statistically significant patient-level demographic or clinical predictors of clinical inertia were identified, suggesting provider- and system-level drivers predominate; limitations include the quasi-experimental design and short 90-day follow-up.

Implications Medical Affairs : The finding that clinical inertia affects more than half of high-risk primary care patients and is driven primarily by provider and system factors rather than patient characteristics represents a major actionable gap in guideline implementation across European primary care settings. From a Medical Affairs perspective, this data supports development of clinician-facing educational programs, clinical decision support tools, and fixed-dose combination products that reduce therapeutic barriers and facilitate proactive treatment intensification. A KOL strategy targeting primary care physicians and internal medicine specialists in Spain and broader European markets should leverage these data to advocate for simplified, algorithm-driven hypertension management protocols.

Alcohol Consumption Index as a Novel Risk Marker for Hypertension.

Revue : Neuropsychopharmacology reports | Tier : NA | IF estime : NA

Date de publication : 2026-09

Resultats principaux : This retrospective cohort study enrolled 6,655 male workers (median age 51 years) from a Japanese occupational cohort and assessed the relationship between the Alcohol Consumption Index (ACI, defined as the product of mean corpuscular volume, gamma-glutamyl transferase, and HDL cholesterol) and incident hypertension at baseline and 5-year follow-up. Regular drinkers showed significantly elevated odds of incident hypertension compared to non-drinkers (OR 1.71; 95% CI 1.42-2.06), and heavy drinkers showed even greater risk (OR 1.95; 95% CI 1.60-2.37). Participants in the highest ACI tertile had significantly higher odds of incident hypertension compared to the lowest tertile (OR 1.81; 95% CI 1.51-2.16), which remained significant after adjustment for baseline blood pressure. Participants who transitioned from regular or heavy drinking to abstinence over the follow-up period showed significant reductions in both systolic and diastolic blood pressure. Limitations include the all-male occupational cohort design limiting generalizability to women and non-occupational populations, retrospective design, and potential residual confounding from unmeasured dietary and physical activity variables.

Implications Medical Affairs : The validation of ACI as a composite biomarker integrating hematological and metabolic markers of alcohol exposure provides a practical and scalable screening tool for primary care and occupational medicine settings, potentially informing guideline recommendations for alcohol-related hypertension risk stratification. From a Medical Affairs perspective, these findings reinforce the importance of lifestyle counseling as a complementary component of antihypertensive treatment strategies, and could support risk-stratified messaging for antihypertensive products in high-risk occupational populations across Japan and Asia. Partnerships with occupational health KOLs and workplace health organizations could leverage these findings to embed antihypertensive management within comprehensive corporate cardiovascular health programs.

Blood Pressure Mediates the Association Between Carotid-Femoral Pulse Wave Velocity and Basal Ganglia Enlarged Perivascular Spaces: A Community-Based Cross-Sectional Study.

Revue : Journal of clinical hypertension (Greenwich, Conn.) | Tier : 3 | IF estime : ~4.5

Date de publication : 2026-09

Resultats principaux : This community-based cross-sectional study included 485 participants from the Kailuan cohort in China and assessed the association between carotid-femoral pulse wave velocity (cf-PWV), a non-invasive gold standard for aortic stiffness, and basal ganglia enlarged perivascular spaces (BG-EPVS) graded on 3.0-Tesla MRI, with mediation analysis quantifying the role of blood pressure. BG-EPVS were identified in 59.4% of participants, and cf-PWV at or above 10 m/s was independently associated with BG-EPVS after cardiovascular risk factor adjustment (OR 2.06; 95% CI 1.27-3.36), with a significant linear dose-response relationship (p for non-linearity=0.668). Mediation analysis revealed that systolic blood pressure (SBP) and pulse pressure (PP) each mediated approximately 43% of the total cf-PWV effect on BG-EPVS, while diastolic blood pressure was not a significant mediator. Subgroup analyses showed that the association was stronger among participants aged 70 years and younger and among males. Limitations include the cross-sectional design precluding causal inference, the Kailuan cohort sample limiting generalizability, and single time-point BP measurements that may not capture variability.

Implications Medical Affairs : The demonstration that blood pressure, particularly pulsatile components (SBP and PP), mediates approximately 43% of the association between arterial stiffness and cerebral small vessel disease reinforces the clinical rationale for stringent BP control in the prevention of hypertension-related neurological complications, with potential guideline implications for BP target thresholds in neurological risk stratification. From a Medical Affairs perspective, these findings support positioning of antihypertensive agents that effectively reduce arterial stiffness and pulse pressure, such as calcium channel blockers and RAS inhibitors, as preferentially beneficial for cerebrovascular protection beyond BP lowering per se. Strategic KOL engagement with neurologists, geriatric specialists, and hypertension experts could expand the therapeutic narrative around antihypertensive products to encompass cognitive preservation and cerebral small vessel disease prevention.

Efficacy and Safety of a Single-Pill Triple Combination of Valsartan, Amlodipine, and Chlorthalidone in Patients With Essential Hypertension Inadequately Controlled on Dual Therapy With Valsartan and Amlodipine: A Randomized, Double-Blind, Multicenter, Phase 3 Trial.

Revue : Journal of clinical hypertension (Greenwich, Conn.) | Tier : 3 | IF estime : ~4.5

Date de publication : 2026-09

Resultats principaux : This randomized, double-blind, multicenter Phase 3 trial conducted in South Korea enrolled 193 patients with essential hypertension inadequately controlled after 4 weeks on dual valsartan/amlodipine (Val/Aml) 80/5 mg therapy, randomizing them 1:1 to single-pill triple combination (Val/Aml/chlorthalidone; Val/Aml/CTD) or continued dual therapy for 8 weeks total. The primary endpoint was change in mean sitting systolic blood pressure (MSSBP) from baseline to week 8; the Val/Aml/CTD group achieved a significantly greater MSSBP reduction of -19.70 mmHg versus -8.71 mmHg in the control group (between-group difference -10.99 mmHg; 95% CI -14.56 to -7.41; p<0.0001). The triple combination also demonstrated a favorable safety profile with no serious adverse events in the Val/Aml/CTD group versus a 1.98% incidence in the control group, and 178 of 193 patients (92.2%) completed the study. The dose was escalated at week 2 from 80/5/12.5 mg to 160/5/25 mg in the triple combination arm, and a corresponding escalation occurred in the dual-therapy arm. Limitations include the short 8-week treatment period, enrollment restricted to South Korea limiting racial/ethnic generalizability, and a double-dummy design that, while ensuring blinding, introduced additional pill burden during the study.

Implications Medical Affairs : This Phase 3 RCT provides high-quality evidence that single-pill triple combination therapy (Val/Aml/CTD) delivers a clinically meaningful additional 11 mmHg systolic BP reduction compared to dual therapy with superior safety, directly supporting guideline recommendations for step-up triple combination strategies in inadequately controlled hypertension. From a Medical Affairs perspective, these data constitute a strong foundation for positioning single-pill triple combinations as a preferred option over polypharmacy in patients failing dual therapy, with expected adherence and clinical inertia benefits that translate into improved cardiovascular outcomes. KOL engagement strategies targeting cardiologists, hypertension specialists, and internists in South Korea and broader Asian markets should prioritize disseminating these findings through peer-reviewed publications, congresses, and advisory boards.

Multiple blood pressure parameters and risk of incident diabetes in adults with elevated blood pressure: a cohort study with non-linear analyses.

Revue : Frontiers in endocrinology | Tier : NA | IF estime : NA

Date de publication : 2026-08-27

Resultats principaux : This retrospective cohort study included 29,377 Chinese adults with elevated blood pressure drawn from a large national health examination database, followed for a median of 2.98 years to assess the association between multiple blood pressure parameters and incident diabetes. Among the parameters evaluated, SBP (HR 1.09; 95% CI 1.05-1.13), MAP (HR 1.07; 95% CI 1.02-1.13), PP (HR 1.06; 95% CI 1.03-1.10), PPI (HR 2.47; 95% CI 1.36-4.46), and SBP/DBP ratio (HR 1.38; 95% CI 1.13-1.70) were all significantly associated with incident diabetes, while DBP alone was not significant. Restricted cubic spline analyses revealed significant non-linear dose-response relationships for SBP, DBP, MAP, and SBP/DBP ratio with incident diabetes, indicating complex threshold effects. Among all BP parameters, SBP demonstrated the highest overall predictive performance for incident diabetes. Limitations include the retrospective design with potential residual confounding, reliance on a single Chinese health examination database, a relatively short median follow-up of approximately 3 years, and the Chinese-only cohort composition limiting generalizability.

Implications Medical Affairs : The demonstration that multiple blood pressure parameters, particularly SBP and pulse pressure index, independently predict incident diabetes in hypertensive individuals reinforces the value of comprehensive BP profiling beyond SBP alone in integrated cardiometabolic risk stratification, with potential to inform updated diabetes prevention guidelines in hypertensive populations. From a Medical Affairs perspective, these findings support co-positioning antihypertensive therapies within a broader cardiometabolic risk reduction framework, with particular emphasis on agents demonstrating metabolic neutrality or benefit in patients at elevated diabetes risk. KOL engagement should be broadened to include endocrinologists and diabetologists, positioning antihypertensive products as tools for integrated cardiometabolic risk management beyond blood pressure control.

A composite tool for predicting gestational diabetes or hypertensive disorders of pregnancy: development and temporal validation.

Revue : Computers in biology and medicine | Tier : NA | IF estime : NA

Date de publication : 2026-09-11

Resultats principaux : This study developed and temporally validated a composite logistic regression-based prognostic model for gestational diabetes mellitus (GDM) and/or hypertensive disorders of pregnancy (HDP) using routinely collected health data from women delivering singleton pregnancies in Australia from 2016 to 2022, with a combined GDM/HDP prevalence of 25.4% (n=48,502). The model incorporated first-trimester sociodemographic and clinical variables including age, ethnicity, BMI, parity, and medical history (pre-existing hypertension, GDM, pre-eclampsia, polycystic ovary syndrome, chronic kidney disease, fetal macrosomia, and family history of diabetes). On temporal validation (n=12,868), the model achieved a C-statistic of 0.739 (95% CI 0.727-0.738), with excellent post-recalibration calibration (calibration-in-the-large -0.001; calibration slope 1.00; 95% CI 0.95-1.05). Decision curve analysis demonstrated net clinical benefit across threshold probabilities from 8 to 72%, consistently outperforming both treat-all and treat-none strategies. Limitations include the single-country Australian database, absence of first-trimester biomarker or ultrasound data, and the need for external validation across diverse multi-ethnic populations.

Implications Medical Affairs : The development of a temporally validated composite prediction tool for first-trimester GDM and HDP risk stratification represents a meaningful advance toward early identification of high-risk pregnancies, with potential to inform updated obstetric and hypertension guidelines on systematic cardiometabolic screening in pregnancy. From a Medical Affairs perspective, this reinforces the growing need for antihypertensive agents with robust pregnancy safety profiles, creating positioning opportunities for products with established data in pregnant and postpartum populations. Engagement with obstetric KOLs, maternal-fetal medicine specialists, and hypertension guideline committees is strategically important to advance risk-stratified prenatal care pathways that integrate safe antihypertensive management.

Blood pressure and cardiovascular risk in adults with non-proteinuric chronic kidney disease without diabetes.

Revue : Hypertension research : official journal of the Japanese Society of Hypertension | Tier : NA | IF estime : NA

Date de publication : 2026-09-10

Resultats principaux : This large-scale nationwide retrospective study utilized Japanese administrative claims and health checkup data from 287,238 non-diabetic adults with CKD (eGFR <60 mL/min/1.73m2) and negative or trace proteinuria to examine the relationship between blood pressure levels and composite cardiovascular outcomes (myocardial infarction, stroke, heart failure, and atrial fibrillation). Higher SBP was independently associated with increased CVD risk in a graded fashion across BP categories, with HRs of 1.03 (95% CI 1.00-1.07) for elevated BP, 1.08 (95% CI 1.05-1.11) for stage 1 hypertension, and 1.20 (95% CI 1.17-1.24) for stage 2 hypertension compared to normal BP. Restricted cubic spline analysis identified an inflection point at approximately 130 mmHg SBP in the overall cohort, consistent with current guideline thresholds. However, among treated patients, a U-shaped association was found with increased CVD risk also observed at SBP levels below approximately 130 mmHg, suggesting potential harm from overly aggressive BP lowering in this subgroup. Limitations include the retrospective administrative database design with potential outcome misclassification, inability to account for CKD etiology and degree of proteinuria, and the Japanese population limiting broad generalizability.

Implications Medical Affairs : The identification of a U-shaped relationship between treated blood pressure and CVD risk in non-proteinuric CKD patients challenges a simple treat-to-lower approach and may prompt reconsideration of optimal BP targets in current nephrology and cardiology guidelines for this specific population. From a Medical Affairs perspective, these findings underscore the importance of careful individualization of antihypertensive therapy in CKD, potentially supporting positioning of agents with well-characterized hemodynamic safety profiles at lower BP ranges and established renoprotective benefits. Strategic KOL collaboration with nephrologists and cardiologists specializing in CKD-related hypertension is essential to translate these real-world data into nuanced treatment frameworks that balance cardiovascular and renal protection.

Impact of a Residential Yoga and Naturopathy-based Lifestyle Modification on Cardio-Metabolic Risk Factors in Patients with Hypertension: A Retrospective Study.

Revue : Advances in mind-body medicine | Tier : NA | IF estime : NA

Date de publication : 2026-09-01

Resultats principaux : This retrospective study analyzed data from 2,102 hypertensive inpatients at a yoga and naturopathy hospital in India admitted between August 2019 and October 2022, with a mean residential stay of 13.81 +/- 8.36 days undergoing integrated yoga, naturopathy, and dietary interventions. The program resulted in significant reductions in systolic blood pressure, diastolic blood pressure, pulse rate, pulse pressure, mean arterial pressure, and rate pressure product at post-test assessment compared to baseline. Additional significant improvements were observed in total cholesterol, fasting blood glucose, perceived stress scale scores, body weight, BMI, and multiple anthropometric measures including hip circumference, waist circumference, and mid-arm and mid-thigh circumferences. These broad improvements across cardiovascular, metabolic, and psychosocial domains highlight the multifactorial impact of integrated lifestyle modification. Limitations include the retrospective uncontrolled design without a comparison group, the specialized inpatient naturopathy setting limiting generalizability to outpatient practice, absence of long-term follow-up data, and potential selection bias among patients seeking complementary therapy.

Implications Medical Affairs : The significant multi-domain improvements in cardiometabolic risk factors with residential yoga and naturopathy-based lifestyle modification strengthen the evidence base for non-pharmacological interventions in hypertension management, with implications for holistic guideline recommendations that encompass complementary medicine in culturally appropriate treatment frameworks, particularly in India and Southeast Asia. From a Medical Affairs perspective, these findings can inform lifestyle-based co-prescription strategies that complement antihypertensive pharmacotherapy, supporting a comprehensive treatment narrative especially in markets with strong traditional medicine traditions where patient acceptance of integrative approaches is high. KOL engagement with integrative medicine specialists and preventive cardiology leaders in India could position antihypertensive products within comprehensive lifestyle modification programs that align with local healthcare philosophies.

Mediators of the Association Between Stroke and Blood Cells: A Two-Step Multivariable Mendelian Randomization Study.

Revue : CNS neuroscience & therapeutics | Tier : NA | IF estime : NA

Date de publication : 2026-09

Resultats principaux : This study analyzed 6,736 participants from the NHANES dataset (2011-2016) applying multivariable logistic regression, Weighted Quantile Sum regression, restricted cubic spline analysis, and Mendelian randomization to assess associations between complete blood count (CBC) parameters and stroke risk, adjusting comprehensively for age, race, socioeconomic status, BMI, lifestyle factors, serum calcium, hypertension, and diabetes. Initial analyses confirmed significant disparities between stroke and non-stroke cohorts in age, education, income, and prevalence of hypertension and diabetes. Red cell distribution width (RDW), hematocrit, hemoglobin, and mean corpuscular hemoglobin concentration (MCHC) emerged as independent predictors of stroke, with restricted cubic spline analysis identifying significant non-linear correlations for RDW and MCHC with stroke events. Mendelian randomization analyses suggested potential causal links between specific CBC parameters, particularly mean erythrocyte hemoglobin concentration and red blood cell distribution width, and stroke risk, with variable effect directions, while sensitivity analyses confirmed robustness through pleiotropy testing and heterogeneity inspection. Limitations include the cross-sectional nature of NHANES precluding definitive causal inference, questionnaire-based stroke ascertainment subject to recall bias, and the diverse NHANES population composition that requires careful interpretation across subgroups.

Implications Medical Affairs : The identification of specific CBC parameters as potentially causal stroke risk biomarkers, operating alongside established risk factors such as hypertension and diabetes, opens new avenues for non-invasive early stroke risk stratification using routine laboratory tests, with possible implications for vascular risk assessment guideline updates. From a Medical Affairs perspective, these findings highlight systemic inflammatory and hematological mechanisms linking hypertension to cerebrovascular events, reinforcing the critical importance of antihypertensive therapy as a central modifiable intervention within a multifactorial stroke prevention strategy. KOL engagement spanning neurology, hematology, and vascular medicine could leverage these findings to position antihypertensive products within comprehensive, biomarker-informed stroke risk reduction frameworks.
