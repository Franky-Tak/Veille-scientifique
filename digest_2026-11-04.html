The American College of Cardiology/American Heart Association classification of chronic hypertension in pregnancy has better clinical applicability and risk assessment than the traditional severe and mild hypertension approach - lessons from a population-based study.

Revue : The Journal of Maternal-Fetal & Neonatal Medicine | Tier : NA | IF estime : NA

Date de publication : 2026-11-03

Resultats principaux : This population-based retrospective cohort study analyzed 136,606 singleton pregnancies from a tertiary Israeli hospital to compare ACC/AHA versus ACOG hypertension classification systems during pregnancy, using Poisson regression to assess maternal and neonatal outcomes. Under ACOG criteria, the prevalence of chronic hypertension (CHTN) was 2.9% (4,127/137,606), while ACC/AHA criteria identified a significantly higher prevalence of 3.9% (5,424/137,606), p<0.001, capturing an additional ~1,300 patients. Even the least severe ACC/AHA category, elevated blood pressure (EBP), carried a two-fold increased risk of preeclampsia or eclampsia (RR 2.34, 95% CI 2.04-2.65) compared to normotensive patients, while Stage 1 CHTN (RR 4.00, 95% CI 3.56-4.50) and Stage 2 CHTN (RR 3.32, 95% CI 2.88-3.80) conferred similarly elevated risks. Both ACC/AHA-defined EBP and Stage 1/2 CHTN were independently associated with preterm birth and small-for-gestational-age neonates, underscoring the clinical relevance of the expanded classification across the full spectrum of hypertensive pregnancy disorders. Limitations include the retrospective single-center design relying on a computerized maternity database, potential selection bias from excluding multiple gestations, and the need for external validation in diverse obstetric populations.

Implications Medical Affairs : The ACC/AHA classification captures a clinically meaningful proportion of pregnant patients with elevated cardiovascular risk who would be missed by current ACOG criteria, suggesting that medical affairs teams should proactively monitor ongoing guideline debates and potential revisions to obstetric hypertension thresholds. This finding is directly relevant to antihypertensive product positioning in pregnancy, as expanding the diagnostic threshold could increase the eligible treatment population for agents indicated in mild-to-moderate hypertension, with implications for label extensions and patient identification strategies. Engaging KOLs in maternal-fetal medicine and obstetric cardiology around the clinical utility of ACC/AHA staging in pregnancy represents a high-impact medical education opportunity, particularly in linking early-stage hypertension with preeclampsia prevention pathways.

Primary Aldosteronism: Evaluation and Management in the Hypertensive Patient.

Revue : Current Hypertension Reports | Tier : NA | IF estime : NA

Date de publication : 2026-11-03

Resultats principaux : This comprehensive review from the University of Pennsylvania summarizes contemporary advances in the screening, diagnosis, and treatment of primary aldosteronism (PA), a frequently underdiagnosed cause of secondary hypertension associated with excess cardiorenal risk beyond blood pressure levels alone. Current guidelines have broadened screening indications and simplified aldosterone-to-renin ratio (ARR) interpretation by permitting medication-tolerant testing, thereby removing practical barriers to PA detection in routine hypertensive care. Confirmatory aldosterone suppression testing is increasingly de-emphasized in high-probability patients when results are unlikely to alter management, shifting the paradigm toward earlier, treatment-directed diagnosis. Adrenal vein sampling remains the gold standard for surgical lateralization, though molecular imaging is emerging as a promising noninvasive alternative, while finerenone and aldosterone synthase inhibitors are highlighted as investigational therapeutic options beyond classic mineralocorticoid receptor antagonists. Limitations include the narrative review design, potential for selection bias in evidence synthesis, and the need for comparative effectiveness data to establish the value of emerging diagnostic and therapeutic strategies.

Implications Medical Affairs : The growing recognition of PA as an underdiagnosed driver of resistant hypertension and cardiovascular risk, combined with emerging aldosterone synthase inhibitors, positions this as a high-priority area for medical affairs pipeline monitoring and competitive intelligence. Finerenone's emerging role in PA management has direct relevance for product lifecycle strategy and label extension discussions, particularly in the context of its established cardiorenal indications. Medical affairs should prioritize supporting KOL education on streamlined PA screening protocols across cardiology and nephrology, as increased detection will accelerate treatment decisions and drive guideline integration at a population level.

Effects of Dialysis Room Noise Exposure on Plasma Renin Activity, Angiotensin II, and Blood Pressure Fluctuations in Patients Undergoing Maintenance Hemodialysis.

Revue : Noise & Health | Tier : NA | IF estime : NA

Date de publication : 2026-11-03

Resultats principaux : This retrospective study from a Chinese tertiary hospital enrolled 100 maintenance hemodialysis (MHD) patients grouped by average dialysis-shift noise level into high-exposure (>55 dB(A), n=50) and low-exposure (≤55 dB(A), n=50) cohorts, with follow-up at 3 and 6 months using repeated-measures ANOVA and multivariable linear regression. At both time points, the high-noise group showed significantly elevated plasma renin activity and angiotensin II levels, as well as greater systolic and diastolic blood pressure fluctuation ranges and a higher incidence of intradialytic hypertension compared to the low-noise group (all p<0.05). Multivariable regression confirmed noise exposure as an independent predictor of plasma renin activity (beta=0.63, 95% CI 0.41-0.86), angiotensin II (beta=13.35, 95% CI 9.10-17.59), systolic BP fluctuation range (beta=7.03, 95% CI 5.06-9.01), and diastolic BP fluctuation range (beta=3.49, 95% CI 2.24-4.74), all p<0.001. These results suggest that environmental acoustic stress in dialysis units activates the renin-angiotensin system, potentially contributing to hemodynamic instability and complicating blood pressure control in this vulnerable population. Key limitations include the retrospective single-center design, small sample size per group, inability to fully account for confounders in dialysis settings, and lack of longer-term cardiovascular outcome data.

Implications Medical Affairs : This study provides mechanistic evidence linking environmental acoustic stressors with renin-angiotensin system activation and intradialytic hypertension, expanding the landscape of non-pharmacological factors that must be considered when managing blood pressure in hemodialysis patients. For medical affairs, these findings underscore the complexity of antihypertensive drug efficacy in dialysis settings and suggest that environmental optimization could complement pharmacological strategies, relevant for designing real-world evidence studies in dialysis-associated hypertension. Engaging nephrologists and dialysis center administrators around evidence-based acoustic environment standards may represent a differentiating medical affairs initiative that positions antihypertensive agents within a comprehensive blood pressure management framework.

Single-cell profiling reveals monocyte mitochondrial dysfunction in patients with cirrhosis progressing to acute-on-chronic liver failure.

Revue : JHEP Reports | Tier : NA | IF estime : NA

Date de publication : 2026-11-03

Resultats principaux : This prospective multicenter study enrolled 63 patients with acute decompensation (AD) of cirrhosis and 15 healthy donors across five European centers, with 90-day follow-up, performing single-cell RNA sequencing on peripheral blood mononuclear cells from 16 patients with distinct clinical trajectories to elucidate mechanisms of progression to acute-on-chronic liver failure (ACLF). A distinct classical monocyte subcluster (C2) was identified in patients who progressed to ACLF, specifically characterized by impaired energy metabolism with reduced oxidative phosphorylation (p<0.001), suggesting a bioenergetic basis for immune dysfunction in this condition. An 18-gene signature derived from this C2 cluster was externally validated in two large international whole-blood cohorts (PREDICT: n=689; ACLARA: n=521), showing enrichment in patients with bacterial infections, those developing ACLF, and non-survivors (p<0.001). Functional respirometry in an independent AD cohort confirmed declining monocyte Complex IV-dependent oxygen consumption in pre-ACLF patients (p<0.05), providing mechanistic corroboration of the transcriptomic findings. The study is limited by the small scRNA-seq discovery cohort (n=16), which may incompletely capture disease trajectory heterogeneity, and the retrospective nature of some validation data.

Implications Medical Affairs : While primarily a hepatology paper, the identification of monocyte mitochondrial dysfunction as an early marker of multi-organ failure progression may provide mechanistic insights into the cardiovascular-hepatic continuum relevant to hypertensive patients with advanced liver disease and systemic inflammation. Medical affairs teams monitoring the emerging field of immune-metabolic dysfunction in cardiorenal syndromes may find this research relevant to understanding how systemic inflammatory burden amplifies cardiovascular risk in complex multimorbid patients, including those with hypertension. The convergence of hepatology and cardiovascular medicine in the context of systemic inflammation could inform KOL engagement strategies at the interface of hepatology, nephrology, and cardiology, particularly for products addressing inflammation-driven organ crosstalk.

Risk of cardiovascular events and mortality associated with LDL and non-HDL cholesterol levels after statin initiation for primary prevention: A multiregional cohort study.

Revue : PLoS Medicine | Tier : NA | IF estime : NA

Date de publication : 2026-11-03

Resultats principaux : This new-user cohort study used electronic healthcare databases from Hong Kong (HK), New Zealand (NZ), and the United Kingdom (UK), enrolling 185,237 statin-naive patients initiating statins for primary cardiovascular prevention between 2007 and 2012, with follow-up to 2017, all without prior atherosclerotic cardiovascular disease, heart failure, or chronic kidney disease. The primary outcome, major adverse cardiovascular events (MACE), was assessed using landmark analyses with multivariable Cox regression adjusted for age, sex, hypertension, atrial fibrillation, diabetes mellitus, dyslipidemia, and multiple other comorbidities and medications. Lower achieved LDL at 2 years was associated with reduced MACE risk in a broadly log-linear fashion; HR 0.80 (95% CI 0.77-0.83) for LDL 1.8-2.5 mmol/L versus ≥2.6 mmol/L, with non-HDL cholesterol showing similar results (HR 0.75, 95% CI 0.72-0.79 for non-HDL 2.6-3.3 versus ≥3.4 mmol/L). Very low LDL (<1.4 mmol/L) was not associated with increased all-cause mortality after adjustment (HR 1.06, 95% CI 0.96-1.16), providing reassuring safety data for intensive lipid lowering. Limitations include cross-cohort heterogeneity potentially reflecting confounding by indication, residual confounding from lifestyle factors and disease severity, and the conditional nature of analyses requiring statin initiation and landmark lipid testing.

Implications Medical Affairs : This large, multiregional, multi-ethnic dataset reinforces the cardiovascular benefit of achieving guideline-recommended cholesterol targets in primary prevention, with hypertension explicitly controlled as a covariate, supporting the complementary role of antihypertensive and lipid-lowering therapy in comprehensive cardiovascular risk reduction. The safety signal regarding very low LDL concentrations and all-cause mortality is reassuring and may help address clinical hesitancy about combining intensive lipid lowering with antihypertensive therapy in complex hypertensive patients, an area where medical affairs communications can add value. Medical affairs teams can leverage this evidence to support integrated cardiovascular risk management messaging that positions antihypertensive agents as part of holistic primary prevention strategies alongside statin therapy.

Dynamics of the Prevalence of Non-communicable Diseases in the Urban Double Cotonou - Abomey-Calavi (South of Benin).

Revue : The Nigerian Postgraduate Medical Journal | Tier : NA | IF estime : NA

Date de publication : 2026-11-02

Resultats principaux : This cross-sectional epidemiological study examined non-communicable disease (NCD) prevalence and determinants in the rapidly urbanizing Cotonou-Abomey-Calavi area of Benin, using 58 semi-structured interviews with medical practitioners and 761 questionnaires distributed to household heads (n=409) and NCD-diagnosed individuals (n=352). Hypertension was the most prevalent NCD at 48.1%, followed by diabetes (14.6%), chronic respiratory diseases (10%), and cancers (2.5%), highlighting the dominant burden of hypertension in this sub-Saharan African urban population undergoing rapid demographic and lifestyle transition. Multinomial logistic regression identified age, gender, urban residence, education level, household size, and marital status as significant determinants; urban living was independently associated with elevated risks of hypertension and cancer, while physical activity was protective against NCDs broadly. Married or monogamously partnered individuals had a lower risk of hypertension, potentially reflecting better social support and stress reduction, consistent with international evidence on psychosocial determinants of blood pressure. Limitations include cross-sectional design precluding causal inference, potential self-report bias in questionnaire data, purposive sampling of medical practitioners, and limited generalizability beyond the studied urban corridor.

Implications Medical Affairs : The finding that hypertension accounts for nearly half of all NCD diagnoses in this West African urban population documents the emergence of a major antihypertensive treatment gap in sub-Saharan Africa, representing a significant and growing market opportunity for affordable antihypertensive therapies. Medical affairs teams should monitor the epidemiological literature from low- and middle-income countries as these regions increasingly contribute to global hypertension burden, informing access strategies, health economics arguments, and local guideline engagement. Partnering with regional KOLs and health ministries in West Africa around hypertension screening and treatment programs could represent a high-impact medical affairs investment aligned with both business development and global health equity objectives.

Temporal associations between severe infection and long-term risk of incident arrhythmias: a population-based cohort study.

Revue : BMC Medicine | Tier : NA | IF estime : NA

Date de publication : 2026-08-04

Resultats principaux : This prospective analysis of 465,374 UK Biobank participants, followed for a median of 14.2 years, used time-dependent Cox proportional hazards models adjusted for sociodemographic factors, lifestyle behaviors, clinical comorbidities including hypertension, and biochemical parameters to assess the association between hospital-treated severe infections and incident arrhythmias. Severe infection was associated with a markedly elevated risk of any incident arrhythmia (adjusted HR 1.85, 95% CI 1.80-1.90), with particularly high risks for sudden cardiac arrest (HR 2.79), ventricular arrhythmias (HR 3.11), and AV block (HR 3.03). Risk followed a temporal gradient, peaking within 1-30 days post-infection (HR 11.40, 95% CI 10.44-12.47 for any arrhythmia) but remaining significantly elevated long-term (HR 1.58, 95% CI 1.53-1.63 at ≥366 days), with sepsis and pneumonia conferring the highest arrhythmia risks. Recurrent infections showed a dose-response relationship, and the population attributable fraction for infection in AV block (16.6%) was comparable to that of traditional risk factors such as hypertension. Limitations include reliance on hospitalization records which may underrepresent mild infections, potential residual confounding, and the UK Biobank's relatively healthy volunteer sample which may limit generalizability.

Implications Medical Affairs : The finding that severe infections carry arrhythmia risks comparable to hypertension as a traditional cardiovascular risk factor highlights the importance of integrating infection history into comprehensive cardiovascular risk assessment, with implications for how medical affairs teams contextualize hypertension control in patients with recurrent infections. This evidence supports the positioning of antihypertensive and cardiovascular protective therapies in patients with high infection burden, particularly in elderly or immunocompromised populations where both infection risk and hypertension prevalence are elevated. Medical affairs could leverage these findings to support educational programs that frame hypertension management within a broader multi-factorial arrhythmia prevention strategy, strengthening the case for tight blood pressure control as part of integrated cardiovascular risk management.
