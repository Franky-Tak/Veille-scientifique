Individualized vs Standard Blood Pressure Control During Thrombectomy for Ischemic Stroke: The DETERMINE Randomized Clinical Trial.

Revue : Neurology | Tier : NA | IF estime : NA

Date de publication : 2026-08-12

Resultats principaux : This multicenter, open-label, blinded-endpoint randomized clinical trial enrolled 433 adult patients with acute ischemic stroke due to anterior large vessel occlusion undergoing mechanical thrombectomy (MT) at 8 academic comprehensive stroke centers in France between March 2021 and September 2023. Patients were randomized 1:1 to individualized blood pressure management, in which mean arterial pressure (MAP) was maintained within 10% of baseline MAP recorded before the procedure, or to standard control targeting systolic BP between 140 and 180 mmHg. The primary outcome was favorable functional outcome, defined as a modified Rankin Scale score of 0-2 at 90 days. Favorable functional outcome occurred in 44.2% of the individualized group versus 48.8% in the control group (adjusted OR 0.82, 95% CI 0.54-1.24; p=0.34), a non-significant difference; mortality at 90 days was similarly not significantly different (18.8% vs 16.4%; adjusted OR 1.19, 95% CI 0.70-2.03), and rates of symptomatic intracranial hemorrhages at 24 hours did not differ between groups. Notably, no significant differences in mean BP levels or BP variability were observed between the two groups during the procedure, which may limit conclusions about the effective separation of the interventions. The study provides Class II evidence that individualized BP management during MT does not improve functional outcomes compared to standard BP management in patients with anterior large vessel occlusion ischemic stroke, with an important subgroup limitation being the absence of collateral circulation or perfusion imaging-guided patient stratification that might identify responders to individualized titration.

Implications Medical Affairs : This Class II evidence RCT from a rigorously conducted multicenter French trial demonstrates that individualized BP control during mechanical thrombectomy does not improve functional outcomes, reinforcing the validity of current guideline-recommended standard BP targets (systolic 140-180 mmHg) during the peri-procedural period and reducing the scientific pressure to adopt complex individualized monitoring protocols. From a Medical Affairs perspective, this finding strengthens the existing clinical positioning of antihypertensive agents used in acute stroke and peri-procedural settings, confirming that standard management is both safe and sufficient as a benchmark of care. Medical Affairs teams can engage stroke neurology and interventional neuroradiology KOLs to disseminate this evidence, emphasizing that product labeling recommendations for BP control in the peri-MT setting remain supported and that investment in individualized precision titration approaches in this indication is not currently warranted by the evidence.

Prophylactic Effects of Dietary Patterns and Indexes, Nutrition and Lifestyle on Hepatocellular Carcinoma.

Revue : Annals of Surgical Oncology | Tier : NA | IF estime : NA

Date de publication : 2026-08-11

Resultats principaux : This narrative review systematically searched four databases (PubMed, Web of Science, Embase, and the Cochrane Library) from inception to June 2026, identifying and synthesizing studies on the role of dietary patterns, nutritional exposures, and lifestyle behaviors in the prevention of hepatocellular carcinoma (HCC), the sixth most common cancer globally and the third leading cause of cancer-related mortality. The review found that the Dietary Approaches to Stop Hypertension (DASH), Mediterranean, prudent, plant-based, traditional Cantonese, and high-quality dietary index patterns are generally associated with reduced HCC risk, while Western dietary patterns, processed meat, ultra-processed foods, sugar-sweetened beverages, alcohol, and smoking are associated with higher risk. Vegetables, fruits, fish, white meat, tea, coffee, and regular physical activity also contribute to HCC risk reduction, though the strength and directness of evidence varied considerably across exposures and study designs. A central limitation of the available evidence is its predominantly observational nature, relying on self-reported dietary assessments and subject to residual confounding, measurement error, reverse causality, and heterogeneous exposure definitions that limit causal interpretation. The authors conclude that dietary and lifestyle recommendations should be incorporated cautiously into comprehensive HCC prevention strategies that also include viral hepatitis vaccination, antiviral therapy, alcohol abstinence, and metabolic risk control, with further prospective, standardized research needed to build an actionable evidence base.

Implications Medical Affairs : Although primarily focused on hepatocellular carcinoma, this review provides important cross-disease validation that the DASH and Mediterranean dietary patterns, which are central to hypertension prevention guidelines, also confer protection against a distinct but metabolically linked oncological endpoint, strengthening the broad cardiometabolic value proposition of diet-oriented prevention messaging in Medical Affairs. The recognition that hypertension and associated metabolic risk factors are key targets within integrated HCC prevention strategies presents a Medical Affairs opportunity to position antihypertensive lifestyle and pharmacological interventions within multi-morbidity prevention programs, appealing to hepatologists, oncologists, and internal medicine specialists as key targets for cross-disciplinary KOL engagement. Medical Affairs teams should monitor evolving DASH diet evidence as it extends beyond cardiovascular endpoints, leveraging this expanding body of literature to advocate for dietary pattern assessment as a routine component of metabolic risk management counseling in hypertensive patients.

Lifestyle and Metabolic Health, Low-Grade to Clinically Elevated Albuminuria, and Cardiovascular-Renal Diseases.

Revue : JACC Asia | Tier : NA | IF estime : NA

Date de publication : 2026-08-06

Resultats principaux : This large prospective cohort analysis included 82,509 participants from the China Cardiometabolic Disease and Cancer Cohort (4C) Study with a median follow-up of 3.0 years, examining the differential roles of lifestyle and metabolic factors in the association between fine-categorized urinary albumin-to-creatinine ratio (UACR) levels and composite cardiorenal outcomes including incident cardiovascular disease (CVD) and chronic kidney disease (CKD). Compared to the lowest UACR category, participants with mildly elevated UACR (greater than or equal to 10 to less than 30 mg/g) had a 39% higher risk of composite cardiorenal outcomes (adjusted HR 1.39, 95% CI 1.29-1.50), and those with clinically elevated UACR (greater than or equal to 30 mg/g) had more than double the risk (HR 2.29, 95% CI 2.12-2.47), with a nonlinear J-shaped continuous dose-response relationship (P=0.0009). Among all metabolic factors assessed, hypertension showed the largest relative contribution to cardiorenal risk, particularly in the UACR less than 30 mg/g range, identifying low-grade albuminuria as a high-priority preventive window. Optimal lifestyle and metabolic status attenuated CVD risk associated with low-grade albuminuria, and improvement of metabolic status additionally reduced CVD and composite cardiorenal risk even in the clinically elevated albuminuria group (additive P less than 0.0001). Limitations include a relatively short median follow-up of 3.0 years, potential residual confounding from unmeasured lifestyle variables, and the generalizability challenge of applying Chinese cohort findings to other ethnic populations.

Implications Medical Affairs : The identification of hypertension as the single largest contributor to cardiorenal risk in patients with low-grade albuminuria (UACR 10-30 mg/g), a range currently below the standard clinical intervention threshold, provides compelling evidence to support earlier antihypertensive treatment initiation in patients with mildly elevated proteinuria and directly challenges the adequacy of current screening and treatment guidelines. This large-scale Asian cohort evidence is particularly valuable for Medical Affairs positioning of RAAS-inhibiting antihypertensive agents, which simultaneously reduce BP and proteinuria, as foundational therapies in an expanded cardiorenal prevention paradigm that encompasses subclinical albuminuria thresholds. Medical Affairs teams can leverage this data for KOL engagement in nephrology and cardiology in Asia-Pacific markets, advocating for guideline revision to lower the UACR intervention threshold and supporting publication strategies that position combination cardiorenal risk management as the emerging standard of care for hypertensive patients.

Prevalence of Kinesiophobia and Associated Factors Among Patients After Percutaneous Coronary Intervention: A Multicenter Cross-Sectional Study.

Revue : Nursing Open | Tier : NA | IF estime : NA

Date de publication : 2026-08

Resultats principaux : This multicenter cross-sectional study recruited 970 post-percutaneous coronary intervention (PCI) patients via convenience sampling from cardiology departments at eight tertiary hospitals across Guangdong Province, China, between November 2022 and December 2023, using the validated Tampa Scale for Kinesiophobia Heart (TSK-SV Heart) to assess fear of movement. The prevalence of kinesiophobia was notably high at 80.41%, indicating that fear of physical activity is nearly universal in this post-PCI population and represents a major barrier to cardiac rehabilitation adherence. Binary logistic regression identified hypertension comorbidity as a significant independent predictor of kinesiophobia (OR 1.49, 95% CI 1.05-2.13, p=0.028), alongside retirement status (OR 1.89), participation in cardiac rehabilitation (OR 1.52), and anxiety severity (OR 1.21 per additional point). Protective factors included single marital status (OR 0.42), moderate physical activity (OR 0.64), and high physical activity (OR 0.40), while higher exercise self-efficacy and lower depression scores were also independently protective. The cross-sectional design limits causal inference, and the convenience sampling from a single Chinese regional healthcare system restricts generalizability to broader populations, including those from Western countries or rural settings.

Implications Medical Affairs : The independent association between hypertension comorbidity and kinesiophobia in post-PCI patients reveals an important but underappreciated psychosocial dimension of hypertension management that may attenuate the cardiovascular benefits of both pharmacological treatment and cardiac rehabilitation, suggesting a need for integrated psychological support protocols in hypertensive secondary prevention programs. From a Medical Affairs perspective, this finding supports the positioning of antihypertensive therapy within holistic secondary prevention frameworks that address psychological barriers to physical activity, reinforcing messaging about the importance of treatment adherence combined with lifestyle engagement for optimal cardiovascular outcomes. Medical Affairs teams can engage cardiac rehabilitation specialists, nursing leaders, and cardiologists as KOLs to promote integrated care pathways that combine hypertension management, anxiety screening, and structured physical activity programs, differentiating a comprehensive disease management approach from purely pharmacological treatment paradigms.

Prevalence of type 1 and type 2 diabetes among chronic kidney disease patients and laboratorial differences between diabetics and non-diabetics.

Revue : Annals of Medicine and Surgery (2012) | Tier : NA | IF estime : NA

Date de publication : 2026-07-20

Resultats principaux : This retrospective chart review examined records of 297 patients at the kidney surgical hospital in Damascus, Syria, aiming to characterize the prevalence of diabetes and hypertension among CKD patients and to investigate laboratory differences between diabetic and non-diabetic CKD patients during a period of ongoing armed conflict and healthcare system disruption. Among the studied population, 64.6% were over 50 years old, 61.6% were male, and 35.4% had diabetes; hypertension was identified alongside diabetes as one of the two leading causes of CKD in this population, exacerbated by the Syrian crisis. Patients with diabetes had lower serum creatinine levels at admission compared to non-diabetics, a finding that may reflect earlier clinical presentation or differential screening patterns rather than less severe renal impairment. The correlation between fasting blood glucose and serum creatinine, urea, and MDRD score was not statistically significant, potentially reflecting the small sample size, cross-sectional timing, and heterogeneous CKD stages of the retrospective sample. Despite the unique challenges of the conflict context, the prevalence of diabetes among Syrian CKD patients was found to be comparable to global rates, suggesting that the underlying epidemiological profile of CKD risk factors is preserved even in severely resource-limited settings, though the ability to manage these risk factors including hypertension is severely compromised.

Implications Medical Affairs : This study highlights the critical but underrecognized burden of hypertension as a leading driver of CKD in conflict-affected populations in the Middle East, a demographic systematically underrepresented in major clinical trials and treatment guidelines, creating an evidence gap that Medical Affairs should acknowledge in global burden narratives and advocacy efforts. The context of ongoing Syrian conflict underscores the urgent unmet medical need for robust, sustainable antihypertensive programs in humanitarian settings and provides Medical Affairs teams with supporting evidence for corporate social responsibility and access-to-medicine initiatives targeting LMIC and conflict-affected regions. Engagement with KOLs in nephrology, global health, and internal medicine can be strengthened by referencing this data to advocate for early hypertension screening and treatment as a pillar of CKD prevention in humanitarian health program design, helping position hypertension products within the broader global health equity agenda.

Associations between dietary factors and serum uric acid levels in Korean women aged 19-65 years.

Revue : Nutrition Research and Practice | Tier : NA | IF estime : NA

Date de publication : 2026-03-16

Resultats principaux : This cross-sectional study analyzed dietary intake data from 4,201 Korean women aged 19-64 years drawn from the 2016-2017 Korea National Health and Nutrition Examination Survey (KNHANES), investigating the relationship between dietary patterns and serum uric acid (SUA) levels, which are recognized risk factors for the development of hypertension, type 2 diabetes, and chronic kidney disease. Dietary intake was assessed via a single 24-hour dietary recall, and SUA levels were stratified into quartiles for analysis using multivariate linear regression adjusted for potential confounders. Higher intakes of saturated fatty acids (SFA) and monounsaturated fatty acids (MUFA) were significantly and independently associated with elevated SUA levels, while higher carbohydrate intake showed a significant inverse association. At the food group level, greater intakes of meat and high-purine seafood were positively associated with SUA, while grain intake showed an inverse association; dietary fiber was lower in the highest SUA quartile but was not independently associated after full covariate adjustment. The reliance on a single 24-hour dietary recall is a substantial limitation for characterizing habitual diet, and the cross-sectional design precludes any causal inference; additionally, results may not generalize beyond Korean women to other ethnic or sex-defined populations.

Implications Medical Affairs : Given the well-established bidirectional relationship between hyperuricemia and hypertension, these findings in a large nationally representative Korean female sample reinforce the mechanistic dietary pathway linking animal fat and protein intake to elevated uric acid and downstream hypertension risk, providing supporting evidence for dietary pattern modification as a complement to antihypertensive pharmacotherapy. Medical Affairs can leverage this data to strengthen the scientific narrative around comprehensive lifestyle modification as an adjunct to antihypertensive treatment, particularly in Asian markets where plant-based dietary transitions and uric acid-related metabolic risk are clinically relevant concerns for a growing proportion of patients. KOL engagement in preventive cardiology, clinical nutrition, and metabolic medicine in Korea and broader East Asian markets can be advanced by positioning hyperuricemia management as an emerging and evidence-supported component of hypertension prevention strategy, supporting the development of educational content and consensus statements that integrate dietary uric acid management into hypertension care pathways.

Current status of premature myocardial infarction research in China: a systematic review and international comparative perspective.

Revue : Frontiers in Cardiovascular Medicine | Tier : NA | IF estime : NA

Date de publication : 2026-07-28

Resultats principaux : This systematic review analyzed 317 eligible observational studies on premature myocardial infarction (PMI) in China from inception to September 2025, applying a novel Strength-Importance Matrix scoring tool to evaluate each risk factor along two weighted composite dimensions: Evidence Strength and Public Health and Clinical Importance. The review identified that Chinese PMI patients exhibit a distinctive premature atherosclerosis phenotype dominated by behavioral and metabolic risk factors, with smoking (prevalence greater than 71% in patients under 55 years) and hypertriglyceridemia as the most prominent hallmarks. The Strength-Importance Matrix categorized risk factors into four strategic quadrants: Quadrant I (High Evidence, High Importance) included smoking, hypertriglyceridemia, family history, and metabolic syndrome as priority intervention targets; Quadrant II (High Importance, Low Evidence) identified psychological stress, overwork, and sleep deprivation as critical evidence gaps; Quadrant IV (High Evidence, Low Importance) included hypertension and diabetes, classified as routine management targets rather than emerging focus areas. Compared to international benchmarks including INTERHEART and PURE, systematic gaps were identified in Chinese PMI research in methodological rigor, depth of psychosocial and environmental risk factor investigation, and translation of evidence into localized risk prediction tools and community interventions. The over-reliance on retrospective designs, inconsistent definitions of premature MI, and underuse of population attributable risk percentage calculations represent fundamental methodological limitations that hamper causal inference and international comparability.

Implications Medical Affairs : The classification of hypertension as a Quadrant IV routine management factor in Chinese PMI research, despite its well-established causal role in cardiovascular risk, signals an opportunity for Medical Affairs to promote higher-quality, real-world evidence generation on the specific impact of antihypertensive treatment on premature MI incidence in younger Chinese adults, a population with growing commercial and epidemiological significance. The identified evidence gaps in psychosocial risk factors and the call for prospective, standardized Chinese registry data represent clear opportunities for pharmaceutical companies to sponsor or co-develop KOL-led longitudinal studies that generate actionable hypertension management evidence tailored to the Chinese premature MI demographic. Aligning antihypertensive product positioning with the unmet need for precision, population-specific cardiovascular prevention in younger patients could differentiate the value proposition in an increasingly competitive Chinese cardiovascular market and support KOL strategy focused on translating global evidence into locally relevant clinical practice guidelines.

Dietary Inflammatory Index, cardiovascular health, and long-term outcomes in hypertension: external validation in a Chinese percutaneous coronary intervention cohort.

Revue : Frontiers in Nutrition | Tier : NA | IF estime : NA

Date de publication : 2026-07-28

Resultats principaux : This two-cohort study conducted a discovery analysis in 12,376 hypertensive adults from NHANES (2005-2018) and performed external validation in 1,053 Chinese hypertensive patients who had undergone percutaneous coronary intervention (PCI) at a single center, examining the association between the Dietary Inflammatory Index (DII) and cardiovascular health (CVH) as measured by the Life's Essential 8 (LE8) score, as well as subsequent major adverse cardiovascular events (MACE). In the NHANES cohort, higher DII was independently associated with suboptimal CVH in a linear dose-response relationship (adjusted OR 1.39 per unit, 95% CI 1.30-1.50; OR 3.84 for highest vs lowest DII tertile, 95% CI 2.67-5.52). This association was robustly replicated in the PCI validation cohort (OR 1.29 per unit, 95% CI 1.19-1.39; OR 2.99 for highest vs lowest tertile, 95% CI 2.11-4.22), with a consistent linear dose-response. In the PCI cohort, higher DII independently predicted MACE (HR 1.19, 95% CI 1.10-1.28), recurrent MI (HR 1.24, 95% CI 1.10-1.39), repeat revascularization (HR 1.14, 95% CI 1.02-1.28), and all-cause mortality (HR 1.19, 95% CI 1.04-1.35). The study's primary limitations include its retrospective design, reliance on single-center validation data in the Chinese cohort, potential residual confounding, and the inability to establish causality between dietary inflammatory potential and cardiovascular outcomes in hypertensive patients.

Implications Medical Affairs : This cross-validated, dual-cohort study provides robust evidence that a pro-inflammatory diet is linearly associated with worsened cardiovascular health and hard clinical endpoints specifically in hypertensive patients across demographically distinct American and Chinese populations, significantly strengthening the scientific case for integrating dietary inflammation assessment into cardiovascular risk stratification for hypertensive patients. For Medical Affairs, this evidence supports positioning antihypertensive therapy within comprehensive, inflammation-aware cardiovascular prevention programs that include dietary inflammatory index counseling, particularly in secondary prevention settings post-PCI where the clinical stakes are highest and patient engagement with lifestyle modification is greatest. Medical Affairs teams can drive KOL engagement with cardiologists, preventive medicine specialists, and clinical dieticians to advocate for routine DII assessment alongside standard cardiovascular risk profiling in hypertensive patients and to support publication and congress presentation of this evidence in high-visibility cardiovascular journals, reinforcing the broader lifestyle-plus-pharmacology narrative in hypertension management.
