Maternal serum uric acid before 24 weeks of gestation and subsequent gestational diabetes mellitus: a multicentre retrospective cohort study.

Revue : Frontiers in Endocrinology | Tier : NA | IF estime : NA

Date de publication : 2026-07-24

Resultats principaux : This multicentre retrospective cohort study enrolled 5,614 pregnancies from three hospitals in China to assess whether maternal serum uric acid measured before 24 weeks of gestation was independently associated with gestational diabetes mellitus (GDM) and hypertensive disorders of pregnancy (HDP). The primary endpoint of GDM occurred in 1,187 pregnancies (21.1%), while HDP served as a secondary cardiometabolic outcome occurring in 275 pregnancies (4.9%). Using modified Poisson regression with robust variance estimation, the fourth uric acid quartile was associated with significantly elevated risks of both GDM (adjusted RR 1.54; 95% CI 1.34-1.77) and HDP (adjusted RR 1.89; 95% CI 1.33-2.67) compared with the first quartile. Each 10 micromol/L increment in uric acid was independently associated with GDM (adjusted RR 1.03; 95% CI 1.02-1.04) and HDP (adjusted RR 1.04; 95% CI 1.02-1.06), with restricted cubic spline analyses revealing a linear dose-response relationship for HDP and modest nonlinearity for GDM. Key limitations include the retrospective single-country design, potential selection bias across three heterogeneous hospitals, absence of longitudinal uric acid tracking during pregnancy, and inability to establish causal directionality between hyperuricemia and cardiometabolic pregnancy complications.

Implications Medical Affairs : This study adds meaningful evidence that elevated first and second trimester uric acid levels predict hypertensive disorders of pregnancy, supporting the inclusion of early uricemia screening in obstetric cardiometabolic risk stratification algorithms that could benefit from antihypertensive prevention programs. For Medical Affairs, partnerships with obstetrics and maternal-fetal medicine societies could be developed around this biomarker to position uric acid measurement alongside established preeclampsia screening panels, highlighting the cardiovascular continuum from pregnancy complications to long-term maternal hypertension. An actionable KOL strategy insight is to initiate investigator-driven studies evaluating whether uric acid-lowering interventions or targeted lifestyle counseling in hyperuricemic pregnant women reduce the incidence of HDP and subsequent postpartum hypertension.

Hearts in motion: Evaluating the utility of a mobile health unit in Ireland for cardiovascular disease prevention.

Revue : Public Health in Practice (Oxford, England) | Tier : NA | IF estime : NA

Date de publication : 2026-07-28

Resultats principaux : This prospective evaluation study with a six-week telephone follow-up assessed the effectiveness of the Irish Heart Foundation's Mobile Health Unit (MHU) across 14 community locations in Ireland, enrolling 300 participants aged 18-84 years with balanced urban-rural representation and a socioeconomically diverse profile (48% not in current employment, 56% without further education). At baseline, 22.1% of participants were found to have high blood pressure and an additional 42.9% were classified as prehypertensive, with males significantly more likely to present with high BP than females (34.2% vs. 14.4%; p less than 0.001); notably, nearly 40% had no prior awareness of their blood pressure levels. Six-week follow-up data (n=182) demonstrated that 70% of those referred to a GP had attended a consultation, with 28.2% undergoing additional diagnostic tests, 10.3% receiving a new hypertension diagnosis, and 42.3% reporting lifestyle changes. The most frequently cited reason for participation was convenience (34.4%), underscoring the value of opportunistic community-based screening for reaching at-risk individuals outside traditional healthcare pathways. Significant limitations include the small uncontrolled sample size, volunteer self-selection bias, potential responder bias in telephone follow-up, and the absence of a formal comparator group to isolate MHU-specific effects.

Implications Medical Affairs : This real-world evidence study demonstrates that opportunistic community screening programs can reveal a substantial burden of undiagnosed and undertreated hypertension, particularly among males and lower socioeconomic groups who are typically underserved by conventional healthcare systems, supporting the case for broader public health investment. For Medical Affairs, these data provide a compelling framework for disease awareness partnerships with cardiovascular foundations and patient advocacy groups, enabling the development of referral pathways that connect community screening outputs with antihypertensive treatment initiation at the primary care level. An actionable KOL strategy insight is to collaborate with mobile health program organizers and primary care networks to co-develop education materials at community health touchpoints, embedding hypertension treatment awareness alongside screening activities to accelerate time-to-diagnosis and time-to-treatment.

Non-Invasive Prenatal Testing in Donor-Oocyte Pregnancies: Biological Determinants, Algorithmic Limitations and Clinical Implications.

Revue : Molecular Diagnosis & Therapy | Tier : NA | IF estime : NA

Date de publication : 2026-08-08

Resultats principaux : This narrative review examines the specific challenges of interpreting non-invasive prenatal testing (NIPT) based on circulating cell-free DNA (cfDNA) in donor-oocyte pregnancies, a population enriched for advanced maternal age, obesity, hypertensive disorders, immunologic complications, and placental dysfunction—all conditions that destabilize cfDNA signal quality and fetal fraction estimates. The review identifies hypertension and placental dysfunction as key pathophysiological perturbations affecting cfDNA analytical performance, arguing that reduced fetal fraction in these settings should be interpreted as a biological marker of placental compromise rather than a purely technical failure. No primary quantitative endpoints or statistical results are reported, as this is a narrative synthesis; the authors highlight that counting-based and single-nucleotide polymorphism-based NIPT platforms behave differently when biological complexity is high, particularly for sex chromosome aneuploidies. The key clinical recommendation is that no-call NIPT results in donor-oocyte pregnancies may be clinically informative rather than analytically neutral, suggesting underlying placental pathology including hypertensive complications. Significant limitations include the absence of original data, reliance on narrative rather than systematic review methodology, and potential for selection bias in source literature.

Implications Medical Affairs : Although primarily focused on prenatal diagnostics, this review reinforces that hypertensive disorders of pregnancy represent a clinically significant pathophysiological variable that impacts biomarker and diagnostic test performance in high-risk obstetric populations, strengthening the case for proactive cardiovascular management in assisted reproduction settings. From a Medical Affairs standpoint, these insights create an opportunity to engage maternal-fetal medicine KOLs in cross-specialty scientific exchange on the cardiovascular comorbidity burden associated with ART pregnancies and its implications for long-term maternal hypertension outcomes. An actionable strategy is to monitor evolving prenatal screening guidelines that integrate cardiometabolic risk factors, as these may create new positioning opportunities for early antihypertensive intervention protocols in obstetric settings.

Association of the combined triglyceride-glucose and frailty index with chronic kidney disease: evidence from two national cohort studies.

Revue : Endocrine | Tier : NA | IF estime : NA

Date de publication : 2026-08-08

Resultats principaux : This dual-cohort study examined the association between the Triglyceride-Glucose and Frailty Index (TyGFI)—a composite biomarker combining metabolic dysfunction and physical vulnerability—and the risk of chronic kidney disease (CKD), using cross-sectional data from CHARLS (n=8,721 Chinese adults) and NHANES (n=9,670 US adults), plus longitudinal follow-up in 4,102 CHARLS participants, with hypertension identified as a significant independent covariate across all models. In fully adjusted models, participants in the highest TyGFI quartile faced substantially elevated odds of prevalent CKD compared to the lowest quartile (CHARLS: OR 3.98, 95% CI 3.03-5.22; NHANES: OR 4.49, 95% CI 3.55-5.69), and longitudinal analyses confirmed a prospective association with incident CKD (HR 4.18, 95% CI 3.09-5.65). K-means clustering revealed that participants on an "increasing TyGFI trajectory" had an HR of 3.56 (95% CI 2.69-4.71) for incident CKD relative to those on a stable-low trajectory, with dose-response relationships confirmed by restricted cubic spline analyses. Hypertension, diabetes, and dyslipidemia were consistently highlighted as effect modifiers in subgroup analyses, underscoring the role of blood pressure dysregulation in the metabolic-renal risk cascade. Limitations include cross-sectional design in primary analyses limiting causal inference, potential residual confounding from unmeasured dietary and lifestyle factors, and the composite nature of TyGFI requiring external validation before clinical implementation.

Implications Medical Affairs : These findings reinforce the mechanistic overlap between hypertension, metabolic dysfunction, and renal impairment, providing robust epidemiological support for integrated therapeutic strategies targeting the cardiometabolic-renal axis simultaneously—a scientific framework directly relevant to the current positioning of antihypertensive agents with documented renal protective properties. From a Medical Affairs perspective, this study supports engagement with nephrologists and cardiometabolic specialists around the growing body of evidence linking hypertension control to CKD prevention, potentially strengthening guideline-aligned messaging for combination therapies. An actionable KOL insight is to sponsor or support scientific sessions at nephrology and endocrinology congresses that highlight TyGFI and related composite risk indices as tools for identifying hypertensive patients at highest cardiorenal risk who may benefit most from early pharmacological intervention.

Weight Management Before Pregnancy to Reduce Cardiovascular Risk.

Revue : Cardiology in Review | Tier : NA | IF estime : NA

Date de publication : 2026-08-08

Resultats principaux : This narrative review synthesizes current evidence on the relationship between excess prepregnancy weight and adverse cardiovascular outcomes, with hypertension-related disorders of pregnancy—including gestational hypertension and preeclampsia—identified as primary endpoints of concern given their well-established association with long-term maternal cardiovascular morbidity. The review describes the mechanistic pathways linking prepregnancy obesity to increased cardiometabolic risk, including chronic low-grade inflammation, insulin resistance, endothelial dysfunction, and activation of the renin-angiotensin-aldosterone system, which collectively predispose women to hypertensive complications during and after pregnancy. No original quantitative data or statistical results were generated; the synthesis concludes that globally rising obesity rates among reproductive-age women make preconception weight management an underutilized primary cardiovascular prevention strategy. Qualitative subgroup considerations address ethnic and comorbidity-based variability in susceptibility to pregnancy-related hypertension, suggesting that intervention targets may need to be population-specific. The primary limitation is the narrative rather than systematic review methodology, which precludes formal quality assessment of included studies and introduces potential selection bias in the evidence cited.

Implications Medical Affairs : This review positions preconception weight management as a critical upstream lever for primary cardiovascular prevention in women of reproductive age, aligning with the growing Medical Affairs focus on sex-specific and life-stage-specific hypertension pathways where early intervention could reduce the trajectory toward chronic hypertension. For Medical Affairs teams, this narrative provides a scientific foundation for KOL engagement at the intersection of reproductive health, obesity medicine, and cardiovascular risk reduction—an area where both clinical guidelines and pharmaceutical pipelines remain underdeveloped. An actionable insight is to support real-world evidence programs evaluating the impact of preconception lifestyle and pharmacological weight-loss interventions on subsequent gestational hypertension incidence, generating data that could reshape obstetric cardiovascular prevention guidelines.

Knowledge, Attitude, and Practices Regarding Metabolic Syndrome Among Ambulance Support Personnel.

Revue : Cureus | Tier : NA | IF estime : NA

Date de publication : 2026-07-07

Resultats principaux : This cross-sectional survey study assessed knowledge, attitudes, and practices (KAP) regarding metabolic syndrome—a cluster of interrelated conditions including hypertension, central adiposity, dyslipidemia, and insulin resistance—among 119 ambulance support personnel in Karad City, Maharashtra, India, using a structured Google Forms questionnaire. The primary endpoints were KAP scores revealing that 58.0% demonstrated moderate knowledge, 67.2% exhibited a positive attitude toward metabolic syndrome prevention and management, and 60.5% reported only fair preventive practices, indicating a clear knowledge-attitude-practice gap. Mean scores were knowledge 5.8 plus or minus 1.4, attitude 4.9 plus or minus 1.1, and practice 5.7 plus or minus 1.8 out of a possible maximum, with practices lagging behind both knowledge and attitudes. Occupational risk factors particularly relevant to hypertension development included prolonged shift durations, erratic meal schedules, chronic sleep deficits, and persistent occupational stress—all factors known to activate sympathoadrenal pathways and elevate blood pressure over time. Significant methodological limitations include the small convenience sample (n=119), single-center recruitment in one city in India, reliance on self-reported measures without objective validation, and the use of a non-validated questionnaire, collectively limiting generalizability and internal validity.

Implications Medical Affairs : These findings highlight a critical and actionable gap between health literacy and preventive behavior in a high-occupational-risk population chronically exposed to multiple hypertension risk factors, underscoring the need for targeted workplace health programs that go beyond education to address structural and behavioral barriers to lifestyle change. From a Medical Affairs perspective, this study supports the development of disease awareness and screening initiatives directed at frontline healthcare workers, a strategically important audience with both personal health needs and professional influence on patient-level hypertension management behaviors. An actionable KOL strategy insight is to engage occupational health specialists and emergency medicine societies in collaborative programs integrating hypertension screening, metabolic syndrome risk education, and behavioral counseling within workplace wellness frameworks, creating a multiplier effect on cardiovascular health literacy across health systems.
