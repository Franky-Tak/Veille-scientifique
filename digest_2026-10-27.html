Periprocedural Pulmonary Hypertension and Outcomes After Mitral Valve Transcatheter Edge-to-Edge Repair.

Revue : JACC. Cardiovascular Interventions | Tier : 2 | IF estimé : ~15

Date de publication : 2026-10-26

Résultats principaux : The OCEAN-Mitral registry prospectively enrolled 3,764 patients with severe mitral regurgitation undergoing M-TEER, with 3,484 patients included in analyses following paired echocardiographic tricuspid regurgitation pressure gradient (TRPG) measurement. Pulmonary hypertension (PH) was defined as TRPG >31 mmHg, and patients were classified as no PH (41.1%), normalized PH (21.9%), new-onset PH (8.6%), and residual PH (28.4%). The 2-year composite endpoint of cardiovascular death or heart failure hospitalization differed significantly across groups (17.6%, 18.1%, 27.2%, and 27.6%, respectively; p<0.001), with new-onset PH (HR: 1.46; 95% CI: 1.10-1.93) and residual PH (HR: 1.37; 95% CI: 1.13-1.68) independently associated with adverse outcomes, while normalized PH showed outcomes comparable to no PH (adjusted HR: 0.98; 95% CI: 0.78-1.24). Predictors of residual PH included severe tricuspid regurgitation, baseline TRPG >50 mmHg, and tricuspid annular dilation, whereas residual mitral regurgitation and postprocedural transmitral gradients >5 mmHg predicted new-onset PH. Key limitations include the observational registry design without randomization, potential selection bias, and the inability to assess medication adjustments during follow-up.

Implications Medical Affairs : These findings establish periprocedural PH dynamics as a robust prognostic biomarker after M-TEER, with direct implications for post-procedural monitoring protocols and patient stratification in structural heart disease guidelines; Medical Affairs should advocate for PH normalization as a procedural success criterion. For pharmaceutical products targeting pulmonary hypertension or heart failure management, this registry data supports messaging around the additive benefit of combined PH optimization with structural intervention, reinforcing product positioning in a high-acuity cardiovascular population. KOL engagement with structural cardiology and heart failure specialists should explore whether pre-procedural PH optimization strategies could improve M-TEER outcomes, generating prospective trial hypotheses and expanding scientific leadership presence.

Association of NMR metabolic signatures of healthy lifestyle with hypertension: a prospective cohort study.

Revue : BMC Cardiovascular Disorders | Tier : NA | IF estimé : NA

Date de publication : 2026-07-29

Résultats principaux : This prospective cohort study used UK Biobank data from 126,677 eligible participants (mean age 54.92+/-8.12 years; 46.47% male; 96.09% White) with a median follow-up of 13.43 years, during which 18,720 participants developed hypertension. A composite healthy lifestyle score was calculated and a lifestyle-related metabolic signature comprising 76 NMR-based metabolites was developed using elastic net regression. Both a higher healthy lifestyle score (HR: 0.927; 95% CI: 0.913-0.940) and a higher metabolic signature level (HR: 0.675; 95% CI: 0.641-0.711) were independently and inversely associated with incident hypertension in a dose-response fashion. Causal mediation analysis demonstrated that the metabolic signature explained 57.08% (95% CI: 31.89%-83.15%) of the protective association between a healthy lifestyle and hypertension risk, with results consistent across subgroups stratified by age, education, and income. The predominance of White British participants limits generalizability, and self-reported lifestyle measures at baseline may introduce misclassification bias.

Implications Medical Affairs : This large-scale data linking a 76-metabolite NMR signature to 57% mediation of the lifestyle-hypertension association provides compelling evidence for metabolomics-guided prevention strategies, directly informing guideline recommendations around lifestyle-first interventions before pharmacotherapy initiation. Medical Affairs teams should leverage these findings to engage preventive cardiology and metabolomics KOLs around the scientific narrative that metabolic health underpins antihypertensive efficacy, strengthening the rationale for combination lifestyle-pharmacotherapy programs. This also opens actionable avenues for advocating metabolomics-based risk stratification as a future companion tool for identifying patients who need early pharmaceutical intervention despite apparently healthy lifestyles.

Long-term trends, age-period-cohort patterns, and future projections of incident stroke with comorbid hypertension and diabetes among older adults in Shenzhen, China: a population-based surveillance study.

Revue : BMC Geriatrics | Tier : NA | IF estimé : NA

Date de publication : 2026-07-28

Résultats principaux : This population-based retrospective surveillance study analyzed 40,128 stroke events with comorbid hypertension and diabetes in adults aged 60 years and over from the Shenzhen Cardiovascular and Cerebrovascular Disease Registry System between 2004 and 2023. Age-standardized incidence rates rose from 141.12 per 100,000 in 2004 to 414.78 per 100,000 in 2023, with average annual percent changes of 5.44% (crude) and 6.14% (age-standardized). Age-period-cohort analysis showed marked age effects, elevated period-related risks in recent years, and increasing cohort effects across successive birth cohorts, with Bayesian projections indicating continued increases from 2024 to 2033, especially in males. Joinpoint regression identified a late-period decline in trends after 2017, suggesting potential prevention impacts, but overall projections remain alarming with male-predominant trajectories. Key limitations include single-city registry data limiting generalizability, ecological study design, and inability to assess medication coverage or glycemic control quality.

Implications Medical Affairs : The near tripling of age-standardized incidence rates over two decades, with projected continued increases, underscores massive unmet cardiometabolic needs in the Chinese elderly population, supporting robust market development strategies for antihypertensive-antidiabetic combination therapies in China. Medical Affairs teams should engage China-based hypertension and cerebrovascular KOLs to highlight the urgent need for earlier and more aggressive blood pressure control in diabetic patients for primary and secondary stroke prevention, strengthening evidence-based clinical messaging. The cohort effects seen in younger birth cohorts signal a growing pipeline of high-risk patients, making this a compelling strategic data point for long-term antihypertensive product development and positioning in Asian markets.

Four weeks of isometric handgrip training results in lower awake ambulatory blood pressure compared with control: a randomized clinical trial.

Revue : Clinical Physiology and Functional Imaging | Tier : NA | IF estimé : NA

Date de publication : 2026-11

Résultats principaux : This randomized controlled trial enrolled 67 adults receiving antihypertensive medication with controlled hypertension, randomized to control (n=22), small muscle mass isometric training (SMM, handgrip; n=22), or large muscle mass isometric training (LMM, knee extension; n=23). After 12 supervised sessions over 4 weeks at 30% of maximal voluntary contraction, ANCOVA analyses adjusted for baseline office blood pressure showed significant between-group differences only for awake ambulatory blood pressure in the SMM versus control comparison: awake systolic BP (121.8+/-3.1 vs. 124.2+/-2.6 mmHg; p=0.017) and awake diastolic BP (76.3+/-3.6 vs. 80.8+/-4.2 mmHg; p=0.009). No significant differences were observed for LMM versus control, between the two exercise groups, or for 24-hour and sleep blood pressure outcomes. The finding that larger muscle mass recruitment did not produce superior antihypertensive effects challenges the volume hypothesis of isometric exercise benefits. Key limitations include the small sample size limiting statistical power, short 4-week follow-up duration, single-center design, and absence of medication adjustment monitoring during the trial.

Implications Medical Affairs : The demonstration that brief, low-equipment isometric handgrip training produces modest but statistically significant reductions in awake ambulatory blood pressure provides a practical non-pharmacological adjunct that can be incorporated into hypertension management protocols and used in Medical Affairs-supported patient education programs. Medical Affairs teams should monitor the growing evidence base for isometric exercise in hypertension to proactively prepare communications that position pharmacotherapy as the essential foundation upon which lifestyle adjuncts such as isometric training provide incremental benefit. The lack of large muscle mass training effect warrants further mechanistic investigation, and Medical Affairs could support investigator-initiated research into the neurovascular mechanisms of isometric exercise to generate deeper scientific engagement with sports medicine and hypertension KOLs.

Beyond Mean Blood Pressure Reduction: Distributional and Responder Effects of Bedtime Antihypertensive Dosing in Nondipping Hypertension. A Secondary Analysis of a Randomized Controlled Study.

Revue : Journal of Clinical Hypertension | Tier : 3 | IF estimé : ~5

Date de publication : 2026-10

Résultats principaux : This post hoc secondary analysis of a randomized controlled trial included 60 patients with uncontrolled hypertension and a nondipping pattern, randomized to morning or bedtime antihypertensive dosing (30 per group). After 2 months, bedtime dosing was associated with dramatically lower adjusted blood pressure: systolic BP difference of -18.1 mmHg (95% CI: -22.4 to -13.8), diastolic BP difference of -11.6 mmHg (95% CI: -14.5 to -8.8), and MAP difference of -13.9 mmHg (95% CI: -16.7 to -11.0) versus morning dosing. Target attainment (BP <140/90 mmHg) was achieved by 96.7% of bedtime versus 10.0% of morning dosing patients (risk ratio 9.67; 95% CI: 3.30-28.34). Median change-based estimates were less precise, with SBP and DBP median change differences of -9.5 and -3.0 mmHg whose confidence intervals included zero, indicating distributional heterogeneity of response. The study is severely limited by its small sample size (n=60), post hoc secondary design, single-center setting, and exploratory nature; the extraordinarily large treatment effect size warrants cautious interpretation and prospective confirmation.

Implications Medical Affairs : This exploratory chronotherapy data showing near-complete target attainment with bedtime versus morning dosing in nondipping patients, despite the small sample caveat, strongly motivates Medical Affairs investment in monitoring and supporting the growing HYGIA-class chronotherapy trial program as potential label extension opportunities for antihypertensive agents. Medical Affairs teams should proactively engage clinical hypertension KOLs around nondipping phenotyping using ambulatory blood pressure monitoring, as widespread adoption of this stratification tool could identify large patient subpopulations for optimized bedtime dosing strategies and associated pharmaceutical market segmentation. Positioning 24-hour blood pressure profile normalization as a superior treatment endpoint over mean daytime values aligns with evolving guideline priorities and may differentiate products demonstrating strong nocturnal and 24-hour BP control.

[Chinese guidelines for the diagnosis and management of chronic obstructive pulmonary disease (2026 revision)].

Revue : Chinese Journal of Tuberculosis and Respiratory Diseases | Tier : NA | IF estimé : NA

Date de publication : 2026-10-26

Résultats principaux : The 2026 revision of the Chinese COPD clinical guidelines was developed by the Chinese Thoracic Society and the Chinese Association of Chest Physicians, addressing 22 clinical questions using GRADE methodology. Key updates include recommendations for active and opportunistic case finding for early COPD diagnosis, ABE classification-based pharmacotherapy selection incorporating peripheral blood eosinophil counts (with triple ICS+LABA+LAMA therapy recommended for Group E patients with eosinophils >=300 cells/uL), and systematic pulmonary rehabilitation protocols. Notably, pulmonary hypertension is established as a criterion for long-term oxygen therapy in COPD patients with PaO2 of 55-60 mmHg or SpO2 88-89% at rest. The guideline also addresses biologic therapy indications, bronchoscopic lung volume reduction, and prevention of acute exacerbations through multimodal approaches including smoking cessation, triple therapy, and vaccination. Limitations include the predominantly Chinese evidence base and lack of specific guidance for COPD patients with concomitant systemic arterial hypertension management. This guideline is published in Chinese with an English abstract.

Implications Medical Affairs : The formal recognition of pulmonary hypertension as an indication for oxygen therapy in COPD within this major national guideline expands the clinical recognition framework for PH as a COPD comorbidity, relevant for Medical Affairs teams promoting pulmonary hypertension diagnostics and treatments in the Chinese healthcare system. For pharmaceutical companies with antihypertensive or pulmonary hypertension portfolios, engagement with Chinese pulmonology KOLs using this guideline as a reference point can open discussions about integrated cardiorespiratory risk management in COPD patients who frequently carry a comorbid systemic hypertension burden. The smoking cessation emphasis and environmental exposure prevention recommendations in the guideline align with Medical Affairs messaging opportunities around primary prevention of both COPD and hypertension in China.

Workers' Behavior and Hygienic Parameters Related to Blood Lead Levels: A Cross-Sectional Biomonitoring Study in an Italian Automotive Plant.

Revue : Toxics | Tier : NA | IF estimé : NA

Date de publication : 2026-10-18

Résultats principaux : This cross-sectional biomonitoring study examined blood lead (Pb) and zinc-protoporphyrin (ZnPP) levels in 464 workers from an Italian automotive plant, investigating associations with occupational, clinical, individual, lifestyle, and hygiene characteristics. Blue-collar and maintenance workers showed significantly higher blood Pb levels than white-collar workers (11.07 and 10.42 vs. 4.81 ug/dL), and forklift users and workers in production units 1 and 2 had higher blood Pb levels in unadjusted analyses. Exploratory multivariable analyses identified associations of ZnPP with hypertension and physical activity, though the model explained only 3.65% of variance, indicating limited explanatory power. Female sex, non-smoking status, and longer hair were associated with lower blood Pb levels, while the expected positive correlation between blood Pb and ZnPP was weak given the low xenobiotic concentrations detected. The cross-sectional design precludes causal inference, the explained variance is very low, the sample includes a single industrial plant potentially limiting generalizability, and effect sizes should be interpreted with caution as exploratory findings.

Implications Medical Affairs : Despite the modest associations reported, this study adds to the epidemiological evidence linking occupational low-level lead exposure to hypertension risk markers, supporting the positioning of workplace health programs as part of a comprehensive hypertension prevention strategy relevant to industrial and occupational medicine settings. Medical Affairs teams could leverage emerging occupational health guidelines on low-level metal exposure as triggers for expanded blood pressure screening in industrial populations, identifying a non-traditional patient access channel for antihypertensive products among working-age adults. Engagement with occupational medicine specialists and public health KOLs on environmental and occupational determinants of hypertension could broaden the Medical Affairs scientific presence beyond traditional cardiovascular clinical networks.

Anxiety Symptoms Assessed by the Goldberg Anxiety and Depression Scale, Lifestyle Habits, and Cardiometabolic Risk in Spanish Workers.

Revue : Actas Espanolas de Psiquiatria | Tier : NA | IF estimé : NA

Date de publication : 2026-10-15

Résultats principaux : This large cross-sectional observational study examined 119,336 Spanish workers undergoing routine occupational health examinations between 2021 and 2023, assessing anxiety symptoms using the Goldberg Anxiety and Depression Scale (GADS) alongside cardiometabolic risk factors. After multivariable adjustment, elevated GADS scores were independently associated with hypertension (OR 6.48; 95% CI: 5.89-7.09), hypertriglyceridemia (OR 5.31; 95% CI: 4.60-6.02), and impaired fasting glucose (OR 5.23; 95% CI: 4.64-5.83). Workers with elevated anxiety presented markedly higher prevalences of all major cardiometabolic abnormalities, including higher blood pressure, triglycerides, fasting glucose, BMI, waist circumference, and lower HDL cholesterol in both sexes. Physical inactivity and low adherence to the Mediterranean diet were associated with a less favorable cardiometabolic profile among anxious workers. The cross-sectional design precludes causal inference, the use of a screening tool rather than diagnostic instrument for anxiety limits precision of anxiety classification, and healthy-worker selection bias may underestimate the true population-level associations.

Implications Medical Affairs : The six-fold increase in hypertension odds associated with elevated anxiety scores in a 119,336-person occupational cohort provides robust epidemiological evidence for the psychosocial-cardiovascular risk axis, directly informing Medical Affairs messaging that hypertension treatment strategies must consider the mental health burden of the target population. Medical Affairs teams can use this evidence to support collaborations with occupational health organizations and health insurers around integrated cardiovascular-mental health screening programs, opening new patient identification pathways in the large employed population. Strategic engagement with KOLs at the interface of psychiatry and cardiovascular medicine could generate high-impact research programs exploring whether antihypertensive therapy or mental health interventions provide superior or synergistic benefits in the highly prevalent anxiety-hypertension comorbidity.

An Interpretable Machine Learning Framework Integrating Multi-Window Environmental Exposure for Hypertension Risk Assessment.

Revue : Toxics | Tier : NA | IF estimé : NA

Date de publication : 2026-09-30

Résultats principaux : This methodological study developed an interpretable machine learning framework integrating multi-window cumulative PM2.5 exposure features for hypertension risk prediction, validated in 2,523 participant-visits from the Beijing subsample of the China Health and Nutrition Survey. Five ML algorithms were evaluated (GLM, Lasso, Decision Tree, Random Forest, XGBoost), with test AUCs of 0.69-0.77 and Brier scores of 0.17-0.23 after person-level and year-based validation splits. Age, BMI, and waist circumference were consistently the most important predictors, with the 60-day PM2.5 window being the most important environmental exposure feature. Adding five PM2.5 window features improved test AUC by approximately 0.02-0.03 in ensemble models (p=0.03-0.05), with window-specific adjusted analyses showing inverse associations of PM2.5 with hypertension that were stronger for longer windows. Key limitations include the relatively small sample size (2,523 visits), single geographic location in Beijing, inability to establish causality, and the modest incremental predictive value of PM2.5 features despite statistical significance.

Implications Medical Affairs : The demonstration that multi-window PM2.5 exposure systematically improves interpretable ML-based hypertension risk prediction provides a replicable methodological framework that Medical Affairs teams can cite when supporting real-world evidence initiatives linking environmental data to patient outcomes in high-pollution markets such as China and Southeast Asia. This approach could inform the development of next-generation hypertension risk tools that incorporate environmental determinants alongside traditional clinical factors, potentially identifying novel high-risk patient subgroups for whom early pharmacotherapy initiation would be most beneficial. KOL engagement with environmental medicine, computational epidemiology, and precision hypertension specialists in Asia can position a pharmaceutical company as a scientific leader in the emerging field of environment-adjusted cardiovascular risk stratification.

Heart and Brain: Linking Obesity-Related Inflammation, Cardiovascular Disease and Cognitive Function.

Revue : Journal of Cardiovascular Development and Disease | Tier : NA | IF estimé : NA

Date de publication : 2026-09-22

Résultats principaux : This narrative review examines the mechanistic interconnections between obesity, cardiovascular disease, and cognitive dysfunction through shared pathways including endothelial dysfunction, chronic low-grade inflammation, dyslipidemia, and hypertension. Obesity and type 2 diabetes are associated with increased likelihood of cognitive impairment and dementia, with hypertension identified as a central mediator of both cardiovascular and cerebrovascular damage through macrovascular and microvascular injury. Obesity-driven obstructive sleep apnea leads to intermittent hypoxemia, neuroinflammation, and cognitive dysfunction, compounding the effect of obesity-related hypertension on brain vascular integrity. Greater brain volume loss was documented in middle-aged and older adults with obesity compared to young adults, suggesting cumulative vascular brain injury accelerated by hypertension and metabolic comorbidities. Dietary and lifestyle interventions are highlighted as protective strategies, though caloric restriction alone shows limited effectiveness. The narrative rather than systematic design may introduce selection bias in the evidence presented, and the lack of quantitative meta-analysis limits the precision of reported associations.

Implications Medical Affairs : This review reinforces hypertension as the central mediator in the heart-brain vascular axis, expanding the benefit framework for antihypertensive therapy beyond purely cardiovascular endpoints to include cognitive preservation, a compelling and differentiated value proposition for payers and patients in an aging population. Medical Affairs teams should develop scientific messaging around brain health protection as an additional benefit of sustained blood pressure control, engaging KOLs in both cardiovascular medicine and cognitive neurology to build a multidisciplinary evidence narrative around early antihypertensive intervention. Positioning antihypertensive therapies within the growing dementia prevention discourse creates an opportunity to expand therapeutic relevance, support guideline adoption of lower BP targets in midlife, and establish pharmaceutical brand leadership at the cardiovascular-neuroscience interface.

Nurses' role in the management of patients living with hypertension in a district hospital in Ghana: a qualitative study.

Revue : BMC Nursing | Tier : NA | IF estimé : NA

Date de publication : 2026-07-27

Résultats principaux : This qualitative exploratory and descriptive study was conducted between January and September 2025 with 15 nurse participants at Ga West Municipal Hospital in Ghana's Greater Accra Region, using semi-structured interviews and thematic analysis. Three overarching themes were identified: nurses' knowledge of hypertension management (encompassing BP monitoring, pharmacological interventions, and discharge planning), nurses' practical experiences (including use of clinical guidelines and barriers in practice), and community and follow-up support systems (peer groups, telephone follow-up, community pharmacies, and telehealth). Nurses reported challenges including limited knowledge, systemic resource constraints, staffing shortages, and weak community linkages that hinder optimal hypertension care delivery in this resource-limited setting. Recommendations focused on strengthening professional training, improving access to evidence-based guidelines, adequate staffing and resources, and integration of digital health tools. The study's primary limitations include the very small sample size (n=15) limited to a single hospital, qualitative non-generalizable design, and potential social desirability bias in interview responses.

Implications Medical Affairs : This qualitative evidence from sub-Saharan Africa highlights critical gaps in nursing capacity and healthcare infrastructure for hypertension management in low-resource settings, identifying high-unmet-need markets where Medical Affairs investment in healthcare provider education programs could significantly improve patient outcomes and brand positioning. Medical Affairs teams should consider developing nurse-targeted hypertension management toolkits and digital health decision-support tools tailored to resource-limited African settings, creating sustainable access pathways while demonstrating corporate social responsibility commitments. Partnerships with national nursing associations, Ministries of Health, and global health organizations in African countries around competency frameworks for hypertension care can serve as long-term market entry and access strategies for antihypertensive products in rapidly growing emerging markets.
