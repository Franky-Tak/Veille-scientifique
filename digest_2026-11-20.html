Cardiorenal biomarker-enhanced model for left ventricular hypertrophy in non-dialysis chronic kidney disease.

Revue : Renal failure | Tier : NA | IF estime : NA

Date de publication : 2026-11

Resultats principaux : This cross-sectional study enrolled 647 non-dialysis patients with chronic kidney disease (CKD) from Shanghai East Hospital to develop and validate a prediction model for left ventricular hypertrophy (LVH). The base clinical model incorporated female sex, age, body mass index, systolic blood pressure, hypertension, cardiovascular disease history, estimated glomerular filtration rate, and serum phosphorus, achieving an AUC of 0.789. Addition of three cardiorenal biomarkers—urine albumin-to-creatinine ratio (UACR), NT-proBNP, and soluble ST2 (sST2)—improved the AUC to 0.812 (95% CI: 0.769-0.856), with lower Brier score and AIC indicating better overall discrimination and calibration. In multivariable analysis, female sex, hypertension, serum phosphorus, UACR, and sST2 were independently associated with LVH, while NT-proBNP was significant only in univariable analysis. The study is limited by its cross-sectional design, single-center cohort, and lack of external validation, which restricts generalizability to other CKD populations.

Implications Medical Affairs : This model offers a practical approach to prioritize echocardiography referrals in CKD patients before symptomatic LVH develops, which may support early cardiovascular risk stratification in a population that commonly receives antihypertensive therapy including ACE inhibitors and ARBs. The finding that hypertension remains a key independent LVH predictor even after adjustment for renal parameters reinforces the cardiovascular benefit narrative for tight blood pressure control in CKD. For Medical Affairs, promoting the role of cardiorenal biomarkers—particularly sST2 and UACR—in integrated risk models aligns with opportunities to highlight comprehensive cardiovascular protection profiles of antihypertensive agents in KOL dialogues.

Subtypes of Multidimensional Frailty and Their Associated Factors Among Elderly Inpatients with Hypertension in a Tropical Region: A Latent Class Analysis.

Revue : Clinical interventions in aging | Tier : NA | IF estime : NA

Date de publication : 2026

Resultats principaux : This cross-sectional study enrolled 530 elderly hypertensive inpatients from three teaching hospitals in tropical Hainan, China, aiming to identify multidimensional frailty subtypes using latent class analysis of the 15-item Tilburg Frailty Indicator (TFI). A four-class solution with high entropy (0.893) was retained, identifying low frailty (46.6%), physical-dominant frailty (34.72%), psychological-social frailty (9.62%), and severe multidimensional frailty (9.06%) subtypes. The overall frailty prevalence was 52.08% using the standard TFI threshold, highlighting the high burden in this hospitalized hypertensive population. Nutritional risk and malnutrition showed the strongest associations with frailty severity, with OR 10.56 (95% CI: 4.38-25.47) for physical-dominant frailty and OR 53.62 (95% CI: 12.48-230.43) for severe frailty. The cross-sectional design prevents causal inference, and hospital-based sampling may introduce selection bias toward more severe or complex hypertensive patients.

Implications Medical Affairs : This study highlights that hospitalized elderly hypertensive patients carry a substantial and heterogeneous frailty burden, with distinct physical and psychosocial profiles requiring tailored management approaches beyond blood pressure control. From a Medical Affairs perspective, the association between nutritional status and frailty severity supports integrating nutritional assessment into hypertension management protocols and informs development of comprehensive care pathways for older patients. KOL engagement should emphasize that antihypertensive drug regimens in elderly frail patients must account for multidimensional vulnerability—particularly regarding drug tolerability and fall risk—to reinforce value propositions extending beyond simple blood pressure lowering.

Associations of gastrointestinal, sleep, and affective symptom combinations with cardiometabolic conditions: a cross-sectional study.

Revue : Frontiers in endocrinology | Tier : NA | IF estime : NA

Date de publication : 2026

Resultats principaux : This cross-sectional study analyzed 5,203 adults aged 20 years or older from the 2009-2010 NHANES database to examine associations between specific combinations of gastrointestinal, sleep, and affective symptoms with prevalent cardiometabolic conditions including hypertension, dyslipidemia, diabetes, and metabolic syndrome. Using survey-weighted logistic regression, the combination of gastrointestinal and sleep symptoms (G+S) was associated with significantly higher odds of hypertension (OR 1.92; 95% CI: 1.27-2.89), while having all three symptom domains (G+S+A) yielded an OR of 2.31 (95% CI: 1.38-3.86). A statistically significant positive trend was observed with increasing numbers of co-occurring symptom domains for both hypertension (p for trend = 0.007) and diabetes (p for trend = 0.041). Sleep and affective symptoms were associated with dyslipidemia (OR 1.67; 95% CI: 1.06-2.61), and the triple combination (G+S+A) was also associated with diabetes (OR 1.63; 95% CI: 1.15-2.30). The cross-sectional design precludes causal inference, and the reliance on NHANES self-reported symptom data introduces recall bias and potential misclassification.

Implications Medical Affairs : This epidemiological finding underscores a multisystem dimension to hypertension risk extending beyond traditional cardiovascular factors, suggesting that multisymptom burden screening in clinical practice could identify higher-risk patients earlier for preventive intervention. For Medical Affairs, this supports engagement with KOLs in psychosomatic medicine and primary care regarding integrated screening protocols identifying patients at elevated cardiovascular risk who may benefit from earlier antihypertensive intervention. Future research addressing causal pathways could reinforce messaging around comprehensive patient assessments in hypertension management, particularly in patients with comorbid GI and sleep disorders who represent an underserved target population.

In-hospital mortality and survival outcomes among admissions with diabetes mellitus or hypertension at a tertiary hospital in southwestern Nigeria: a five-year retrospective cohort study.

Revue : BMC public health | Tier : NA | IF estime : NA

Date de publication : 2026-08-25

Resultats principaux : This five-year retrospective cohort study analyzed 630 inpatient admissions at Obafemi Awolowo University Teaching Hospitals Complex in southwestern Nigeria (2017-2021), comprising 242 admissions with primary diagnosis of diabetes mellitus and 388 with hypertension, to compare in-hospital survival outcomes. The overall case fatality rate was 13.2%, with diabetes showing higher mortality (17.8%) compared to hypertension (10.3%). After adjustment for age and sex, hypertension admissions had a significantly lower cause-specific hazard of in-hospital death than diabetes admissions (adjusted HR 0.618; 95% CI: 0.397-0.960; p=0.032), with the Fine-Gray competing risks model corroborating a subdistribution HR of 0.499 (95% CI: 0.323-0.770; p=0.002). Notably, 61.4% of deaths occurred within the first 30 days of admission, emphasizing early critical vulnerability regardless of primary diagnosis. The study is limited by its retrospective single-center design, focus on primary diagnoses only, and absence of detailed medication data, which prevents assessment of treatment effects on mortality outcomes.

Implications Medical Affairs : These data highlight the relatively higher in-hospital mortality burden of diabetes compared to hypertension in a sub-Saharan African tertiary setting, reinforcing the need for integrated management of these two comorbid conditions prevalent in emerging markets. For Medical Affairs teams focused on the African market, these findings support dialogue with KOLs on early pharmacological intervention strategies for hypertension patients to prevent progression and hospitalization, with emphasis on cost-effective, guideline-adherent antihypertensive regimens. This evidence base can inform scientific platform development for healthcare systems strengthening presentations in West African cardiovascular conferences and regulatory submissions emphasizing population-specific outcomes data.

Prevalence and factors associated with ambulatory blood pressure phenotypes in a semi-urban Nigerian population.

Revue : BMC cardiovascular disorders | Tier : NA | IF estime : NA

Date de publication : 2026-08-25

Resultats principaux : This community-based cross-sectional study enrolled 348 adults in Ido-Ekiti, Nigeria, characterizing ambulatory blood pressure monitoring (ABPM)-derived hypertension phenotypes using 2024 European Society of Hypertension thresholds alongside standardized office BP measurements. Sustained hypertension was present in 36.9% of participants, white-coat hypertension in 17.8%, masked hypertension in 5.0%, and true normotension in 40.3%. Circadian abnormalities were highly prevalent, with nocturnal hypertension in 56.8% and morning hypertension in 40.6% of the population—rates substantially higher than those typically reported in higher-income settings. Age showed the strongest and most consistent gradient across all phenotypes (p < 0.001), while female sex was associated with higher morning hypertension (46.4% vs. 33.3%; p=0.013) and lower true normotension (29.6% vs. 41.0%; p=0.025) compared to males. The cross-sectional design limits causal inference, and the single semi-urban community limits national generalizability.

Implications Medical Affairs : The high prevalence of nocturnal and morning hypertension phenotypes in this African community underscores a critical unmet diagnostic need, as office-based measurements alone would miss a substantial proportion of patients requiring antihypertensive treatment. This study provides Medical Affairs with compelling epidemiological data to support ABPM advocacy in African primary care settings, reinforcing the diagnostic value and clinical rationale for comprehensive blood pressure phenotyping beyond office measurements. Findings on age-related and sex-specific phenotype distribution inform stratified patient identification strategies and can be leveraged in KOL engagements to promote evidence-based diagnosis that subsequently drives appropriate and timely antihypertensive prescribing.

Association of blood-pressure time-in-therapeutic range with morbidity and mortality in hypertensive patients: a retrospective cohort study in the community setting.

Revue : BMC public health | Tier : NA | IF estime : NA

Date de publication : 2026-08-24

Resultats principaux : This large population-based retrospective cohort included 38,002 adults newly diagnosed with hypertension in Madrid primary care settings between 2007 and 2008, followed for a median of 130.6 months (IQR: 123.1-137.3 months). Good blood pressure control (GBPC), defined as the highest time-in-therapeutic-range (TTR) quartile (≥81% for indirect or ≥67% for direct TTR) or last office BP < 140/90 mmHg, was associated with 28% lower all-cause mortality risk in patients diagnosed before age 55 years (95% CI: 5-45%) and 10% lower risk in those aged 55-74 years. Kidney/cardiovascular (K/CV) event risk was also reduced by 11-17% in patients under 55 years and 10-11% in those aged 55-74 years, depending on TTR methodology. No statistically significant associations between GBPC and outcomes were observed in patients aged 75 years or older, raising questions about treatment targets in the elderly. The study's retrospective design and reliance on electronic medical records carry inherent risks of coding errors and incomplete follow-up data for some patients.

Implications Medical Affairs : This large real-world study of over 38,000 patients provides compelling longitudinal evidence that sustained blood pressure control within the first year of diagnosis translates to meaningful reductions in mortality and cardiovascular events, particularly in patients diagnosed under age 75. The absence of benefit at age 75 and older raises important clinical questions about treatment targets in the elderly that Medical Affairs teams should monitor as clinical guidelines continue to evolve for this population. These findings directly support the positioning of antihypertensive agents with favorable adherence profiles and predictable BP control, reinforcing the value of therapeutic consistency in real-world primary care settings as a commercially differentiating attribute.

Joint modeling of hypertension and cardiovascular disease among Iranian Hajj pilgrims using Bayesian multivariate generalized linear mixed model.

Revue : BMC cardiovascular disorders | Tier : NA | IF estime : NA

Date de publication : 2026-08-24

Resultats principaux : This retrospective cross-sectional study analyzed medical records of 478,360 Iranian Hajj pilgrims from seven pilgrimage seasons (2013-2022), jointly modeling hypertension and cardiovascular disease using a Bayesian multivariate generalized linear mixed model (GLMM) accounting for province-level geographic clustering. The joint Bayesian model demonstrated substantially better fit than separate univariate models (ΔDIC = 51,720.5) and identified a strong residual correlation between hypertension and CVD (ρ = 0.75), indicating shared biological determinants. Hypertension prevalence was 20.90% and CVD prevalence was 3.93%; each additional year of age increased hypertension odds by 14% (OR 1.14; 95% CrI: 1.13-1.15) and CVD odds by 11% (OR 1.11; 95% CrI: 1.10-1.11). Diabetes was the strongest modifiable predictor, increasing hypertension odds 9.15-fold (95% CrI: 7.57-10.95) and CVD odds 3.07-fold (95% CrI: 2.90-3.27), while women had higher odds of hypertension (OR 2.15) but lower CVD odds (OR 0.80) than men. Geographic heterogeneity was substantial—HTN prevalence ranged from 13.52% (Qom) to 32.39% (East Azerbaijan)—though the retrospective design and medical record sourcing limit causal inference.

Implications Medical Affairs : This uniquely large epidemiological dataset from mass gatherings reveals a strong correlated burden of hypertension and cardiovascular disease across Iranian provinces, providing Medical Affairs with important geographic targeting data for cardiovascular prevention initiatives. The identified key risk factors—aging, female sex, and diabetes—align with established cardiovascular risk stratification models and reinforce value messaging for comprehensive antihypertensive strategies that also address diabetic comorbidity in a Middle Eastern population. Regional variability in hypertension burden (nearly 3-fold difference across provinces) suggests opportunities for geographically targeted KOL engagement and differentiated market access strategies in regions with disproportionately high cardiovascular risk.

Coexistence of primary aldosteronism and hypercortisolism in patients investigated for secondary hypertension and/or adrenal incidentaloma.

Revue : BMC endocrine disorders | Tier : NA | IF estime : NA

Date de publication : 2026-08-21

Resultats principaux : This retrospective single-center study at Marmara University Hospital (2018-2023) analyzed 110 patients with confirmed primary aldosteronism (PA) who were also evaluated for hypercortisolism, examining the coexistence of Cushing's syndrome and mild autonomous cortisol secretion (MACS). The prevalence of overt Cushing's syndrome and MACS among PA patients was 8.1% and 10.0%, respectively, indicating that approximately 18% of PA patients have concurrent glucocorticoid secretion abnormalities. Diabetes mellitus was significantly more common in patients with concurrent hypercortisolism (45% vs. 21.1%; p=0.035), and mean HbA1c levels were higher in this group (5.85% vs. 5.61%; p=0.013), suggesting additive metabolic disruption from dual hormonal excess. Serum potassium levels were significantly lower in patients with overt Cushing's syndrome (p=0.032), pointing to enhanced mineralocorticoid effects in this subgroup. Major study limitations include the small sample size (110 patients), single-center retrospective design, and absence of prospective follow-up limiting assessment of long-term cardiovascular outcomes in this complex patient population.

Implications Medical Affairs : The finding that nearly one in five PA patients may have concurrent cortisol excess has significant implications for secondary hypertension management, as standard antihypertensive therapy alone may be insufficient without addressing the underlying endocrine pathology. For Medical Affairs, this study supports scientific platform messaging around the importance of comprehensive endocrine evaluation in treatment-resistant or secondary hypertension, particularly in advocating for structured diagnostic algorithms in endocrinology-hypertension clinical pathways. This evidence can be leveraged in KOL interactions to highlight the specialized endocrine origin of a subset of hypertensive patients, reinforcing the need for multidisciplinary cardiovascular-endocrine collaboration and potentially positioning specific therapies targeting aldosterone excess.

Apixaban reduces hepatic decompensation in patients with cirrhosis (ApiHep): A randomized controlled trial.

Revue : Hepatology communications | Tier : NA | IF estime : NA

Date de publication : 2026-12-01

Resultats principaux : This open-label randomized controlled trial (ApiHep) with blinded endpoint assessment enrolled 134 adults with cirrhosis (Child-Pugh B7-C10) without splenoportal axis thrombosis, randomizing them 1:1 to apixaban 2.5 mg twice daily plus standard medical therapy (SMT) or SMT alone for 12 months. Patients receiving apixaban experienced significantly lower rates of hepatic decompensation compared to SMT alone (33.1% vs. 54.3%; absolute risk reduction 21.2%; 95% CI: 4.5%-38.2%; p=0.013). Although numerically lower rates were observed for ascites (6.4% vs. 17.4%), hepatic encephalopathy (21.2% vs. 36.0%), and variceal bleeding (7.5% vs. 14.6%) in the apixaban arm, none reached statistical significance for these secondary endpoints. Importantly, apixaban did not significantly increase bleeding events—major bleeding occurred in 9.0% vs. 11.9% and overall bleeding in 41.8% vs. 46.3%, demonstrating a favorable safety profile in this vulnerable population. Key limitations include the open-label design, small sample size (134 patients), and the fact that the primary outcome relates to portal rather than systemic arterial hypertension, limiting direct relevance to conventional antihypertensive Medical Affairs messaging.

Implications Medical Affairs : Although this trial concerns portal hypertension in liver cirrhosis rather than primary arterial hypertension, it demonstrates the safety and therapeutic benefit of a direct oral anticoagulant in a comorbid population where anticoagulation has historically been avoided due to bleeding concerns. For Medical Affairs, this RCT provides evidence that could support expanded clinical use discussions around anticoagulants in patients with portal hypertension, though the population's specificity (Child-Pugh B/C cirrhosis) limits direct extrapolation to the general hypertensive patient population. The study reinforces the growing evidence for anticoagulation in cirrhosis and may prompt KOL discussions about managing hypertension in patients with complex liver pathology, particularly where beta-blockers are concurrently used for portal hypertension prophylaxis.

Adiposity profiles in relation to cardiometabolic disease risk in South African adults with HIV on long-term antiretroviral therapy.

Revue : HIV medicine | Tier : NA | IF estime : NA

Date de publication : 2026-11-19

Resultats principaux : This cross-sectional propensity score-matched study from the Middle-Aged Soweto Cohort, South Africa, compared adiposity and cardiometabolic profiles between ART-experienced people with HIV (PWH) and HIV-uninfected peers (PWoH), stratified by sex and matched on age, lifestyle, socioeconomics, and BMI category. No significant HIV-related differences in regional fat distribution (VAT, SAT, android, gynoid fat) were observed in either male or female participants, contrasting with evidence from high-income country studies. Unexpectedly, male HIV-uninfected participants had significantly higher prevalence of both type 2 diabetes (9.0% vs. 3.8%; SMD=0.22; p=0.031) and hypertension (48.7% vs. 42.5%; SMD=0.33; p=0.022) compared to matched male PWH. Visceral adipose tissue (VAT), but not subcutaneous adipose tissue (SAT), was positively associated with post-load glucose in both sexes (p<0.017), regardless of HIV status, underscoring VAT as a shared metabolic risk driver. The study is limited by its cross-sectional design, relatively small sex-stratified sample sizes, and inability to assess qualitative adipose tissue differences at ectopic sites such as liver, skeletal muscle, and pancreas.

Implications Medical Affairs : This study challenges the prevailing paradigm that long-term ART exposure in sub-Saharan Africa leads to excess cardiometabolic risk, providing important reassurance data relevant to HIV-hypertension co-management strategies in this high-burden region. For Medical Affairs teams active in Africa, these findings suggest that hypertension management in HIV-positive populations on established ART may not require fundamentally different cardiovascular risk assumptions than in HIV-negative patients of similar demographic profiles. This evidence base can support KOL engagements focused on integrated HIV-cardiovascular care pathways, highlighting visceral adiposity—rather than HIV status alone—as the key metabolic driver warranting antihypertensive and metabolic intervention.

Low-valine diet ameliorates pathological cardiac hypertrophy by inhibiting LONP1-mediated degradation of ATP5F1B in male rodents.

Revue : Nature communications | Tier : NA | IF estime : NA

Date de publication : 2026-10-21

Resultats principaux : This study combined a meta-analysis of dietary branched-chain amino acid (BCAA) intake data with NHANES epidemiological findings and pre-clinical experiments in hypertensive male rodents to investigate the cardiovascular effects of dietary valine restriction. The meta-analysis and NHANES data indicated a positive association between cumulative dietary valine intake and cardiovascular risk in humans. In hypertensive male rodents, a low-valine diet significantly alleviated pathological cardiac hypertrophy and improved cardiac function compared to normal-valine diet controls. Mechanistically, valine accumulation under hypertension enhanced binding to ATP synthase subunit beta (ATP5F1B), promoting interaction with and degradation by mitochondrial peptidase LONP1; a low-valine diet reduced this degradation, preserving mitochondrial function and homeostasis. The primary limitation is that mechanistic evidence is exclusively from male rodent models, with clinical translation of these findings to human hypertensive patients, particularly females, remaining unestablished.

Implications Medical Affairs : Published in Nature Communications, this high-visibility research provides novel mechanistic insight into how dietary valine restriction may confer cardioprotective effects in hypertension by preserving mitochondrial integrity, representing a potentially actionable lifestyle intervention to complement antihypertensive pharmacotherapy. For Medical Affairs, this study signals growing scientific interest in BCAA metabolism as a cardiovascular risk modifier, warranting monitoring for investigator-initiated studies or combined lifestyle-pharmacological intervention research opportunities. The male-only rodent design limits immediate clinical applicability, but the NHANES epidemiological correlation provides a translational bridge warranting prospective human trials and consideration of dietary valine as a novel biomarker or therapeutic target in hypertension management.

Risk factor control and type 2 diabetes event risks in people with high genetic susceptibility to diabetes.

Revue : Nutrition journal | Tier : NA | IF estime : NA

Date de publication : 2026-08-21

Resultats principaux : This prospective cohort study from the UK Biobank included 382,313 participants with a median follow-up of 13.5 years to examine whether optimal control of modifiable risk factors—including non-smoking, high physical activity, healthy diet, healthy sleep, low BMI, and absence of hypertension—could mitigate genetic excess risk for type 2 diabetes (T2D). Among individuals with high genetic risk who achieved control of all six risk factors, T2D incidence was actually lower than in the low genetic risk group (HR 0.79; 95% CI: 0.68-0.92), demonstrating that comprehensive risk factor management can overcome genetic predisposition. During the median 13.5-year follow-up, 9,210 incident T2D cases were identified in the high genetic risk group, underscoring the population-level burden of diabetes in genetically susceptible individuals. However, individuals with both high genetic risk and prediabetes who achieved all six controlled factors still faced a higher T2D risk compared to those with low genetic risk and normal glucose levels. The study is limited by its observational design preventing causal inference, the UK Biobank demographic toward healthier older adults of predominantly European ancestry, and binary classification of genetic risk based on median polygenic risk score.

Implications Medical Affairs : This large-scale UK Biobank evidence demonstrates that rigorous hypertension control—as one of six key modifiable factors—contributes meaningfully to overcoming genetic predisposition to type 2 diabetes, reinforcing the cardiovascular-metabolic continuum benefits of antihypertensive therapy. For Medical Affairs, this supports messaging that positions antihypertensive agents not merely as BP-lowering drugs but as integral components of comprehensive metabolic risk reduction strategies, particularly in patients with known genetic predisposition to diabetes. The persistence of elevated T2D risk in prediabetic individuals despite optimal risk control underscores an important unmet need and positions the hypertension-diabetes interface as a critical target for combination pharmaceutical and lifestyle intervention strategies.
