[The method of counter differentiation in analysis of co-morbid relationships of arterial hypertension].

Revue : Problemy sotsial'noi gigieny, zdravookhraneniia i istorii meditsiny | Tier : NA | IF estimé : NA

Date de publication : 2026-12-15

Résultats principaux : This large-scale retrospective epidemiological study enrolled 259,388 patients at the N.A. Semashko National Research Institute of Public Health in Russia to assess the directionality and strength of comorbid relationships of arterial hypertension (AH) using an original counter differentiation method. The study design allowed separate analysis of afferent associations (influence of other diseases on AH development risk) and efferent associations (influence of AH on the risk of other diseases) across age groups spanning 10 to 90+ years, providing an age-stratified view of hypertension comorbidity. The primary finding was that AH almost never occurs in isolation, with statistically highly significant associations (p<0.001) identified across all evaluated comorbid pairs, highlighting the systemic comorbidity burden of hypertension. No detailed sub-group quantitative outcomes were provided in the abstract, though the directional methodology adds a novel dimension beyond standard cross-sectional comorbidity analyses. Key limitations include the retrospective design relying on administrative data from a single research institute, potential ICD coding inaccuracies, and restriction to a Russian patient population limiting external generalizability; the article is published in Russian with only an English abstract.

Implications Medical Affairs : This study reinforces the systemic comorbidity burden of arterial hypertension with bidirectional causality mapping, providing an evidence base for positioning antihypertensive therapies within broader multi-morbidity management frameworks for Medical Affairs communications. The Russian epidemiological context opens a strategic dialogue for Eastern European and CIS market KOL engagement around integrated care pathways that address hypertension as both a consequence and driver of other chronic diseases. The counter differentiation methodology represents an innovative epidemiological approach that could be adopted in future real-world evidence studies to better characterize treatment gaps in comorbid hypertensive populations.

Prevalence of systemic and ocular comorbidities in patients aged 18-50 years with retinal vein occlusion.

Revue : International ophthalmology | Tier : NA | IF estimé : NA

Date de publication : 2026-10-29

Résultats principaux : This retrospective multicenter cohort study used the TriNetX US Collaborative Network to identify 5,257 young adults (aged 18-50 years) with retinal vein occlusion (RVO), examining both branch (BRVO) and central (CRVO) subtypes, and characterized comorbidity prevalence both prior to and within 90 days of RVO diagnosis. Before RVO diagnosis, 66.4% of patients had at least one documented risk factor, with hypertension being the most prevalent (25%), followed by obesity (17.4%) and type 2 diabetes mellitus (13.3%), while primary thrombophilia (2.2%) and hematologic malignancy (1.6%) were comparatively rare. Within 90 days after RVO diagnosis, 12.3% of patients received at least one newly coded comorbidity, most commonly hypertension (4.5%), obesity (2.3%), and hyperlipidemia (1.9%), suggesting RVO may serve as a sentinel event prompting vascular risk factor screening. Among patients without any prior documented risk factors, 3.7% received a new risk factor diagnosis in the post-RVO period, reinforcing the value of systemic workup following RVO in younger patients. The study is limited by reliance on diagnostic coding which may underestimate true comorbidity rates, the retrospective design precluding causal inference, and the lack of RVO subtype-specific comorbidity analysis.

Implications Medical Affairs : The finding that hypertension is the single most common comorbidity in young adults with RVO—preceding the eye event in 25% of cases—provides compelling evidence for microvascular damage as a clinically visible manifestation of uncontrolled blood pressure in younger populations, supporting the positioning of antihypertensive therapies around holistic end-organ protection. From a Medical Affairs perspective, this study creates an opportunity to engage ophthalmology KOLs in cross-specialty educational programs focused on cardiovascular risk screening in RVO patients, potentially expanding hypertension awareness campaigns beyond traditional cardiology and nephrology specialties. These data strengthen the argument for proactive hypertension detection in young adults attending ophthalmology clinics, an actionable insight for patient identification strategies and collaboration with professional societies to incorporate BP measurement into routine eye care protocols.

Early echocardiographic hemodynamic phenotypes and clinical outcomes in congenital diaphragmatic hernia.

Revue : European journal of pediatrics | Tier : NA | IF estimé : NA

Date de publication : 2026-10-28

Résultats principaux : This retrospective cohort study included 66 infants with congenital diaphragmatic hernia (CDH) admitted to a tertiary neonatal ICU between 2021 and 2025, all undergoing echocardiography within 24 hours of birth, with classification into three hemodynamic phenotypes: no/mild pulmonary hypertension (PH, n=21), right ventricular (RV)-predominant PH (n=22), and left ventricular (LV)-involved PH (n=23). The composite primary endpoint of ECMO use or death occurred in 0%, 27.3%, and 65.2% of patients in the respective phenotype groups (P<0.001), demonstrating a strong graded association between phenotype severity and adverse outcomes. After adjustment for observed-to-expected lung-to-head ratio (O/E LHR), ordinal phenotype severity remained significantly associated with ECMO or death (OR 5.98; 95% CI 1.76-20.31; P=0.004), though the specific comparison between LV-involved and RV-predominant PH lost statistical significance after this adjustment (OR 3.42; 95% CI 0.87-15.10). LV-involved PH was associated with greater respiratory severity scores and more frequent LV dysfunction, while O/E LHR did not significantly differ between the two PH groups, suggesting echocardiographic phenotyping adds prognostic value beyond prenatal anatomic markers alone. Study limitations include the small sample size (n=66), single-center design, and uncertain independent prognostic value of LV-involved PH beyond O/E LHR, requiring multi-center validation.

Implications Medical Affairs : This study addresses pulmonary hypertension in neonatal CDH rather than systemic arterial hypertension; its relevance to the classical hypertension epidemiology framework is limited, but it contributes to the broader understanding of PH phenotyping as a prognostic tool in critical care settings. From a Medical Affairs perspective, companies with pulmonary arterial hypertension (PAH) products or pipeline assets in neonatal PH may find this study valuable for KOL engagement in neonatal cardiology, where echocardiographic-guided treatment strategies are gaining traction. The demonstrated value of phenotype-based risk stratification in complex pediatric cases aligns with a precision medicine approach that Medical Affairs teams can leverage when engaging with rare disease or neonatology specialist networks.

Examining the Association Between Food Insecurity and Pregnancy Outcomes.

Revue : Maternal & child nutrition | Tier : NA | IF estimé : NA

Date de publication : 2026-10

Résultats principaux : This cross-sectional analysis used state-representative data from Phase 8 of the Pregnancy Risk Assessment Monitoring System (PRAMS) across 10 US states, restricted to WIC-eligible mothers (income <185% federal poverty level or Medicaid enrollment), yielding a sample of 776 women. The primary exposure was prenatal food insecurity (reported by 13.65% of participants), and outcomes included gestational hypertension, gestational diabetes, postpartum depression, and gestational weight gain. After multivariable adjustment, food insecurity was significantly associated with higher odds of gestational hypertension (OR 1.38; 95% CI 1.18-1.60), gestational diabetes (OR 1.22; 95% CI 1.00-1.49), postpartum depression (OR 2.47; 95% CI 2.16-2.82), and suboptimal gestational weight gain (OR 1.29; 95% CI 1.09-1.52). Subgroup analysis showed WIC participation did not significantly moderate these associations, suggesting food assistance programs alone are insufficient to mitigate the full impact of food insecurity on pregnancy health; adjustment for depression attenuated some associations. Limitations include the cross-sectional design preventing causal inference, reliance on self-reported outcomes, restriction to WIC-eligible populations limiting generalizability to higher-income pregnant women, and potential residual confounding.

Implications Medical Affairs : The significant association between food insecurity and gestational hypertension (OR 1.38) establishes a key social determinant pathway for pregnancy-induced hypertension, expanding the conventional biomedical risk framework and supporting the inclusion of social screening tools in obstetric care. From a Medical Affairs perspective, these data provide a compelling narrative for health equity-focused educational initiatives targeting obstetricians, midwives, and payers, linking antihypertensive treatment access to addressing upstream social determinants of maternal cardiovascular health. Partnering with maternal health and food security advocacy organizations for community-based screening programs could generate real-world evidence supporting early hypertension detection among socioeconomically vulnerable pregnant women, a population with significant unmet medical need.

Above-Threshold Ambulatory Systolic Pressure and Left Ventricular Mass in Chronic Kidney Disease: Does AUC Add Beyond Mean Pressure?

Revue : Journal of clinical hypertension (Greenwich, Conn.) | Tier : 3 | IF estimé : ~3-4

Date de publication : 2026-10

Résultats principaux : This cross-sectional study enrolled 212 Chinese adults with non-dialysis chronic kidney disease (CKD) who underwent both ambulatory blood pressure monitoring (ABPM) and echocardiography, with the primary question being whether above-threshold systolic blood pressure (SBP) area under the curve (AUC) — capturing both the magnitude and duration of SBP above reference values — provided prognostic information about left ventricular mass index (LVMI) beyond daytime and nighttime mean SBP. Daytime AUC was defined as time above 135 mmHg and nighttime AUC above 120 mmHg; together they were significantly associated with LVMI (P<0.001; adjusted R²=0.345). From the 25th to 75th percentile, adjusted LVMI was 3.46 g/m² higher per daytime AUC increment and 5.56 g/m² higher per nighttime AUC increment, with nighttime AUC showing greater impact on cardiac hypertrophy. AUC attributable to daytime and nighttime mean SBP accounted for 89.7% of the cohort-average above-threshold burden, and the residual AUC not captured by period means was not significantly associated with LVMI after adjustment (0.69 g/m² per IQR; 95% CI -2.00 to 3.37), suggesting mean SBP largely captures the relevant prognostic information. Limitations include the cross-sectional design, single-center Chinese CKD cohort, and inability to establish causality; the findings may not generalize to Western CKD populations or dialysis patients.

Implications Medical Affairs : This study clarifies that above-threshold SBP AUC does not add substantial prognostic value beyond conventional mean ABPM measures for predicting left ventricular hypertrophy in CKD, providing important practical reassurance that standard ABPM interpretation remains the appropriate monitoring standard for this high-risk population. From a Medical Affairs perspective, these findings support scientific exchange with nephrology and cardiology KOLs around the adequacy of mean ABPM as a monitoring tool, and can inform the clinical rationale for antihypertensive products — particularly those with demonstrated effects on nighttime BP reduction — in protecting against LV hypertrophy in CKD. Incorporating nighttime blood pressure as a key pharmacodynamic endpoint in future investigator-initiated studies or sponsored research in CKD populations could strengthen the product differentiation narrative for agents with pronounced nocturnal antihypertensive effects.

[Clinical characteristics and in-hospital outcomes of patients receiving antithrombotic therapy in a cardiac intensive care unit in sub-Saharan Africa].

Revue : The Pan African medical journal | Tier : NA | IF estimé : NA

Date de publication : 2026-06-09

Résultats principaux : This retrospective, observational study enrolled 1,000 patients admitted to the Cardiac ICU of the Institute of Cardiology of Abidjan (Côte d'Ivoire) over 12 years, analyzing antithrombotic therapy use and complications across two time periods: 2013-2017 and 2018-2024, with a mean patient age of 59.5 ± 14.1 years. Hypertension was identified as the most prevalent cardiovascular risk factor in the cohort, and acute coronary syndrome was the primary indication for antithrombotic therapy, reflecting the evolving epidemiological transition in sub-Saharan Africa. The proportion of patients receiving antithrombotic therapy increased significantly between the two periods (76.5% vs 78.7%, p=0.004), with fibrinolytic agent use nearly tripling (2.6% vs 6.6%), reflecting improved access to thrombolytic therapies in the region. Adverse events were predominantly hemorrhagic, with independent risk factors including advanced age (>65 years), use of fibrinolytics, and concomitant use of multiple antithrombotic agents; in-hospital mortality was significantly higher in patients experiencing hemorrhagic events, with no significant improvement in mortality between periods. Limitations include retrospective design, potential for coding inconsistencies in the sub-Saharan African administrative context, single-center setting at a tertiary referral center, and lack of long-term follow-up data.

Implications Medical Affairs : The identification of hypertension as the dominant cardiovascular risk factor in this sub-Saharan African cardiac ICU cohort — driving increased need for antithrombotic therapy — underscores the growing unmet need for effective hypertension management in low- and middle-income countries (LMICs), with direct implications for acute cardiovascular event burden. From a Medical Affairs perspective, these findings support investment in access programs and KOL partnerships in sub-Saharan Africa, where hypertension prevalence is rising yet treatment rates remain inadequate, creating a strategic opportunity for pharmaceutical engagement with regional cardiology societies and health ministries. Leveraging this real-world evidence to inform educational initiatives about the consequences of uncontrolled hypertension — including acute coronary events necessitating intensive antithrombotic regimens — could position the company as a credible partner in addressing LMIC cardiovascular health equity challenges.

Prospective Associations of Diet Quality During Pregnancy With Cardiovascular Health (Life's Essential 8) Among Women in Midlife.

Revue : Journal of human nutrition and dietetics | Tier : NA | IF estimé : NA

Date de publication : 2026-12

Résultats principaux : This prospective cohort study from Project Viva enrolled 776 women during early pregnancy (1999-2002, mean age 32.7 years) and followed them to a midlife visit (2017-2021, mean age 51.1 years), assessing the association between dietary quality in pregnancy and cardiovascular health (CVH) approximately 20 years later, using the AHA's Life's Essential 8 scoring tool (range 0-100). Three diet scores were calculated from food frequency questionnaires completed during the first and second trimesters: DASH, Mediterranean Diet (MD), and Dietary Inflammatory Index (DII ×-1); internal z-scores were generated and averaged across trimesters. Higher DASH z-scores were significantly associated with better overall CVH (β 3.3; 95% CI 2.2-4.3), behavioral (3.5; 2.5-4.4), and biomedical (3.1; 1.5-4.7) subscores; similarly, higher MD z-scores were associated with better overall CVH (2.4; 1.4-3.4), and higher DII scores with better overall CVH (2.6; 1.6-3.7). Sub-domain analyses revealed that better pregnancy diet was specifically associated with higher CVH scores for diet, physical activity, and BMI, and that both DASH and DII scores were associated with blood pressure at midlife, highlighting a specific anti-hypertensive effect of pregnancy dietary quality. Limitations include the predominantly non-Hispanic White (70%) and college-educated (76%) sample limiting generalizability to diverse populations, the approximately 20-year exposure-outcome gap introducing many potential confounders, and the inability to disentangle sustained dietary habits from pregnancy-specific effects.

Implications Medical Affairs : This study provides prospective evidence that dietary quality during pregnancy — particularly adherence to the DASH and Mediterranean diets — is independently associated with better blood pressure status approximately two decades later, establishing a compelling lifecourse prevention perspective with important implications for hypertension prevention strategies targeting women of reproductive age. From a Medical Affairs perspective, these findings strengthen the scientific foundation for lifestyle-based messaging in hypertension prevention and create opportunities to engage KOLs in maternal health, nutrition, and women's cardiovascular health around early dietary interventions as components of long-term hypertension risk reduction. Sponsoring or supporting extended follow-up studies in diverse cohorts to validate the blood pressure findings could generate impactful real-world evidence supporting preventive care narratives aligned with current hypertension guidelines that increasingly emphasize lifestyle modification.

Maternity versus non-maternity healthcare settings for the diagnosis of gestational diabetes or diabetes in pregnancy.

Revue : The Cochrane database of systematic reviews | Tier : NA | IF estimé : NA

Date de publication : 2026-10-29

Résultats principaux : This Cochrane systematic review assessed the effectiveness of maternity versus non-maternity healthcare settings for diagnosing gestational diabetes mellitus (GDM) or diabetes in pregnancy, searching CENTRAL, MEDLINE, Embase, and CINAHL through April 2026 for randomized controlled trials and controlled non-randomized studies. Only one eligible RCT with 781 participants was identified (390 in maternity settings, 391 in non-maternity settings), and all outcomes except screening compliance were evaluated by per-protocol rather than intention-to-treat analysis, introducing substantial methodological limitations. Low-certainty evidence suggested maternity settings may increase compliance with screening and follow-up guidelines (RR 1.69; 95% CI 1.53-1.87) and may reduce time to antenatal diabetes care access (MD -4.70 days; 95% CI -6.03 to -3.37); effects on GDM diagnosis, pre-eclampsia/hypertensive disorders, and neonatal outcomes were highly uncertain (very low certainty). No significant difference was observed for pre-eclampsia risk (RR 1.24; 95% CI 0.56-2.75; very low certainty), and many critical outcomes including maternal death, intensive care admission, and infections were not reported by the included trial. Limitations are profound: single included RCT, per-protocol analysis design that nullifies randomization advantages, lack of diabetes-in-pregnancy (as distinct from GDM) data, and absence of economic or long-term follow-up data.

Implications Medical Affairs : This Cochrane review exposes a critical evidence gap in understanding how healthcare delivery organization affects GDM screening uptake — a condition strongly linked to subsequent development of hypertension, type 2 diabetes, and cardiovascular disease — creating an opportunity for Medical Affairs teams to support pragmatic research in this area. From a Medical Affairs perspective, the very low certainty of evidence for hypertensive disorder outcomes in pregnancy underscores the need for well-designed RCTs with robust intention-to-treat analyses to inform clinical guidelines and health system policy, a gap that could be addressed through company-sponsored investigator grants or collaborative research programs. Engaging KOLs in obstetrics, endocrinology, and health systems research around the cardiometabolic consequences of GDM — including downstream hypertension risk — could position the company as a thought leader in integrated maternal-cardiovascular care pathways.

Analysis of risk factors for glaucoma associated with environmental persistent pollutant exposure based on the NHANES database.

Revue : Computational biology and chemistry | Tier : NA | IF estimé : NA

Date de publication : 2026-10-27

Résultats principaux : This cross-sectional analysis used NHANES data from 2,272 US participants to examine associations between 12 PFAS compounds and 4 heavy metals (serum concentrations) and glaucoma prevalence, applying multivariable logistic regression, restricted cubic spline analysis, and machine learning models with SHAP interpretability. Participants with glaucoma had significantly higher prevalence of diabetes and hypertension, as well as nominally elevated blood lead and MPAH levels, suggesting vascular and systemic comorbidity clustering in glaucoma cases. In the primary analysis, inorganic mercury showed a nominal inverse association with glaucoma, while higher MPAH and blood lead quartiles showed nominal positive associations; however, none of these associations survived Benjamini-Hochberg FDR correction (minimum q=0.128), rendering all findings hypothesis-generating rather than confirmatory. Exploratory pathway decomposition suggested that the nominal MPAH-glaucoma association might be partly mediated through age, race/ethnicity, and blood pressure, implicating vascular mechanisms — including hypertension — in the environmental pollutant-glaucoma pathway, though these results are very preliminary. Machine learning models achieved modest discriminative performance (AUC 0.62-0.74) for glaucoma risk prediction; study limitations include cross-sectional design, self-reported glaucoma, inability to distinguish glaucoma subtypes, and lack of FDR-corrected significant findings.

Implications Medical Affairs : While the primary focus is glaucoma and environmental pollutants, the consistent finding of hypertension as a comorbidity and potential mediator in the pollutant-glaucoma pathway reinforces the systemic vascular hypothesis linking BP control to microvascular eye disease, an area of increasing interest for ophthalmology-cardiovascular KOL collaboration. From a Medical Affairs perspective, the lack of FDR-corrected significant associations for environmental pollutants limits the immediate clinical messaging relevance of this study, but the NHANES-based methodology and machine learning approach may inform future real-world evidence strategies examining hypertension-related comorbidity clustering. Monitoring the emerging environmental epidemiology literature for validated hypertension risk associations could inform a broader preventive narrative linking pollution exposure, vascular inflammation, and hypertension that may align with advocacy and public health positioning strategies.

Redlining and Prevalent Obesity, Hypertension, and Diabetes in GuLF Study Participants.

Revue : Obesity science & practice | Tier : NA | IF estimé : NA

Date de publication : 2026-03-13

Résultats principaux : This cross-sectional analysis from the Gulf Long-Term Follow-Up (GuLF) Study examined associations between historical Home Owners' Loan Corporation (HOLC) redlining grades and prevalent obesity, hypertension, and diabetes in 1,653 participants with available residential address data linked to HOLC grades at the census-tract level. Historic redlining (grade D vs. A/B) was associated with significantly higher prevalence of obesity (PR 1.42; 95% CI 1.09-1.87) and hypertension (PR 1.19; 95% CI 1.09-1.87), with a non-significant trend for diabetes (PR 1.58; 95% CI 0.71-3.48), demonstrating that structural racism's legacy persists in present-day cardiovascular risk profiles. In race-stratified analyses, associations of the most severely redlined areas (D vs. A/B/C) with hypertension (PR 1.19; 95% CI 0.91-1.55) and diabetes (PR 1.47; 95% CI 0.66-3.66) were suggested particularly among Black participants, though CIs were wide due to limited subgroup sample sizes. Redlining was associated with obesity specifically among oil spill cleanup workers (PR 1.49; 95% CI 1.23-1.82) but not among non-workers, suggesting occupation-environment interaction effects that may amplify cardiometabolic risk. The study is limited by its cross-sectional design, potential selection bias in GuLF Study enrollment, geographic restriction to Gulf Coast states, wide confidence intervals in subgroup analyses, and residual confounding by current-day neighborhood socioeconomic factors.

Implications Medical Affairs : This study provides robust evidence that historically racist housing policies (HOLC redlining) have measurable, persistent effects on present-day hypertension prevalence — particularly among Black residents — establishing a social-structural determinant framework for cardiovascular health disparities that Medical Affairs teams must recognize in health equity communications. From a product positioning perspective, these data support engagement with KOLs working at the intersection of social determinants of health, health equity, and cardiovascular medicine, creating opportunities to develop community-based hypertension detection and treatment access programs targeting historically redlined neighborhoods with high undiagnosed hypertension burden. Collaborating with community health organizations and public health advocates to address structural barriers to hypertension care in underserved communities could enhance the company's health equity profile and generate meaningful real-world evidence on treatment access and outcomes in populations historically excluded from clinical trials.
