Prevention of new-onset chronic kidney disease with renin-angiotensin system blockers in type 2 diabetes with preserved kidney function.

Revue : Renal Failure | Tier : NA | IF estime : NA

Date de publication : 2026-08-05

Resultats principaux : This multicenter retrospective cohort study enrolled 316,693 individuals with type 2 diabetes and newly diagnosed hypertension in Taiwan, evaluating whether RAS blockers (ACEIs/ARBs) could prevent new-onset CKD in patients with preserved kidney function. After generalized boosted model weighting to equalize baseline characteristics, the analytic cohort comprised 3,316 ACEI/ARB users and 1,409 nonusers. Three renal endpoints were tracked: sustained eGFR below 60 mL/min/1.73 m2, incident albuminuria (UACR at or above 30 mg/g), and the composite of either. ACEI/ARB therapy was associated with a significantly lower risk of new-onset CKD (HR 0.72; 95% CI 0.60-0.86), with consistent findings in competing-risk analysis (HR 0.73; 95% CI 0.61-0.87) and time-dependent exposure analysis (HR 0.81; 95% CI 0.68-0.96). Notable limitations include the retrospective observational design, potential residual confounding despite propensity weighting, and the Taiwanese cohort limiting direct generalizability to other ethnic populations.

Implications Medical Affairs : These findings support expanded guideline recommendations for early initiation of ACEI/ARB therapy in diabetic hypertensive patients with preserved kidney function, potentially shifting the treatment paradigm from CKD progression-slowing to active prevention. From a product positioning perspective, this real-world evidence substantially strengthens the nephroprotective narrative for RAS blocker portfolios, providing compelling data to differentiate early-use indications in the diabetic hypertensive population. KOL engagement with nephrologists and diabetologists is warranted, and sponsoring a prospective randomized trial to confirm preventive benefit would represent a high-value strategic investment.

Screening performance of community-based assessment checklist compared with point of care screening tests to identify high-risk individuals for diabetes and hypertension in urban Mysore, India.

Revue : Indian Journal of Medical Research | Tier : NA | IF estime : NA

Date de publication : 2026-08

Resultats principaux : This cross-sectional validation study conducted in 2019-2020 in urban Mysuru, India assessed the discriminative ability of the Community-Based Assessment Checklist (CBAC) against point-of-care tests (random capillary blood glucose and blood pressure) for identifying adults aged 30 years and above at high risk for diabetes and hypertension, excluding known cases. Among 32,686 participants with CBAC data, 31,594 had complete RCBG data and 32,645 had BP readings available for comparative analysis. ROC analysis yielded poor AUC values of 0.62 (95% CI 0.61-0.63) for diabetes risk identification and 0.61 (95% CI 0.60-0.61) for hypertension risk identification. The optimal CBAC cut-off of greater than 4 via Youden's index performed poorly, with index values of 0.17 and 0.15 for diabetes and hypertension respectively. The authors recommend that trained frontline health staff administer both RCBG and BP tests alongside CBAC for more efficient public health triaging, acknowledging that the CBAC alone is insufficient as a standalone screening tool.

Implications Medical Affairs : The finding that CBAC has poor discriminative performance for hypertension screening in the Indian community context challenges its standalone deployment in national NCD control programs, with important implications for screening guideline revisions in low- and middle-income country settings. From a Medical Affairs standpoint, this creates opportunities to support the development and adoption of more reliable point-of-care blood pressure devices, reinforcing the need for accessible diagnostic tools as a prerequisite to pharmacological intervention. KOL engagement with public health and community medicine specialists in India may support advocacy for improved screening protocols in primary health centers, expanding the addressable market for hypertension diagnosis and treatment.

Community health worker-led interventions for cardio-metabolic diseases in India: Evidence from a systematic review and meta-analysis.

Revue : Indian Journal of Medical Research | Tier : NA | IF estime : NA

Date de publication : 2026-08

Resultats principaux : This systematic review and meta-analysis included 13 studies (10 randomized trials and 3 pre-post studies) conducted in India between 2000 and 2024, involving adults with hypertension, diabetes, stroke, and overweight, with mean ages ranging from 45 to 65 years. Nine studies were included in the meta-analysis, and the primary finding demonstrated that CHW-led interventions significantly reduced systolic blood pressure (n=7,114; MD -5.5 mmHg; 95% CI -9.0 to -1.9; P=0.041; I2=79.3%), with a larger effect among participants specifically with hypertension (MD -7.5 mmHg). Body mass index was also significantly reduced (MD -0.63 kg/m2; P=0.038) in the intervention group. No significant changes were observed for diastolic BP, fasting blood glucose, HbA1c, or lipid profiles, and sensitivity analysis revealed no differential effect. GRADE certainty of evidence was rated as low to very low for all outcome variables, reflecting heterogeneity (I2=79.3%) and study design limitations that warrant cautious interpretation.

Implications Medical Affairs : The clinically meaningful 5.5 mmHg reduction in systolic blood pressure through CHW-led interventions supports integration of task-shifting strategies into hypertension management guidelines for India and similar LMIC settings, where healthcare worker density remains a significant barrier. For Medical Affairs, this evidence strengthens the rationale for developing patient support programs that leverage community health worker models alongside antihypertensive pharmacotherapy, enabling a differentiated holistic value proposition. Future KOL collaborations should target demonstration of synergistic effects between CHW-supported adherence programs and antihypertensive medications, reinforcing that behavioral and pharmacological approaches are complementary rather than competing.

Hypertension Control Rates Before and After Implementation of a Multicomponent Quality Improvement Program in Medicare Beneficiaries Primarily of Hispanic Origin in a Large Health System in South Florida, 2011-2018.

Revue : Journal of Clinical Hypertension (Greenwich, Conn.) | Tier : 3 | IF estime : 3-10

Date de publication : 2026-08

Resultats principaux : This longitudinal observational study analyzed hypertension control rates from 2008 to 2018 in Leon Medical Centers (LMC), a major integrated health system serving Medicare beneficiaries predominantly of Hispanic origin (greater than 98%) in Miami-Dade County, Florida, with the analytic sample growing from 4,710 to 21,540 patients and mean age increasing from 73.4 to 77.1 years over the study period. The multicomponent quality improvement program, implemented in 2011, encompassed team-based care, standardized BP measurement protocols, guideline-based management, EHR integration, enhanced pharmacy and transportation services, and culturally tailored patient engagement strategies. Adjusted odds of hypertension control were 5.7 times higher (95% CI 5.4-6.0) after full program implementation, with crude AVG-BP-based control rates reaching approximately 90%. Improvements in hypertension control were comparable in women and men, demonstrating equitable program effects across sexes. A key methodological finding showed that single-time-point LAST-BP control rates consistently underestimated those derived from time-averaged AVG-BP metrics, with only fair kappa agreement between the two measurement approaches.

Implications Medical Affairs : Achieving approximately 90% hypertension control rates in a previously underserved Hispanic Medicare population through a multicomponent quality program sets a landmark real-world benchmark that may directly inform health system performance standards and guideline recommendations for minority-serving integrated care settings. From a Medical Affairs perspective, this demonstrates that culturally tailored care programs are a necessary complement to antihypertensive pharmacotherapy, reinforcing the product value story within structured, guideline-adherent care frameworks. KOL engagement with health system administrators, population health specialists, and geriatricians serving the Hispanic community should be prioritized, alongside advocacy for adoption of longitudinal BP metrics as the standard for performance assessment.

Time-varying eating behaviors and long-term risk of incident hypertension in a Japanese cohort.

Revue : Hypertension Research | Tier : NA | IF estime : NA

Date de publication : 2026-08-04

Resultats principaux : This prospective cohort study analyzed data from 236,343 adults participating in an annual health check-up program in Japan between 2009 and 2023, examining the long-term association between time-varying eating behaviors (breakfast skipping, eating speed, and bedtime eating) and incident hypertension over 14 years using marginal structural models with inverse probability weighting to account for time-dependent confounding. The analytic samples comprised approximately 86,157 to 86,241 participants depending on the eating behavior examined, drawn from a large occupational cohort (Panasonic employees). At 14 years, breakfast skipping was associated with a 12% higher relative risk of hypertension compared with non-skippers (RR 1.12; 95% CI 1.09-1.15; absolute risk difference 5.8%; 95% CI 4.3%-7.3%), while fast eating was associated with a 9% higher relative risk versus slow eating (RR 1.09; 95% CI 1.03-1.14; absolute risk difference 4.1%; 95% CI 1.7%-6.4%). Bedtime eating showed no meaningful association with hypertension risk (RR 1.00; 95% CI 0.98-1.03; absolute risk difference 0.2%). Strengths include the large sample size, 14-year follow-up, and use of advanced causal inference methodology; limitations include potential residual confounding and restriction to a predominantly Japanese occupational population, limiting broader generalizability.

Implications Medical Affairs : The identification of breakfast skipping and fast eating as independent, modifiable behavioral risk factors for incident hypertension with absolute risk differences exceeding 4% over 14 years provides a compelling evidence base for incorporating specific dietary behavior targets into hypertension prevention guidelines, moving beyond generic dietary recommendations. For Medical Affairs, these findings highlight actionable lifestyle risk factors that complement pharmacological management, supporting the development of patient education materials and adherence programs that integrate eating behavior modification alongside antihypertensive therapy, strengthening the holistic product value proposition. This evidence also presents opportunities for KOL engagement with nutritionists, endocrinologists, and primary care physicians to develop integrated prevention messages linking behavioral dietary interventions with early blood pressure management strategies in at-risk populations.

Recent Updates on the Role of Genetic and Epigenetic Factors in the Pathogenesis of the Metabolic Syndrome.

Revue : Diabetes, Metabolic Syndrome and Obesity: Targets and Therapy | Tier : NA | IF estime : NA

Date de publication : 2026-07-31

Resultats principaux : This systematic review analyzed 55 relevant studies published over the last decade, examining the interplay of genetic polymorphisms, DNA methylation changes, and non-coding RNAs (ncRNAs) in the pathogenesis of metabolic syndrome (MetS), a multifactorial cardiometabolic disorder characterized by obesity, hypertension, dyslipidemia, and diabetes with a major contribution to global cardiovascular disease burden. The review found that MetS arises from the convergence of inherited genomic risk and environmentally driven epigenetic reprogramming, with epigenetic modifications affecting genes involved in neuroendocrine regulation, adipogenesis and insulin signaling, glucose and lipid metabolism, and immune and inflammatory signaling pathways. Specific epigenetic mechanisms including DNA methylation patterns and ncRNA expression (miRNAs and lncRNAs) were identified as modulators of susceptibility to individual MetS components including hypertension. No formal subgroup analyses were reported, but the review spanned broad biological mechanisms across diverse study populations. Limitations include heterogeneity in study designs and methods, varying population characteristics, and inherent risk of publication bias given the qualitative nature of the systematic review.

Implications Medical Affairs : This comprehensive synthesis of genetic and epigenetic mechanisms underlying MetS and its hypertensive component provides a scientific framework that may guide the identification of epigenetic biomarkers for cardiovascular risk stratification, with potential to enable a precision medicine approach to antihypertensive therapy selection. From a Medical Affairs perspective, the epigenetic targets identified represent future therapeutic opportunities for pipeline investment in novel antihypertensive or cardiometabolic combination agents, and proactive monitoring of this space is critical for competitive intelligence. Strategic engagement with researchers in nutrigenomics, epigenetics, and precision cardiology, alongside sponsorship of biomarker-based subgroup identification studies, should be prioritized to position the company at the forefront of personalized hypertension management.

A brief checklist of modifiable dementia risk factors (RF12): associations with cognitive and affective measures in an Italian cohort.

Revue : Frontiers in Aging Neuroscience | Tier : NA | IF estime : NA

Date de publication : 2026-07-21

Resultats principaux : This cross-sectional study in 563 community-dwelling, cognitively healthy Italian adults (326 females; mean age 61.79 plus or minus 10.19 years; age range 27-91; mean education 11.76 plus or minus 4.60 years) introduced and validated the 12-item Risk Factor Questionnaire (RF12), a brief checklist capturing 12 modifiable dementia risk factors including hypertension, and examined its associations with cognitive and affective outcomes. Three RF12 scoring methods were compared: self-report, informant-report, and a weighted score, with a moderate correlation observed between self- and informant-reported scores (Spearman's rho = 0.408; p less than 0.001). Hierarchical regression analyses demonstrated that each RF12 scoring method explained small but statistically unique proportions of variance in cognitive and affective outcomes (incremental R-squared ranging from 0.8% to 2.3%) after controlling for age, sex, and education. Standard cognitive assessments (MMSE, MoCA, SATURN, FAB) and the Geriatric Depression Scale were used as outcome measures across the regression models. Key limitations include the cross-sectional design precluding causal inference, the restriction to cognitively healthy community-dwellers limiting clinical generalizability, and the modest explained variance suggesting the RF12 captures only a fraction of dementia-relevant risk factors.

Implications Medical Affairs : The RF12 checklist, by formally capturing hypertension as a key modifiable dementia risk factor within a validated screening instrument, supports its application in primary care for early identification of individuals at combined cardiovascular and neurocognitive risk, with potential relevance for joint hypertension-dementia prevention guideline development. From a Medical Affairs standpoint, this reinforces the emerging brain-protection value proposition for antihypertensive therapies, enabling a dual-benefit positioning of BP control as an intervention targeting both cardiovascular events and cognitive decline, a compelling narrative for KOL engagement spanning both cardiology and neurology. Longitudinal studies using the RF12 in hypertensive patient cohorts would substantially strengthen the evidence base for antihypertensive therapy's role in dementia prevention, representing a strategically valuable endpoint for future clinical development programs.
