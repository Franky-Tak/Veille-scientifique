Hypertensive disorders of pregnancy: epidemiology, pathophysiology, therapeutic strategies, and maternal-offspring outcomes.

Revue : Hypertension in Pregnancy | Tier : NA | IF estime : NA

Date de publication : 2026-10-13

Resultats principaux : This comprehensive narrative review examines hypertensive disorders of pregnancy (HDP), a category encompassing chronic hypertension, gestational hypertension, preeclampsia, and preeclampsia superimposed on chronic hypertension, affecting 5 to 10% of pregnancies globally and representing a major cause of maternal and perinatal morbidity and mortality. The burden of HDP is increasing, driven by rising obesity, advanced maternal age, evolving diagnostic criteria, and marked socioeconomic and racial-ethnic disparities. Mechanistically, HDP is characterized by systemic endothelial dysfunction and angiogenic imbalance, particularly involving the sFlt-1/PlGF ratio and soluble endoglin, alongside immune maladaptation, oxidative stress, and genetic and environmental contributors including endocrine-disrupting chemicals and sleep-disordered breathing. Trial evidence supports treating mild chronic hypertension to below 140/90 mmHg to reduce adverse outcomes without compromising fetal growth, while the sFlt-1/PlGF ratio is among the most validated tools for short-term risk assessment in suspected preeclampsia. A notable limitation is that offspring neurodevelopmental associations are largely observational and may be confounded by prematurity and shared familial or socioeconomic factors, and experimental therapies such as PPARgamma agonists and placenta-targeted nanomedicine remain at preclinical stages.

Implications Medical Affairs : This review reinforces the need to position antihypertensive therapies with evidence of fetal safety as first-line agents for mild chronic hypertension in pregnancy, given the guideline support for BP targets below 140/90 mmHg. The validated sFlt-1/PlGF ratio represents a biomarker opportunity for diagnostic partner products and KOL messaging around precision stratification in preeclampsia management. Actionable insights include engaging maternal-fetal medicine specialists in collaborative research on machine learning and multi-omics precision risk stratification for pregnant hypertensive patients.

Study Protocol for the Daasanach Human Biology Project: A Longitudinal Study of the Impact of Drinking Water Salinity and Heat Stress on Water Insecurity, Hydration, Energetics, Hypertension, and Kidney Function in Northern Kenya.

Revue : American Journal of Human Biology | Tier : NA | IF estime : NA

Date de publication : 2026-10

Resultats principaux : This protocol paper describes a longitudinal cohort study recruiting 600 adults across approximately 450 households in 12 communities in Illeret Sub-County, Kenya, designed to assess how drinking water salinity and heat stress affect hydration status, kidney function, and blood pressure among Daasanach pastoralists in a hot-arid environment over six seasonal study waves. The primary endpoints include hydration biomarkers, kidney disease markers, and cardiometabolic outcomes including blood pressure, assessed across wet (March-April) and dry (June-July) seasons using household surveys, anthropometrics, doubly labeled water, point-of-care assays, and laboratory biomarkers. While quantitative results are not yet available as this is a protocol paper, the study employs multi-level regression models to assess dose-specific effects of water sodium-chloride content on cardiometabolic outcomes, acknowledging the absence of existing health standards for drinking water salinity. Subgroup analyses will compare outcomes by season and by level of community groundwater salinity, with the doubly labeled water method providing objective measures of total water turnover. A notable limitation is the focus on a highly specific pastoralist population in northern Kenya, which may limit generalizability, and the study does not yet provide dose-response data to anchor policy recommendations.

Implications Medical Affairs : This study addresses a critical and underappreciated environmental hypertension risk factor, as rising groundwater salinity represents a global health threat that could inform future guideline modifications regarding dietary sodium from non-food water sources. The potential causal relationship between environmental sodium exposure and blood pressure elevation could expand the evidence base for antihypertensive therapies in populations previously considered outside traditional cardiometabolic risk frameworks. Engagement with global health and environmental medicine KOLs is warranted to develop cross-disciplinary research on non-dietary sodium burden and cardiovascular consequences in climate-vulnerable low-income populations.

Ambulatory and Unattended, Automated Office Blood Pressure in People With and Without HIV in Tanzania: A Cohort Study of Repeatability and Regression to the Mean.

Revue : Journal of Clinical Hypertension (Greenwich, Conn.) | Tier : Tier 3 | IF estime : 3–10

Date de publication : 2026-10

Resultats principaux : This prospective cohort study from the Mwanza HIV and CVD cohort in Tanzania analyzed 750 participants (384 with HIV and 366 without) to assess short-term reproducibility of unattended automated office blood pressure (uAOBP) and 24-hour ambulatory blood pressure monitoring (ABPM) at enrollment and one month later. The primary endpoint was reproducibility of blood pressure measurements and hypertension classification, evaluated with Bland-Altman plots and Cohen's kappa. For uAOBP, the mean systolic blood pressure difference was statistically significant (-1.02 mmHg; 95% CI -1.76 to -0.27), while 24-hour ABPM showed no significant mean difference (-0.46; 95% CI -1.02 to 0.10), with kappa statistics of 0.45 for uAOBP and 0.56 for 24-hour ABPM indicating moderate agreement. Regression dilution ratios ranged from 0.73 to 0.82 for both methods, and Bland-Altman plots revealed narrower limits of agreement for ABPM, reflecting greater classification stability. Critically, reproducibility did not differ significantly by HIV status, supporting the validity of both measurement approaches in HIV-positive and HIV-negative populations in Sub-Saharan Africa.

Implications Medical Affairs : These findings support the inclusion of ABPM as the preferred hypertension classification method in Sub-Saharan Africa, including in HIV-affected populations, with direct implications for guideline harmonization in low- and middle-income countries. From a product positioning standpoint, this evidence strengthens the case for ABPM-guided antihypertensive therapy initiation in the growing population of HIV-positive patients receiving antiretroviral therapy who also carry a hypertension burden. KOL engagement should focus on infectious disease cardiologists and global health hypertension specialists in Sub-Saharan Africa, given the dual epidemic of HIV and hypertension and the urgent need for evidence-based measurement standards.

Parental obesity and offspring cardiometabolic trajectories from childhood to adulthood.

Revue : Frontiers in Public Health | Tier : NA | IF estime : NA

Date de publication : 2026-09-28

Resultats principaux : This prospective analysis used data from 4,982 offspring in the China Health and Nutrition Survey (1989-2015), employing linear mixed-effects models with polynomial age terms and Cox proportional hazards models to assess cardiometabolic trajectories by parental obesity status across four groups: neither parent affected (reference), father only, mother only, or both parents affected. Male offspring with both parents obese faced the highest risks for incident overweight/obesity (HR 3.72; 95% CI 1.73-7.99) and central obesity (HR 2.46; 95% CI 1.31-4.62), while the association with hypertension was elevated but did not reach statistical significance (HR 1.55; 95% CI 0.86-2.77). Female offspring with two obese parents showed similarly elevated overweight/obesity risk (HR 4.60; 95% CI 1.93-10.94), and formal sex-by-parental obesity interaction tests were non-significant for all outcomes. Offspring with both parents affected had the highest baseline cardiometabolic risk levels, but annual rates of increase were not consistently higher across groups, suggesting that the risk accumulates through initial-level differences rather than differential aging trajectories. A key limitation is the use of self-reported parental weight data and dietary information over a 26-year follow-up spanning substantial changes in Chinese nutrition and socioeconomic conditions.

Implications Medical Affairs : These data strengthen the scientific basis for family-level interventions in hypertension and cardiometabolic disease prevention, potentially influencing guideline recommendations to incorporate parental BMI as part of pediatric cardiovascular risk screening algorithms. From a product positioning perspective, the elevated but non-significant HR for hypertension in offspring of two obese parents (1.55) supports the commercial rationale for early antihypertensive intervention studies in young adults with high familial cardiometabolic burden. KOL strategy should prioritize engagement with pediatric cardiologists and preventive medicine specialists in China and Asia, where intergenerational obesity and hypertension represent an accelerating public health challenge.

Health outcomes of U.S. active component service members working in high risk occupations for blast overpressure exposure: a retrospective cohort study.

Revue : MSMR | Tier : NA | IF estime : NA

Date de publication : 2026-09-20

Resultats principaux : This large retrospective cohort study examined 831,050 U.S. active component service members (ACSMs) in high-risk blast overpressure (BOP) occupations and 2,229,594 in low-risk occupations across the Army, Air Force, and Marine Corps, assessing incidence of traumatic brain injury, insomnia, migraine, anxiety/depressive disorders, noise-induced hearing injury (NIHI), and essential hypertension. Overall TBI incidence was 243.8 per 10,000 person-years in high-risk versus 140.9 in low-risk cohorts, while NIHI rates were 342.7 versus 218.1 per 10,000 person-years respectively. After adjusting for demographic and military characteristics, high-risk BOP occupations were associated with significantly higher adjusted incidence rate ratios for TBI (aIRR 1.43; 95% CI 1.42-1.44) and NIHI (aIRR 1.39; 95% CI 1.38-1.40), and increasing cohort tenure was associated with greater acceleration of migraine headache incidence in high-risk occupations. Essential hypertension was among the outcomes tracked, and despite the elevated overall disease burden in high-risk occupations, the distribution of disease burden categories from 2024 health care encounters was similar between cohorts, suggesting relatively uniform management access. A key limitation is reliance on administrative health encounter data, which may underestimate actual disease incidence and does not capture cumulative blast dose or pre-service baseline health status.

Implications Medical Affairs : The identification of essential hypertension as a tracked outcome in a large, well-characterized occupational cohort of blast-exposed military personnel creates an important opportunity to study occupational cardiovascular risk and evaluate targeted hypertension screening protocols in this population. From a Medical Affairs perspective, the finding that disease burden distribution was similar between cohorts for hypertension suggests potential gaps in early detection and antihypertensive management protocols for blast-exposed service members. KOL engagement with military medicine specialists, occupational cardiologists, and blast injury researchers would be strategically valuable for developing evidence-based cardiovascular risk reduction protocols and advocating for inclusion of occupational blast exposure in hypertension risk stratification guidelines.

Identity-by-descent captures shared environmental factors at biobank scale.

Revue : Genome Research | Tier : NA | IF estime : NA

Date de publication : 2026-10-13

Resultats principaux : This genomic study applied a hierarchical community detection algorithm to 13,143 individuals in the Biorepository and Integrative Genomics (BIG) biobank, grouping participants into four major communities and 17 subcommunities based on genome-wide identity-by-descent (IBD) sharing proportions. Integration of neighborhood-level geographic data with census-derived environmental metrics revealed unequal exposure to environmental stressors between subcommunities, with elevated respiratory condition prevalence specifically in subcommunities under greater pollution burden, a pathway directly relevant to hypertension risk. Interventional mediation analysis showed that a substantial proportion of excess subcommunity-level disease risk remained unexplained by measured environmental factors alone, consistent with a contribution from unmeasured shared exposures captured by IBD-based grouping beyond those captured by standard environmental metrics. Subcommunity-level disparities persisted in sensitivity analyses adjusting for self-reported race, indicating that IBD-based clustering captures health-relevant information beyond traditional demographic proxies used for social determinants of health, offering a more biologically grounded stratification approach. A key limitation is that the analysis was conducted in a single biobank and may not generalize across diverse biobanks, and hypertension-specific incidence rates were not directly quantified, limiting immediate clinical applicability.

Implications Medical Affairs : This novel IBD-based genomic-environmental framework offers a precision medicine approach to identifying at-risk subpopulations for hypertension and cardiovascular disease based on shared genetic and environmental determinants, which could refine population-level risk stratification in future guideline frameworks. From a product positioning standpoint, identifying biologically and environmentally homogeneous subcommunities could enable more targeted clinical trial enrollment and reduce confounding in antihypertensive drug development studies focused on environmental risk factor modification. KOL strategy should engage genomics, precision medicine, and environmental health specialists, as the open-source IBD-community dashboard could become a widely-used tool for environmental hypertension risk research.

Dietary Patterns and Chronic Disease Risk: A Proteomic Framework to Strengthen Causal Inference.

Revue : Advances in Nutrition | Tier : NA | IF estime : NA

Date de publication : 2026-10-13

Resultats principaux : This methodological review proposes a complementary research framework combining randomized feeding trials with Mendelian randomization (MR) using proteomic markers to overcome limitations of traditional observational nutrition research, illustrated with evidence from the Dietary Approaches to Stop Hypertension (DASH) diet. The framework uses short-term feeding trials to identify proteins responding to dietary patterns, then applies MR with cis-protein quantitative trait loci (pQTLs) to assess whether these diet-responsive proteins are causally related to chronic disease outcomes, with cis-pQTLs reducing concerns about horizontal pleiotropy compared to other MR approaches. DASH diet studies demonstrated that randomized feeding trials can identify reproducible proteomic signatures, several of which were confirmed in population-based cohort studies and associated with type 2 diabetes risk in prospective and MR analyses, providing a proof-of-concept pathway applicable to hypertension endpoints. The proposed use of genetic scores to instrument proteomic signatures of dietary patterns in MR analyses represents a novel extension of the framework that could address the inherent limitation of maintaining long-term dietary compliance in randomized trials. Key limitations include incomplete proteomic coverage, measurement error, epitope effects, challenges satisfying MR assumptions, and the current focus on diabetes outcomes rather than direct hypertension endpoints.

Implications Medical Affairs : This framework provides a methodological path to generate causal evidence for DASH and other dietary approaches in hypertension prevention, potentially strengthening the evidence base for dietary recommendations currently relying heavily on observational data and contributing to future guideline updates. From a Medical Affairs perspective, proteomic signatures of dietary patterns could serve as novel surrogate endpoints in clinical trials evaluating the additive benefit of combining antihypertensive drugs with lifestyle interventions, creating differentiated trial design opportunities. KOL engagement should target clinical nutritionists, genetic epidemiologists, and precision medicine researchers developing protein-based biomarker panels for cardiovascular and hypertension risk assessment.

Linking obesity and type 2 diabetes: Mechanistic insights and personalized nutritional strategies.

Revue : Nutrition and Health | Tier : NA | IF estime : NA

Date de publication : 2026-10-12

Resultats principaux : This narrative review examines mechanistic pathways linking obesity to type 2 diabetes mellitus (T2DM) under the concept of diabesity, synthesizing global epidemiological data from WHO indicating sharp rises in both conditions over the past two decades, with hypertension identified as a key comorbid condition in obese individuals. The review details how visceral adiposity drives chronic low-grade inflammation through altered inflammatory cytokines (interleukin-6, TNF-alpha, C-reactive protein), which promotes insulin resistance as the primary mechanism in T2DM development, and notes that obesity directly and indirectly contributes to atherosclerosis, hypertension, and certain cancers. The proposed personalized nutrition approach is positioned as an alternative to one-size-fits-all dietary recommendations, accounting for individual metabolic, genetic, and lifestyle profiles, though no original quantitative data are presented as this is a narrative review. Subgroup considerations address differential risk profiles based on metabolic phenotype and genetic background, which underpin the personalized nutrition framework as a preventive strategy against diabesity. Notable limitations include the narrative design without systematic literature search methodology, reliance on secondary sources, and absence of meta-analytic quantification of effect sizes for personalized dietary interventions versus standard dietary guidance.

Implications Medical Affairs : This review reinforces the mechanistic overlap between obesity, T2DM, and hypertension, supporting integrated therapeutic positioning strategies that address all three conditions simultaneously, which has implications for fixed-dose combination therapies with demonstrated metabolic benefits. From a Medical Affairs perspective, the personalized nutrition framework could complement antihypertensive treatment by providing evidence-based lifestyle co-interventions, strengthening the efficacy narrative for drugs that also demonstrate weight reduction or metabolic benefits. KOL strategy should involve endocrinologists, cardiologists, and clinical nutritionists specializing in the cardiometabolic continuum, particularly as GLP-1 receptor agonists and other metabolic agents increasingly intersect with the hypertension treatment space.

Elevated lipoprotein(a) and adverse pregnancy outcomes.

Revue : Annals of Medicine and Surgery | Tier : NA | IF estime : NA

Date de publication : 2026-09-10

Resultats principaux : This narrative review synthesizes current evidence on lipoprotein(a) [Lp(a)] as a potential biomarker for adverse pregnancy outcomes, including hypertensive disorders of pregnancy, gestational diabetes mellitus, fetal growth restriction, and preterm birth, in addition to its established role in cardiovascular disease. The review reports that Lp(a) levels nearly double between weeks 10 and 35 of pregnancy universally, though the clinical significance of this increase remains unclear given that research on Lp(a)'s specific role in hypertensive disorders of pregnancy has produced mixed results. Lp(a) exerts well-characterized pro-inflammatory, pro-thrombotic, and pro-atherogenic effects through mechanisms detailed in the review, but direct causal evidence for its role in preeclampsia and gestational hypertension requires further investigation from adequately powered prospective studies. Current management recommendations include preconception counseling and lifestyle modifications (dietary improvement, increased physical activity, smoking cessation), while pharmacological options such as statins and PCSK9 inhibitors have demonstrated efficacy in non-pregnant populations but carry uncertain safety profiles during pregnancy, limiting their use. A key limitation is the absence of randomized controlled trials of Lp(a)-lowering therapies in pregnant populations, and the mixed evidence from observational studies suggests significant confounding from other concurrent cardiovascular risk factors.

Implications Medical Affairs : The emerging evidence linking Lp(a) to hypertensive disorders of pregnancy presents an opportunity to position Lp(a) as a novel screening biomarker within preeclampsia risk stratification frameworks, potentially influencing future guideline updates on gestational cardiovascular risk assessment. From a Medical Affairs perspective, the safety concerns around statins and PCSK9 inhibitors in pregnancy highlight an unmet therapeutic need for safe Lp(a)-lowering strategies in pregnant patients, representing a potential market opportunity for novel therapeutic approaches such as RNA-based Lp(a)-lowering therapies. KOL strategy should engage maternal-fetal medicine specialists, cardiovascular geneticists, and obstetric cardiologists who specialize in pregnancy-related cardiovascular risk as novel Lp(a)-lowering therapies approach broader clinical availability.
