Burden of stroke attributable to environmental, behavioral, and metabolic risk factors in Eastern Sub-Saharan Africa, from 1990 to 2023.

Revue : The Journal of Medicine Access | Tier : NA | IF estime : NA

Date de publication : 2026-09-25

Resultats principaux : This cross-sectional burden analysis utilized Global Burden of Disease (GBD) 2023 estimates to quantify stroke mortality and disability-adjusted life years (DALYs) attributable to environmental, behavioral, and metabolic risk factors across 21 countries in Eastern Sub-Saharan Africa (ESSA) from 1990 to 2023, with a focus on adults aged 55 and older. The primary endpoints were stroke-attributable death rates and DALY rates per 100,000 population, stratified by risk factor category (metabolic, environmental, behavioral) and by individual country. In 2023, all-risk-factor stroke contributed to 110,013 deaths (rate: 359 per 100,000; 95% UI: 275-449) and 2,359,559 DALYs (rate: 7,702 per 100,000; 95% UI: 6,043-9,495) across the region, with metabolic risk factors constituting the dominant burden at 6,286 DALYs per 100,000 (95% UI: 4,573-8,279), followed by environmental (4,146; 95% UI: 2,621-5,668) and behavioral risks (2,505; 95% UI: 1,342-3,956). Among individual risk factors, high systolic blood pressure was the single largest contributor to stroke burden, followed by dietary risks, household air pollution, and high LDL cholesterol; country-level heterogeneity was substantial, with Zambia recording the highest DALY rate (12,418 per 100,000; 95% UI: 9,658-15,576) and Comoros the lowest (4,063 per 100,000; 95% UI: 2,969-5,120). No significant annualized reductions were observed in any risk factor category over the 33-year study period, indicating stagnant progress in stroke prevention. A major limitation is the dependence on GBD-modeled estimates that may obscure subnational heterogeneity and country-specific trends given sparse primary data collection infrastructure in low-resource ESSA settings.

Implications Medical Affairs : The identification of high systolic blood pressure as the leading attributable risk factor for stroke burden in Eastern Sub-Saharan Africa — surpassing dietary, environmental, and lipid-related risks — provides compelling epidemiological evidence supporting prioritization of antihypertensive agents in regional treatment guidelines and national essential medicines lists. For pharmaceutical product positioning, this 33-year trend of stagnant stroke burden in ESSA defines a substantial unmet need that justifies investment in market access strategies and pharmacoeconomic dossiers targeting sub-Saharan African health authorities. Medical Affairs teams should use this GBD-based data to engage regional key opinion leaders, WHO AFRO representatives, and health ministries in hypertension awareness and control initiatives, while advocating for locally generated real-world evidence studies to complement modeled estimates.
