Dementia Risk With Combined Statin and Antihypertensive Drugs That Increase Versus Decrease Angiotensin-II Formation: Findings From the 45 and Up Study.

Revue : International Journal of Geriatric Psychiatry | Tier : NA | IF estime : NA

Date de publication : 2026-08 (Epub ahead)

Resultats principaux : This prospective cohort study leveraged data from the 45 and Up Study, a large population-based cohort in New South Wales, Australia, including 34,610 propensity-score-matched participants (17,305 per group, mean age 65.8 years) with comorbid hypertension and dyslipidaemia on concurrent statin and antihypertensive medication (AHM) with a mean follow-up of 12.4 years. The primary endpoints were dementia incidence and all-cause mortality, comparing users of statin plus angiotensin-II (Ang-II) promoting AHMs (ARBs, thiazides, dihydropyridine calcium channel blockers) versus statin plus Ang-II suppressing AHMs (ACEIs, beta-blockers, non-DHP CCBs). The statin plus Ang-II promoting AHM combination was associated with a 12% lower dementia risk (HR 0.88, 95% CI 0.79-0.98) and a 13% lower all-cause mortality (HR 0.87, 95% CI 0.82-0.93) compared to the suppressing AHM combination. Subgroup analysis showed that rosuvastatin or atorvastatin combined with Ang-II promoting AHMs conferred greater cognitive benefit (HR 0.43, 95% CI 0.36-0.50 and HR 0.74, 95% CI 0.64-0.84, respectively) versus simvastatin, and the protective association was restricted to the 65-74-year age group. Key limitations include the observational design precluding causal inference despite rigorous propensity score matching, potential residual confounding by indication, and the Australian cohort population limiting direct generalizability to other ethnic groups.

Implications Medical Affairs : These findings provide novel evidence supporting differentiated cognitive benefits of ARBs over ACEIs in hypertensive patients on statin therapy, an angle that Medical Affairs teams managing ARB portfolios can incorporate into clinical evidence summaries and guideline discussion documents. The data create a compelling opportunity for KOL engagement with geriatric cardiologists and neurologists at scientific congresses, positioning ARBs within a broader cognitive risk reduction narrative beyond blood pressure control alone. Initiating a proactive research strategy around cognitive endpoints in hypertension-statin combination trials would strengthen the evidentiary base and differentiate ARB-containing products in an increasingly competitive antihypertensive market.

Multimorbidity patterns and their associations with demographic characteristics, behavioral factors, and urban-rural residence: A latent class analysis in the Thai population.

Revue : PLoS One | Tier : NA | IF estime : NA

Date de publication : 2026-07-29

Resultats principaux : This population-based cross-sectional study analyzed data from the National Health Survey in Thailand, including 142,753 participants, to characterize multimorbidity patterns using latent class analysis across nine non-communicable diseases (NCDs). Four distinct multimorbidity classes were identified: low burden, hypertension-metabolic, predominant hypertension, and high multimorbidity, with the hypertension-centric classes representing the most clinically significant groupings. Advancing age was associated with increased probability of membership in all multimorbidity classes; female sex elevated risk for hypertension-metabolic and predominant hypertension classes; and urban residency increased probability of hypertension-metabolic and high-multimorbidity class membership. Behavioral factors were strongly class-predictive: frequent high-fat and instant food consumption was linked to the hypertension-metabolic class, whereas vegetable consumption reduced likelihood of predominant hypertension class membership; current smoking increased multimorbidity class probability across all groups. The main limitations are the cross-sectional design preventing causal inference, potential selection bias in survey participation, and self-reported dietary data subject to recall bias.

Implications Medical Affairs : The dominance of hypertension-centered multimorbidity clusters in a large Thai national dataset underscores the scale of the cardiometabolic burden in Southeast Asia and supports the strategic expansion of antihypertensive product access programs in emerging ASEAN markets. Medical Affairs should collaborate with local hypertension KOLs and public health authorities in Thailand to position these epidemiological findings within national NCD prevention strategies, creating reputational and market access opportunities. The identified behavioral risk profiles, particularly urban residency combined with high-fat diet and obesity, provide precise targets for disease awareness campaigns and patient identification programs that could accelerate early diagnosis and treatment initiation in at-risk Thai populations.

Burden of metabolic diseases in mainland China from 1990 to 2021: results from the global burden of disease study 2021.

Revue : Frontiers in Public Health | Tier : NA | IF estime : NA

Date de publication : 2026-07-14

Resultats principaux : This study used Global Burden of Disease (GBD) 2021 data to analyze disability-adjusted life years (DALYs) and deaths from five common metabolic diseases (type 2 diabetes, hypertension, obesity, hypercholesterolemia, and metabolic dysfunction-associated steatotic liver disease) in mainland China from 1990 to 2021. In 2021, hypertension carried the highest burden with 55.69 million DALYs (95% UI: 43.30-70.33), followed by obesity at 20.87 million DALYs, hypercholesterolemia at 18.41 million, and T2DM at 11.47 million, establishing hypertension as the dominant metabolic disease burden in China. Over 30 years, all five metabolic diseases showed increasing burden, with obesity and T2DM showing the largest total percentage changes (2.84-fold and 1.90-fold, respectively), suggesting a worsening trajectory for the full cardiometabolic disease spectrum. Age-sex subgroup analysis revealed that males had consistently higher DALYs and greater percentage changes than females across nearly all metabolic conditions, and the 55-74-year age group carried the greatest overall burden, identifying high-risk demographic targets. Important limitations include the inherent uncertainty in GBD model estimates, variability in data quality across Chinese provinces, and the ecological nature of trend analyses.

Implications Medical Affairs : Hypertension's status as the leading metabolic disease burden in China with 55.69 million DALYs provides compelling health economic data to underpin market access submissions and value dossiers for antihypertensive therapies in the world's largest pharmaceutical market. Medical Affairs should leverage these GBD findings in engagement with Chinese health authorities and payer bodies, positioning hypertension treatment as a high-impact intervention to reduce the country's overall non-communicable disease burden. The age-sex disparity data, specifically the higher burden in males aged 55-74, should be incorporated into patient identification strategies and KOL educational programs targeting internal medicine and cardiology practitioners in China.

Primary care engagement in hidradenitis suppurativa: Influence of disease severity, cardiometabolic risk, and access barriers.

Revue : Dermatology Online Journal | Tier : NA | IF estime : NA

Date de publication : 2026-05-08

Resultats principaux : This cross-sectional study analyzed 307 patients with hidradenitis suppurativa (HS) at the University of Alabama at Birmingham to examine predictors of primary care provider (PCP) adherence in a population with significant cardiometabolic comorbidity burden. Mean patient age was 39.4 years, 26.4% had Hurley stage III disease, and cardiometabolic comorbidities were highly prevalent: hypertension in 33.9%, type 2 diabetes in 17.6%, and hypercholesterolemia in 32.9%, establishing HS as a disease with a high hypertension burden. Overall PCP adherence was 85.0% but significantly lower in patients under 30 years (71.0%), with CMD-free patients 74% less likely to adhere to annual PCP visits (AOR 0.26, p=0.001) and uninsured patients 82% less likely to adhere (AOR 0.18, p=0.01). HS disease severity (Hurley stage) did not independently predict PCP adherence, though Hurley stage III was associated with more PCP visits and independently predicted type 2 diabetes (AOR 2.26, p=0.02). The cross-sectional design, single-center recruitment, and potential selection bias limit generalizability of these findings to the broader HS population.

Implications Medical Affairs : The 33.9% hypertension prevalence in HS patients underscores an underappreciated cardiometabolic comorbidity burden that creates opportunities for Medical Affairs to develop disease awareness educational content targeting dermatologists about systematic cardiovascular risk factor screening in their patient populations. Coordinated dermatology-cardiology care pathways for HS patients represent an actionable KOL engagement strategy, particularly for reaching younger patients who currently lack adequate primary care contact and may miss hypertension detection. Insurance-related barriers identified in this study should inform patient support and access programs, ensuring that commercially-valuable younger HS patients with hypertension receive appropriate pharmacological management.

Hypertension awareness, treatment, and control in coronary heart disease patients: results of the EUROASPIRE V survey conducted in 27 countries.

Revue : European Journal of Preventive Cardiology | Tier : NA | IF estime : NA

Date de publication : 2026-07-29

Resultats principaux : The EUROASPIRE V survey enrolled 8,261 coronary heart disease (CHD) patients from 27 countries, of whom 6,496 were identified as hypertensive, to assess hypertension awareness, treatment, and control as primary endpoints in a secondary prevention setting. Despite high treatment rates (91.7% on antihypertensive medication), hypertension awareness was only 60.4% and blood pressure control (BP <140/90 mmHg) was achieved in only 46.8% of patients, revealing a critical treatment-control gap in a high-risk CHD population. Number of antihypertensive drugs (OR 1.23, 95% CI 1.16-1.31) and medication adherence (OR 2.18, 95% CI 1.74-2.74) were independently associated with awareness, while adherence was also strongly linked to control (OR 1.93, 95% CI 1.57-2.37). Subgroup analysis showed that secondary and tertiary education levels and undertaking lifestyle changes were associated with higher awareness, and controlled patients had a significantly healthier overall cardiovascular risk profile including lower BMI, lower diabetes prevalence, and lower LDL compared to uncontrolled patients. Limitations include the cross-sectional assessment of hypertension control at a single timepoint, heterogeneity in healthcare systems across 27 countries, and potential survival bias in the enrolled CHD patient population.

Implications Medical Affairs : The alarming control rate of only 46.8% in treated hypertensive CHD patients across 27 European countries directly quantifies the scale of residual cardiovascular risk and provides powerful real-world evidence supporting the medical need for more effective antihypertensive regimens or adherence-optimized formulations. Medical Affairs can use EUROASPIRE V data in guideline committee discussions and health technology assessments to argue for treatment intensification or fixed-dose combination strategies in secondary prevention patients, given the documented adherence-control linkage. KOL engagement at major European cardiology congresses (ESC, EHA) around this adherence-control nexus creates strategic opportunities to position adherence-improving products or patient support programs as solutions to one of secondary prevention's most persistent challenges.

Sleep health matters for blood pressure: Actigraphy-based evidence from a Chinese adult population.

Revue : Sleep Medicine | Tier : NA | IF estime : NA

Date de publication : 2026-07-28

Resultats principaux : This cross-sectional study enrolled 500 adults (66.4% female, mean age 39 years) in Hong Kong and generated a composite sleep health score combining actigraphy-derived dimensions (efficiency, regularity, duration, timing) with subjectively reported satisfaction and alertness from the Pittsburgh Sleep Quality Index and Nonrestorative Sleep Scale. Primary outcomes were systolic (SBP) and diastolic blood pressure (DBP) measured via portable monitor, with average values of 116.2 mmHg and 75.4 mmHg respectively across the cohort. After controlling for sociodemographics, lifestyle, hypertension diagnosis, somatic symptoms, and stress, individuals with the highest sleep health scores (5-6) had SBP 4.06 mmHg lower (p=0.031) and DBP 3.01 mmHg lower (p=0.035) versus those with the lowest scores (0-2). Sleep regularity was independently associated with lower SBP (beta=-2.43, p=0.029) and DBP (beta=-1.93, p=0.022) after adjusting for other sleep domains, and better sleep timing was associated with lower hypertension risk (OR 0.25, p=0.043). The cross-sectional design prevents causal interpretation, the sample was predominantly female and young (mean age 39), and the Hong Kong urban population may not represent broader Asian or global populations.

Implications Medical Affairs : The independent association between sleep regularity, sleep timing, and blood pressure provides quantified behavioral targets that Medical Affairs teams can incorporate into comprehensive patient education materials positioning sleep optimization as an adjunct to pharmacological antihypertensive treatment. These findings strengthen the scientific case for including sleep health assessment in hypertension management guidelines, creating an opportunity for Medical Affairs to collaborate with sleep medicine and hypertension KOLs on advisory board discussions and consensus statement development. Digital health partnerships with sleep tracking applications or wearable device companies could position antihypertensive brands within a broader cardiovascular risk reduction ecosystem, offering differentiated patient engagement strategies for lifestyle-conscious hypertensive patient segments.

Date de publication : 2026-07-29

Resultats principaux : This retrospective study analyzed data from Mexico's National Epidemiological Surveillance System (SiNaVE) from 2015 to 2023, identifying 7,958 laboratory-confirmed RSV cases across all ages, of which 9.4% occurred in adults aged 50 years or older. Among RSV-positive older adults, 60.5% were female, 53.8% had more than one comorbidity, and hypertension and diabetes were the most common comorbidities, with a fatality rate of 12.6%, establishing hypertension as the leading comorbid condition in this high-risk respiratory infection population. Spatial regression analysis found that socioeconomic disadvantage was associated with fewer reported RSV cases, suggesting systemic under-detection and under-reporting in lower-resource settings. Key limitations include the retrospective surveillance design, substantial under-reporting of RSV in the national system, and the inability to assess disease severity or treatment outcomes from registry data alone.

Telecardiology assessment of patients undergoing non-cardiac surgery.

Revue : Einstein (Sao Paulo) | Tier : NA | IF estime : NA

Date de publication : 2026-07-27

Resultats principaux : This retrospective observational study from a Telemedicine Center in São Paulo, Brazil evaluated 3,476 patients referred from 380 primary care facilities for preoperative cardiovascular risk assessment via telecardiology from May 2021 to March 2024. Participants were predominantly female (63%), with a mean age of 52.1 years; 35.7% had hypertension and 48.4% had a sedentary lifestyle, identifying hypertension as the most prevalent cardiovascular comorbidity in the preoperative telecardiology population. Risk stratification classified 90.93% of patients as low risk, 2.59% as intermediate risk, and 1.03% as high risk, with ancillary testing requested in only 14.5% of cases; telecardiology interventions initiated antihypertensive medications in 5.52% of patients. Cardiologists successfully completed perioperative risk stratification in real-time teleconsultations with general practitioners, demonstrating the feasibility of telemedicine for cardiovascular assessment at scale in an emerging market setting. The retrospective single-center design, exploratory nature, and absence of follow-up outcome data limit the strength of conclusions about telecardiology's impact on surgical outcomes.

Implications Medical Affairs : The 35.7% hypertension prevalence in preoperative telecardiology patients underscores the need for evidence-based perioperative antihypertensive management protocols and creates KOL engagement opportunities with anesthesiology and perioperative cardiology societies. Associations of accelerometer-derived daily step counts with future health risks: A phenome-wide association study among over 60,000 UK adults.

Revue : International Journal of Nursing Studies | Tier : NA | IF estime : NA

Date de publication : 2026-07-13

Resultats principaux : This prospective Phenome-Wide Association Study analyzed 61,047 to 69,853 UK Biobank participants with seven-day accelerometer data collected from 2013 to 2015, followed for a median of 7.9 years across 435 disease phenotypes grouped by Phecode classification. An increase of 5,000 steps/day was significantly associated with lower risk of 64 incident diseases, with essential hypertension showing a hazard ratio of 0.93 (95% CI 0.91-0.95), representing a 7% risk reduction per 5,000-step increment. The strongest inverse associations were for Parkinson's disease (HR 0.57), acute renal failure (HR 0.82), and type 2 diabetes (HR 0.80), while the minimum step count for substantial hypertension risk reduction was estimated at approximately 8,697 steps/day. Dose-response analysis using restricted cubic splines revealed non-linear relationships for most disease phenotypes, and conversely, five musculoskeletal conditions showed higher risk with increased step counts, including internal knee derangement (HR 1.15) and osteoarthrosis (HR 1.15). Limitations include potential reverse causation bias (healthier individuals walk more), predominantly European ancestry limiting ethnic generalizability, and residual confounding despite adjustment for multiple lifestyle factors.

Implications Medical Affairs : The quantified 7% hypertension risk reduction per 5,000 daily steps provides a patient-accessible, evidence-based lifestyle recommendation that Medical Affairs can incorporate into patient educational materials and healthcare professional guides as a complement to antihypertensive pharmacotherapy. The broad phenotypic benefit profile across 64 diseases supports positioning antihypertensive treatment within a comprehensive cardiovascular and metabolic disease prevention narrative, reinforcing Medical Affairs messaging around the systemic benefits of blood pressure control combined with lifestyle modification. Collaboration with digital health and wearable technology companies to integrate step count targets into hypertension disease management programs represents an innovative and differentiating KOL strategy, positioning antihypertensive brands within the growing preventive digital health ecosystem.

Workplace Environment and Lifestyle Factors Associated With Hyperuricemia Risk Among Male Manufacturing Workers: A Cross-Sectional Study at a Single Company in Japan.

Revue : Cureus | Tier : NA | IF estime : NA

Date de publication : 2026-06-27

Resultats principaux : This cross-sectional study enrolled 613 male workers at a single Japanese manufacturing company (mean age 39.9 years) to investigate workplace environment and lifestyle factors associated with hyperuricemia (serum uric acid ≥7.0 mg/dL), identifying 20.6% of participants as hyperuricemic. The hyperuricemia group showed significantly elevated blood pressure, BMI, waist circumference, and metabolic syndrome prevalence compared to the normal uric acid group, reinforcing the epidemiological association between hyperuricemia and hypertension in a working-age male population. Multivariable logistic regression identified smoking (OR 1.861, 95% CI 1.124-3.080), breakfast skipping ≥3 times/week (OR 1.684, 95% CI 1.036-2.737), and elevated ALT as independent risk-increasing factors, while company cafeteria use (OR 0.528), regular exercise (OR 0.501), and older age were independently protective. The study found that both occupational environment factors (cafeteria access) and personal lifestyle behaviors (exercise, dietary habits) were independently predictive of hyperuricemia, suggesting that workplace-based interventions targeting these modifiable factors could be effective. The single-company, male-only sample limits generalizability, and the cross-sectional design prevents determination of causality between lifestyle factors and hyperuricemia.

Implications Medical Affairs : The strong association between hyperuricemia and elevated blood pressure in working-age Japanese males provides epidemiological support for the mechanistic link between uric acid metabolism and hypertension, relevant for Medical Affairs teams tracking evidence on novel cardiovascular risk biomarkers. Modifiable workplace factors (cafeteria nutrition, exercise programs) that protect against hyperuricemia overlap with established antihypertensive lifestyle interventions, reinforcing the integrated cardiometabolic risk messaging that Medical Affairs teams can leverage in occupational health and corporate wellness program engagements. The high hyperuricemia prevalence (20.6%) in manufacturing workers, a segment often underserved by traditional clinical outreach, suggests an opportunity for Medical Affairs to engage occupational health physicians as a non-traditional KOL segment for early cardiovascular risk factor identification and treatment.
