Clarifying the Association Between Food Insecurity and Chronic Disease: Why Methods Matter

Revue : Current Nutrition Reports | Tier : NA | IF estime : NA

Date de publication : 2026-07-27

Resultats principaux : This narrative review examined the methodological considerations underlying the associations between food insecurity (FIS) and five chronic conditions — depression, anxiety, hypertension, diabetes, and dyslipidemia — synthesizing data from prior systematic reviews and more recent studies conducted across multiple geographic regions and population groups. The study design allowed comparison between approaches relying on self-reported disease diagnosis versus objective biomarker measurement, revealing that the research methodology profoundly shapes reported associations. For mental health outcomes, numerous meta-analyses consistently demonstrated that individuals screening positive for FIS were significantly more likely to screen positive for depression and anxiety using validated instruments. In contrast, associations between FIS and cardiometabolic conditions including hypertension showed marked inconsistency, with rigorous meta-analyses failing to establish strong statistical links when disease presence was assessed through objective blood pressure measurement or biomarkers rather than self-report. Key limitations identified include reliance on self-reported hypertension diagnoses (versus measured blood pressure), substantial regional and population heterogeneity, inadequate covariate control in cross-sectional and longitudinal studies, and failure to account for somatic symptom burden when self-report measures are used as disease indicators.

Implications Medical Affairs : This review challenges the widely cited claim that food insecurity drives cardiometabolic disease, suggesting that much of the apparent association with hypertension may reflect methodological artifact rather than true causal relationships, which has direct implications for how pharmaceutical companies frame unmet medical need in socioeconomically disadvantaged populations. From a Medical Affairs perspective, these findings call for more rigorous evidence standards when positioning antihypertensive therapies in high-risk social determinants of health (SDOH) populations, as the actual disease burden may differ substantially from what self-report data suggest. Supporting research initiatives that combine objective blood pressure measurement with food insecurity screening would help establish more reliable epidemiological foundations and differentiate a company's commitment to evidence-based cardiovascular risk management — a compelling narrative for both KOL engagement and payer discussions.

A Study of Parent-of-Origin Effect of Single Nucleotide Polymorphisms of Imprinted Gene on Risk for Hypertension

Revue : Zhonghua Liu Xing Bing Xue Za Zhi | Tier : NA | IF estime : NA

Date de publication : 2026-07-10

Resultats principaux : This family-based genetic epidemiology study, conducted among probands and their relatives in 9 rural areas of Fangshan District, Beijing, investigated the parent-of-origin effect (POE) of the KCNQ1 imprinted gene on hypertension risk using a mixed-effects model in 1,261 participants and a log-linear model in 167 participants from 54 core families after quality control. A total of 195 SNPs in the KCNQ1 gene were analyzed, and association analysis identified 13 loci significantly associated with hypertension (all p<0.05). POE analysis identified 6 loci with possible parent-of-origin effects on hypertension (all p<0.05), with the most significant locus being rs231879, for which the maternal relative risk was 3.46 times higher than the paternal relative risk (95% CI: 1.36–8.57; p=0.009), indicating that inheriting the risk allele maternally confers substantially greater hypertension risk. Interaction analysis revealed that sleep duration may modify the POE of rs170786 on hypertension risk (interaction p=0.023), suggesting a meaningful epigenetic–environmental interaction pathway. Limitations include the small family sample size (54 core families, 167 participants) available for POE analysis, the rural northern Chinese population limiting generalizability, and the exploratory nature of the interaction findings.

Implications Medical Affairs : This study adds important evidence that epigenetic mechanisms — specifically genomic imprinting via KCNQ1 — may contribute to heritable hypertension risk in ways that standard additive genetic analyses overlook, potentially supporting precision medicine approaches in hypertension management. From a Medical Affairs perspective, KCNQ1-related research is particularly relevant given the gene's established role in cardiac ion channel function and its existing associations with QT prolongation and cardiac arrhythmias, warranting pharmacovigilance attention when assessing antihypertensive drug interactions in genetically susceptible populations. Engaging KOLs in pharmacogenomics and epigenetics to support replication studies in diverse ethnic populations would strengthen the scientific foundation and position a company as a leader in genetically-guided precision hypertension management.

Cardiovascular Risk Factor Prevalence and Control by Education Strata in Older Adults in 3 Countries

Revue : JAMA Network Open | Tier : NA | IF estime : NA

Date de publication : 2026-07-01

Resultats principaux : This secondary analysis of a serial cross-sectional study used nationally representative data from 120,678 adults aged 50 years and older in the US, England, and South Korea across periods from 1998 to 2020, comparing cardiovascular risk factor prevalence (cholesterol, blood pressure, diabetes) and management performance by education strata. The US showed consistently higher baseline risk than England (approximately 11.1 percentage points lower proportion at risk for cholesterol in period 4) and South Korea, yet US patients at risk were more likely to be aware of, treated for, and achieve controlled risk factor levels, resulting in broadly similar rates of uncontrolled risk across countries — both US and England showed 29% uncontrolled cholesterol risk. South Korea demonstrated dramatic gains in hypertension control, rising from 6% controlled in 1998–2001 to 42% in 2005–2008 (p for trend <0.001), and cholesterol control from 6% in 2005–2008 to 31% in 2015–2020 (p for trend <0.001), reflecting the impact of national health system investments. Across all three countries, risk factor prevalence was higher among adults without a college degree, but control rates among those at risk were similar by education strata, pointing to upstream risk accumulation rather than differential access to care as the driver of disparities. Limitations include the secondary cross-sectional nature of the analysis, risk of residual confounding, and variability in risk factor definitions across national surveys.

Implications Medical Affairs : The finding that cardiovascular risk factor prevalence — including hypertension — diverges markedly by education level while treatment and control rates remain broadly comparable across countries and education groups challenges the dominant narrative that healthcare access gaps drive cardiovascular disparities, and instead implicates upstream lifestyle and social determinants as the primary lever. From a Medical Affairs perspective, this cross-national evidence validates the real-world effectiveness of antihypertensive treatment programs once patients are engaged in care, providing a compelling argument for optimizing adherence and retention rather than access alone. KOL engagement and disease awareness investments should prioritize primary prevention and early detection among lower-education populations, as these data indicate the critical unmet need lies in identifying and initiating treatment in at-risk individuals before substantial risk accumulation occurs.

The Menopausal Transition and Cardiovascular Aging: Implications for Prevention in Midlife

Revue : Obstetrics and Gynecology Clinics of North America | Tier : NA | IF estime : NA

Date de publication : 2026-05-14

Resultats principaux : This review examined the complex interplay between the menopausal transition and cardiovascular aging in midlife women, focusing on how normal cardiovascular aging may accelerate in the presence of unhealthy lifestyle behaviors and cardiometabolic risk factors including hypertension, dyslipidemia, insulin resistance, obesity, and chronic inflammation, as well as environmental exposures. The review synthesized data on vascular and myocardial structural and functional remodeling occurring during the menopause transition, alongside cellular hallmarks of cardiovascular aging, highlighting the biological complexity of this critical period and the role of estrogen deficiency in modifying risk factor trajectories. Hypertension was identified as one of the central modifiable cardiometabolic risk factors converging during the menopausal transition, with data supporting accelerated blood pressure increases in the perimenopause and early postmenopause phases. The review emphasizes that cardiovascular health scores and lifestyle behaviors serve as key modifiable targets during this window, underscoring the potential for early preventive strategies to alter long-term cardiovascular aging trajectories in women. Limitations include the narrative nature of the review without formal meta-analytic synthesis, and the specific contributions of individual risk factors such as hypertension versus combined hormonal changes remain incompletely characterized, calling for prospective longitudinal studies.

Implications Medical Affairs : The menopausal transition represents a clinically underutilized window for cardiovascular risk factor identification and intervention, with hypertension control being particularly critical during this period, presenting Medical Affairs with a clear opportunity to advocate for earlier antihypertensive treatment initiation and monitoring in perimenopausal women. Pharmaceutical companies with antihypertensive portfolios should develop targeted evidence packages and patient education materials for women in midlife, particularly those with multiple converging cardiometabolic risk factors, to support prescribers — including gynecologists and menopause specialists — in identifying and treating hypertension earlier. Broadening KOL engagement beyond cardiologists to include reproductive health specialists and menopause medicine experts would expand the prescribing community and create a robust patient identification pipeline for earlier hypertension intervention in this high-risk, high-growth population.

Ischemic Heart Disease in the Middle East and North Africa, 1990-2021: An Integrated Analysis of Temporal Trends, Country-Level Variation, SDI Patterns, and Risk Factors

Revue : Health Science Reports | Tier : NA | IF estime : NA

Date de publication : 2026-07-25

Resultats principaux : This secondary analysis of Global Burden of Disease (GBD) 2021 estimates provided an integrated assessment of ischemic heart disease (IHD) burden across 21 MENA countries and territories from 1990 to 2021, reporting age-standardized prevalence, mortality, and disability-adjusted life years (DALYs) with 95% uncertainty intervals. In 2021, the age-standardized prevalence of IHD in MENA was 6,404.8 per 100,000 population (95% UI: 5,872.0–7,041.1), with a mortality rate of 202.8 (95% UI: 180.6–223.7) and DALY rate of 4,023.2 (95% UI: 3,581.7–4,507.5) per 100,000, representing a major public health burden. Substantial inter-country heterogeneity was observed: Kuwait had the highest age-standardized prevalence (7,806.4/100,000), Turkey the lowest (5,365.9/100,000), and Saudi Arabia saw the largest increase in age-standardized prevalence (+17.1%; 95% UI: 11.4–23.0) while Turkey had the largest decrease (-11.9%; 95% UI: -17.3 to -6.9) over the study period. Elevated systolic blood pressure was identified as the leading contributor to IHD DALYs at 49.6%, followed by elevated LDL cholesterol (39.6%) and exposure to ambient particulate matter pollution (28.2%), firmly establishing hypertension as the dominant modifiable risk factor for IHD in the MENA region. Limitations include inherent uncertainties in GBD modeled estimates, potential underreporting in data-limited countries, and the ecological study design that precludes individual-level causal inference.

Implications Medical Affairs : With elevated systolic blood pressure accounting for nearly 50% of IHD DALYs in the MENA region, there is a critical and commercially significant opportunity for hypertension management in this geographically diverse and economically growing market, particularly in high-prevalence and rapidly increasing-burden countries such as Kuwait and Saudi Arabia. Medical Affairs teams should collaborate with regional cardiovascular KOLs to generate country-specific data supporting antihypertensive therapy positioning aligned with national cardiovascular prevention strategies, leveraging the quantified risk attribution of hypertension to make compelling health system arguments. The co-occurrence of hypertension, dyslipidemia, and air pollution as the leading triad of IHD risk factors in MENA creates an opportunity for portfolio-level positioning that combines antihypertensive and lipid-lowering agents, supported by regionally tailored health economic analyses.

Effects of the Preventive Hypertension Self-Management Education Program for Deaf and Hard of Hearing Adults: A Randomized Controlled Trial

Revue : Journal of Deaf Studies and Deaf Education | Tier : NA | IF estime : NA

Date de publication : 2026-07-27

Resultats principaux : This single-center randomized controlled trial conducted in South Korea evaluated PRE-H-SMILE (PREventive Hypertension Self-Management Intervention using sign LanguagE), an 8-week sign language-based preventive hypertension program targeting deaf and hard of hearing (DHH) adults without prior hypertension who face increased cardiovascular risk due to communication barriers limiting health information access. A total of 37 participants completed the study (intervention: n=20; control: n=17), with the intervention group receiving weekly sign language educational videos and two in-person sessions, and the control group receiving standard care without targeted hypertension education. The primary outcome — total health-promoting behavior scores — increased significantly in the intervention group (chi-squared=5.530, p=0.019), demonstrating improved self-management behaviors overall. Sedentary time decreased significantly in the intervention group compared to controls (F=4.766, p=0.039), suggesting the program's capacity to reduce a key hypertension risk factor. Despite improvements in behavioral outcomes, no statistically significant changes in blood pressure were observed between groups, likely reflecting the study's small sample size (n=37), the 8-week duration insufficient for hemodynamic change, and the lack of long-term follow-up to assess sustained behavioral and physiological effects.

Implications Medical Affairs : This trial provides evidence that accessible, sign language-based health education can successfully modify hypertension risk behaviors in the deaf and hard of hearing population — a traditionally underserved and understudied group — highlighting a real-world gap in inclusive cardiovascular prevention programs that Medical Affairs teams should acknowledge in their equity-in-health and corporate social responsibility frameworks. Incorporating accessible patient education materials into hypertension disease awareness programs, and supporting research on health literacy interventions in disability communities, could differentiate a pharmaceutical company's medical engagement approach and strengthen relationships with payers and regulators increasingly focused on health equity. Partnering with disability advocacy organizations and nursing researchers in this space would create novel KOL relationships, extend the reach of hypertension awareness campaigns to underserved populations, and generate real-world adherence data in communities with high barriers to standard care.

Mangiferin as a Multilevel Modulator of Metabolic Syndrome: Current Evidence and Future Perspectives

Revue : Metabolites | Tier : NA | IF estime : NA

Date de publication : 2026-06-27

Resultats principaux : This systematic literature review searched PubMed, Scopus, and Web of Science from 2016 to 2025 and synthesized evidence on the pharmacological potential of mangiferin (Mgf), a C-glycosylated xanthone phytochemical, in managing metabolic syndrome (MetS) components including hypertension, dyslipidemia, insulin resistance, and obesity. Mangiferin was found to improve overall metabolic dysfunction through activation of the AMPK pathway, reduce inflammation via Nrf2 activation and suppression of NF-kappaB, COX-2, IL-6, and NLRP3 pro-inflammatory mediators, and modulate CB1 and PPAR receptors along with obesity-related adipokines (adiponectin, leptin, resistin) and autophagy processes. Potential hypertension-relevant effects were observed through anti-inflammatory and vasodilatory mechanisms in preclinical models, though blood pressure was not consistently measured as a primary endpoint across reviewed studies. Critically, the review identified that the vast majority of evidence derives from in silico, in vitro, and preclinical (animal) studies, with very few human clinical observations, representing a fundamental evidence gap before clinical translation can be considered feasible. Limitations explicitly noted include limited human clinical trial data, inadequate studies on mangiferin and norathyriol bioavailability, and absent data on gut microbiota interactions and metabolomic/epigenetic mechanisms in MetS patients.

Implications Medical Affairs : The near-complete absence of human clinical trial evidence for mangiferin means this compound represents an early-stage scientific interest rather than a near-term competitive threat or licensing opportunity for established antihypertensive pharmaceutical portfolios, but its multi-pathway MetS activity warrants continued horizon-scanning by Medical Affairs. Companies monitoring the natural products and nutraceutical space for adjunctive therapy development or phytopharmacology partnerships should maintain surveillance of mangiferin clinical trial registrations, particularly given growing regulatory interest in plant-derived cardiovascular agents in emerging markets. Ensuring that scientific leadership is prepared to contextualize phytopharmacological claims relative to established antihypertensive therapy — particularly in markets where alternative medicine is prevalent — represents a prudent proactive KOL education and medical communication strategy.

Acute Aortic Syndrome: From Risk Factors to Hospital Burden and Healthcare Resource Utilization

Revue : Clinics and Practice | Tier : NA | IF estime : NA

Date de publication : 2026-06-27

Resultats principaux : This narrative review synthesized contemporary evidence from PubMed/MEDLINE, Scopus, Web of Science, and Google Scholar (January 2000 to May 2026) on clinical, genetic, and environmental determinants of hospital resource utilization in acute aortic syndrome (AAS) — comprising acute aortic dissection, intramural haematoma, penetrating atherosclerotic ulcer, and limited intimal tear — with emphasis on studies from the last five years. Hypertension was identified as a principal modifiable risk factor for AAS alongside advanced age, female sex, smoking-related comorbidity, and hereditary aortopathies (connective tissue disorders), establishing blood pressure control as a key determinant of AAS occurrence. The review established resource-outcome links between hypertension, haemodynamic instability, malperfusion, delayed diagnosis, operative complexity, and postoperative complications on the one hand, and measurable downstream burdens on the other — including ICU length of stay, total hospital stay, reoperation rates, readmission, and longitudinal imaging surveillance costs. The authors conclude that earlier diagnosis, structured risk stratification, targeted genetic evaluation, and aggressive control of modifiable risk factors especially hypertension, alongside dedicated aortic network pathways, may shorten hospital stay and reduce avoidable costs in AAS. Limitations include the non-meta-analytic review design, heterogeneity in study definitions and designs reviewed, and the absence of prospective data directly linking antihypertensive adherence metrics to AAS incidence.

Implications Medical Affairs : Hypertension is firmly positioned as the primary modifiable risk factor driving both the occurrence and the disproportionate hospital resource burden of acute aortic syndrome — one of the most dramatic and costly consequences of uncontrolled blood pressure — providing a compelling health economics narrative for tighter treatment targets and improved long-term antihypertensive adherence. Medical Affairs teams should leverage this evidence in HEOR arguments directed at payers and hospital systems, quantifying the downstream cost avoidance achievable through improved hypertension management as part of a cardiovascular portfolio value proposition. Partnering with vascular surgery, cardiothoracic surgery, and aortic disease KOLs to generate real-world evidence linking antihypertensive adherence to AAS incidence and resource utilization would create high-impact publications and conference materials differentiating a company's commitment to catastrophic cardiovascular event prevention.

Risk Factors Associated with Maternal Postpartum Hospital Readmission: A Systematic Review

Revue : Nursing Reports | Tier : NA | IF estime : NA

Date de publication : 2026-06-26

Resultats principaux : This PRISMA-compliant systematic review searched CINAHL, EMBASE (Ovid), and MEDLINE for studies published from January 2010 to June 2024 (protocol registered on PROSPERO: CRD42023442269), ultimately including 60 studies from an initial 7,758 articles retrieved, examining risk factors for maternal postpartum hospital readmission within 42 days of discharge following birth hospitalisation. The rate of maternal postpartum readmissions varied widely across studies from 0.1236 per 1,000 to 26%, reflecting significant heterogeneity in study populations, health system contexts, and readmission definitions. Risk factors were categorized into five groups: maternal demographic and socioeconomic factors (including age and race/ethnicity); behavioral and lifestyle factors; health institution structural factors; obstetric and delivery characteristics (caesarean delivery, preterm birth); and maternal morbidities. Hypertensive disorders of pregnancy (HDP) emerged as one of the most frequently cited maternal morbidities associated with postpartum readmission, alongside mental health disorders and severe maternal morbidity, with risk of bias assessed using the Newcastle-Ottawa Scale confirming adequate study quality in the majority of included reports. Limitations include significant heterogeneity in readmission definition, follow-up periods, and reporting quality across the 60 included studies, precluding formal meta-analytic pooling of risk estimates.

Implications Medical Affairs : Hypertensive disorders of pregnancy — already recognized as harbingers of long-term maternal cardiovascular risk — are now confirmed by this systematic review as a major driver of costly postpartum hospital readmissions, making the immediate postpartum period a critical yet underutilized window for structured antihypertensive management that Medical Affairs should actively promote. Companies with antihypertensive portfolios should develop evidence-based postpartum hypertension management protocols in collaboration with maternal-fetal medicine specialists, obstetricians, and obstetric nurses, supporting both clinical guidelines and patient education campaigns targeting the 42-day high-risk window. Investing in real-world evidence generation quantifying how structured postpartum antihypertensive therapy reduces readmission rates and associated health system costs could yield high-impact publications and differentiate a pharmaceutical company's medical engagement across both cardiovascular and maternal health specialties, broadening KOL relationships and prescriber reach.
