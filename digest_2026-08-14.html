Assessing quality gaps in hypertensive disorders of pregnancy care in China: an unannounced standardised patient study protocol.

Revue : BMJ Open | Tier : NA | IF estime : NA

Date de publication : 2026-08-13

Resultats principaux : This study protocol employs unannounced standardised patients (USPs) to objectively evaluate outpatient obstetric care quality across six cities in China's Hebei, Shanxi, and Yunnan provinces, targeting hypertensive disorders of pregnancy (HDP) in a resource-constrained low-to-middle-income setting. A total of 108 clinical visits by trained USPs are planned between October and December 2025, assessing technical capabilities across four metrics: completeness of history-taking (0-1 scale), adherence to testing protocols (0-1 scale), diagnostic accuracy (ordinal 0-2), and therapeutic appropriateness (ordinal 0-2), alongside patient-centred care measured via the validated Chinese PPPC-R scale (range 1-4). OLS regression will be applied for continuous outcomes and logistic regression for binary outcomes, adjusting for city indicators and USP-scenario interactions; no primary quantitative results are yet available as this is a protocol publication. The study is registered with the Chinese Clinical Trial Registry (ChiCTR2500106140) and has received full ethics approval. Key limitations include the inability of USP methodology to fully replicate authentic patient encounters and geographic restriction to three Chinese provinces, limiting external generalizability.

Implications Medical Affairs : This protocol addresses a critical evidence gap in care quality for hypertensive disorders of pregnancy in LMICs, a topic highly relevant to maternal cardiovascular risk and guideline implementation fidelity. From a Medical Affairs perspective, the multidimensional quality assessment framework could inform standard-of-care benchmarks and support KOL advocacy for adherence to evidence-based antihypertensive protocols in obstetric settings. The anticipated results by December 2026 may generate opportunities to position maternal hypertension management as a distinct public health priority and stimulate investment in point-of-care diagnostic improvement in resource-limited countries.

Chronic kidney disease progression rate and predictors in hypertensive patients: A 5-year retrospective follow-up study in Ethiopia.

Revue : PloS one | Tier : NA | IF estime : NA

Date de publication : 2026-08-13

Resultats principaux : This retrospective follow-up study analyzed 510 medical records of hypertensive patients in South Gondar Zone hospitals, Northwest Ethiopia, from September 2019 to June 2024, using Kaplan-Meier, log-rank, and Cox proportional hazard regression analyses. CKD progressed at a median of 46 months (IQR: 37-55), with an incidence rate of 9.45 per 1000 person-months (95% CI: 8.07-11.01). Uncontrolled blood pressure was an independent predictor of rapid CKD progression (AHR=1.52, 95% CI: 1.05-2.20), along with obesity (AHR=2.56, 95% CI: 1.63-4.01) and high triglyceride levels (AHR=1.72, 95% CI: 1.03-2.91). No subgroup analyses were reported; however, the convergence of multiple metabolic risk factors driving progression was a notable finding. Limitations include retrospective design, single-region focus limiting generalizability, and lack of data on specific antihypertensive agents used.

Implications Medical Affairs : Uncontrolled hypertension significantly accelerating CKD progression reinforces current guideline recommendations for tight blood pressure control as a renal-protective strategy, particularly in low-resource African settings. From a Medical Affairs standpoint, the elevated risk associated with obesity and hypertriglyceridemia underscores the need for combination cardiometabolic management and could support expanded indication narratives for antihypertensive agents with proven renal outcomes. Engaging KOLs in sub-Saharan Africa to disseminate these data may strengthen advocacy for routine lipid and renal function monitoring in all hypertensive patients.

Analysis of demographic and clinical characteristics of patients with chronic renal failure in Mosul City, Iraq.

Revue : Medicinski glasnik | Tier : NA | IF estime : NA

Date de publication : 2026-08-07

Resultats principaux : This descriptive cross-sectional study recruited 89 patients with confirmed CKD undergoing hemodialysis at a public dialysis center in Mosul, Iraq between January and April 2024, using purposive sampling, structured interviews, and medical records review with chi-square and logistic regression analyses. Diabetes mellitus was the leading etiology (27%), followed by hypertension (23.6%), with ESRD representing 48.3% of cases and 71.9% of patients presenting in severe or ESRD stages, reflecting late diagnosis. Mean serum creatinine rose from 1.6+/-0.3 mg/dL in mild CKD to 8.5+/-2.3 mg/dL in ESRD, with BUN increasing from 22.5+/-5.2 to 89.6+/-15.1 mg/dL. Multivariate logistic regression identified male sex as an independent predictor of advanced CKD (OR=2.14; 95% CI: 1.05-4.36; p=0.031). Limitations include small sample size (n=89), single-center design, and the post-conflict healthcare context limiting representativeness.

Implications Medical Affairs : The predominance of hypertension as a leading CKD cause in a conflict-affected setting illustrates the catastrophic consequences of inadequate blood pressure management and delayed diagnosis, calling for urgent preventive investment. Medical Affairs teams should consider these data as evidence for strengthening early-detection advocacy programs and ensuring antihypertensive access in resource-limited Middle Eastern settings. Supporting KOL engagement around primary prevention and early nephrology referral protocols in post-conflict communities could represent a high-impact Medical Affairs contribution.

Association Between Maternal Protein Intake and the Risk of Adverse Pregnancy Outcomes.

Revue : Nutrients | Tier : NA | IF estime : NA

Date de publication : 2026-08-04

Resultats principaux : This secondary analysis of the NuMoM2b prospective cohort included 7,067 nulliparous pregnant women stratified into quartiles of daily protein intake (Q1 <0.6 to Q4 >=1.1 g/kg/day), evaluating associations with HDP, GDM, SGA, and LGA outcomes using multivariable logistic regression. Lower protein intake was significantly more prevalent in Black women, less-educated women, unemployed individuals, those with lower income, and government-insured participants (all p<0.05). Moderate protein intake in Q3 (0.8-1.1 g/kg) was associated with lower odds of SGA below the 10th percentile (aOR 0.78, 95% CI 0.61-0.99) after adjusting for age, insurance, education, income, chronic hypertension, and dietary kcal. Protein intake was not significantly associated with HDP, GDM, or LGA outcomes. Limitations include observational design, potential dietary recall bias, and the exploratory nature requiring prospective confirmation.

Implications Medical Affairs : While no direct association with HDP was detected, the social determinants of low protein intake disproportionately affecting minority and low-income pregnant women highlight intersectional risks for hypertensive complications warranting further investigation. Medical Affairs strategies could leverage these findings to advocate for nutritional screening as part of prenatal hypertension risk management, especially in underserved populations. This study supports the rationale for trials integrating dietary optimization with antihypertensive interventions in pregnant women at elevated cardiovascular risk.

Association Between Body Roundness Index and Periodontitis in Adults With Hypertension: A Cross-Sectional Study.

Revue : Clinical and experimental dental research | Tier : NA | IF estime : NA

Date de publication : 2026-08

Resultats principaux : This cross-sectional study analyzed data from 4,117 US adults with hypertension from NHANES 2009-2014, investigating body roundness index (BRI) and periodontitis using multivariable logistic regression, generalized additive models, and two-piecewise regression. After covariate adjustment, participants in the highest BRI quartile had significantly higher odds of periodontitis vs. the lowest quartile (OR=1.27, 95% CI: 1.05-1.54, p=0.0122 in Model I; OR=1.25, 95% CI: 1.03-1.53, p=0.0258 in Model II). Stratified and sensitivity analyses yielded broadly consistent results. Limitations include cross-sectional design precluding causal inference and potential unmeasured confounders.

Implications Medical Affairs : The association between visceral adiposity measured by BRI and periodontitis in hypertensive patients underscores the systemic inflammatory burden contributing to cardiovascular risk, supporting an integrated oral-cardiovascular health monitoring approach. Medical Affairs teams could highlight the importance of oral health assessment as part of a comprehensive cardiovascular risk reduction strategy in hypertensive patients. This evidence supports collaboration with dental health KOLs and could inspire studies examining whether periodontal treatment modulates blood pressure and inflammatory outcomes.

Rethinking Mediterranean Diet Adherence and Body Composition: A 3-Year Prospective Observational Study in Patients with Type 2 Diabetes and Arterial Hypertension.

Revue : Nutrients | Tier : NA | IF estime : NA

Date de publication : 2026-08-01

Resultats principaux : This three-year prospective observational study enrolled 158 adults with comorbid T2DM and arterial hypertension at a Croatian university hospital nutrition clinic, assessing Mediterranean diet adherence (MDSS), body composition via multi-frequency BIA, and cardiometabolic parameters longitudinally. Significant reductions were observed in body weight, BMI, waist circumference, muscle mass, skeletal muscle index, and phase angle (all p<0.001). Lipid profile improved with reductions in total cholesterol and LDL-C and an increase in HDL-C; however, renal function declined (increased creatinine/urea, decreased eGFR). Implications Medical Affairs : High Mediterranean diet adherence remains a minority achievement in real-world high-risk patients despite structured clinical support, highlighting persistent gaps between dietary guidelines and actual practice. For Medical Affairs, the simultaneous lipid improvement but progressive renal decline and increased CVD events reinforces the indispensability of pharmacological cardiovascular protection alongside lifestyle interventions. KOL engagement strategies should emphasize multidisciplinary care integrating antihypertensive, cardiological, and nutritional management to decelerate cardiovascular and renal deterioration.

Association and causality between triglycerides and hypertension: evidence from NHANES 2007-2018 and two-sample Mendelian randomisation analysis.

Revue : Cardiovascular journal of Africa | Tier : NA | IF estime : NA

Date de publication : 2026-07-31

Resultats principaux : This study combined cross-sectional analysis of 14,567 NHANES participants (2007-2018; 6,428 with hypertension) with two-sample Mendelian randomization using GWAS data to evaluate the association and causal relationship between triglyceride levels and hypertension. Weighted multivariable logistic regression showed a significant positive association between triglycerides and hypertension (OR: 1.002, 95% CI: 1.001-1.003; p<0.001). Restricted cubic spline confirmed a significant nonlinear positive association (p for non-linearity <0.001), and subgroup analysis revealed a significant race interaction (p for interaction <0.001). TSMR analysis supported causality with no significant pleiotropy or heterogeneity across sensitivity analyses. Limitations include cross-sectional observational component and restricted GWAS racial representation.

Implications Medical Affairs : Mendelian randomization substantially strengthens causal inference between hypertriglyceridemia and hypertension, providing evidence that lipid management should be formally integrated with blood pressure control in current hypertension guidelines. Medical Affairs teams can leverage these data to position lipid-lowering approaches as complementary to antihypertensives, reinforcing a combined cardiometabolic risk reduction narrative. This opens avenues for KOL discussions around updating guidelines to include triglyceride assessment and investigating whether triglyceride-lowering agents confer indirect antihypertensive benefits.

Determinants of hypertension medication adherence among older adults in a resource-limited peri-urban setting, Ghana.

Revue : Frontiers in public health | Tier : NA | IF estime : NA

Date de publication : 2026-07-29

Resultats principaux : This community-based cross-sectional study enrolled 202 older adults (>=60 years) with hypertension in Sokode, Ho, Ghana, assessing knowledge and medication adherence via structured questionnaire. Despite 80.2% demonstrating good knowledge, 60.9% showed poor medication adherence; common barriers included forgetfulness (46%), stopping when feeling better (46.5%), and cultural/religious beliefs (50.5%). Income between 1000-2000 GHS (aOR=0.57, 95% CI: 0.41-0.81, p=0.002) and above 2000 GHS (aOR=0.87, p=0.002) were paradoxically associated with lower adherence, while age >79 years (aOR=2.3, p=0.050) and >10 years since diagnosis (aOR=3.11, p=0.045) were associated with higher adherence. Analysis used chi-square tests and multivariate regression. Limitations include cross-sectional design, self-reported measures susceptible to social desirability bias, and small single-community sample.

Implications Medical Affairs : The stark disconnect between knowledge and adherence—over 60% showing poor adherence—demonstrates patient education alone is insufficient for blood pressure control in resource-limited African settings and that practical barriers require targeted solutions. Medical Affairs teams should design patient support programs incorporating medication reminders, simplified regimens, and culturally sensitive counseling as evidence-based adherence-enhancing tools. Collaborating with local KOLs to develop community-based adherence interventions and advocating for affordable antihypertensive access in sub-Saharan Africa represent high-impact strategic opportunities.

Arterial stiffness parameters during six-month antihypertensive therapy: a prospective observational study.

Revue : Medicinski glasnik | Tier : NA | IF estime : NA

Date de publication : 2026-07-21

Resultats principaux : This prospective open-label observational study enrolled 200 hypertensive patients in Bosnia and Herzegovina, evaluating the effects of 13 antihypertensive treatment combinations on arterial stiffness (PWV and AIx) over six months. Six months of treatment significantly reduced SBP from 136.06+/-15 to 130.69+/-12 mmHg and DBP from 85.35+/-9 to 82.69+/-7 mmHg. PWV changed minimally overall (8.09+/-1.5 to 8.05+/-1.2 m/s, p=0.354), but AIx improved from 25.17% to 23.33%; the greatest AIx reduction was with ACEi/CCB/BB/diuretic/statin combination (-15.64%, p<0.001). Baseline SBP was the strongest PWV predictor (AUC=0.627, p=0.002). Limitations include open-label design without a control arm and small patient numbers per treatment combination.

Implications Medical Affairs : Combination antihypertensive regimens including ACE inhibitors can meaningfully reduce surrogate cardiovascular risk markers such as the augmentation index, even when overall PWV improvements are modest. Medical Affairs teams can reinforce the value of arterial stiffness monitoring as a complementary cardiovascular risk assessment tool alongside standard blood pressure measurement. These findings provide actionable evidence for KOL engagement around personalized antihypertensive regimen selection in patients with elevated baseline arterial stiffness.

Sex-specific associations of the CALLY index with mortality in hypertension: Stronger relative protection in males but greater absolute benefit in females — a retrospective cohort study.

Revue : Science progress | Tier : NA | IF estime : NA

Date de publication : 2026-08-13

Resultats principaux : This retrospective cohort study used NHANES 1999-2010 data to examine the CALLY index (CRP-albumin-lymphocyte composite) as a prognostic marker in 9,199 hypertensive adults, linked to the National Death Index through December 2019 (median follow-up 11.2 years). 3,259 all-cause deaths (35.4%) and 1,111 cardiovascular deaths (12.1%) occurred. Results are hypothesis-generating given the observational design.

Implications Medical Affairs : The CALLY index offers a novel inflammatory-nutritional biomarker dimension for cardiovascular risk stratification in hypertensive patients with sex-specific utility warranting further prospective investigation. Medical Affairs teams should monitor inflammation-nutrition indices as adjuncts to standard cardiovascular risk tools, potentially supporting positioning of anti-inflammatory or nutritional support therapies in cardiovascular care pathways. Sex-differentiated findings reinforce the need for sex-stratified outcomes studies in hypertension research, aligning with evolving regulatory guidance on sex-disaggregated clinical evidence.

Upregulated Renal Mineralocorticoid Receptor and 11beta-Hydroxysteroid Dehydrogenase Type 2 Are Associated with K+ Retention During MR Antagonism in Female Spontaneously Hypertensive Rats.

Revue : International journal of molecular sciences | Tier : NA | IF estime : NA

Date de publication : 2026-08-02

Resultats principaux : This preclinical study assessed sex-specific effects of mineralocorticoid receptor (MR) antagonism with spironolactone on blood pressure and electrolyte balance in spontaneously hypertensive rats (SHRs) aged 10-15 weeks, comparing male and female animals to normotensive Wistar-Kyoto rats as controls. Female SHRs showed higher serum K+ with transiently reduced urinary K+ excretion and a fourfold compensatory rise in aldosterone after spironolactone, effects absent in male SHRs. Females had approximately twofold higher renal MR protein expression and 1.7-fold greater renal 11beta-HSD2 levels vs. males, with plasma renin activity and corticosterone 19% and 41% lower in females, respectively. MR antagonism did not alter ENaC activity, blood pressure, or amiloride-sensitive urinary electrolyte excretion in either sex, suggesting low RAAS activity limits antihypertensive efficacy of MR antagonists in SHRs. Key limitations include preclinical model constraints and uncertain translational validity of SHR sex differences to human MR pharmacology.

Implications Medical Affairs : These findings provide mechanistic insight into sex-specific renal MR expression and K+ regulation under MR antagonism, with direct implications for understanding hyperkalemia risk in female patients on spironolactone or other MR antagonists. Medical Affairs teams developing safety communications for MR antagonist products should consider these as supportive preclinical evidence for sex-disaggregated potassium monitoring protocols in clinical practice. The differential RAAS activity suggests biomarker-guided patient selection based on renin-aldosterone status may be needed to optimize antihypertensive efficacy of MR antagonists, warranting dedicated clinical study.

Association between Long-Term Ambient PM2.5 Exposure and Hypertension and Metabolic Syndrome among Ghanaian Adults.

Revue : Environmental research | Tier : NA | IF estime : NA

Date de publication : 2026-08-13

Resultats principaux : This cross-sectional analysis included 1,833 Ghanaian adults aged 40-60 years from the Awi-Gen study, using satellite-based PM2.5 exposure estimates (2007-2015) and multivariable logistic regression to assess associations with metabolic syndrome and its components including hypertension. Each 1 microg/m3 PM2.5 increase was associated with 6% higher hypertension odds (OR: 1.06, 95% CI: 1.02-1.11), with a dose-response relationship across quartiles (p-trend=0.003) and highest quartile linked to 50% greater odds. Limitations include cross-sectional design and reliance on satellite-based rather than individual PM2.5 measurements.

Implications Medical Affairs : This study provides important evidence that long-term ambient air pollution is a significant population-attributable driver of hypertension in sub-Saharan Africa, a relationship critically underrecognized in global health policy. Medical Affairs teams should advocate for integration of environmental risk assessment into hypertension management guidelines and support air quality management as a cardiovascular prevention policy priority in low-income countries. Partnering with KOLs in environmental epidemiology and cardiovascular medicine could position pharmaceutical companies as advocates for comprehensive public health approaches to hypertension prevention.

Revue : Blood pressure | Tier : Tier 3 | IF estime : ~3

Date de publication : 2026-08-13

Resultats principaux : This cross-sectional study analyzed 15,650 Chinese older adults (mean age 87.16 years; 57% female) from the 2008 CLHLS wave, examining self-reported sleep duration and hypertension (SBP >=140 mmHg and/or DBP >=90 mmHg or physician diagnosis). Among participants, 46.9% had hypertension; short sleep (<6h) was associated with 1.30-fold higher hypertension odds (aOR 1.30; 95% CI 1.17-1.44) vs. 6-9h. The relationship followed a non-linear J-shaped curve (p for non-linearity <0.05), and the association was more pronounced in adults without ADL limitations (p interaction=0.009). Limitations include self-reported sleep susceptible to recall bias and cross-sectional design precluding causal inference.

Implications Medical Affairs : This large dataset from the oldest-old confirms a significant dose-response relationship between short sleep and hypertension risk, reinforcing the clinical relevance of sleep assessment in geriatric hypertension management and potentially informing guideline updates. Medical Affairs teams should recognize sleep hygiene as an emerging non-pharmacological intervention target alongside antihypertensive therapy, supporting collaborative sleep intervention trials in elderly hypertensive populations. Engaging KOLs in geriatric cardiology to advocate for sleep duration screening in primary care hypertension protocols represents a timely and evidence-supported Medical Affairs strategy.

Dietary Quality Along a Plant-Based Continuum: The Role of Added Salt, Oil, and Sugar in Chronic Disease Risk.

Revue : Nutrients | Tier : NA | IF estime : NA

Date de publication : 2026-08-04

Resultats principaux : This narrative review synthesized evidence from systematic reviews, meta-analyses, Mendelian randomization studies, and key clinical trials to evaluate whether whole-food plant-based diets (WFPBDs) minimizing added salt, oil, and sugar (SOS) provide incremental cardiometabolic benefit over WFPBDs including these additives. Dietary sodium was found to increase blood pressure linearly and dose-dependently, promoting overconsuption and adiposity. Implications Medical Affairs : This review consolidates the mechanistic case for dietary sodium restriction as a cornerstone of non-pharmacological hypertension management, reinforcing the guideline-pharmaceutical positioning relationship in the cardiovascular prevention continuum. Medical Affairs teams can support partnerships with nutritional KOLs around integrated cardiometabolic risk reduction messaging complementing antihypertensive pharmacotherapy. The identification of SOS as high-impact dietary risk modulators opens avenues for clinical research on tailored dietary interventions as adjuncts to antihypertensive therapy.
