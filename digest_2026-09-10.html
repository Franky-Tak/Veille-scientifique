Hypertension identified in early adulthood as an endocrine-vascular risk factor for uterine fibroids in reproductive-age women: a nationwide cohort study.

Revue : Frontiers in endocrinology | Tier : NA | IF estime : NA

Date de publication : 2026-08-25

Resultats principaux : This nationwide population-based cohort study used data from the Korean National Health Insurance Service, enrolling 2,558,659 women aged 20-39 years without prior uterine fibroid diagnosis, followed for a mean of 11.54 years until December 31, 2023. The study categorized blood pressure as normotension, elevated/stage 1 hypertension, stage 2 hypertension, or treated hypertension per national guidelines, with incident uterine fibroids identified by ICD-10 code D25. During follow-up, 373,379 women (14.59%) developed uterine fibroids, with elevated/stage 1 hypertension associated with adjusted HR 1.04 (95% CI 1.03-1.05) and stage 2 hypertension with HR 1.06 (95% CI 1.04-1.09, both P<0.001). Women receiving antihypertensive medication showed the highest risk (HR 1.10, 95% CI 1.06-1.14, P<0.001), with associations stronger in women with BMI <25 kg/m2 and waist circumference <85 cm (both interaction P<0.001). No differential association was observed by antihypertensive medication class, suggesting blood pressure dysregulation itself rather than specific drug classes drives fibroid risk.

Implications Medical Affairs : This study establishes early-adulthood hypertension as a clinically relevant reproductive health risk factor, suggesting current guidelines may need to broaden their consideration of hypertension consequences beyond traditional cardiovascular endpoints. From a Medical Affairs perspective, these findings underscore the value of monitoring reproductive health outcomes in clinical trials targeting young women with hypertension, providing a new dimension for product differentiation beyond classical cardiovascular endpoints. The data support investment in KOL engagement with OB-GYN specialists and reproductive endocrinologists, positioning antihypertensive therapy not only as cardiovascular protection but also as a potential modifier of womens reproductive health outcomes.

Atherogenic, cardiometabolic, and adiposity composite indices for identifying prehypertension and hypertension: conventional regression and explainable machine learning.

Revue : Frontiers in endocrinology | Tier : NA | IF estime : NA

Date de publication : 2026-08-25

Resultats principaux : This hospital-based cross-sectional study enrolled 2,136 adults from a health examination center in China (2024-2025) and classified participants into normal blood pressure (46.02%), prehypertension (19.76%), and hypertension (34.22%) groups. The study evaluated multiple atherogenic, cardiometabolic, and adiposity composite indices including CVAI, TyG-WC, TyG-BMI, and METS-IR using multivariable regression, restricted cubic splines, mixture analysis, and explainable machine learning with SHAP values. In fully adjusted analyses, CVAI (OR 2.17, 95% CI 1.80-2.62), TyG-WC (OR 1.94, 95% CI 1.64-2.28), TyG-BMI (OR 1.93, 95% CI 1.66-2.26), and METS-IR (OR 1.91, 95% CI 1.63-2.24) showed the strongest standardized associations with abnormal blood pressure. The cross-validated AUROC improved from 0.80 to 0.83 after adding clinical and composite index information, with SHAP analysis highlighting CVAI, age, fasting glucose, waist circumference, eGFR, and uric acid as the key predictors. Notable limitations include the cross-sectional design preventing causal inference, single-center recruitment limiting generalizability, and a predominantly Chinese study population restricting applicability to other ethnicities.

Implications Medical Affairs : The integration of composite metabolic indices with machine learning for blood pressure risk stratification represents a methodological advance that may enhance early identification of at-risk patients in both clinical practice and clinical trial screening protocols. From a Medical Affairs standpoint, these findings support the framing of hypertension within a broader cardiometabolic context, relevant to positioning treatments that address insulin resistance and visceral adiposity alongside blood pressure control. Engaging KOLs in endocrinology and metabolic medicine, alongside cardiovascular specialists, could amplify the medical communication strategy around multi-target risk reduction in hypertensive patients with metabolic comorbidities.

Comparison of a Modified Regimen of Prophylactic Phenylephrine Boluses Versus Variable Rate Infusion During Elective Cesarean Delivery Under Spinal Anesthesia: A Noninferiority Randomized Double-Blind Study.

Revue : A&A practice | Tier : NA | IF estime : NA

Date de publication : 2026-09-08

Resultats principaux : This noninferiority randomized double-blind study enrolled 80 healthy pregnant women undergoing elective cesarean delivery under spinal anesthesia, comparing a modified phenylephrine bolus regimen (100 mcg after spinal anesthesia and at every SBP <90% of baseline) to a variable-rate prophylactic infusion (starting at 50 mcg/min). The primary outcome was the number of physician interventions needed to maintain target systolic blood pressure, analyzed with a noninferiority margin of two interventions. The bolus group required significantly fewer physician interventions (median 3, IQR 2-4) versus the infusion group (median 6, IQR 5-8; P<0.001), with phenylephrine requirements also lower in the bolus group (300 mcg vs 630 mcg; P<0.001). The noninferiority criterion was met (difference of medians -3, 95% CI -4 to -2), with no significant differences in hypotension, hypertension, or bradycardia incidence, neonatal outcomes, or maternal complications between the groups. Study limitations include the single-center design, relatively small sample size (n=80), and restriction to healthy uncomplicated singleton pregnancies, which may limit generalizability to higher-risk obstetric populations.

Implications Medical Affairs : This study provides level 1 evidence that a modified bolus phenylephrine regimen is non-inferior to continuous infusion for blood pressure management during spinal anesthesia for cesarean delivery, potentially simplifying clinical protocols without compromising maternal or neonatal safety. From a Medical Affairs perspective, these findings are relevant to anesthesia practice guidelines and support more resource-efficient administration strategies in obstetric settings, particularly in healthcare systems with limited infusion pump availability. This evidence base can be leveraged for KOL engagement in obstetric anesthesiology circles, highlighting the role of vasopressor optimization in the prevention and management of obstetric hypotension.

Application effects of information-based health management combined with knowledge-attitude-practice health education model in hypertensive patients.

Revue : Brazilian journal of medical and biological research | Tier : NA | IF estime : NA

Date de publication : 2026-09-07

Resultats principaux : This randomized controlled study enrolled 200 hypertensive patients divided equally between a control group receiving conventional health management and an intervention group receiving information-based health management combined with the knowledge-attitude-practice (KAP) model. The primary and secondary outcomes included blood pressure control rate, systolic and diastolic blood pressure levels, medication adherence, self-management abilities assessed by the HPSMBRS, health behaviors by HPLP-II, quality of life by GQOLI-74, and patient satisfaction with management. Following the intervention, the blood pressure control rate was significantly higher in the intervention group versus the control group (P<0.05), with lower post-management systolic and diastolic blood pressure values also observed in the intervention group. Medication adherence, self-management ability, health behavior scores, quality of life, and patient satisfaction were all significantly higher in the intervention group (all P<0.05), indicating a multidimensional benefit of the combined approach. Key limitations include the single-center design, Chinese population context limiting global generalizability, a relatively short follow-up period, and the absence of long-term cardiovascular outcomes data.

Implications Medical Affairs : The integration of digital health management with behavioral education models demonstrates meaningful improvements in hypertension self-management, supporting the growing body of evidence for digital health as an adjunct to pharmacological treatment and its potential incorporation into updated clinical guidelines. From a Medical Affairs perspective, these findings reinforce the value of patient education programs as a key component of adherence-focused strategies, directly relevant to real-world effectiveness positioning of antihypertensive treatments in emerging markets like China. Partnerships with digital health companies and health education KOLs could be strategically explored to develop patient engagement programs that complement pharmacological interventions and strengthen value-based healthcare positioning.

Resultats principaux : This narrative review systematically examines how social, reproductive, and environmental determinants interact across the female life course to influence cardiovascular health, including hypertension risk and outcomes. The review identifies socioeconomic disadvantage, limited healthcare access, caregiving responsibilities, psychosocial stress, discrimination, and neighborhood deprivation as key non-traditional cardiovascular risk factors that interact with cardiometabolic profiles and preventive care access. Reproductive health factors such as infertility, parity, assisted reproductive technology use, and hypertensive disorders of pregnancy were identified as clinically relevant markers for subsequent cardiovascular risk assessment. Using Machine Learning to Identify Social Risk Factors of Hypertension and Diabetes in New York City: Evidence to Support the HealthyNYC Initiative.

Revue : Journal of the American Heart Association | Tier : NA | IF estime : NA

Date de publication : 2026-09-09

Resultats principaux : This retrospective cohort study analyzed clinical data on 3.2 million New York City residents integrated with social determinants of health (SDoH) data at the census tract level, using Extreme Gradient Boosting machine learning to identify social risk factors for hypertension and diabetes prevalence. The model explained 70-80% of cross-tract variation in hypertension prevalence (R2=66.2-79.5%, RMSE=2.6-3.2) and diabetes prevalence (R2=72.9-79.3%, RMSE=2.1-2.2) across NYC census tracts. SHAP analysis identified the top 10 SDoH contributing to 70-73% of model predictions, centered on socioeconomic disadvantage, built environment, and commute time domains. The study found neighborhood-specific SDoH patterns varying across NYC census tracts and boroughs, providing evidence to prioritize place-based interventions under the HealthyNYC initiative targeting a 5% reduction in cardiovascular disease and diabetes deaths by 2030. Limitations include the observational retrospective design precluding causal claims, potential residual confounding from unmeasured social factors, and geographic restriction to NYC which may limit generalizability to other urban environments.

Implications Medical Affairs : This study provides robust large-scale evidence that social determinants of health account for the majority of cross-neighborhood variation in hypertension prevalence, directly supporting calls for integrated social-medical approaches to hypertension management embedded in public health frameworks. From a Medical Affairs perspective, these findings underscore the importance of addressing social determinants alongside pharmacological interventions in real-world evidence strategies, informing both payer value narratives and population health program designs. Revue : Journal of the American Heart Association | Tier : NA | IF estime : NA

Date de publication : 2026-09-09

Resultats principaux : This time-stratified case-crossover study conducted in Hangzhou, China (2015-2023) investigated the acute associations of major PM2.5 components with acute aortic dissection (AAD) and its Stanford subtypes (Type A and Type B), applying conditional logistic regression and weighted quantile sum models for single and joint exposure analyses. At lag 0-1 days, black carbon, organic matter, and sulfate were significantly associated with AAD, particularly type A, with non-significant positive associations for type B. An interquartile range increase in black carbon, organic matter, and sulfate was associated with 42% (OR 1.42, 95% CI 1.15-1.75), 35% (OR 1.35, 95% CI 1.10-1.64), and 36% (OR 1.36, 95% CI 1.09-1.69) increased risk of type A AAD, respectively. Weighted quantile sum modeling confirmed significant joint associations of PM2.5 components with overall AAD and type A, with black carbon contributing >50% of the joint associations. Age, smoking, alcohol consumption, and hypertension were identified as significant effect modifiers, with hypertensive patients appearing particularly vulnerable to PM2.5 component-associated AAD risk.

Implications Medical Affairs : These findings establish specific PM2.5 components, particularly black carbon, as acute environmental triggers for aortic dissection, with hypertension identified as a key vulnerability modifier, reinforcing the need for environmental risk factors to be incorporated into cardiovascular emergency prevention strategies. From a Medical Affairs standpoint, the interaction between air pollution and hypertension in AAD risk provides a compelling scientific narrative for the importance of optimal blood pressure control as a protective measure against catastrophic environmental cardiovascular events. This evidence could be leveraged in medical education programs targeting cardiovascular specialists and emergency physicians, highlighting the clinical urgency of antihypertensive treatment adherence in pollution-exposed and urban patient populations.

Revue : Journal of nutrition education and behavior | Tier : NA | IF estime : NA

Date de publication : 2026-09-09

Resultats principaux : This secondary analysis used cross-sectional nationally representative data from the ninth Korea National Health and Nutrition Examination Survey (KNHANES 2022-2023), involving adults over 19 years stratified into four age groups (19-44, 45-64, 65-74, and 75+ years), assessing behavioral and dietary risk factors for diabetes, hypertension, and hypercholesterolemia. The study applied multivariable logistic regression to examine associations of demographic, behavioral, and dietary factors with each chronic condition, stratified by age group. In the full sample, older age and obesity were consistently associated with higher odds of all three conditions (all P<0.001), with obesity showing its strongest association with diabetes in younger adults aged 19-44 (P<0.001). Key limitations include the cross-sectional design preventing causal inference, potential recall bias in dietary and lifestyle self-reporting, and restricted applicability given the exclusively Korean study population.

Implications Medical Affairs : The age-stratified identification of behavioral risk factors for hypertension, particularly the role of dietary patterns in older adults, supports the development of targeted lifestyle intervention programs integrated within pharmacological treatment strategies. Revue : Journal of general internal medicine | Tier : NA | IF estime : NA

Date de publication : 2026-09-08

Resultats principaux : This qualitative study conducted nine semi-structured virtual focus groups (April 2022-April 2023) with 43 hypertensive patients from Latino, Black/African American, Filipino, Korean, and Chinese ethnic groups in the Los Angeles safety-net healthcare system, specifically targeting populations with the lowest blood pressure control rates in a large public healthcare system. The study applied reflexive thematic analysis to explore barriers and contributors to equitable hypertension care across four key themes: diagnosis acceptance, desire for personalized guidance, clinical factors, and community support environments. Key barriers identified included gradual processing of hypertension diagnosis, medication concerns, physical mobility or mental health limitations affecting self-care, primarily patient-initiated clinical follow-through, and insufficient awareness of or access to community resources. Most participants were very low-income (77%), foreign-born (72%), and preferred a language other than English (58%), underscoring structural socioeconomic and linguistic barriers compounding hypertension self-management in multiethnic safety-net populations. Limitations include the qualitative design precluding quantitative generalization, possible selection bias toward more engaged patients, and restriction to the Los Angeles safety-net context limiting broader applicability.

Implications Medical Affairs : This study provides actionable insights for designing culturally sensitive and linguistically appropriate hypertension management interventions in multiethnic low-income populations, directly relevant to the development of patient support programs accompanying antihypertensive therapies. The association between metabolic dysfunction-associated fatty liver disease and left ventricular mass index in patients with schizophrenia with long-term hospitalization.

Revue : Frontiers in endocrinology | Tier : NA | IF estime : NA

Date de publication : 2026-08-25

Resultats principaux : This cross-sectional study enrolled 430 long-term hospitalized patients with schizophrenia, investigating the association between metabolic dysfunction-associated fatty liver disease (MAFLD) and left ventricular mass index (LVMI) using echocardiography and ultrasound, with hypertension and fasting blood glucose assessed as potential mediators via bias-corrected bootstrap mediation analysis. MAFLD was identified in 231 cases (53.7% prevalence), and those with MAFLD had significantly higher LVMI values (78.79 vs 73.95 g/m2, mean difference 4.84, 95% CI 1.2-8.5, P=0.009). Multivariate regression confirmed a positive association between MAFLD and LVMI after adjusting for age, sex, and hypertension (beta=3.53, 95% CI 0.49-6.57, P=0.023), while Spearman correlation showed r=0.126 (P=0.009). Mediation analysis revealed that hypertension played a key mediating role in the MAFLD-LVMI relationship (indirect effect=1.244, 95% CI 0.35-2.14, P=0.007), accounting for 29.8% of the total effect, while the direct effect of MAFLD on LVMI was not significant after accounting for hypertension. Notable limitations include the cross-sectional design preventing causal conclusions, the specific long-term hospitalized schizophrenia population limiting broader applicability, and potential confounding from antipsychotic medications.

Implications Medical Affairs : This study highlights hypertension as a critical mediator between metabolic liver disease and cardiac remodeling in a high-risk psychiatric population, supporting the integration of cardiovascular risk screening protocols into psychiatric care pathways. From a Medical Affairs perspective, these findings provide evidence for the importance of blood pressure management in patients with psychiatric comorbidities, a population often underrepresented in clinical trials and at disproportionately high risk for cardiovascular disease. Engaging psychiatry and cardiovascular KOLs in collaborative research initiatives could generate compelling real-world evidence supporting antihypertensive treatment benefits in cardiometabolic-psychiatric comorbidity contexts.
