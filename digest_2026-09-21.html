Component- and dimension-level network associations between sleep quality and health-related quality of life in older adults with hypertension.

Revue : Frontiers in Public Health | Tier : NA | IF estime : NA

Date de publication : 2026-09-15

Resultats principaux : This cross-sectional network analysis enrolled 2,257 older adults with hypertension in China, assessing sleep quality with the B-PSQI, health-related quality of life (HRQoL) with the EQ-5D-5L, and depressive symptoms with the PHQ-9. The investigators constructed a component- and dimension-level Gaussian graphical network (EBICGlasso regularization) yielding 10 nodes and 32 non-zero edges out of 45 possible connections, producing a network density of 0.711. The strongest intra-network associations were observed between sleep efficiency and sleep duration (edge weight 0.793), sleep interruption and subjective sleep quality (0.583), and self-care and usual activities (0.560), indicating tightly coupled symptom clusters. Usual activities demonstrated the highest strength centrality and expected influence across the entire network, while sleep efficiency exhibited the highest bridge expected influence linking sleep components to HRQoL dimensions; centrality stability was excellent (CS = 0.75). Importantly, no statistically significant differences were detected in network structure (M = 0.224, p > 0.05) or global network strength (S = 0.119, p > 0.05) between participants with and without depressive symptoms, suggesting the sleep-HRQoL architecture is robust across depressive status, though the authors caution these are correlational findings unsuitable for direct intervention inference.

Implications Medical Affairs : The identification of usual activities and sleep efficiency as the most central and bridging nodes within the hypertension sleep-HRQoL network suggests these domains represent high-yield candidate co-primary or secondary patient-reported outcomes (PROs) for clinical trials evaluating antihypertensive or comorbid treatments in elderly patients, and could inform label expansions focused on functional quality of life. Pharmaceutical companies positioning antihypertensive products in the geriatric segment should consider incorporating validated multi-dimensional HRQoL instruments (e.g., EQ-5D-5L) alongside sleep assessments in Phase III or Phase IV study designs to capture the full patient benefit profile and support health technology assessment submissions. Strategic KOL engagement in geriatric cardiology and sleep medicine would be valuable to co-develop consensus on network-informed outcome measures, potentially driving future guideline revisions that recognize sleep quality as a modifiable dimension of hypertension burden.

Theme 7 : Environment and Risk Factors (1 article)

A dangerous combination: how co-occurring floods and blackouts increase cardiovascular hospitalizations.

Revue : Journal of Exposure Science & Environmental Epidemiology | Tier : NA | IF estime : NA

Date de publication : 2026-09-19

Resultats principaux : This population-based ecological study employed distributed lag nonlinear models (DLNMs) within a quasi-Poisson regression framework to quantify the independent and joint effects of flooding events and power outages (POs) on cardiovascular disease (CVD) hospitalizations across New York State, adjusting for meteorological covariates and time-varying confounders using a real-world administrative hospitalization dataset. Both flooding and POs were independently associated with significant increases in total CVD hospitalizations, but their co-occurrence produced the strongest effect with a highest relative risk of RR = 1.09 (95% CI: 1.03-1.15). Temporal and threshold analyses revealed that CVD risk escalated when flooding persisted beyond 3 days and when the proportion of power outages exceeded 15.3%, identifying actionable exposure thresholds for emergency planning. Pre-specified subgroup analyses demonstrated disproportionately stronger joint hazard effects on hypertension, stroke, and ischemic heart disease subtypes, with greater vulnerability in adults over 65 years, Medicaid recipients and uninsured individuals, rural residents, and during winter and spring seasons. Co-occurring flooding and POs were further associated with longer hospital lengths of stay and higher comorbidity burdens, underscoring the compound health and economic toll of simultaneous environmental hazards.

Implications Medical Affairs : This study provides robust real-world evidence that compound climate hazards are independent and synergistic cardiovascular risk amplifiers, directly relevant to the positioning of antihypertensive therapies as part of climate-resilient preventive cardiovascular care, and this evidence should be actively leveraged in patient advocacy materials and access discussions with payers emphasizing unmet need in vulnerable populations. From a Medical Affairs perspective, these data support the design of prospective real-world evidence studies linking antihypertensive treatment adherence and outcomes during extreme weather events, as well as the development of patient support programs (e.g., emergency medication supply, digital adherence tools) targeting the identified high-risk subgroups of elderly, rural, and socioeconomically disadvantaged hypertensive patients. Strategic KOL engagement with environmental cardiologists, emergency medicine specialists, and public health authorities would be highly valuable to amplify these findings in cardiovascular guideline revisions and to position the pharmaceutical company as a scientific leader at the intersection of climate change, cardiovascular risk, and hypertension management.
