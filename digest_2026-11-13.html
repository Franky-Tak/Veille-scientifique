Clinical risk factors associated with acute hypertension-mediated organ damage in patients presenting with severe hypertension: A retrospective cohort study from Qatar.

Revue : Clinical and Experimental Hypertension | Tier : Tier 3 | IF estime : 2.5

Date de publication : 2026-11-11

Resultats principaux : This large retrospective cohort study enrolled 881 adults presenting to emergency departments at four hospitals in Qatar between January 2015 and January 2020 with severe hypertension (SBP ≥180 mmHg and/or DBP ≥110 mmHg), with a median patient age of 50 years and 68.8% male. The primary outcome was hypertensive emergency defined as acute hypertension-mediated organ damage (HMOD) at index presentation, identified in only 5.3% of patients while 94.7% had severe asymptomatic hypertension. Multivariable logistic regression identified higher admission SBP (aOR 1.016, 95% CI 1.000-1.033), higher DBP (aOR 1.027, 95% CI 1.006-1.049), chronic kidney disease (aOR 2.38, 95% CI 1.02-5.57), hypertensive retinopathy (aOR 3.82, 95% CI 1.14-12.83), and current smoking (aOR 2.83, 95% CI 1.32-6.06) as independent predictors of acute HMOD at presentation. The model demonstrated good discrimination with an AUC of 0.77 and adequate calibration by the Hosmer-Lemeshow test, suggesting clinical utility for ED risk stratification. A longitudinal secondary analysis over three years identified kidney disease progression as the most common follow-up complication, with significant cardiovascular and cerebrovascular morbidity underscoring the long-term burden of severe hypertensive presentations.

Implications Medical Affairs : This study provides clinically actionable risk stratification criteria for hypertensive emergencies in emergency settings, reinforcing the critical role of RAAS-modifying agents in patients with pre-existing CKD and severe hypertension who are at heightened HMOD risk. Medical Affairs teams can leverage the five-variable model (SBP, DBP, CKD, retinopathy, smoking) for targeted educational programs directed at emergency physicians and cardiologists in MENA regions, where hypertension management remains suboptimal. The identification of retinopathy and CKD as potent HMOD risk amplifiers creates a compelling opportunity to position intensive combination antihypertensive regimens as essential in high-risk ED populations, with supporting evidence for dedicated real-world outcome studies.

PEth as a marker of alcohol consumption in relation to arterial cardiovascular risk factors.

Revue : Scandinavian Journal of Primary Health Care | Tier : NA | IF estime : NA

Date de publication : 2026-11-12

Resultats principaux : This cross-sectional study from the Vasterbotten Intervention Programme in Sweden analyzed 2,820 individuals with a median age of 50.2 years and median BMI of 25.9, using phosphatidylethanol (PEth 16:0/18:1) as a highly sensitive biomarker of alcohol consumption from erythrocyte samples collected between 1985 and 2014 and stored at -80°C. High PEth concentrations (>0.30 µmol/L) were observed in 8.9% of participants, and logistic regression revealed significant associations with hypertension (OR 1.58, 95% CI 1.14-2.18), diabetes (OR 1.89, 95% CI 1.08-3.28), and ever smoking (OR 5.31, 95% CI 3.75-7.52). Male sex (OR 2.58) and lower education (OR 2.06) were also significantly associated with high PEth concentrations, defining a composite cardiovascular risk profile linked to heavy alcohol use. The cross-sectional study design limits causal inference, and the retrospective use of archived erythrocyte samples introduces potential pre-analytical variability. These findings establish high-PEth individuals as a distinct high-risk cardiovascular group warranting comprehensive multifactorial risk management, including dedicated hypertension treatment pathways.

Implications Medical Affairs : This study reinforces alcohol as a key modifiable cardiovascular risk factor closely associated with hypertension (1.58-fold increased odds), strengthening the case for integrating PEth biomarker testing into hypertension screening protocols in primary care. Medical Affairs teams can leverage these findings to support guidelines that advocate for comprehensive lifestyle assessment in hypertensive patients, including structured alcohol use disorder screening as a standard of care co-intervention. The 1.58-fold increased odds of hypertension among high-PEth individuals creates a compelling message for cardiometabolic risk bundling in product positioning strategies for antihypertensive therapies, particularly in Scandinavian and Northern European markets where primary care lifestyle assessment is embedded in clinical practice.

Effect of intensive blood pressure control on cognitive outcomes and brain structures in adults with type 2 diabetes: pragmatic, multicentre, open-label RCT.

Revue : BMJ (Clinical Research Edition) | Tier : Tier 1 | IF estime : 105

Date de publication : 2026-11-12

Resultats principaux : This multicentre, open-label, outcome-assessor-masked randomized controlled trial enrolled 12,434 adults aged ≥50 years with type 2 diabetes, raised SBP, and elevated cardiovascular risk across 145 clinical centers in China, randomizing them to intensive SBP target <120 mmHg versus standard target <140 mmHg for up to five years (median follow-up 3.8 years). After one year, mean SBP was 121.6 mmHg in the intensive group and 133.2 mmHg in the standard group, confirming effective separation of treatment targets. The primary cognitive outcome—a composite of all-cause dementia or mild cognitive impairment—did not differ significantly between groups (3.69 vs. 3.89 events/100 person-years; hazard ratio 0.96, 95% CI 0.86-1.06; P=0.38). In an MRI substudy of 862 participants, intensive treatment was associated with a significantly smaller increase in white matter lesion (WML) volume (between-group difference -0.09 asinh-transformed cm3, 95% CI -0.15 to -0.02; P=0.01), suggesting cerebrovascular structural benefit. The absence of a primary cognitive endpoint difference despite favorable WML effects suggests that approximately four years may be insufficient to translate cerebrovascular protection into detectable clinical cognitive benefit, warranting longer follow-up trials.

Implications Medical Affairs : This landmark BMJ RCT provides important evidence that intensive SBP lowering in diabetic patients does not significantly reduce clinically diagnosed dementia or MCI over a median 3.8-year follow-up, though the favorable effect on white matter lesion burden warrants further investigation with longer follow-up periods and enriched neuroimaging endpoints. For Medical Affairs, the data support nuanced messaging around antihypertensive therapy in diabetic patients: brain structural protection through WML reduction may occur earlier than cognitive symptom prevention, an important distinction when positioning antihypertensive combinations in the aging diabetic population where dementia risk is high. This study creates a priority opportunity for KOL engagement around optimal SBP targets in high-risk diabetic patients and research agenda development for longer-term cognitive trials incorporating neuroimaging endpoints as surrogate measures of neuroprotection.

Optimal Kidney Protection by Moderate Aggressive Treatment of Hypertension.

Revue : Current Hypertension Reports | Tier : NA | IF estime : NA

Date de publication : 2026-11-12

Resultats principaux : This narrative review critically examines randomized clinical trial evidence regarding kidney-protective effects of antihypertensive treatment in essential hypertension, noting that incident CKD has rarely been used as an adjudicated primary endpoint in hypertension RCTs, limiting guidance for renal protection. Major RCTs targeting SBP <120 mmHg have consistently demonstrated greater short-term declines in estimated GFR compared with less intensive targets, raising concern that aggressive BP lowering may cause renal harm in some populations. Some trials showed neutral or even beneficial kidney effects with achieved SBP below 130 mmHg, alongside possible cardiovascular outcome benefits, though the inclusion of soft endpoints in some trials dilutes interpretability of the cardiovascular message. The review synthesizes evidence supporting a SBP target of <130 mmHg as the optimal balance between cardiovascular benefit and renal safety in middle-aged and older hypertensive patients if treatment is otherwise well tolerated. It also highlights the emerging role of SGLT2 inhibitors, aldosterone antagonists, and GLP1 agonists as kidney-protective adjuncts, suggesting future trials should reassess optimal BP targets in the context of these newer agents.

Implications Medical Affairs : This review highlights a critical evidence gap in hypertension management: the absence of incident CKD as a primary endpoint in most antihypertensive RCTs significantly limits guidance for nephrologists and cardiologists managing patients with progressive kidney disease, creating an opportunity for future trials with renal endpoints. Medical Affairs teams supporting RAAS inhibitors or combination antihypertensive therapies should acknowledge the nuanced benefit-risk of intensive BP lowering on the kidney and develop educational materials that address how antihypertensive target selection interacts with renal function trajectories. The emergence of SGLT2 inhibitors and GLP1 agonists as kidney-protective agents in this context represents both a competitor landscape consideration and an opportunity for combination therapy positioning in hypertensive patients with concurrent CKD.

Prognostic value of pulmonary hypertension in lung cancer: a longitudinal cohort study.

Revue : Clinical and Experimental Medicine | Tier : NA | IF estime : NA

Date de publication : 2026-08-16

Resultats principaux : This longitudinal cohort study used UK Biobank data from 500,530 participants prospectively followed from 2006-2010 through October 2022 to investigate the prevalence and prognostic impact of pulmonary hypertension (PH) as a comorbidity in lung cancer patients, identifying 6,095 incident lung cancer cases over a median follow-up exceeding 12 years. PH was significantly more prevalent among lung cancer patients compared to those without lung cancer. Time-dependent Cox regression models showed that after multivariable adjustment for demographics, smoking status, comorbidities, and histological subtype, PH remained independently associated with adverse outcomes, with progressively greater prognostic impact over time. At 10 years, adjusted hazard ratios were 2.88 for overall survival and 3.45 for major adverse cardiac and cerebrovascular events (MACCE). These findings suggest that pulmonary hypertension represents an important cardiopulmonary comorbidity in lung cancer that should prompt systematic longitudinal surveillance and risk stratification beyond standard oncological assessment.

Implications Medical Affairs : This large-scale UK Biobank study establishes pulmonary hypertension as a critical independent prognostic marker in lung cancer, with more than 2.8-fold increased mortality risk at 10 years, calling for systematic cardiopulmonary monitoring programs to be integrated into oncology treatment pathways. Medical Affairs teams supporting pulmonary vasodilators and PAH therapies should monitor this growing evidence base, as it creates clinical justification for proactive PH screening in lung cancer patients with high cardiovascular burden. The progressive strengthening of PH's prognostic impact over time, with MACCE hazard ratio of 3.45 at 10 years, creates priority opportunities for KOL engagement in both cardio-oncology and pulmonology for multidisciplinary surveillance and treatment pathway development.

Effects of mandibular advancement device versus continuous positive airway pressure on cardiac remodeling in obstructive sleep apnea: CMR substudy of the CRESCENT trial.

Revue : Scientific Reports | Tier : NA | IF estime : NA

Date de publication : 2026-08-24

Resultats principaux : This prespecified cardiovascular magnetic resonance (CMR) substudy of the randomized CRESCENT trial included 85 patients with moderate-to-severe obstructive sleep apnea (OSA) and elevated cardiovascular risk (mean age 59±8 years; 89% male; mean SBP 126±11 mmHg; BMI 28.6±4.6) randomized to 12 months of either mandibular advancement device (MAD, n=36) or continuous positive airway pressure (CPAP, n=49). Both MAD and CPAP produced a modest but statistically significant reduction in extracellular volume (ECV) fraction—a marker of diffuse myocardial fibrosis—at 12 months (MAD: 25.0±2.0% to 24.0±2.2%, P=0.047; CPAP: 25.1±1.9% to 24.4±1.6%, P=0.004), with no significant between-group difference (P=0.576). No significant changes in LV mass, cardiac volumes, or cardiac function were observed in either group, likely reflecting the predominantly preserved myocardial phenotype at baseline with only 27% meeting LV hypertrophy criteria. These findings extend the CRESCENT trial primary results, confirming that effective OSA treatment attenuates myocardial fibrosis regardless of device modality, with equivalent cardioprotective effects between MAD and CPAP. The small substudy sample size limits statistical power to detect modest between-group differences in structural CMR endpoints.

Implications Medical Affairs : This trial provides the first head-to-head CMR comparison of MAD versus CPAP on myocardial fibrosis reduction in OSA patients with hypertension, demonstrating equivalent cardioprotection between both treatment modalities and supporting integrated cardiovascular risk management in hypertensive OSA patients. Medical Affairs can leverage these findings to reinforce messaging that effective OSA treatment—regardless of device—contributes to cardiovascular risk reduction in hypertensive patients, supporting integrated cardiology-sleep medicine care pathways where antihypertensive and OSA therapies are co-managed. The absence of significant LV mass change despite ECV improvement suggests a ceiling effect in well-controlled patients, calling for future trials enriched with patients demonstrating baseline LV hypertrophy, which is a priority recruitment criterion for cardiovascular outcomes studies in hypertensive OSA patients.

Date de publication : 2027-08-12

Implications Medical Affairs : PM2.5 should be recognized as an independent, modifiable cardiovascular risk factor in clinical practice and hypertension management guidelines, with direct implications for physician messaging that blood pressure may be partly driven by environmental exposures beyond lifestyle and genetics—reinforcing the need for comprehensive risk assessment.
