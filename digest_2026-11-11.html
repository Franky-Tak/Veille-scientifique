Psychosocial influences on hypertension in Southeastern Nigeria: A mixed-methods study protocol.

Revue : PloS one | Tier : NA | IF estime : NA

Date de publication : 2026-11-10

Resultats principaux : This study protocol describes a sequential explanatory mixed-methods design targeting adults living with hypertension in Southeastern Nigeria, a region where the condition represents a severe public health challenge with high prevalence rates. The quantitative phase will deploy validated psychometric instruments to measure perceived stress, anxiety, depression, coping mechanisms, and social support, with the primary outcome being a composite Health Behaviour Index capturing hypertension-related health behaviours. Statistical analyses will include regression, correlation tests, and mediation/moderation models to examine the interplay between psychosocial determinants and health behaviour outcomes. The qualitative phase will conduct semi-structured interviews, analyzed using Braun and Clarke's six-phase thematic framework, to capture participants' lived experiences of stress and coping in the context of hypertension management. A primary limitation is the protocol-stage design, meaning no outcome data are yet available; generalizability to other Nigerian regions or Sub-Saharan African populations with different socio-cultural contexts will require further investigation.

Implications Medical Affairs : This study addresses a critical evidence gap in psychosocial determinants of hypertension in Sub-Saharan Africa, a region with significant unmet medical need and limited access to integrated mental-physical health care. From a Medical Affairs perspective, findings could support positioning of antihypertensive combination therapies alongside mental health co-interventions, particularly in markets where adherence is driven by psychosocial barriers. An actionable insight for KOL strategy is early engagement with Nigerian cardiology and public health academics to position the company as a contributor to holistic hypertension care models in low-resource settings.

Hypertension and Blood Pressure Dysregulation in Cancer Survivors: Mechanisms, Management, and Future Directions.

Revue : Current hypertension reports | Tier : NA | IF estime : NA

Date de publication : 2026-11-10

Resultats principaux : This narrative review synthesizes evidence on the epidemiology, mechanisms, and management of hypertension in cancer patients, with a particular focus on cancer survivors during and after treatment. Hypertension is highly prevalent in this population and is independently associated with increased cardiovascular morbidity and mortality beyond the oncological disease burden. VEGF (vascular endothelial growth factor) inhibitors are highlighted as one of the most common drivers of treatment-induced hypertension, though multiple other cancer therapies also contribute to blood pressure dysregulation. Emerging evidence reveals a clinically important coexistence of orthostatic hypotension and hypertension in this population, likely mediated by autonomic dysfunction, arterial stiffening, baroreflex impairment, and endothelial dysfunction caused by cancer and its treatments. A key limitation is the lack of cancer-specific blood pressure targets; current management largely follows general population guidelines despite the unique pathophysiology, and prospective evidence for optimal antihypertensive strategies in cardio-oncology is insufficient.

Implications Medical Affairs : This review underscores a rapidly growing cardio-oncology niche where antihypertensive management is both under-evidence-based and under-resourced, with direct implications for guideline development and therapeutic innovation. From a product positioning standpoint, antihypertensives with favorable profiles for autonomic dysfunction (e.g., centrally acting agents or alpha-blockers) may be repositionable for cancer-treatment-related hypertension given the complex autonomic dysregulation described. An immediate KOL strategy opportunity is to partner with cardio-oncology centers to co-design prospective registry studies that could generate real-world evidence supporting regulatory submissions and label extensions in this underserved population.

Digital Phenotyping, Remote Monitoring, and Risk Prediction of Heart Failure in Hypertensive Disorders of Pregnancy: A Systematic Review With Narrative Synthesis.

Revue : Journal of cardiac failure | Tier : NA | IF estime : NA

Date de publication : 2026-11-01

Resultats principaux : This systematic review identified 6 studies from 3,366 screened records evaluating digital health, remote monitoring, clinical prediction models, and machine learning approaches for cardiovascular risk in hypertensive disorders of pregnancy (HDPs) including preeclampsia. Studies used EHR-based models, clinical prediction tools, and remote monitoring technologies to assess acute cardiovascular morbidity, heart failure with preserved ejection fraction (HFpEF), cardiac remodeling, and readmission risk. Model discrimination ranged from modest to strong, with AUROC values approximately 0.70 to 0.94, indicating variable but sometimes excellent predictive performance for selected outcomes. Recurrent predictors across models included severe hypertensive disease, early gestational age at delivery, pre-existing kidney disease, cardiac disease, pulmonary hypertension, maternal obesity, and postpartum volume retention. A central limitation is the lack of external validation for most prediction models; heterogeneity across study designs, outcome definitions, and populations precluded meta-analysis and limits clinical translation.

Implications Medical Affairs : This systematic review confirms a nascent but growing evidence base supporting digital tools for cardiovascular risk stratification in pregnancy-related hypertension, a window for intervention that has long-term implications for maternal cardiovascular outcomes. For Medical Affairs, this represents an opportunity to position remote blood pressure monitoring platforms and digital therapeutics alongside antihypertensive drug regimens targeting pregnant and postpartum women, a population with high unmet need. Engaging with KOLs in maternal-fetal medicine and cardiology to co-develop prospective validation studies for predictive models could strengthen evidence for guideline inclusion of digital health interventions in HDP management protocols.

Prevalence of obesity and proteinuria with associated factors among diabetic patients attending Kumasi South Hospital, Ghana.

Revue : BMC public health | Tier : NA | IF estime : NA

Date de publication : 2026-08-13

Resultats principaux : This analytical cross-sectional study enrolled 100 adult diabetic patients at Kumasi South Hospital, Ghana, all of whom were also hypertensive, with a mean age of 63.2 years and a female predominance of 65%. Prevalence of overweight and obesity was high at 52% and 32% respectively, and dipstick-detected proteinuria was found in 46% of participants, with 23% having trace and 23% having quantitatively positive proteinuria. Multivariate logistic regression identified female sex (AOR 6.35, 95% CI 1.75-27.50), systolic blood pressure ≥140 mmHg (AOR 3.31, 95% CI 1.15-10.20), fair glycaemic control (AOR 3.75, 95% CI 1.08-14.20), and poor glycaemic control (AOR 17.40, 95% CI 3.13-139.00) as independent predictors of proteinuria. Notably, obesity was not independently associated with proteinuria after multivariate adjustment, suggesting blood pressure and glycaemic control are the dominant modifiable risk factors in this population. Limitations include the small sample size (n=100), single-center design, and use of semi-quantitative dipstick analysis rather than quantitative albumin-to-creatinine ratio, reducing sensitivity and specificity for early diabetic kidney disease detection.

Implications Medical Affairs : This study reinforces the central role of blood pressure control, particularly systolic pressure, in preventing diabetic kidney disease in Sub-Saharan African populations with high comorbidity burdens, with direct relevance to antihypertensive drug strategies. For product positioning, the strong association between systolic hypertension and proteinuria in this dual-burden (diabetes + hypertension) population supports messaging for renin-angiotensin system (RAS) blockers as renoprotective agents in markets like Ghana where diabetes-hypertension overlap is prevalent. Medical Affairs should consider supporting epidemiological studies in West African settings to build robust evidence for guideline recommendations on combined antihypertensive and antidiabetic management, strengthening advocacy for access and prescription among local cardiologists and nephrologists.

Risk factors for gestational hypertension and preeclampsia: a multivariate logistic regression analysis in a less-developed province in south-central China, 2012-2022.

Revue : BMC pregnancy and childbirth | Tier : NA | IF estime : NA

Date de publication : 2026-08-13

Resultats principaux : This large retrospective cohort study analyzed 780,359 pregnant women from Hunan Province, China, over a 10-year period (2012-2022) using the Maternal Near-Miss Surveillance System, identifying 15,889 cases of gestational hypertension (prevalence 2.04%) and 17,782 cases of preeclampsia (prevalence 2.28%). Multivariate logistic regression identified advanced maternal age ≥35 years (OR 2.19 for GH, OR 2.42 for PE), nulliparity (OR 1.61 for GH, OR 1.83 for PE), caesarean section history, multi-fetal pregnancy (OR 2.32 for GH, OR 5.81 for PE), gestational diabetes (OR ~1.9 for both), and kidney disease (OR 2.00 for GH, OR 4.17 for PE) as significant independent risk factors. The dramatically higher OR for multi-fetal pregnancy in preeclampsia (5.81) compared to gestational hypertension (2.32) highlights a distinct pathophysiological pathway for the more severe condition. An additional risk factor exclusive to preeclampsia was lower educational attainment (primary school and below, OR 1.24), suggesting socioeconomic gradients in the more severe hypertensive phenotype. Limitations include reliance on routine surveillance data with potential misclassification, and the ecological focus on a single province in China limiting generalizability to other populations.

Implications Medical Affairs : This large-scale study strengthens epidemiological evidence linking gestational hypertension and preeclampsia to modifiable and non-modifiable risk factors in Asian populations, potentially informing targeted screening strategies in countries with aging maternal demographics and rising assisted reproduction rates. From a Medical Affairs perspective, the strong association of gestational diabetes with both conditions supports therapeutic opportunities at the diabetes-hypertension interface in obstetric care, an indication space with limited pharmacological options. Engagement with maternal-fetal medicine KOLs in China and regional health authorities could position the company to contribute to local clinical practice guideline updates targeting prevention in high-risk pregnant women, particularly those with kidney disease and multiple gestations.

The relationship between 100 cases of severe and recurrent epistaxis and long-term oral anticoagulants, age and hypertension.

Revue : BMC research notes | Tier : NA | IF estime : NA

Date de publication : 2026-08-11

Resultats principaux : This single-center retrospective cohort study analyzed 100 patients presenting with spontaneous epistaxis, of whom 23% were chronic oral anticoagulant (OAC) users, to explore risk factors for severe and recurrent bleeding episodes. Long-term OAC use was significantly associated with severe epistaxis (52.17% vs 23.38% in non-users, p=0.003) and recurrent epistaxis within 3 months (43.48% vs 19.48%, p=0.008). Multivariate logistic regression confirmed OAC use (OR 2.91 for severe, OR 2.69 for recurrent) and age ≥70 years (OR 2.36 for severe, OR 2.07 for recurrent) as independent predictors after adjustment for covariates. Importantly, hypertension did not independently predict severe or recurrent epistaxis after multivariate adjustment, challenging the common clinical assumption that blood pressure dysregulation is the primary driver of serious epistaxis in treated hypertensive patients. Limitations include the small sample size (n=100), single-center design, and retrospective methodology, all of which limit generalizability and introduce potential selection and information biases.

Implications Medical Affairs : While primarily a bleeding complication study, this paper has indirect relevance to hypertension clinical management by suggesting that in patients on combined antihypertensive-anticoagulant regimens, the anticoagulant rather than blood pressure status may be the dominant epistaxis risk factor, potentially influencing therapeutic decision-making. From a Medical Affairs perspective, this finding could support messaging for antihypertensives with neutral or favorable coagulation profiles in elderly patients, where anticoagulant therapy is common, particularly in atrial fibrillation management. An actionable insight is to monitor the evolving literature on drug-drug interactions between novel oral anticoagulants and antihypertensives, as this may become a guideline-relevant safety signal in elderly polypharmacy patients.

Health-related quality of life phenotypes in patients receiving maintenance hemodialysis: a latent class analysis of EQ-5D-5L responses.

Revue : Clinical and experimental nephrology | Tier : NA | IF estime : NA

Date de publication : 2026-11-10

Resultats principaux : This cross-sectional study enrolled 350 maintenance hemodialysis patients from five facilities in the Fukushima Dialysis Cohort Study in Japan and applied latent class analysis to EQ-5D-5L dimension-level responses to identify multidimensional health-related quality of life (HRQOL) phenotypes. Three distinct phenotypes were identified: relatively preserved HRQOL (n=182, 52%), intermediate burden (n=125, 36%), and physical limitation (n=43, 12%), with declining EQ-5D-5L utility index and EQ-VAS scores across phenotype severity. The intermediate burden phenotype was characterized by moderate impairment in pain/discomfort, mobility, and usual activities, with relatively preserved self-care; this group was associated with older age, worse performance status, and higher PHQ-9 depression scores. The physical limitation phenotype demonstrated the broadest impairment across domains and was distinctively associated with female sex, lower geriatric nutritional risk index, worse performance status, older age, and elevated PHQ-9 scores. Limitations include the cross-sectional design preventing causal inference, restriction to a single Japanese cohort limiting ethnic generalizability, and the use of dipstick-level analysis rather than dimension-weighted utility values for phenotype derivation.

Implications Medical Affairs : Although this study focuses on hemodialysis patients rather than ambulatory hypertensives, the hypertension-driven progression to end-stage renal disease means these HRQOL phenotypes reflect the chronic trajectory of uncontrolled or inadequately treated hypertension, with direct relevance to treatment burden arguments. From a Medical Affairs perspective, evidence of distinct quality of life phenotypes in dialysis patients supports health economic modeling that quantifies the downstream HRQOL cost of hypertension progression to CKD stage 5, strengthening value propositions for early intensive blood pressure treatment. An actionable insight for KOL strategy is to engage nephrologists and renal care teams in discussions around early antihypertensive optimization as a preventive measure against the worst HRQOL phenotypes observed in dialysis, potentially informing renal protection label extensions.

Dietary amino acid composition and elevated fasting glucose in children and adolescents from four Chinese provinces: a cross-sectional study combining regression and machine learning.

Revue : Frontiers in public health | Tier : NA | IF estime : NA

Date de publication : 2026-10-26

Resultats principaux : This cross-sectional study analyzed 6,960 children and adolescents aged 6-18 years from a national nutrition and health surveillance program across four Chinese provinces, using an integrated pipeline combining the Boruta feature selection algorithm, propensity score matching, conditional logistic regression, and six machine learning models to investigate dietary amino acid composition in relation to elevated fasting glucose (EFG). The best-performing model was LightGBM (AUC=0.831), incorporating 20 amino acids plus 12 confounders including fat intake, protein intake, hypertension, and obesity measures, confirming the importance of hypertension as a confounding variable in pediatric metabolic risk. SHAP-based feature importance and conditional logistic regression identified methionine (OR 2.03, 95% CI 1.61-2.57) and threonine (OR 1.59, 95% CI 1.32-1.92) as positively associated, while arginine (OR 0.82, 95% CI 0.74-0.92) and tyrosine (OR 0.71, 95% CI 0.59-0.86) were inversely associated with EFG. Generalized additive models demonstrated significant nonlinear dose-response relationships for methionine, arginine, and tyrosine with EFG risk, suggesting threshold effects that may have dietary intervention implications. Key limitations are the cross-sectional design preventing causal inference, restriction to four Chinese provinces, potential measurement error in self-reported dietary recall, and the exclusion of relevant confounders such as physical activity objectively measured.

Implications Medical Affairs : While this study focuses on pediatric glucose metabolism rather than hypertension as a primary outcome, the fact that hypertension was included as a significant confounder highlights the early clustering of cardiometabolic risk in children, reinforcing the need for primordial prevention strategies targeting dietary patterns as a pathway to hypertension prevention. From a Medical Affairs perspective, this pediatric metabolic risk study underscores the importance of lifecycle-based hypertension prevention messaging that starts at childhood, supporting disease awareness campaigns and partnerships with public health authorities focused on nutritional education. An actionable insight for future research priorities is to prospectively study whether dietary protein composition modifications in pediatric high-risk groups translate into lower hypertension incidence in adulthood, filling a critical gap that could strengthen prevention guideline recommendations.
