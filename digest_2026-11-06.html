Effectiveness of nurse-led hypertension interventions on retention, adherence, and blood pressure control among adults in low- and middle-income countries: A systematic review.

Revue : PloS one | Tier : NA | IF estime : NA

Date de publication : 2026-11-05

Resultats principaux : This systematic review and narrative synthesis (PROSPERO CRD420251136515) evaluated nurse-led interventions targeting hypertension management in primary care settings across low- and middle-income countries (LMICs), following PRISMA guidelines and searching PubMed, Scopus, and Cochrane for literature from 2000 to 2026. Eleven unique studies (n=19,005) were included: 63.6% randomised controlled trials, 18.2% quasi-experimental, and 18.2% observational; seven studies were conducted in Africa and four in Asia, with a mean age range of 52.0-67.4 years and 51.3%-76.0% female participants. The primary endpoint was retention in care (appointment attendance, clinical retention, study completion), and secondary endpoints included medication adherence and blood pressure (BP) control; ten studies reported multicomponent nurse-led interventions. Appointment attendance retention ranged from 12.6% to 96.5%, routine clinical retention from 27.0% to 62.8%, and study completion follow-up from 83.8% to 97.2%, reflecting marked heterogeneity; nine out of ten studies reporting systolic BP outcomes showed a mean reduction exceeding 10 mmHg, and all nine studies reporting diastolic BP found reductions ranging from -1.8 mmHg to -20.6 mmHg. Major limitations include high or unclear risk of bias in outcome-data domains for most RCTs, inconsistent definitions of retention and adherence across studies, absence of pooled effect estimates due to design heterogeneity, and limited evidence on medication adherence outcomes.

Implications Medical Affairs : These findings confirm that nurse-led, task-shifted care models are a clinically viable and scalable strategy for hypertension control in LMICs, which are under-resourced for physician-led care, reinforcing their alignment with WHO global hypertension treatment targets. From a Medical Affairs perspective, this systematic evidence base strengthens the rationale for partnering with ministries of health in LMIC markets to position antihypertensive agents within nurse-led integrated care pathways, potentially accelerating access and volume in high-growth markets. Future research should prioritise harmonised outcome definitions and adequately powered cluster-RCTs to generate the robust evidence needed for guideline inclusion and health technology assessment submissions in LMIC contexts.

Prognostic Implications of High HFpEF Probability in Patients With Chronic Thromboembolic Pulmonary Hypertension.

Revue : Clinical cardiology | Tier : NA | IF estime : NA

Date de publication : 2026-11

Resultats principaux : This retrospective cohort study enrolled 466 patients with confirmed chronic thromboembolic pulmonary hypertension (CTEPH) at Fuwai Hospital, China, and stratified them by pretest HFpEF probability using the HFpEF-ABA algorithm (incorporating age, BMI, and atrial fibrillation) into low (<25%), intermediate (25%-74%), and high (>=75%) probability groups. Intermediate and high HFpEF probability was identified in 52.4% (n=244) and 9.0% (n=42) of patients, respectively, demonstrating that left heart disease comorbidity is common in this population. Kaplan-Meier analysis and Cox regression showed that each standard deviation increase in HFpEF probability was independently associated with an approximately 73% increase in clinical worsening (p<0.001), consistent across subgroup analyses by sex, smoking status, alcohol use, systemic hypertension, coronary artery disease, and obstructive sleep apnea. Adding the HFpEF-ABA score to the validated COMPERA 2.0 risk model significantly improved discrimination, increasing the C-index from 0.532 to 0.634 (p=0.012). Limitations include the retrospective single-center design, potential selection bias inherent to a tertiary referral center, and the absence of invasive hemodynamic confirmation of HFpEF in all patients.

Implications Medical Affairs : This study underscores that systemic hypertension and its downstream complication, HFpEF, are prevalent comorbidities in CTEPH patients and independently worsen prognosis, highlighting the need for integrated cardiovascular risk management beyond pulmonary vascular treatment. For Medical Affairs, these findings support positioning comprehensive BP management as an adjunct treatment priority in CTEPH patients, with potential to refine product labelling narratives around cardiovascular risk reduction in this overlap population. KOL engagement should focus on pulmonary hypertension specialists and cardiologists managing CTEPH, advocating for routine HFpEF screening protocols and collaborative management algorithms that include antihypertensive therapy optimisation.

Risk factors for Post-COVID-19 Syndrome in patients hospitalized for severe acute respiratory syndrome: systematic review and meta-analysis.

Revue : Ciencia & saude coletiva | Tier : NA | IF estime : NA

Date de publication : 2026-10-30

Resultats principaux : This systematic review and meta-analysis examined risk factors for post-COVID-19 syndrome (long COVID) among adults discharged after hospitalisation for severe acute respiratory syndrome, searching six databases for observational studies published between 2020 and 2023 and identifying 1,010 publications, of which 45 were included in qualitative analysis and seven in the meta-analysis using a random-effects model. The study population comprised hospitalised adults with severe COVID-19 from multiple countries, assessing the association between demographic variables, comorbidities, disease severity, and development of post-COVID-19 syndrome. Meta-analytic results revealed that female sex (RR 1.52; 95% CI 1.26-1.85), presence of hypertension (RR 1.25; 95% CI 1.06-1.49), and longer hospital stay (RR 1.01; 95% CI 1.01-1.03) were independently associated with post-COVID-19 syndrome development. Hypertension as a comorbidity increased the relative risk of long COVID by 25%, establishing it as a clinically significant modifier of post-acute sequelae. Limitations include the observational design of included studies, heterogeneity in long-COVID definitions across studies, and the relatively small number of studies eligible for meta-analysis, which may limit precision of pooled estimates.

Implications Medical Affairs : The finding that hypertension independently increases the risk of post-COVID-19 syndrome by 25% expands the clinical burden narrative around inadequately controlled blood pressure, potentially arguing for more aggressive hypertension management during and after acute COVID-19 infection to mitigate long-term morbidity. This evidence could be leveraged in Medical Affairs communications to reinforce the systemic consequences of uncontrolled hypertension, supporting the broader value proposition of antihypertensive therapies beyond traditional cardiovascular endpoints. KOL strategy should engage infectious disease specialists, pulmonologists, and general practitioners treating post-COVID patients to highlight hypertension as a key modifiable determinant of long-COVID outcomes.

Designing a culturally responsive mindfulness-based blood pressure program: the IndigenousMIND/MB-BP protocol.

Revue : Frontiers in public health | Tier : NA | IF estime : NA

Date de publication : 2026-10-21

Resultats principaux : This pilot implementation study evaluated the feasibility and acceptability of IndigenousMIND/MB-BP, a culturally adapted Mindfulness-Based Blood Pressure Reduction (MB-BP) program co-developed with Narragansett tribal cultural leaders, enrolling 13 Indigenous adult participants in an eight-week intervention integrating tribal language, storytelling, ceremony, ecological relationships, and relational learning practices with standard MB-BP core mechanisms. The study employed a rapid-cycle cultural adaptation process and collected qualitative reflections at a final community gathering, analysed thematically to assess feasibility, acceptability, and perceived impact on well-being and hypertension prevention. Five primary themes emerged from qualitative analysis: connection and community, cultural identity and preservation, mindfulness and personal transformation, intergenerational healing and responsibility, and challenges and resilience, all of which participants identified as meaningful pathways into practice. Participants reported high engagement and acceptability, describing mindfulness as consistent with Indigenous ways of knowing, and cultural reconnection and community belonging as important components of well-being and cardiovascular health. Key limitations include the very small sample (n=13), absence of quantitative blood pressure outcome data, single-tribe focus limiting generalizability, and lack of a control group, leaving clinical efficacy unestablished.

Implications Medical Affairs : This pilot establishes proof-of-concept for culturally grounded, community-led non-pharmacological hypertension prevention in Indigenous populations, a historically underserved group with disproportionate cardiovascular disease burden, and aligns with health equity imperatives increasingly embedded in regulatory and payer expectations. Medical Affairs should monitor this area as a potential complement to pharmacotherapy in Indigenous health programs, particularly in markets where government partnerships prioritise cardiovascular health equity. Future RCT-level evidence will be needed before clinical guidelines can incorporate culturally adapted mindfulness-based programs as adjunct hypertension prevention strategies, making this a space for collaborative research investment with academic partners.

The influence of maternal complications on small for gestational age in very preterm, late preterm and term pregnancies: a Chinese study.

Revue : BMC pregnancy and childbirth | Tier : NA | IF estime : NA

Date de publication : 2026-08-10

Resultats principaux : This retrospective cross-sectional study from Shanghai First Maternity and Infant Hospital included 46,829 singleton pregnancies and examined the association between maternal complications—gestational hypertension (GH), preeclampsia (PE), and gestational diabetes mellitus (GDM)—and small-for-gestational-age (SGA) birth across very preterm, late preterm, and term gestational groups using binary logistic regression with restricted cubic spline analysis. The mean maternal age was 31.7 years (SD 3.8 years), and restricted cubic spline analysis revealed a significant nonlinear inverse association between gestational age and SGA risk, with odds highest at earlier gestational ages. PE demonstrated the strongest association with SGA across all gestational periods, particularly in the very preterm group, while GH was significantly associated with increased SGA risk in very preterm pregnancies only (OR=2.430; 95% CI 1.156-5.111) but not in late preterm or term groups. GDM was paradoxically associated with lower SGA odds, though the co-occurrence of PE and GDM was associated with increased SGA risk. Interaction analysis confirmed that the association between maternal complications and SGA significantly weakened with advancing gestational age, with substantially attenuated effects at term.

Implications Medical Affairs : These data reinforce the critical role of hypertensive disorders of pregnancy—particularly gestational hypertension and preeclampsia—as independent determinants of adverse neonatal outcomes including SGA, underscoring the downstream neonatal impact of inadequately managed maternal blood pressure. From a Medical Affairs perspective, this large dataset adds to the evidence base supporting safer and more intensive hypertension management during pregnancy, an area where pharmacological options remain limited by safety concerns, creating unmet need for future therapeutic development. KOL engagement with maternal-fetal medicine specialists and obstetricians should frame hypertension management as a dual maternal-neonatal outcome priority, relevant to future trials incorporating neonatal growth endpoints.

Non-high-density lipoprotein cholesterol to high-density lipoprotein cholesterol ratio as a lipid-based marker of hypertension: a large-scale cross-sectional investigation.

Revue : BMC cardiovascular disorders | Tier : NA | IF estime : NA

Date de publication : 2026-08-08

Resultats principaux : This large-scale cross-sectional study from the Second Affiliated Hospital of Zhejiang University School of Medicine enrolled 10,735 adults undergoing routine health examinations and evaluated the association between the non-HDL-C to HDL-C ratio (NHHR) and hypertension prevalence using modified Poisson regression models with progressive covariate adjustment. Hypertension was present in 32.8% of participants, and participants were stratified into NHHR quartiles. After adjustment for age, sex, and eGFR, each one-unit increase in NHHR was significantly associated with hypertension (PR 1.09; 95% CI 1.05-1.12; p<0.001), with the highest quartile showing 24% greater prevalence compared to the lowest (PR 1.24; 95% CI 1.13-1.37; p<0.001). However, after additional adjustment for BMI, the association was substantially attenuated to non-significance (PR 1.03; 95% CI 1.00-1.07; p=0.068), suggesting that adiposity is a major mediator or confounder of the NHHR-hypertension relationship. Limitations include the cross-sectional design precluding causal inference, potential residual confounding, and the single-center health examination cohort that may not represent the general population.

Implications Medical Affairs : The finding that BMI substantially mediates the NHHR-hypertension association reinforces the central role of adiposity in cardiometabolic risk clustering, suggesting that lipid ratios alone may have limited independent utility as hypertension screening tools when weight management is not concurrently addressed. For Medical Affairs, this supports the narrative that combined metabolic risk factor management—including dyslipidaemia, adiposity, and hypertension—offers the most robust cardiovascular risk reduction, potentially strengthening the positioning of combination therapies or drugs with pleiotropic metabolic benefits. This dataset also highlights the utility of NHHR as a simple, calculable clinical marker that may serve as an alerting signal for hypertension risk in busy outpatient settings, warranting prospective validation studies for clinical guideline consideration.

FMT combined with atezolizumab-bevacizumab in hepatocellular carcinoma refractory to immunotherapy: an open label, single-arm, phase II pilot study.

Revue : Nature communications | Tier : NA | IF estime : NA

Date de publication : 2026-10-07

Resultats principaux : This open-label, single-center, single-arm phase II pilot study (FAB-HCC; NCT05750030) enrolled 12 patients with advanced hepatocellular carcinoma (HCC) who had progressed on first-line atezolizumab plus bevacizumab, administering a single fecal microbiota transplantation (FMT) via colonoscopy from a donor who had responded to anti-PD-1 therapy or from a healthy donor, in combination with continued atezolizumab plus bevacizumab. The primary endpoint was safety, assessed by incidence and severity of treatment-related adverse events (TRAEs); grade 3-4 TRAEs included arterial hypertension (n=5, attributed to bevacizumab), proteinuria (n=3, bevacizumab-related), and diarrhea (n=1, FMT-related), with no serious FMT-related TRAEs observed. Secondary endpoints included radiological response and survival: the objective response rate was 33% (n=4/12) and median progression-free survival was 6.9 months (95% CI 3.0-10.8), exceeding historical benchmarks for second-line HCC therapy. Microbiome engraftment analyses showed recipient microbiome composition shifted toward the donor profile, with healthy donor FMT associated with higher engraftment, and circulating PD-1+CD8+ T cells decreased while CD32b+CD14+CD16+ monocytes increased post-FMT. Critical limitations include the very small sample size (n=12) preventing definitive efficacy conclusions, absence of a control arm, single-center design, and arterial hypertension occurring in 5 of 12 patients as a clinically significant safety signal requiring monitoring.

Implications Medical Affairs : Although this study is primarily an oncology trial, the high incidence of grade 3-4 arterial hypertension (42% of patients, n=5) as a bevacizumab-related TRAE underscores the need for proactive hypertension monitoring and management protocols in cancer patients receiving anti-angiogenic therapies, an increasingly important intersection between oncology and cardiovascular medicine. Medical Affairs should track the growing body of evidence on hypertension as a dose-limiting toxicity of VEGF-pathway inhibitors, as this may create market opportunities for antihypertensive agents with favorable renal safety profiles in oncological settings. Future research should investigate whether optimised antihypertensive management during anti-VEGF therapy impacts treatment tolerability, dose intensity, and ultimately oncological outcomes, a niche area of cardioncology with growing guideline interest.

Lipotoxicity and Inflammation Linking between Diet, Obesity, Diabetes, and Kidney Disease.

Revue : Molecular and cellular endocrinology | Tier : NA | IF estime : NA

Date de publication : 2026-11-05

Resultats principaux : This narrative review synthesises current molecular and cellular evidence on the pathophysiological links between dietary patterns, lipotoxicity, obesity, type 2 diabetes mellitus (T2DM), and chronic kidney disease (CKD), with particular focus on the role of insulin resistance and inflammation as interconnected mechanisms. The review describes how excessive consumption of saturated fats and refined carbohydrates disrupts the IRS-1/PI3K/Akt insulin signalling axis, promoting systemic insulin resistance and ectopic lipid accumulation in the kidney (fatty kidney disease), generating reactive oxygen species (ROS) and activating pro-inflammatory NF-kB pathways. These mechanisms contribute to cellular injury, renal fibrosis, and progressive decline in renal function even in the absence of overt T2DM or hypertension, challenging traditional risk factor paradigms. The self-perpetuating cycle of lipotoxicity, oxidative stress, and low-grade inflammation establishes multi-organ damage including endocrine disruption, emphasising diet as a primary upstream driver of cardiometabolic and renal risk. Limitations include the narrative (non-systematic) review design, absence of meta-analytic synthesis, and limited discussion of sex-specific or genetic determinants of the described pathways.

Implications Medical Affairs : This review reinforces that dietary patterns are upstream modifiers of hypertension and renal risk, strengthening the scientific case for nutritional interventions as a foundation of hypertension prevention strategies, particularly in the context of overlapping obesity, T2DM, and CKD. For Medical Affairs, the mechanistic framing of diet-induced cardiometabolic risk provides educational content for KOL engagement with endocrinologists, nephrologists, and cardiologists, supporting integrated risk factor management narratives for antihypertensive combination therapies with renoprotective profiles. This evidence also contextualises the growing regulatory interest in real-world lifestyle modification data as an adjunct to pharmacological endpoints in hypertension clinical trial designs.

Hypertension among Civil Servants: A Cross-sectional Study in Osun State, Nigeria.

Revue : Current problems in cardiology | Tier : NA | IF estime : NA

Date de publication : 2026-11-05

Resultats principaux : This cross-sectional study recruited 305 civil servants from Osun State, Nigeria, using multistage sampling, and assessed hypertension knowledge, observed prevalence, and associated modifiable risk factors using a semi-structured questionnaire and binary logistic regression, with statistical significance set at p<0.05. The mean participant age was 39.48 +/- 7.50 years, and despite 75.7% having good knowledge of hypertension, the observed hypertension prevalence was notably high at 44.6%, reflecting a significant knowledge-action gap in a relatively educated workforce. Younger participants (20-39 years) had higher odds of good hypertension knowledge than those aged 40-59 years (AOR=1.74; 95% CI 1.02-2.96), while males had substantially higher odds of smoking (AOR=13.58; 95% CI 1.78-103.64) and alcohol consumption (AOR=3.99; 95% CI 2.08-7.65) compared to females. Polygamous marital status was associated with higher odds of alcohol consumption (AOR=0.40; 95% CI 0.20-0.80 for monogamous vs polygamous), and younger age was inversely associated with low physical activity. Limitations include cross-sectional design precluding causal inference, potential social-desirability bias in self-reported risk behaviours, single-state sampling limiting national generalizability, and reliance on observed (measured) rather than confirmed diagnosed hypertension.

Implications Medical Affairs : A 44.6% hypertension prevalence in a working-age Nigerian workforce despite high knowledge levels highlights the critical gap between awareness and effective behaviour change or treatment engagement, underscoring the need for workplace-based hypertension screening and treatment programs across sub-Saharan Africa. Medical Affairs should consider this prevalence data to support market access arguments for antihypertensive portfolio expansion in West Africa, where disease burden is substantial but treatment rates remain low. KOL strategy should engage occupational health physicians and public health policymakers in Nigeria to co-design evidence-based workplace cardiovascular programs, creating both advocacy opportunities and real-world data generation platforms.

Adipocyte-derived PDGF-D counteracts age-associated obesity through ameliorating lymphatic function.

Revue : Frontiers in endocrinology | Tier : NA | IF estime : NA

Date de publication : 2026-11-04

Resultats principaux : This experimental study using murine models investigated the role of adipocyte-derived platelet-derived growth factor D (PDGF-D) in age-associated obesity, demonstrating that middle-aged mice showed greater susceptibility to high-fat diet (HFD)-induced obesity than young mice, accompanied by reduced lymphatic vessel density, impaired lymphatic function, and decreased PDGF-D expression in adipose tissue. Using adipocyte-specific PDGF-D knockout and overexpression mouse models, the authors showed that PDGF-D deficiency, despite having no direct effect on adipocytes, induced lymphatic dysfunction sufficient to exacerbate HFD-induced obesity in young mice, while adipocyte-specific PDGF-D overexpression restored lymphatic function and reversed severe HFD-induced obesity in middle-aged mice. Mechanistically, adipocyte-derived PDGF-D promoted tube formation, proliferation, and integrity of lymphatic endothelial cells (LECs), preventing lipid permeation and suppressing adipogenesis. Stimulating lymphangiogenesis via AAV-mediated delivery of VEGF-C156S successfully suppressed HFD-induced obesity in young adipocyte-PDGF-D knockout mice, confirming that the PDGF-D/lymphatic axis is functionally operative. Limitations include the exclusively murine experimental design with uncertain translatability to human physiology, absence of blood pressure measurements linking obesity to hypertension in this model, and the mechanistic focus limiting direct clinical applicability.

Implications Medical Affairs : Although this is a preclinical study, the PDGF-D/lymphatic axis represents a novel mechanistic pathway linking adipose tissue function and obesity—a primary driver of hypertension—potentially revealing new therapeutic targets for obesity-related hypertension beyond traditional adrenergic or renin-angiotensin-aldosterone system pathways. Medical Affairs should monitor this area of translational research, as validation in human adipose tissue could ultimately inform the development of biomarker-driven patient stratification tools for identifying adiposity-driven hypertension phenotypes. Future research priorities should include translational studies examining PDGF-D levels and lymphatic function in obese hypertensive patients and exploring whether VEGF-C pathway stimulation could reduce adiposity and secondarily lower blood pressure in clinical populations.

Dietary Approaches to Stop Hypertension score is associated with depressive symptoms among Chinese adults: a cross-sectional study.

Revue : Frontiers in nutrition | Tier : NA | IF estime : NA

Date de publication : 2026-10-21

Resultats principaux : This cross-sectional study enrolled 3,465 Chinese adults whose dietary intake was assessed by food frequency questionnaire to calculate DASH (Dietary Approaches to Stop Hypertension) diet scores, and depressive symptoms were evaluated using the Zung Self-Rating Depression Scale, with multivariable logistic regression, restricted cubic spline, and two-piecewise logistic regression used to explore associations and dose-response relationships. Multivariable-adjusted logistic regression demonstrated a significant negative association between higher DASH score and odds of depressive symptoms (trend p=0.045), which was stable across different adjusted models. A significant non-linear dose-response relationship was identified (p for non-linearity=0.001), with an exploratory inflection point at a DASH score of 4.124 (95% CI 2.050-6.450): below this threshold, higher DASH score was associated with substantially lower odds of depressive symptoms (OR=0.397; 95% CI 0.261-0.605; p<0.001), while above this threshold the association was not statistically significant. This is the first study to report this inverse association in a Chinese adult population, extending the evidence beyond Western cohorts. Limitations include the cross-sectional design precluding causal inference, potential measurement error in self-reported dietary intake, the use of a self-rated depression scale rather than clinical diagnostic criteria, and the single-centre design.

Implications Medical Affairs : This study extends the clinical benefits of the DASH diet beyond blood pressure reduction to include mental health outcomes, providing Medical Affairs with a broader value narrative for DASH diet promotion as a holistic cardiovascular and mental health intervention that may improve patient adherence through dual benefit framing. The identification of a threshold effect at a DASH score of 4.124 suggests that even modest dietary improvements below this level provide meaningful mental health protection, which could inform patient education materials around incremental dietary change as a hypertension prevention strategy. KOL engagement should include cardiologists, psychiatrists, and dietitians to explore interdisciplinary protocols where antihypertensive lifestyle programs are co-designed with mental health benefit messaging, potentially differentiating patient support programs associated with antihypertensive product portfolios.

Self-management behaviors in patients with H-type hypertension: a latent profile analysis within the health ecological model.

Revue : Frontiers in cardiovascular medicine | Tier : NA | IF estime : NA

Date de publication : 2026-10-21

Resultats principaux : This cross-sectional study recruited 310 patients with H-type hypertension (combined hypertension and hyperhomocysteinemia) from a tertiary hospital in eastern China using convenience sampling, and applied latent profile analysis (Mplus 8.3) within a health ecological model framework to identify distinct self-management behaviour patterns, with eHealth literacy, perceived social support, perceived stress, hospital anxiety and depression, and resilience as correlates. The mean standardised self-management behaviour score was 67.34 +/- 13.82, indicating a moderate overall level, and four latent profiles were identified: Low self-management (18.4%), Moderate self-management-treatment monitoring (21.0%), Moderate self-management-lifestyle (19.0%), and High self-management (41.6%). Profile membership was significantly associated with presence of comorbidity, eHealth literacy, and perceived social support, suggesting that digital health literacy and social environment are key modifiable determinants of self-management behaviour. H-type hypertension confers substantially greater stroke risk than hypertension or hyperhomocysteinemia alone, making self-management optimisation clinically critical in this high-risk subgroup. Limitations include convenience sampling from a single tertiary center limiting generalizability, cross-sectional design precluding causal conclusions, and use of standardised instruments not specifically validated for H-type hypertension patients.

Implications Medical Affairs : These findings identify four actionable patient segments within the H-type hypertension population that differ in self-management capacity, providing a framework for targeted, differentiated patient support interventions rather than one-size-fits-all disease management programs. Medical Affairs should leverage this segmentation approach to co-develop companion digital health or nurse-led support tools with the antihypertensive product, particularly in markets where B-vitamin supplementation for homocysteine lowering is integrated with antihypertensive regimens in H-type hypertension management guidelines. The finding that eHealth literacy and social support are key modifiable correlates argues for investment in digital patient education platforms and community health worker programs as commercially viable patient support strategies.

How is the relationship between life style and cardiovascular diseases? A case-control study.

Revue : BMC public health | Tier : NA | IF estime : NA

Date de publication : 2026-08-10

Resultats principaux : This case-control study from Kermanshah, Iran, enrolled 99 patients with confirmed cardiovascular diseases (CVDs) including hypertension, angina, myocardial infarction, coronary artery disease, heart failure, arrhythmia, and valvular disease, matched with 396 controls without CVD, and assessed lifestyle using a validated 46-item questionnaire across five domains: preventive measures, physical activity and sports, nutrition, stress management, and social-interpersonal relationships. The mean lifestyle score was significantly lower in CVD patients (146.66 +/- 23.87) compared to controls (169.4 +/- 25.86), reflecting a moderate lifestyle level in both groups. Multivariable logistic regression controlling for age, education, income, employment, and marital status revealed that each one-unit increase in the total lifestyle score was associated with a 6% reduction in CVD odds (OR=0.94; 95% CI 0.93-0.96; p<0.001). Each individual lifestyle domain—including prevention, physical activity, healthy diet, stress management, and social relationships—was independently associated with at least a 12% reduction in CVD odds per unit improvement (p<0.001). Key limitations include the relatively small case group (n=99), single-centre design from two hospitals in one city, potential recall bias in self-reported lifestyle data, and cross-sectional-equivalent limitations inherent to the case-control methodology for lifestyle exposure assessment.

Implications Medical Affairs : This study quantifies the dose-response relationship between lifestyle quality and CVD risk reduction across multiple lifestyle domains, providing evidence that comprehensive lifestyle programs targeting nutrition, physical activity, and stress management collectively reduce cardiovascular risk beyond any single intervention, reinforcing multimodal prevention strategies. Medical Affairs can use this evidence to advocate for hypertension lifestyle management programs as evidence-based adjuncts to pharmacotherapy, supporting integrated product packages that combine antihypertensive medications with structured lifestyle support in markets where patient support programs are commercially viable. Future research should use prospective designs with validated hypertension-specific lifestyle instruments and larger samples to generate evidence suitable for health technology assessments and guideline updates in the Middle East and North Africa region.
