[Perioperative outcomes and anesthetic management of pregnancy termination in patients with pulmonary hypertension].

Revue : Nan Fang Yi Ke Da Xue Xue Bao (Journal of Southern Medical University) | Tier : NA | IF estime : NA

Date de publication : 2026-08-20

Resultats principaux : This retrospective single-center observational study enrolled 62 women who underwent pregnancy termination complicated by pulmonary hypertension (PH) at Guangdong Provincial People's Hospital between June 2019 and December 2024; 24 had mild-to-moderate PH and 38 had severe PH. The predominant PH subtype was pulmonary arterial hypertension (PAH) in 82.3% of cases, caused primarily by congenital heart disease such as atrial or ventricular septal defects, and most procedures were performed under general anesthesia via dilation and curettage. A total of 5 postoperative adverse events were recorded, including 3 PH crises, 1 arrhythmia requiring intervention, and 1 pulmonary embolism, all concentrated in the severe PH group. After multivariate adjustment, PH severity was significantly correlated with both postoperative ICU admission and prolonged length of stay (p<0.05), and among patients undergoing dilation and curettage, intrathecal anesthesia was associated with a significantly higher incidence of adverse events (p=0.014). High intraoperative blood pressure variability was an independent predictor of adverse outcomes (OR=10.25, 95% CI 1.06-98.84, p=0.034 for patients below the third tertile of variability), while inhaled nitric oxide was noted as an effective intraoperative adjunct for reducing pulmonary arterial pressure; notable limitations include the small sample size, single-center design, and retrospective nature of the data.

Implications Medical Affairs : This study fills a critical evidence gap in perioperative risk stratification for the rare but high-risk intersection of pulmonary hypertension and pregnancy termination, reinforcing the need for individualized anesthetic protocols and the potential role of vasodilatory therapies such as inhaled nitric oxide in guideline updates for PH management. From a Medical Affairs perspective, these findings support targeted educational initiatives aimed at anesthesiologists and obstetric multidisciplinary teams, particularly in markets where pulmonary arterial hypertension therapies are positioned, highlighting the importance of perioperative hemodynamic control. KOL engagement with pulmonary hypertension specialists and obstetric anesthesiologists could leverage this real-world data to advocate for formalized care pathways, supporting both product positioning and disease awareness campaigns for PAH-targeted therapies.

Validation of Self-Reported Disease History in Korea: A National Health Insurance Cohort Study.

Revue : Journal of Korean Medical Science | Tier : NA | IF estime : NA

Date de publication : 2026-08-10

Resultats principaux : This validation study used data from the National Health Insurance Service (NHIS) National Sample Cohort (2002-2019) in South Korea, analyzing 312,367 participants aged 40 years or older who underwent health check-ups, comparing self-reported disease history against medical claims records as the reference standard for hypertension, diabetes, dyslipidemia, and stroke. Self-reported prevalence of hypertension was substantially lower than claims-based prevalence (33.9% vs. 47.9%), indicating widespread underreporting, while sensitivity for hypertension was the highest among all tested conditions at 69.9% and specificity exceeded 99%. Sensitivity for other conditions was considerably lower: diabetes 40.7%, dyslipidemia 21.6%, and stroke 21.8%, confirming that underreporting is the primary validity concern across all conditions. Logistic regression identified greater healthcare engagement (advanced age, higher comorbidity burden, shorter time since last hospital visit) as being associated with improved self-reporting accuracy, while longer time since initial diagnosis improved sensitivity for chronic conditions. Key limitations include the cross-sectional nature of the validation analysis, potential misclassification in claims data itself, and limited generalizability to countries with different healthcare systems or insurance structures.

Implications Medical Affairs : The substantial underreporting of hypertension in self-reported surveys (sensitivity 69.9%) has direct implications for real-world evidence generation, suggesting that epidemiological studies relying on patient-reported outcomes may systematically underestimate hypertension prevalence and treatment gaps in Asia, which could affect market sizing and access strategy. For Medical Affairs teams designing registry studies, observational trials, or patient satisfaction surveys, these findings mandate cross-referencing with claims or administrative data to ensure validity, particularly for outcomes-based managed care contracting or health technology assessments. Proactive engagement with KOLs in health informatics and cardiovascular epidemiology in South Korea and comparable healthcare systems could position the company as a partner in improving disease burden quantification and optimizing evidence standards for regulatory submissions.

Durability of Blood Pressure Reduction After Renal Denervation in Resistant Hypertension: Mid-Term Real-World Outcomes From Kuwait.

Revue : Journal of Clinical Hypertension | Tier : Tier 3 | IF estime : 3-10

Date de publication : 2026-08

Resultats principaux : This single-center observational cohort study included 105 consecutive patients with resistant hypertension who underwent renal sympathetic denervation (RDN) at a Kuwaiti cardiac center between February 2023 and December 2025, with follow-up data collected during routine clinical visits up to 30 months. Mean systolic blood pressure (SBP) decreased significantly from 162.1 ± 18.3 mmHg at baseline to 142.0 ± 18.9 mmHg at 12 months (-20.1 mmHg; p<0.001), with sustained reductions maintained through 30 months of follow-up, demonstrating durable efficacy beyond what has been reported in most controlled trials. Responder rates remained consistently high across all follow-up time points, and medication escalation was uncommon, occurring in only 6.7% of patients, with most patients requiring no increase in their antihypertensive drug burden. Renal function remained stable throughout the observation period, with no clinically meaningful changes in serum creatinine or estimated glomerular filtration rate, and no major procedure-related complications were recorded. Limitations include the single-center non-randomized design, absence of a control group, potential selection bias in patient enrollment, and the lack of ambulatory blood pressure monitoring data to complement office readings.

Implications Medical Affairs : This real-world dataset from the Middle East fills a significant geographic gap in RDN durability evidence, extending efficacy data to 30 months and providing reassurance on renal safety in a population underrepresented in pivotal RDN trials, which could influence regional guideline development by cardiology and hypertension societies in the MENA region. From a Medical Affairs perspective, these findings reinforce that pharmacological therapy remains a backbone even after device-based intervention given that patients maintained or reduced their medication burden, supporting the positioning of antihypertensive agents as complementary rather than competing with RDN in the resistant hypertension treatment algorithm. Strategic KOL engagement with interventional cardiologists and hypertension specialists in Gulf Cooperation Council countries would be highly valuable, leveraging local data to support both product education and the development of multidisciplinary resistant hypertension care pathways.

Performance of Primary Aldosteronism Screening Tests Before Washout of Dihydropyridine Calcium Channel Blockers: An Exploratory Retrospective Study.

Revue : Journal of Clinical Hypertension | Tier : Tier 3 | IF estime : 3-10

Date de publication : 2026-08

Resultats principaux : This retrospective cohort study at a Chinese hypertension referral center included 198 patients (median age 49.5 years, 59% male) on dihydropyridine calcium channel blocker (DHP-CCB) monotherapy undergoing diagnostic workup for secondary hypertension between January 2017 and May 2022, with aldosterone and renin concentrations measured both before and after CCB washout. Confirmatory testing identified primary aldosteronism (PA) in 31 (15.7%) patients, and the aldosterone-to-renin ratio (ARR) increased in 158 (79.8%) patients after CCB washout, confirming the suppressive effect of DHP-CCBs on renin that inflates ARR and drives screening sensitivity. Of 168 patients with a negative pre-washout screen, 22 turned positive after washout, and 14 of those were ultimately diagnosed with PA, yielding a missed diagnosis rate of 45.2% (14/31) when relying on pre-washout screening criteria alone. Exploratory analysis demonstrated that a pre-washout ARR cutoff of >1.2 (ng/dL)/(µIU/mL) achieved 0.97 sensitivity and 0.75 specificity, suggesting a potential alternative threshold for ruling out PA without mandatory CCB washout. Limitations include the retrospective single-center design, small PA-confirmed sample size (n=31), hypothesis-generating nature of the ARR cutoff, and absence of external validation.

Implications Medical Affairs : The 45.2% missed PA diagnosis rate when CCB washout is omitted prior to aldosterone screening underscores a significant real-world clinical practice gap that has direct implications for guidelines issued by hypertension and endocrine societies, particularly as PA is a potentially curable cause of secondary hypertension that responds specifically to mineralocorticoid receptor antagonist (MRA) therapy. From a Medical Affairs standpoint, these findings strengthen the case for positioning MRA products in the secondary hypertension screening and treatment pathway, and support the development of KOL-driven educational content around optimal PA screening protocols for hypertension specialists, nephrologists, and cardiologists managing patients on CCBs. Collaboration with endocrinology KOLs and advocacy for updated screening algorithms in national guidelines represent actionable priorities, especially in Asian markets where CCB use is highly prevalent and PA is an underdiagnosed secondary hypertension etiology.

Effects of a fall prevention program on falls among rural older adults with cardiovascular disease in China: a secondary analysis of the FAMILY cluster randomised trial.

Revue : Age and Ageing | Tier : NA | IF estime : NA

Date de publication : 2026-08-03

Resultats principaux : This pre-specified secondary analysis of the FAMILY open-label cluster randomized controlled trial enrolled rural Chinese adults aged 60 years or older with physician-diagnosed hypertension or heart disease from 128 villages (randomized 1:1) between September and November 2023, evaluating a 12-month village doctor-led intervention comprising balance and functional exercise combined with health education versus usual care. The primary outcome showed that the intervention significantly reduced the proportion of participants reporting at least one fall (31.6% vs 42.7%; OR 0.60; 95% CI 0.43-0.84), as well as the overall falls rate (0.9 vs 1.6 per person-year; RR 0.62; 95% CI 0.43-0.89). Fall-related injuries were also substantially reduced (15.9% vs 24.9%; OR 0.57; 95% CI 0.42-0.78), representing a clinically important outcome in this elderly cardiovascular population. Secondary functional outcomes including chair stand performance, balance performance, and health-related quality of life score all improved significantly, although the Timed Up and Go score did not differ between arms. Subgroup analyses showed broadly consistent benefits across demographic groups with a greater effect observed in men, though the secondary analysis design, use of self-reported fall outcomes, and potential for cluster-level contamination represent key limitations.

Implications Medical Affairs : The demonstration that a scalable, low-cost community-based fall prevention intervention delivered by village-level healthcare workers significantly reduces falls and injuries in elderly hypertensive patients supports integration of non-pharmacological interventions into cardiovascular disease management guidelines, particularly in low-resource rural settings. For Medical Affairs, this evidence reinforces the importance of comprehensive cardiovascular risk management that extends beyond blood pressure control to functional status and fall prevention, particularly as elderly patients on antihypertensives face orthostatic hypotension-related fall risks that represent a key treatment safety concern for prescribers. Companies with antihypertensive portfolios targeting elderly patients in China and similar markets should consider partnering with public health stakeholders to incorporate fall risk assessment into standard hypertension management protocols, supporting both patient safety messaging and KOL initiatives around integrated geriatric cardiovascular care.

Effects of aged black garlic (ABG10+) on lipoprotein subclass composition in Grade I hypertension: a randomized, triple-blind controlled trial with sex-stratified sequential allocation.

Revue : Frontiers in Nutrition | Tier : NA | IF estime : NA

Date de publication : 2026-07-27

Resultats principaux : This randomized, triple-blind, placebo-controlled trial with sex-stratified sequential allocation enrolled 75 Grade I hypertensive participants who received either 250 mg of aged black garlic (ABG10+, standardized for S-allyl-cysteine content) or placebo daily for 12 weeks to evaluate the effect on blood lipid profiles and lipoprotein subclass composition measured by NMR spectroscopy. ABG supplementation did not produce statistically significant changes in any conventional lipid marker including total cholesterol, LDL-C, HDL-C, or triglycerides, indicating that the primary lipid endpoints were not met. In secondary exploratory NMR-derived analyses, nominally significant differences were observed: ABG was associated with reduction in total particle count and HDL particle number, while large HDL particles showed compositional remodeling with increased phospholipid content and decreased triglyceride percentage. Exploratory k-means cluster analyses suggested that participants with a more adverse baseline metabolic profile experienced nominally significant reductions in total triglycerides and VLDL-lipid content following ABG supplementation. Key limitations include the small sample size (n=75), the hypothesis-generating and exploratory nature of all NMR-based secondary findings, absence of pre-specified NMR endpoints, short 12-week duration, and industry involvement through a co-author affiliated with the ABG manufacturer.

Implications Medical Affairs : Although this trial failed to demonstrate significant effects on conventional lipid markers, the hypothesis-generating NMR data suggesting lipoprotein compositional remodeling in high-risk hypertensive patients warrants monitoring as confirmatory adequately powered trials with pre-specified NMR endpoints could provide more definitive evidence for the role of nutraceuticals as adjuncts in cardiometabolic risk management. From a Medical Affairs perspective, the nutraceutical landscape for hypertensive patients is an increasingly competitive space that may affect patient adherence to pharmacological therapies, and companies should track this area to understand potential positioning challenges or partnership opportunities with dietary supplement adjunct therapies. The trial's exploratory findings, particularly regarding VLDL and HDL remodeling in metabolically higher-risk hypertensive individuals, may inform subgroup targeting hypotheses for future cardiovascular prevention studies and represent talking points for KOL discussions around precision medicine approaches in lipid management within the hypertensive population.

Sustainable diets and cardiometabolic conditions among Ghanaian adults under transition: Findings from the RODAM-Pros Cohort Study.

Revue : The American Journal of Clinical Nutrition | Tier : NA | IF estime : NA

Date de publication : 2026-08-11

Resultats principaux : This longitudinal cohort study used data from the RODAM-Pros (Research on Obesity and Diabetes among African Migrants) study involving 1,499 Ghanaian adults (women 66%, mean age 47±11 years) from both Ghana and Amsterdam, with a mean follow-up of 7±0.5 years, to assess associations between an Optimized Diet Index (ODI, 0-90 score) and incident cardiometabolic outcomes. The ODI was constructed to reflect prior site- and sex-specific dietary patterns meeting nutritional adequacy, cultural acceptability, and 50% reduction in greenhouse gas emissions and food costs, with a median baseline score of 29 (IQR 23-37), indicating overall low adherence to the sustainable diet. No significant associations were observed between baseline ODI and incident general obesity, type 2 diabetes, or hypertension in multivariable-adjusted logistic regression models. Unexpectedly, a 10-point increase in ODI was associated with a 19% higher risk of incident abdominal obesity (OR 1.19; 95% CI 1.00-1.42; p=0.049), with this association being significant in both rural and urban Ghana but not in Amsterdam. The authors attribute the unexpected finding partly to low variation in poor adherence and potential residual confounding, and key limitations include baseline dietary assessment only, reliance on food frequency questionnaires, and potential misclassification of dietary patterns across culturally diverse sites.

Implications Medical Affairs : The failure of the sustainable diet index to demonstrate protective effects against incident hypertension in this transitional African cohort highlights the complexity of dietary prevention in populations undergoing nutritional transition and suggests that pharmacological management of hypertension cannot be supplanted by dietary optimization alone in sub-Saharan African populations. For Medical Affairs, these findings reinforce that antihypertensive therapy remains the cornerstone of blood pressure management in high-prevalence populations such as Ghanaian adults, where structural barriers including food insecurity, cultural dietary patterns, and economic constraints limit the effectiveness of dietary interventions. Engaging KOLs in global cardiovascular health, nutritional epidemiology, and African migrant health could position the company as a partner in comprehensive cardiovascular prevention strategies in LMICs, supporting advocacy for access to pharmacological therapies alongside realistic dietary counseling approaches in these settings.

Impact of adrenalectomy on metabolic alterations and quality of life in mild autonomous cortisol secretion: results from the randomized CHIRACIC trial.

Revue : European Journal of Endocrinology | Tier : NA | IF estime : NA

Date de publication : 2026-08-11

Resultats principaux : This secondary analysis of the multicenter CHIRACIC superiority open-label randomized controlled trial compared adrenalectomy (n=23) to conservative management (n=25) in patients with unilateral adrenal incidentalomas associated with mild autonomous cortisol secretion (MACS), focusing on metabolic outcomes and quality of life after a 13-month follow-up. At baseline, the cohort had high rates of metabolic comorbidities: 93% were overweight or obese, 71% were prediabetic or diabetic, and 90% were dyslipidemic, reflecting the heavy cardiometabolic burden of MACS patients beyond hypertension. Adrenalectomy did not produce statistically significant improvements in any anthropometric or metabolic parameter compared to conservative management; 21% vs 4% of adrenalectomized vs conservatively treated obese/overweight patients lost greater than 5% of body weight (difference 22%, 95% CI -2% to 39%), and glycemic status improved in 44% vs 33% of dysglycemic patients respectively (difference 10%, 95% CI -22% to 43%). Dyslipidemia improved in 20% of adrenalectomized patients and in none of the conservatively treated patients, suggesting a numerical trend that was not statistically significant. Quality of life changes were not significantly different between groups, and the study's key limitations include the small sample size (n=48), open-label design, short follow-up of 13 months, and possible underpowering to detect moderate metabolic effects.

Implications Medical Affairs : While the primary CHIRACIC results previously demonstrated adrenalectomy benefits for hypertension in MACS, this secondary analysis reveals that surgical intervention alone provides limited and non-statistically significant improvements in metabolic comorbidities including obesity, dysglycemia, and dyslipidemia, establishing a clear therapeutic gap that represents an opportunity for adjunct pharmacological management. For Medical Affairs, the persistence of metabolic comorbidities post-adrenalectomy in MACS patients positions cardiometabolic drugs—including antihypertensives, lipid-lowering agents, and metabolic therapies—as essential complements to surgical management, an insight that can be leveraged in targeted medical education for endocrinologists managing adrenal incidentalomas. KOL engagement strategies should prioritize endocrinology and adrenal disease specialists to build awareness of the incomplete cardiometabolic resolution following adrenalectomy and promote integrated, multimodal cardiovascular risk reduction as the standard of care for MACS patients.

Cohort profile: Guangdong Pharmaceutical University Occupational Health Cohort in Xishan Coal Electricity Group (GDPU-OHC).

Revue : Frontiers in Public Health | Tier : NA | IF estime : NA

Date de publication : 2026-07-27

Resultats principaux : The GDPU-OHC is an ambispective occupational cohort established among workers at the Xishan Coal Electricity Group in Shanxi Province, China, with a retrospective component covering 2009-2022 and a prospective component initiated in May 2023, enrolling 25,597 eligible employees (median age 41 years, IQR 35-50; 89.19% male) with baseline fasting blood and urine samples collected and scheduled follow-up every 3-5 years. Crude prevalence rates at baseline were notably high for several non-communicable diseases: hypertension 23.18%, obesity 22.64%, type 2 diabetes 13.89%, hyperuricemia 27.87%, and hyperlipidemia 47.93%, establishing a high cardiometabolic burden in this occupational population. Multiple occupational hazards were prevalent, including dust, noise, toxic gases, and shift work, alongside unhealthy lifestyle factors such as high oil and salt intake, smoking, and alcohol consumption, all of which will serve as key longitudinal exposures in future analyses. Planned sub-studies include occupational protection improvement (n=200), work pattern optimization (n=600), dietary optimization (n=600), and a gut microbiota modulation pilot study (n=50), with future mechanistic analyses targeting oxidative stress, inflammatory markers, metabolomics, and gene-environment interactions. Baseline findings are purely descriptive with no temporal or exposure-outcome associations yet assessed, and the male predominance (89.19%) limits generalizability to female workers in the mining sector.

Implications Medical Affairs : The high crude hypertension prevalence of 23.18% among a predominantly younger male workforce (median age 41 years) exposed to compound occupational hazards underscores a significant unmet need in occupational cardiovascular health, representing a target population where earlier detection and antihypertensive therapy initiation could substantially reduce future cardiovascular event burden. For Medical Affairs, this cohort's planned longitudinal analyses linking occupational exposures to hypertension outcomes will generate real-world evidence relevant to product positioning in occupationally exposed populations, which are underrepresented in standard cardiovascular trials and may benefit from distinct treatment algorithms or earlier intervention thresholds. Strategic partnership with occupational health KOLs, industrial medicine specialists, and public health authorities in China could support company-sponsored workplace cardiovascular screening programs, strengthening brand presence in the occupational health segment while generating evidence for antihypertensive therapy uptake in high-risk industrial worker populations.
