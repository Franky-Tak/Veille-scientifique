Multi-Trait GWAS Analyses of Blood Pressure Traits Provide Limited Gains in Locus Discovery or Polygenic Risk Score Performance Compared to Single-Trait GWAS.

Revue : Genetic Epidemiology | Tier : NA | IF estime : NA

Date de publication : 2026-12

Resultats principaux : This study employed two multi-trait GWAS approaches (MTAG and GenomicSEM) applied to summary statistics from single-trait GWAS of systolic BP, diastolic BP, and pulse pressure drawn from a sample exceeding one million individuals, with polygenic risk score (PRS) validation performed in the Lifelines Cohort Study. The primary endpoint was discovery of novel BP-associated loci not detected by standard single-trait GWAS, with secondary endpoints including SNP-based heritability estimates and PRS predictive performance for hypertension. Multi-trait GWAS identified only one novel locus (ZNF268) beyond what single-trait analyses already captured, representing a negligible gain relative to the analytical complexity involved. Neither heritability estimates nor PRS performance improved meaningfully when multi-trait methods were used under conditions of complete sample overlap across input GWAS. Key limitation of the study is the complete sample overlap scenario, which systematically limits the potential benefits of multi-trait approaches and may not generalise to settings with partial overlap or more heterogeneous trait architectures.

Implications Medical Affairs : These findings suggest that the current genetic architecture of blood pressure is sufficiently captured by large-scale single-trait GWAS, and that multi-trait methods offer diminishing returns in this specific context, which limits their near-term utility for developing next-generation PRS-based hypertension risk stratification tools. From a Medical Affairs perspective, this study tempers expectations around rapid genomic breakthroughs in hypertension prediction and reinforces that incremental locus discovery will require substantially larger, independent cohort data rather than novel analytical strategies applied to existing datasets. An actionable KOL strategy insight is to engage genetic epidemiologists and precision medicine thought leaders in discussions around the translational gap between BP GWAS findings and clinically actionable hypertension risk tools.

Temporal Patterns of Multimorbidity Progression Among Inpatients With a Recorded Hypertension Diagnosis Across Three Counties in Rural Henan, China: Retrospective Cohort Study Using Temporal Network Analysis.

Revue : JMIR Public Health and Surveillance | Tier : NA | IF estime : NA

Date de publication : 2026-10-23

Resultats principaux : This retrospective cohort study analyzed electronic health records of 34,099 inpatients with recorded hypertension diagnoses across three rural counties in Henan, China, covering the period 2019 to 2023, constructing a temporal comorbidity network of 250 disease nodes and 1,512 validated disease transitions using bootstrap resampling and permutation testing. The primary endpoint was characterizing ordered temporal pathways of multimorbidity progression and examining whether these pathways differed by sex, age, insurance type, and county. A densely interconnected core was identified, involving hypertension, atherosclerotic heart disease, and cerebral infarction, with type 2 diabetes occupying a bridging position linking cardiovascular and multisystem disease clusters. Subgroup analysis showed sex-specific ordering, with earlier coronary disease involvement among female inpatients versus more direct progression to stroke among male inpatients; age-related complexity was greater in those over 70 years; and insurance type modified disease diffusion patterns. Key limitations include reliance on inpatient records only, potential for ascertainment bias, and the observational nature limiting causal inference.

Implications Medical Affairs : The identification of type 2 diabetes as a network bridge node between cardiovascular and multisystem disease progression pathways in hypertensive patients reinforces the strategic importance of cardiometabolic combination therapies and positions antihypertensive-metabolic co-management as an area of high guideline relevance. From a Medical Affairs standpoint, this study provides real-world epidemiological scaffolding to support value messaging around integrated cardiometabolic risk management, particularly in underserved rural populations where disease complexity clusters early. An actionable KOL engagement opportunity lies in collaborating with Chinese public health epidemiologists to design prospective validation of these multimorbidity pathways and to explore sex-stratified prevention strategies.

Longitudinal Blood Pressure Trajectories and Time to Stroke Among Hypertensive Patients: A Bayesian Multivariate Joint Modeling Study in Benishangul-Gumuz, Ethiopia.

Revue : PLoS One | Tier : NA | IF estime : NA

Date de publication : 2026-10-23

Resultats principaux : This retrospective cohort study enrolled 494 newly diagnosed hypertensive patients in two general hospitals in Benishangul-Gumuz, Ethiopia, followed from January 2015 to September 2024, applying a Bayesian multivariate joint model integrating longitudinal BP measurements with a Cox survival framework for time-to-stroke. The primary endpoint was incident stroke, with a rate of 2.19 per 100 person-years (95% CI 1.60-2.64). Higher current underlying systolic BP was associated with a nearly 3.25-fold increased stroke risk (95% CrI 1.85-5.83) and higher diastolic BP with a 2.32-fold increase (95% CrI 1.35-4.27). Independent predictors of earlier stroke included age >=65 years (AHR 6.18, 95% CrI 2.32-18.05), comorbidity presence (AHR 4.00, 95% CrI 1.72-10.25), and stage II hypertension (AHR 4.06, 95% CrI 1.49-16.36), while rural residence was protective (AHR 0.16, 95% CrI 0.05-0.40). Important limitations include the single-region sample restricting generalizability, the retrospective design with potential information bias, and an absence of data on antihypertensive medication adherence.

Implications Medical Affairs : This study's Bayesian joint modeling approach provides granular quantification of stroke risk attributable to sustained BP elevation in a resource-limited African context, complementing Western-centric evidence and highlighting the urgency of BP control programs in sub-Saharan Africa. From a Medical Affairs perspective, these data reinforce the global burden of uncontrolled hypertension and can be leveraged to support access initiatives and health-system strengthening messaging in emerging markets. Strategic priority should be placed on KOL engagement with African cardiovascular societies and researchers to develop region-specific hypertension management guidelines and position long-acting antihypertensive agents as tools for stroke prevention in these populations.

Date de publication : 2026-10-23

Interpretable Machine Learning for Predicting Early Onset Hypertension in the US.

Revue : Preventing Chronic Disease | Tier : NA | IF estime : NA

Date de publication : 2026-10-22

Resultats principaux : This study analyzed data from 9,850 US adults aged <=55 years drawn from the 2022 Medical Expenditure Panel Survey, defining early-onset hypertension by ICD-10 code I10 or self-reported diagnosis at or before age 55, with a weighted prevalence of 15.2% (n=1,647 cases). An extreme gradient boosting (XGBoost) model was trained with 45 variables selected via LASSO from an initial 84-variable pool using stratified 5-fold cross-validation, achieving ROC AUC of 0.807 (95% CI 0.787-0.827), PR AUC of 0.486, recall of 0.848, and F2 score of 0.606. Key predictors identified via SHAP analysis included older age, high BMI, and high cholesterol diagnosis; high cholesterol was particularly influential among adults over 40 years, while race and ethnicity were important predictors among African American adults. Fairness analyses revealed modestly lower performance for African Americans versus White participants, and slightly higher true positive rates for Hispanic and Asian individuals. Key limitations include cross-sectional survey design, self-reported outcomes, and the absence of prospective longitudinal validation.

Implications Medical Affairs : An ML model with 84.8% recall for early-onset hypertension using non-clinical socioeconomic and administrative variables underscores the feasibility of embedding scalable digital screening tools into primary care and population health management platforms, which is directly relevant to early detection strategies. From a Medical Affairs perspective, the identification of high cholesterol and BMI as leading modifiable predictors aligns with the value narrative for cardiometabolic risk management programs and combined lipid-BP intervention approaches. Strategic investment in real-world digital health partnerships to validate and deploy such models could establish a KOL leadership position in the precision prevention space for hypertension.

Date de publication : 2026-10-15

Artificial Light at Night and Hypertensive Disorders of Pregnancy: Socioeconomic Inequality in the China Birth Cohort Study.

Revue : Environmental Science & Technology | Tier : NA | IF estime : NA

Date de publication : 2026-10-13

Resultats principaux : This large prospective cohort study enrolled 241,608 pregnant participants from 17 Chinese provinces within the China Birth Cohort Study (CBCS) from February 2018 to December 2022, linking outdoor artificial light at night (ALAN) exposure estimated within 500 m of residential address using VIIRS/DNB satellite data to physician-diagnosed hypertensive disorders of pregnancy (HDPs). Overall, 3.88% of participants (n=9,383) were diagnosed with HDPs, and exposure to ALAN in quartiles 2, 3, and 4 was associated with ORs of 1.14 (95% CI 1.07-1.21), 1.21 (95% CI 1.13-1.29), and 1.09 (95% CI 1.02-1.17) for HDPs respectively compared to the lowest quartile. Effect modification by socioeconomic factors was demonstrated: the strongest association was observed in women in high-GDP provinces with low household income (OR 1.15, 95% CI 1.09-1.20), versus near-null effects in low-GDP areas with low income. Key limitations include cross-sectional exposure assessment without individual chronotype data, potential residual confounding by noise or other urban exposures co-occurring with ALAN, and reliance on physician diagnosis.

Implications Medical Affairs : This study establishes ALAN as a novel, population-scale environmental risk factor for hypertensive disorders of pregnancy, with socioeconomic disparities amplifying risk and representing an important new dimension in gestational hypertension pathophysiology. The intersection of environmental exposure and socioeconomic vulnerability has direct implications for public health messaging and urban planning advocacy, which Medical Affairs teams can leverage in positioning comprehensive hypertension prevention beyond pharmacological management. Engaging with maternal health KOLs and global environmental health researchers to explore circadian rhythm disruption as a mechanistic pathway for gestational hypertension could differentiate scientific leadership and open avenues for novel prevention research collaborations.

Hypertriglyceridemic Waist-to-Height Ratio and the Triple Burden of Hypertension, Diabetes, and Dyslipidemia in Adults: Association and Network Analysis.

Revue : Frontiers in Endocrinology | Tier : NA | IF estime : NA

Date de publication : 2026-10-07

Resultats principaux : This cross-sectional study analyzed 10,796 adults (>=18 years) from the Ningxia Hui Autonomous Region in Northwest China to evaluate the hypertriglyceridemic waist-to-height ratio (HTH/WH) phenotype as a predictor of concurrent hypertension, diabetes, and dyslipidemia (the triple burden). The prevalence of the triple burden was 3.99% in Ningxia, exceeding the Northwest China average of 2.33%. The HTH/WH phenotype (defined as TG >=1.7 mmol/L plus waist-to-height ratio >=0.5) was present in 21.3% of the population and was significantly and dose-dependently associated with the triple burden (adjusted OR 5.29, 95% CI 4.14-6.74). Mixed graphical modeling identified HTH/WH as a central bridge node (bridge strength 0.67) in the metabolic disorder network, while BMI showed the highest overall bridge centrality (bridge strength 1.72) and smoking also demonstrated bridge connectivity (0.32). Additional independent risk factors for the triple burden included age >=40 years, smoking, alcohol consumption, and elevated BMI, while higher education and married status were protective. Limitations include cross-sectional design and reliance on a single-region sample in a predominantly Han and Hui population.

Implications Medical Affairs : The identification of the HTH/WH phenotype as a simple, low-cost screening tool capable of identifying individuals at 5.29-fold elevated risk for the cardiometabolic triple burden provides actionable intelligence for cost-effective triage in resource-limited primary care settings, which is relevant to positioning antihypertensive therapies within a broader cardiometabolic risk framework. Medical Affairs teams can use this evidence to support the development of composite cardiometabolic risk education campaigns targeting primary care physicians in Asian markets where waist circumference and triglycerides are readily accessible clinical parameters. The network analysis findings identifying BMI as the highest-centrality node suggest that weight reduction strategies represent the highest-yield modifiable target within integrated hypertension-diabetes-dyslipidemia management, which should be incorporated into KOL education materials.

Conventional and Novel Hypoxemia-Related Indices for Obstructive Sleep Apnea Severity and Incident Hypertension: A Prospective Cohort Study.

Revue : Frontiers in Neurology | Tier : NA | IF estime : NA

Date de publication : 2026-10-07

Resultats principaux : This prospective cohort study analyzed 601 participants with OSA (AHI >=15 events/hour) from the Sleep Heart Health Study who were free of hypertension at baseline, with a median follow-up of approximately 5 years, to compare the predictive utility of novel hypoxemia indices (sleep breathing impairment index [SBII] and hypoxic burden [HB]) against conventional metrics (AHI and oxygen desaturation index [ODI]) for incident hypertension. During follow-up, 194 participants (32.3%) developed hypertension. In fully adjusted models, the highest quartiles of SBII (OR 1.71, 95% CI 1.02-2.90, p=0.044) and HB (OR 1.69, 95% CI 1.02-2.79, p=0.041) were associated with incident HTN, while AHI and ODI quartile associations were not statistically significant. In T90-adjusted sensitivity analyses, both SBII and HB associations remained significant. Limitations include borderline statistical significance in continuous analyses, the exploratory quartile-based nature of primary findings, and the need for external validation cohorts.

Implications Medical Affairs : The superior discriminative performance of novel hypoxemia burden indices over the traditional AHI for predicting incident hypertension in OSA patients suggests that refining sleep disorder phenotyping may identify a subgroup of patients with the highest hypertension risk who would benefit most from proactive antihypertensive intervention alongside CPAP therapy. Medical Affairs teams should monitor this emerging evidence as it could inform clinical algorithms integrating sleep medicine and cardiology, potentially widening the patient identification opportunity for antihypertensive agents in the OSA-hypertension comorbidity space. Strategic collaboration with sleep medicine KOLs to co-develop evidence on OSA-specific hypertension management pathways would reinforce scientific leadership at the cardiovascular-pulmonary interface.

Association Between Adverse Pregnancy Outcomes and Long-Term Postpartum Blood Pressure Trajectories: A Community-Based Cohort Study.

Revue : Frontiers in Public Health | Tier : NA | IF estime : NA

Date de publication : 2026-10-07

Resultats principaux : This community-based cohort study analyzed 1,346 women from the Kailuan Study who had documented deliveries between 2004 and 2020 and underwent at least three postpartum health examinations between 2006 and 2022, with mean follow-up of 9.2 years, to characterize long-term postpartum BP trajectories and their association with adverse pregnancy outcomes (APOs). Group-based trajectory modeling identified three distinct BP trajectory groups, with APOs associated with higher risks of membership in high-level SBP (aRR 1.64, 95% CI 1.19-2.66), DBP (aRR 1.61, 95% CI 1.24-1.87), and MAP (aRR 2.11, 95% CI 1.16-3.64) trajectory groups. Gestational diabetes mellitus and preterm birth were specifically associated with higher likelihood of membership in unfavorable high-level trajectory groups for all three BP components. Systolic BP showed a nonlinear increase over follow-up averaging 1.13 mmHg/year during the first 6.4 years before decelerating, while diastolic BP increased near-linearly. Limitations include potential selection bias from the community-based sample, a predominantly Chinese female population limiting generalizability, and residual confounding by unmeasured factors.

Implications Medical Affairs : The demonstration that gestational diabetes mellitus and preterm birth each predict sustained unfavorable postpartum blood pressure trajectories for nearly a decade post-delivery strengthens the evidence base for implementing systematic long-term cardiovascular follow-up protocols for women with APO histories, which directly supports guideline expansion in this underserved population. From a Medical Affairs perspective, this study identifies women with prior adverse pregnancy outcomes as a high-priority target segment for early antihypertensive intervention and cardiovascular risk education, potentially warranting sex-specific indication development discussions. Engagement with obstetric and cardiology KOLs to promote integrated obstetric-cardiovascular follow-up programs (cardio-obstetrics) represents a timely strategic opportunity given growing guideline attention to this lifecycle-based risk framework.

Association Between the Triglyceride-Glucose Index and Carotid-Femoral Pulse Wave Velocity in Rural Hypertensive Adults.

Revue : BMC Cardiovascular Disorders | Tier : NA | IF estime : NA

Date de publication : 2026-07-27

Resultats principaux : This cross-sectional study analyzed 12,751 hypertensive participants from the Hypertension and Stroke Prevention and Control Project (HSPCP) in rural China, with a mean age of 59.8 years (37.3% male), to investigate the relationship between the triglyceride-glucose (TyG) index and carotid-femoral pulse wave velocity (cfPWV) as a measure of arterial stiffness. The mean TyG index was 8.9 (SD 0.7) and mean cfPWV was 9.3 m/s; in multivariable-adjusted models, each unit increase in TyG index was associated with 0.15 m/s higher cfPWV (95% CI 0.10-0.21), and participants in the highest TyG quartile (>=9.2) had 0.28 m/s higher cfPWV compared to the lowest quartile (95% CI 0.17-0.38) in a dose-dependent fashion. Each unit increase in TyG index was associated with a 12% increased risk of elevated cfPWV, and the highest quartile had a 26% greater likelihood of cfPWV >10 m/s. Subgroup analysis identified a stronger association in non-obese participants; sensitivity analysis adjusting for fasting glucose attenuated but did not eliminate the association (beta 0.07, 95% CI 0.03-0.11). Limitations include the cross-sectional design precluding causality, restricted rural sample, and potential unmeasured confounders.

Implications Medical Affairs : The TyG index, a simple insulin resistance surrogate calculable from routine lipid and glucose measurements, emerges as a clinically accessible predictor of subclinical arterial stiffness in hypertensive populations, which supports its integration into cardiovascular risk stratification algorithms alongside traditional BP measurements. From a Medical Affairs perspective, this study reinforces the cardiometabolic interconnection between insulin resistance and vascular damage, strengthening the positioning of antihypertensive agents with metabolic neutrality or benefit in patients with concurrent insulin resistance as a differentiated clinical advantage. Engaging cardiovascular and endocrinology KOLs to validate TyG-based arterial stiffness stratification in multinational cohorts would extend this evidence base and support guideline-level recommendations.

Resultats principaux : This cross-sectional descriptive and analytical study reviewed records of patients with temporary work disability (TWD) secondary to COVID-19 in a rehabilitation unit in Mexico, including all cases with available data; prolonged TWD was defined as exceeding the 80th percentile threshold (203 days). A Novel Angiotensin I-Converting Enzyme Inhibitory Pentapeptide from Red Mold Rice: Identification, Stability, In Vivo Antihypertensive Effects, and Underlying Mechanisms.

Revue : Journal of Agricultural and Food Chemistry | Tier : NA | IF estime : NA

Date de publication : 2026-10-07

Resultats principaux : This study isolated a novel pentapeptide Val-Leu-Thr-Gly-Trp (VLTGW) from red mold rice (RMR), a traditional fermented food produced using Monascus strains, and characterized its ACE inhibitory activity, stability, in vivo antihypertensive efficacy, and mechanistic basis. VLTGW demonstrated potent ACE inhibitory activity with an IC50 of 17.4 microM and showed 550-fold selectivity for ACE over renin, which may confer a safety advantage by reducing off-target effects. The peptide retained over 90% of its inhibitory activity across a broad temperature range (20-80 degrees C) and pH range (2.0-12.0), suggesting favorable stability for oral delivery and gastrointestinal transit. In spontaneously hypertensive rats, VLTGW administration significantly reduced blood pressure, accompanied by decreased ACE activity, reduced angiotensin II levels, and increased nitric oxide levels, indicating a mechanism consistent with RAAS modulation and endothelial nitric oxide pathway activation. Key limitations include the preclinical in vivo model, absence of pharmacokinetic data in humans, and no dose-response or comparative data against established antihypertensive agents.

Implications Medical Affairs : The identification of a highly selective, thermostable ACE inhibitory peptide from fermented food with demonstrated in vivo antihypertensive efficacy in SHR models adds to the growing body of evidence for bioactive food-derived peptides as adjunctive or complementary tools for BP management, relevant to the nutraceutical and functional food markets. From a Medical Affairs perspective, while this compound is years from clinical application, its selectivity profile over renin represents a differentiated mechanistic characteristic that warrants monitoring as the pharmaceutical industry increasingly explores natural compound-derived leads for RAAS modulation. A strategic opportunity exists to engage food science and clinical nutrition KOLs to position the interface between dietary bioactive compounds and pharmacological BP management as an area where evidence-based dietary guidance complements first-line antihypertensive therapy.

Revue : JMIR Formative Research | Tier : NA | IF estime : NA

Date de publication : 2026-10-22

Resultats principaux : This 6-month two-arm pilot RCT enrolled 200 analyzed participants (wireless HBPM: n=73; standard HBPM: n=127) with uncontrolled or newly diagnosed hypertension (BP >=140/90 mmHg) from three federally qualified health center (FQHC) networks in San Diego serving predominantly Hispanic populations (51%), with both arms receiving standardized health coaching and randomized to either wireless Bluetooth BP cuffs with health coach-reviewed weekly transmissions or standard home monitors with paper logs. Implications Medical Affairs : This pilot demonstrates the feasibility and high acceptability of wireless home BP monitoring in a medically underserved, predominantly Hispanic population historically underrepresented in hypertension digital health trials, establishing foundational evidence for a fully powered RCT in similar FQHC settings.
