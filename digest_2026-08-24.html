Assessment of the Prevalence of Hypertension and Its Associated Risk Factors in a Rural Community in Gwalior District, India.

Revue : Cureus | Tier : NA | IF estime : NA

Date de publication : 2026-07-23

Resultats principaux : This community-based cross-sectional study enrolled 360 adults through multistage sampling in a rural community of Gwalior district, India, between May 2023 and April 2024, with 42.2% aged 36-50 years and a balanced sex distribution (50% male). The primary endpoint was the prevalence of hypertension, defined as SBP >= 140 mmHg and/or DBP >= 90 mmHg or current antihypertensive treatment, with secondary assessment of associated sociodemographic, anthropometric, dietary, and behavioral risk factors. Hypertension was documented in 38.6% of participants (n=139), comprising stage 1 in 27.8% and stage 2 in 10.8%, with an additional 29.4% classified as pre-hypertensive, meaning that more than two thirds of the sample had elevated blood pressure; overweight and obesity affected 33.1% and 18.1% respectively, increased waist circumference was found in 45.0%, extra salt intake was reported by 58.9%, smoking by 27.5%, alcohol consumption by 29.4%, and smokeless tobacco use by 33.3%. Subgroup analyses showed statistically significant associations (p < 0.05) between hypertension and male sex, overweight/obesity, excess salt intake, smoking, alcohol consumption, and family history of hypertension. Notable limitations include the cross-sectional design preventing causal inference, restriction to a single rural district limiting external generalizability, reliance on self-reported behavioral and dietary variables prone to recall and social desirability bias, and use of univariate chi-square tests without multivariable adjustment for confounders.

Implications Medical Affairs : The findings reinforce WHO and Indian national guideline recommendations to intensify community-based screening, salt-reduction interventions, and lifestyle-modification programs at the primary healthcare level in rural low- and middle-income settings where more than two thirds of adults already show elevated blood pressure. For product positioning, this evidence supports the case for accessible, affordable single-pill combinations and fixed-dose antihypertensive regimens paired with adherence-support programs tailored to newly diagnosed, largely treatment-naive rural populations. Actionable KOL strategy: engage Indian community medicine and public health leaders on co-designed real-world evidence studies and awareness campaigns targeting excess salt intake, obesity, tobacco/alcohol use, and family-history-based case finding, which can shape early-detection pathways and future health-authority guidance.
