Temporal Trends in Preterm Birth Associated With Hypertensive Disorders of Pregnancy in Victoria, Australia: A Population-Based Interrupted Time-Series Study.

Revue : The Medical Journal of Australia | Tier : NA | IF estime : NA

Date de publication : Aug 2026

Resultats principaux : This population-based interrupted time-series study analyzed 779,325 singleton births in Victoria, Australia, from January 2012 to March 2022, using seasonal autoregressive integrated moving average (SARIMA) modeling, with July 2018 set as a temporal reference point corresponding to the launch of a national preterm birth prevention program. Overall preterm birth (PTB) rates declined modestly from 5.8% to 5.6% after July 2018, while hypertensive disorders of pregnancy (HDP) prevalence rose from 6.4% to 7.0%. HDP-associated PTB increased before the 2018 reference point (+0.75 per 1,000 births per month; 95% CI 0.50-1.00) but then declined significantly (-1.12 per 1,000 births per month; 95% CI -1.88 to -0.36), driven predominantly by a reduction in iatrogenic rather than spontaneous preterm delivery. Decomposition analyses estimated 89 fewer HDP-associated PTB cases than expected under pre-2018 patterns, reflecting a decrease in the proportion of HDP pregnancies delivered preterm. Spontaneous PTB with HDP showed no significant temporal change, and PTB unrelated to HDP also showed no significant trend; key limitations include the ecological design and inability to attribute outcomes to specific interventions.

Implications Medical Affairs : These findings demonstrate that clinical care improvements in hypertensive pregnancy management can meaningfully reduce iatrogenic preterm birth rates even as HDP prevalence rises, providing a real-world evidence base supporting the value of antihypertensive and preventive care programs in pregnancy. For pharmaceutical companies with antihypertensive products targeting gestational hypertension or preeclampsia, this study reinforces the market need for safe and effective blood pressure management in pregnancy and supports a value narrative around reducing HDP-related complications. Medical Affairs teams should leverage this evidence to engage maternal-fetal medicine KOLs at the intersection of hypertension management and perinatal outcomes, positioning antihypertensive strategies within broader preterm birth prevention frameworks.

Durvalumab and tremelimumab, with or without lenvatinib, combined with transarterial chemoembolisation in participants with embolisation-eligible hepatocellular carcinoma (EMERALD-3): a global, randomised, open-label, sponsor-blinded, phase 3 study.

Revue : The Lancet. Oncology | Tier : NA | IF estime : NA

Date de publication : Sep 2026

Resultats principaux : EMERALD-3 is a global phase 3 randomised, open-label, sponsor-blinded trial conducted across 177 sites in 21 countries, enrolling 760 participants with embolisation-eligible hepatocellular carcinoma (HCC) not amenable to curative surgery, randomised 1:1:1 to STRIDE (single tremelimumab regular interval durvalumab) plus lenvatinib plus TACE, STRIDE plus TACE, or TACE alone. The primary endpoint of progression-free survival (PFS) for STRIDE plus lenvatinib plus TACE versus TACE was met, with a median PFS of 13.0 months versus 9.8 months (HR 0.70, 95% CI 0.57-0.86; p=0.0007) at a median follow-up of 10.0 months. The key secondary endpoint of overall survival (OS) did not reach statistical significance at the second data cutoff (median OS 39.5 vs 34.7 months; HR 0.84, 95% CI 0.65-1.09; p=0.18), with follow-up ongoing for final OS analysis. Hypertension was the most common grade 3-4 adverse event in the STRIDE plus lenvatinib plus TACE arm (12% of 287 participants), and serious adverse events occurred in 64% of patients in this triplet arm versus 23% in the TACE-only group. STRIDE plus TACE also demonstrated improved PFS (HR 0.71, 95% CI 0.56-0.91) versus matched TACE controls; study limitations include the open-label design and immature OS data requiring additional follow-up.

Implications Medical Affairs : The significant PFS benefit with STRIDE-based regimens in embolisation-eligible HCC establishes a new treatment paradigm bridging locoregional and systemic immunotherapy, with important cardiovascular implications given the 12% rate of grade 3-4 hypertension in the triplet arm, underscoring the need for structured blood pressure management protocols in immuno-oncology regimens. From a Medical Affairs perspective, this safety finding creates an opportunity to position antihypertensive products as essential supportive care components within HCC immunotherapy treatment pathways, complementing efficacy-focused KOL messaging with a cardiometabolic safety narrative. Medical Affairs teams should partner with hepato-oncology and cardiovascular co-management KOLs to develop practical hypertension monitoring and management guidelines for patients receiving anti-angiogenic immunotherapy combinations such as lenvatinib-based regimens.

Cardiac volumes, function, and arterial stiffness five years after severe preeclampsia: A comparison with sedentary and active controls.

Revue : Clinical Physiology and Functional Imaging | Tier : NA | IF estime : NA

Date de publication : Sep 2026

Resultats principaux : This case-control study examined cardiovascular structure and function 4-7 years after severe preeclampsia in 10 affected women compared to 20 women with normotensive pregnancies, including 10 lifestyle-matched controls and 10 physically active controls, using cardiac magnetic resonance (CMR), echocardiography, and pulse wave velocity (PWV) measurement. All three groups were normotensive at follow-up, with no statistically significant difference in systolic (103 vs 106 vs 108 mmHg, p=0.67) or diastolic (66 vs 66 vs 64 mmHg, p=0.78) blood pressure. Indexed left ventricular end-diastolic volume was significantly larger in physically active controls compared to the preeclampsia group (98 vs 84 mL/m2, p=0.004), as was end-systolic volume (41 vs 32 mL/m2, p=0.01), a difference attributed to physiological exercise adaptation rather than pathological remodeling. Left ventricular mass, overall cardiac function, PWV, and diastolic dysfunction criteria did not differ significantly between groups. The absence of persistent cardiovascular abnormalities in normotensive preeclampsia survivors suggests that sustained hypertension may be the primary mediator of post-preeclampsia cardiac remodeling; limitations include a very small sample size (n=30) and restriction to participants who remained normotensive long-term.

Implications Medical Affairs : The finding that women who remain normotensive after severe preeclampsia show no significant cardiovascular structural abnormalities reinforces the mechanistic hypothesis that blood pressure elevation is the key driver of long-term cardiac risk in this population, strengthening the evidence base for antihypertensive therapy as a primary preventive strategy when hypertension develops post-preeclampsia. From a Medical Affairs perspective, this study highlights the critical importance of structured long-term blood pressure surveillance programs for preeclampsia survivors, representing an opportunity to position antihypertensive products as essential tools for cardiovascular risk reduction in this high-risk group. Medical Affairs teams should engage obstetric cardiology and gynecological KOLs to develop post-preeclampsia cardiovascular follow-up guidelines that integrate blood pressure monitoring and early antihypertensive intervention as standard care elements.

Alcohol, Cardiometabolic Risk Factors, and Cirrhosis: Ten-Year Risk Estimates from a Population-based Danish Cohort.

Revue : Journal of Hepatology | Tier : NA | IF estime : NA

Date de publication : 24 Aug 2026

Resultats principaux : This large population-based cohort study included 354,624 participants from the Danish National Health Surveys (2010-2017) followed for 3.3 million person-years, during which 1,211 individuals developed cirrhosis; alcohol consumption emerged as the dominant modifiable risk factor, with a population attributable fraction (PAF) of 59.9% and 10-year absolute cirrhosis risks ranging from 0.14% for 1-7 drinks/week to 4.6% for over 42 drinks/week. Hypertension was the second largest risk factor by PAF at 29.3%, associated with a two- to threefold higher cirrhosis risk, with this association concentrated in participants consuming 1-28 drinks per week rather than heavier drinkers. BMI 30 kg/m2 or higher, diabetes, and smoking showed PAFs of 13.8%, 9.0%, and 21.2% respectively, while dyslipidemia showed no statistically significant association with cirrhosis risk except among moderate drinkers (8-14 drinks/week). Supra-additive interactions were observed for smoking across all alcohol consumption levels and for obesity, diabetes, and hypertension at low-to-moderate alcohol intake, suggesting synergistic harm among non-heavy drinkers. Limitations include reliance on self-reported alcohol consumption and cardiometabolic status, potential underrepresentation of the heaviest drinkers, and inability to account for changes in risk factor exposure over time.

Implications Medical Affairs : The substantial 29.3% population attributable fraction for hypertension in cirrhosis risk—second only to alcohol—positions antihypertensive therapy as a potentially important preventive strategy in patients with moderate alcohol use, representing a previously underappreciated indication that Medical Affairs should proactively communicate to hepatology and general medicine KOLs. This study provides quantitative epidemiological evidence that hypertension control could meaningfully reduce cirrhosis burden at the population level, particularly among moderate drinkers, which may support real-world evidence initiatives and expanded positioning of antihypertensive products in liver disease prevention. Medical Affairs teams should monitor future mechanistic research validating a causal role for hypertension in cirrhosis pathogenesis and engage with public health bodies to consider incorporating blood pressure management into liver disease prevention messaging and guidelines.

Explaining Variance in Pulse Wave Velocity in Youth: The Youth Vascular Consortium.

Revue : Hypertension | Tier : 2 | IF estime : ~8

Date de publication : 24 Aug 2026

Resultats principaux : The Youth Vascular Consortium study included 6,763 participants aged 5-40 years with carotid-femoral pulse wave velocity (cfPWV) measured using the SphygmoCor device, conducting multiple linear regression models stratified by sex and age group (19 years or younger vs older than 19 years), guided by the Akaike information criterion. Age and systolic blood pressure were the most consistent predictors of cfPWV across all groups, but collectively explained less than 40% of cfPWV variance in most subgroups. Heart rate and waist circumference emerged as additional key predictors, while lipid levels contributed modestly only in young adult participants (older than 19 years). Waist circumference was identified as the strongest anthropometric predictor in most subgroups, highlighting the early role of central adiposity in vascular aging, and systolic blood pressure demonstrated predictive importance even in the youngest age group (5-19 years). A notably large proportion of cfPWV variance remained unexplained by all traditional risk factors combined, suggesting that genetic, prenatal, environmental, and behavioral determinants substantially shape arterial stiffness from childhood; key limitations include the cross-sectional design of most contributing datasets and variability in participant selection across consortium sites.

Implications Medical Affairs : Published in the high-impact journal Hypertension, this consortium study provides robust cross-national evidence that systolic blood pressure is a significant predictor of arterial stiffness even in children and adolescents, strengthening the scientific rationale for early antihypertensive intervention to delay progressive vascular aging and reduce long-term cardiovascular risk. From a Medical Affairs perspective, these findings support developing a compelling scientific narrative linking blood pressure control to preservation of arterial elasticity across the lifespan, which could underpin pediatric and young adult positioning strategies for antihypertensive products in hypertensive youth. Medical Affairs teams should collaborate with pediatric cardiology and vascular medicine KOLs to integrate cfPWV as a relevant surrogate endpoint in clinical programs targeting hypertension in younger populations, facilitating earlier intervention trial design and reimbursement arguments.

Risk factors and population attributable fraction for dementia in American Indians: The Strong Heart Study.

Revue : Alzheimer's & Dementia | Tier : NA | IF estime : NA

Date de publication : Aug 2026

Resultats principaux : The Strong Heart Study analyzed dementia risk and population attributable fractions (PAF) in American Indians from the Southwest, Southern Plains, and Northern Plains, using longitudinal data from multiple visits between 1989 and 2019, with cognitive status adjudicated by panel consensus in participants aged 70-95 years. American Indians showed distinctive patterns of modifiable dementia risk factor prevalence compared to general population models, with higher rates of mid-life depression, head injury, smoking, hypertension, obesity, and diabetes. Logistic regression models identified hypertension as a significant risk factor for dementia with a PAF of 11%, along with education limitation (PAF 19%), social isolation (PAF 13%), and depression as the dominant risk factor (PAF 60%), a substantially higher burden than observed in non-Indigenous populations. High LDL cholesterol, physical inactivity, and heavy alcohol use were less prevalent in this cohort than comparable general population studies, contrasting with the Lancet Commission's existing dementia risk factor model. The study did not assess hearing loss, vision loss, or air pollution, which are recognized dementia risk factors in other populations, and generalizability may be limited by the geographic and cultural specificity of the enrolled American Indian communities.

Implications Medical Affairs : The 11% population attributable fraction for hypertension in dementia among American Indians contributes to the growing evidence linking sustained blood pressure control to cognitive preservation in diverse ethnic populations, reinforcing a cross-disease value proposition for antihypertensive therapies that extends beyond cardiovascular endpoints to include cognitive health. These findings support a Medical Affairs strategy of cross-positioning antihypertensive products at the intersection of cardiovascular risk reduction and dementia prevention, particularly in underserved and ethnically diverse populations where cardiometabolic burden is disproportionately high. Medical Affairs teams should engage with geriatric neurology, public health, and minority health disparity KOLs to develop evidence-based protocols incorporating blood pressure management within comprehensive dementia prevention initiatives tailored to Indigenous communities.
