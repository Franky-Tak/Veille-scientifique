Outcome of 30 Pregnant Women with Pulmonary Hypertension at Bangladesh Medical University, Dhaka, Bangladesh.

Revue : Mymensingh Medical Journal | Tier : NA | IF estime : NA

Date de publication : 2026-10

Resultats principaux : This retrospective descriptive study analyzed clinical data from 30 pregnant women with pulmonary hypertension (PH) admitted to a cardiology department in Dhaka, Bangladesh between January 2020 and December 2022. The cohort included five patients with idiopathic PAH (16.6%), 17 with PAH associated with congenital heart disease (CHD-PAH, 56.6%), two with left heart disease-related PH (6.7%), and six with PAH due to other diseases (20.0%). Fifty percent of patients had mean systolic pulmonary artery pressure exceeding 40 mmHg, and the overall maternal mortality rate was 13.33%, with CHD-PAH carrying the highest mortality (3/17, 17.64%). Neonatal outcomes were poor: 63.33% of deliveries were preterm, 40% of neonates had low birth weight, and the small-for-gestational-age rate was 56.66%. A key limitation is the small sample size of 30 patients from a single center, which precludes definitive statistical comparison across PH subtypes, though CHD-PAH patients showed the worst outcomes.

Implications Medical Affairs : These data reinforce international guidance advising women with PAH, particularly CHD-PAH, to avoid pregnancy given the high maternal and fetal morbidity and mortality, with implications for patient counseling strategies in Medical Affairs. The dominance of CHD-PAH in this low-resource setting highlights an unmet need for preconception cardiovascular care and pulmonary vasodilator therapy access in South Asia and similar markets. From a KOL engagement perspective, these findings support partnerships with obstetric cardiology specialists in South Asia to promote awareness of targeted PAH therapies and multidisciplinary pregnancy management protocols.

Left Ventricular Hypertrophy and Proteinuria in Hypertensive Patient with Retinopathy in Tertiary Care Hospital.

Revue : Mymensingh Medical Journal | Tier : NA | IF estime : NA

Date de publication : 2026-10

Resultats principaux : This cross-sectional observational study enrolled 100 hypertensive retinopathy patients at Dhaka Medical College Hospital over one year (July 2016–June 2017), with a mean age of 57.15 years and a male predominance (61%). Systolic and diastolic blood pressure values escalated significantly across retinopathy grades (Grade 1: 150.8/94.0 mmHg; Grade 2: 170.3/101.0 mmHg; Grade 3: 188.0/102.6 mmHg), with severity of left ventricular hypertrophy (LVH) significantly associated with blood pressure levels (p<0.001). Proteinuria was also significantly associated with hypertension severity (p<0.001), suggesting a parallel progression of renal and cardiac target organ damage. However, the study found no statistically significant association between hypertensive retinopathy grade and either LVH (p=0.32) or proteinuria (p=0.27), challenging assumptions about retinopathy as a marker for other organ damage. The study is limited by its single-center design, retrospective data collection, and small sample size of 100 patients, indicating that larger prospective cohort studies are needed to confirm or refute the retinopathy-LVH relationship.

Implications Medical Affairs : The dissociation between retinopathy severity and LVH or proteinuria severity suggests that eye examination alone may be insufficient as a proxy for total cardiovascular and renal organ damage assessment in hypertension management guidelines. For Medical Affairs, these findings support the need for comprehensive target organ damage evaluation—including echocardiography and urinalysis—beyond ophthalmologic examination in hypertensive patients, relevant to positioning diagnostic algorithms alongside antihypertensive therapies. KOL strategy should emphasize collaboration with cardiologists and nephrologists alongside ophthalmologists to ensure holistic hypertension management, particularly in resource-constrained settings in South and Southeast Asia.

Age- and sex-stratified prevalence of obstructive sleep apnea and stroke risk comorbidities: A large cross-sectional EHR study.

Revue : PLoS One | Tier : NA | IF estime : NA | DOI : 10.1371/journal.pone.0357824

Date de publication : 2026-09-30

Resultats principaux : This large cross-sectional study analyzed electronic health records of 556,474 de-identified patients with obstructive sleep apnea (OSA) from the HIPAA-compliant Cerner database (1999–2016) using structured SQL queries with age, sex, and comorbidity stratification. OSA prevalence peaked at age 55 for both sexes (males 10.43%, females 9.28%), while cardiovascular comorbidities were most prevalent in the 65-70 age group. Hypertension was the most prevalent stroke risk comorbidity in OSA patients (57.83%), substantially exceeding diabetes (34.7%), congestive heart failure (17.7%), and atrial fibrillation (13.31%). The Major Diagnostic Category analysis showed that ENT, cardiovascular, and musculoskeletal systems were most involved, consistent with chronic intermittent hypoxia pathophysiology. Limitations include the retrospective observational design precluding causal inference, potential coding biases in ICD-9 diagnosis, and the absence of data on OSA severity, treatment status, or blood pressure control.

Implications Medical Affairs : The very high prevalence of hypertension in the OSA population (57.83%) underscores OSA as a major driver of uncontrolled blood pressure and a key comorbidity to address in hypertension treatment guidelines and clinical decision algorithms. From a Medical Affairs perspective, this data supports positioning antihypertensive therapies within multimorbidity care pathways for OSA patients, particularly in cardiovascular risk reduction programs targeting middle-aged adults. Engagement with sleep medicine and pulmonology KOLs could be strategically valuable to integrate hypertension management into OSA diagnosis and follow-up protocols, broadening the therapeutic context for antihypertensive agents.

Risk factors profile and outcomes of stroke in low resources settings: a prospective and multicentric study in a sub-Saharan Africa country.

Revue : Frontiers in Neurology | Tier : NA | IF estime : NA | DOI : 10.3389/fneur.2026.1903821

Date de publication : 2026-09-15

Resultats principaux : This prospective multicenter cohort study enrolled 165 patients with ischemic or hemorrhagic stroke admitted to three tertiary hospitals in Luanda, Angola between May and October 2024. The mean patient age was 57.1 years, with 55.7% under 60 years, and hypertension was identified as the most prevalent modifiable risk factor (84.4%), followed by sedentary lifestyle (81.8%), with hemorrhagic stroke representing an unusually high 46% of cases. At hospital discharge, only 18% of patients achieved functional independence, while 77% had functional dependence; the 90-day overall mortality was 13.3%, comprising 4.8% in-hospital and 8.5% post-discharge mortality. Subgroup patterns showed that the high proportion of hemorrhagic stroke aligns with the poorly controlled hypertension burden in this population, suggesting systemic failures in hypertension detection and treatment. Limitations include the short recruitment window, single-country data, lack of neuroimaging standardization across sites, and absence of systematic medication use data prior to stroke onset.

Implications Medical Affairs : The 84.4% hypertension prevalence among stroke patients in this sub-Saharan cohort highlights a massive, urgent unmet need for primary hypertension prevention and treatment programs in low-income African settings, relevant to global health access strategies. Medical Affairs teams should consider these real-world data from sub-Saharan Africa as supporting evidence for expanding antihypertensive therapy access programs and market development in underserved African markets. KOL engagement in African academic neurology and cardiology centers could be prioritized to support clinical guidelines development, epidemiological research funding, and advocacy for improved stroke care infrastructure.

Risk Factor Analysis of Cardiovascular Events in Patients With Diabetic Kidney Disease and Hypertension: A Retrospective Study.

Revue : British Journal of Hospital Medicine | Tier : NA | IF estime : NA | DOI : 10.31083/BJHM49746

Date de publication : 2026-09-15

Resultats principaux : This single-center retrospective cohort study enrolled 204 patients diagnosed with both diabetic kidney disease (DKD) and hypertension from January 2021 to January 2024, with a 12-month follow-up period for cardiovascular event occurrence. Univariate and multivariate logistic regression identified prior hyperlipidemia, smoking, elevated LDL-C, reduced HDL-C, and increased kidney injury molecule-1 (KIM-1) as independent predictors of cardiovascular events (all p<0.05). Kaplan-Meier survival analysis demonstrated that patients with hyperlipidemia had significantly lower event-free survival than those without (p=0.034), as did patients with a smoking history versus non-smokers (p=0.024). The novel finding of KIM-1 as an independent cardiovascular risk predictor in this population suggests that renal tubular injury biomarkers may add prognostic value beyond traditional cardiovascular risk markers in the DKD-hypertension comorbidity context. Limitations include single-center design, relatively small sample size, short follow-up duration, and possible residual confounding from unmeasured variables.

Implications Medical Affairs : The identification of KIM-1 as an independent cardiovascular risk predictor in DKD-hypertension patients offers a potential new biomarker strategy for risk stratification, which could complement existing RAAS-based treatment algorithms. These findings reinforce the importance of comprehensive cardiometabolic management—including aggressive lipid control and smoking cessation—in DKD patients with hypertension, supporting guideline advocacy and clinical education initiatives by Medical Affairs. KOL strategy should target nephrologists and cardiologists working in cardiorenal syndrome to position integrated management approaches that address hypertension, renal protection, and lipid control simultaneously.

Childhood maltreatment and adult blood pressure-based outcomes: age- and sex-specific associations in the German National Cohort (NAKO).

Revue : BMC Public Health | Tier : NA | IF estime : NA | DOI : 10.1186/s12889-026-29019-z

Date de publication : 2026-09-09

Resultats principaux : This large cross-sectional study used data from 150,983 participants (49.3% female) of the German National Cohort (NAKO), collected between 2014 and 2019, assessing childhood maltreatment via the Childhood Trauma Screener and blood pressure by standardized resting measurement. Counterintuitively, the study found inverse associations between cumulative childhood maltreatment exposure and blood pressure markers, with females reporting high cumulative maltreatment showing lower odds of hypertension (OR=0.87, 95% CI: 0.80–0.94) but no significant association in males (OR=1.01, 95% CI: 0.91–1.13). Age-dependent analyses revealed sex-specific patterns in the maltreatment-blood pressure relationship, with physical and emotional abuse subscales showing more consistent inverse associations than neglect subscales. These unexpected inverse findings may reflect survivor bias, healthcare-seeking behaviors, or complex neuroendocrine-cardiovascular pathways that differ by sex and age cohort. Key limitations include the cross-sectional design, retrospective self-reporting of childhood trauma, and the inherent challenges of recall bias and underreporting in trauma assessment.

Implications Medical Affairs : The sex-specific and age-dependent nature of the childhood maltreatment-hypertension association highlights the importance of psychosocial risk factor assessment in cardiovascular risk models, with potential implications for precision public health strategies. Medical Affairs teams can leverage these data to support initiatives integrating mental health screening into cardiovascular risk assessment pathways, particularly for antihypertensive treatment algorithms that account for psychosocial determinants. Collaboration with psychiatric and public health KOLs in Germany and Europe may support joint research programs and conference initiatives connecting adverse childhood experiences to adult cardiovascular risk.

Mechanism and Individualized Management of Hypertension Associated With Bruton Tyrosine Kinase Inhibitors: The Notch3 Signaling Pathway.

Revue : Frontiers in Bioscience (Landmark Edition) | Tier : NA | IF estime : NA | DOI : 10.31083/FBL47763

Date de publication : 2026-09-07

Resultats principaux : This systematic review searched PubMed, Embase, and Web of Science through 2025 to analyze the epidemiology, molecular mechanisms, individual risk factors, and management strategies of hypertension associated with Bruton's tyrosine kinase inhibitors (BTKIs) used in B-cell malignancies. BTKI-related hypertension incidence ranges from 23% to 75%, with ibrutinib being the most studied agent; the pathophysiology involves endothelial dysfunction, vascular inflammation, RAAS upregulation, and NADPH oxidase-mediated oxidative stress primarily mediated through the Notch3 signaling pathway. Off-target inhibition of TEC and interleukin-2-inducible T-cell kinase contributes to adverse vascular effects, while Notch3 gene polymorphisms and existing cardiovascular disease further modulate individual risk. Current antihypertensive management relies on ACE inhibitors, ARBs, and calcium channel blockers; novel interventions including PCSK9 inhibitors and curcumin show potential mechanistic advantages but lack robust clinical trial data. Limitations of this review include the lack of head-to-head comparative antihypertensive trials in BTKI-treated patients, heterogeneity of published studies, and absence of standardized hypertension monitoring protocols in BTKI clinical trials.

Implications Medical Affairs : The high incidence of BTKI-induced hypertension (up to 75%) and the centrality of the Notch3 pathway present a compelling argument for pre-treatment cardiovascular risk stratification and proactive antihypertensive management protocols in hematology-oncology settings. Medical Affairs should monitor evolving evidence on BTKI cardiotoxicity management as a cross-therapeutic area opportunity, particularly for positioning RAS-blocking antihypertensives and CCBs in oncology-cardiology (cardio-oncology) joint guidelines. Engaging KOLs in cardio-oncology to participate in prospective registries of BTKI-treated patients could generate real-world evidence supporting individualized antihypertensive treatment algorithms and new product differentiation strategies.

Prevalence and associated factors of single pill combination therapy among people with hypertension in a tertiary hospital.

Revue : Medical Journal of Malaysia | Tier : NA | IF estime : NA

Date de publication : 2026-09

Resultats principaux : This cross-sectional study enrolled 280 hypertensive patients attending specialty clinics at Hospital Sultan Abdul Aziz Shah in Selangor, Malaysia, who were on antihypertensive medication for at least six months. The prevalence of single pill combination (SPC) therapy use was only 27.9% (78/280), indicating that the majority of hypertensive patients in this tertiary care setting receive multi-pill combination therapy. Multivariate logistic regression identified chronic kidney disease (CKD) presence, physician preference for SPC, and absence of diabetes mellitus as independent factors associated with SPC use (all p<0.05). Notably, the presence of diabetes was negatively associated with SPC use, possibly reflecting physician caution about titrating fixed-dose combinations in complex metabolic comorbidities. Limitations include the single-center cross-sectional design, potential selection bias from the specialist clinic population, and limited generalizability to primary care settings.

Implications Medical Affairs : The low SPC uptake (27.9%) despite strong evidence for its benefits in improving adherence and blood pressure control highlights a significant implementation gap, with direct relevance to pharmaceutical companies promoting SPC antihypertensive formulations in Southeast Asia. Medical Affairs strategies should target physician education programs emphasizing SPC advantages in adherence, pill burden reduction, and cardiovascular outcomes, particularly addressing the paradoxical underuse in diabetic patients where cardiovascular risk is highest. KOL engagement in Malaysian hypertension societies and family medicine networks could accelerate guideline uptake and drive prescribing behavior change toward SPC regimens in tertiary and primary care settings.

Global, regional, and national burden of exposure to chewing tobacco, 1990-2023: a systematic analysis of the Global Burden of Disease Study 2023.

Revue : The Lancet. Global Health | Tier : Tier 1 | IF estime : ~25 | DOI : 10.1016/j.langlo.2026.104043

Date de publication : 2026

Resultats principaux : This systematic analysis from the Global Burden of Disease Study 2023 estimated the prevalence of chewing tobacco use and attributable health burden across 204 countries and territories from 1990 to 2023, covering individuals aged 15 years and older using self-reported survey data from 188 countries. In 2023, an estimated 241 million people (95% UI: 165–355 million) used chewing tobacco globally, a prevalence of 3.9%, with use concentrated in South Asia (India accounting for 64.3% of male and 54.8% of female users). Chewing tobacco use was attributable to 251,000 deaths and 6.48 million disability-adjusted life-years (DALYs) in 2023, with stroke being the largest contributor (55.9 DALYs per 100,000 individuals), followed by lip and oral cavity cancers. Hypertension, as a key pathway through which chewing tobacco increases stroke risk, is implicated in this global burden though the direct hypertension-attributable fraction was not separately quantified. Major limitations include reliance on self-reported survey data, heterogeneous definitions of smokeless tobacco across surveys, and the characterization of the underlying epidemiological evidence as ranging from moderate to weak in strength.

Implications Medical Affairs : The identification of stroke as the leading outcome attributable to chewing tobacco—with hypertension as a principal mediating risk factor—underscores the role of blood pressure control in reducing tobacco-related cardiovascular burden in high-burden regions like South Asia. Medical Affairs teams operating in South Asian markets should incorporate chewing tobacco exposure into hypertension risk stratification tools and patient education materials, particularly for markets in India and Bangladesh where prevalence is highest. Engagement with WHO tobacco control bodies and South Asian hypertension KOLs could support advocacy for integrated tobacco cessation and blood pressure management programs, creating an opportunity for dual-indication positioning of cardiovascular risk reduction therapies.

Association Between Fruits and Vegetables Consumption and Premature Coronary Artery Disease (PCAD): A Multi-Center Case Control Study.

Revue : Food Science & Nutrition | Tier : NA | IF estime : NA | DOI : 10.1002/fsn3.72371

Date de publication : 2026-09-29

Resultats principaux : This multicenter case-control study from the Iran Premature Coronary Artery Disease (IPAD) study enrolled 3,249 participants aged under 60 years (men) or under 70 years (women) across 14 Iranian cities representing eight major ethnic groups. Cases (n=2,068) had coronary stenosis ≥75% in at least one vessel or ≥50% in the left main artery, while controls (n=1,181) had normal angiography; dietary intake was assessed by validated semi-quantitative food frequency questionnaire. After full confounder adjustment, most frequent fruit consumption was associated with a 91% reduction in PCAD risk (OR: 0.09, 95% CI: 0.06–0.13), vegetable intake with a 96% reduction (OR: 0.04, 95% CI: 0.02–0.06), and combined intake with a 95% reduction (OR: 0.05, 95% CI: 0.03–0.07). The inverse association was consistent across both sexes and correlated with PCAD severity, suggesting a dose-response relationship between plant-based diet quality and coronary disease risk. Study limitations include its cross-sectional dietary assessment design, potential recall bias in the FFQ, and the case-control methodology which limits causal inference.

Implications Medical Affairs : The dramatic protective effect of fruits and vegetables on premature CAD risk reinforces the DASH and Mediterranean dietary pattern guidelines for cardiovascular risk reduction and supports their integration into hypertension management guidelines in the Middle East region. Medical Affairs should leverage these data to support lifestyle modification programming as a complement to pharmacological antihypertensive therapy, emphasizing the additional cardiovascular benefits of plant-rich diets beyond blood pressure control. Engaging dietitian KOLs and cardiology societies in the Eastern Mediterranean region could support guideline advocacy and real-world implementation programs that position dietary intervention alongside antihypertensive pharmacotherapy.

Revue : JAMA Cardiology | Tier : Tier 2 | IF estime : ~24 | DOI : 10.1001/jamacardio.2026.4068

Date de publication : 2026-09-30

Resultats principaux : This cross-sectional GBD 2023 study estimated the global prevalence and years lived with disability (YLDs) for heart failure (HF) across 204 countries and territories from 1990 to 2023, using a comprehensive multi-source data integration methodology. Globally, an estimated 56.0 million cases of HF existed in 2023 (95% UI: 49.9–62.2 million), with prevalence increasing with age and expected to grow as global populations age. Hypertensive heart disease and ischemic heart disease were the two most common etiologies globally among older adults, while congenital anomalies and rheumatic heart disease predominated in children and young adults. Substantial geographical variation was observed, with rheumatic heart disease prominent in South and East Asia and sub-Saharan Africa, alcohol-associated HF peaking in Eastern Europe, and Chagas disease dominating in Latin America. The study's limitations include methodological assumptions in GBD modeling, incomplete data availability in many low-income countries, and the cross-sectional design which cannot capture temporal trends in individual etiology incidence.

Implications Medical Affairs : The dual dominance of hypertensive and ischemic heart disease as HF etiologies in older adults globally reinforces hypertension control as the primary modifiable intervention point for HF prevention, with major implications for antihypertensive therapy positioning in heart failure prevention guidelines. Medical Affairs should actively engage with heart failure KOLs and cardiology guideline committees to ensure that blood pressure control remains central in HF prevention messaging, particularly in the context of the 56 million affected patients globally. This high-impact publication in JAMA Cardiology provides a compelling scientific platform for KOL collaboration on global HF burden presentations and for supporting evidence-based advocacy for hypertension treatment access programs worldwide.

Date de publication : 2026-09-30

Resultats principaux : This multicenter Norwegian study examined adherence to an evidence-based physical healthcare model in 230 patients aged 16 years or older with schizophrenia, bipolar spectrum disorders, or antipsychotic medication use, at clinical sites within specialist mental health services. Using the Physical Health Care Fidelity Scale, the study found generally poor fidelity scores across sites, indicating inadequate implementation of evidence-based cardiovascular risk management practices in psychiatric care settings. Cardiovascular risk evaluation of foreign national incarcerated men in a closed prison in Istanbul, Türkiye.

Revue : Primary Health Care Research & Development | Tier : NA | IF estime : NA | DOI : 10.1017/S1463423626101947

Date de publication : 2026-09-30

Resultats principaux : This cross-sectional study evaluated cardiovascular disease risk in 366 foreign-national male inmates aged 40-69 at a closed prison in Istanbul, Turkey, using WHO risk assessment tools through in-person interviews, anthropometric assessments, and laboratory tests conducted between January and March 2023. The median cardiovascular risk score was 6%, with 74.6% classified as low risk, 21.0% as moderate risk, and 4.4% as high or very high risk; the cohort was predominantly Asian (74.6%) with a mean age of 47.4 years. Significant predictors of elevated cardiovascular risk included advanced age (p<0.001), smoking (p<0.001), and both systolic and diastolic hypertension (p<0.001), as well as abdominal obesity (p=0.005). High-risk individuals showed significantly elevated total cholesterol, LDL-C, HbA1c, and fasting blood glucose (p<0.001), highlighting the clustering of cardiometabolic risk factors in this vulnerable population. Limitations include the exclusively male and foreign-national sample from a single prison, limiting generalizability, and the cross-sectional design preventing causal inference about the effect of incarceration on cardiovascular risk.

Implications Medical Affairs : The high prevalence of hypertension and clustering of cardiovascular risk factors in incarcerated populations highlights a neglected healthcare setting where preventive cardiology—including antihypertensive treatment—is likely underimplemented. Medical Affairs and market access teams can consider correctional health services as an underserved institutional market segment, particularly in countries with large incarcerated populations, supporting access programs for cardiovascular preventive therapies. Collaborating with public health and correctional health KOLs to establish standardized cardiovascular risk screening and treatment protocols in prison health systems could advance health equity objectives while expanding the evidence base for preventive cardiology programs.
