Comparison of Labetalol With Versus Without Magnesium Sulfate on Intraoperative Blood Pressure Variability During Cesarean Delivery in Hypertensive Disorders of Pregnancy: A Retrospective Cohort Study.

Revue : Annali italiani di chirurgia | Tier : NA | IF estime : NA

Date de publication : 2026-08-13

Resultats principaux : This retrospective cohort study enrolled 170 patients with hypertensive disorders of pregnancy (HDP) undergoing cesarean delivery at TongXiang Maternity and Child Health Care Hospital (January 2023 to June 2025), comparing labetalol combined with magnesium sulfate (n=86) versus labetalol monotherapy (n=84); after propensity score matching, 54 patients per group were analyzed. The primary endpoint was the standard deviation of intraoperative systolic blood pressure (SD of SBP), with secondary endpoints including target blood pressure attainment rate, incidence of severe hypertensive events, and maternal-neonatal outcomes. The combination group demonstrated a significantly lower intraoperative SD of SBP (p<0.001), a higher blood pressure target attainment rate (p<0.001), and a lower rate of severe hypertensive events (p=0.030) compared to the monotherapy group. Neonatal outcomes were also improved: 1-minute and 5-minute Apgar scores were significantly higher in the combination group (p=0.035 and p=0.036), and NICU admission rates were significantly lower (p=0.002). Rates of adverse events including hypotension and bradycardia did not differ significantly between groups, though the retrospective single-center design and limited sample size post-matching constrain generalizability.

Implications Medical Affairs : These findings provide clinical evidence that labetalol combined with magnesium sulfate may represent a superior perioperative antihypertensive regimen in HDP, potentially supporting updates to obstetric hypertension management guidelines. From a Medical Affairs perspective, the data reinforce the value of combined antihypertensive approaches in high-risk obstetric populations, relevant for life-cycle management strategies of labetalol-based products. This study could serve as a basis for prospective multicenter trials and KOL-driven discussions on optimizing blood pressure management protocols during cesarean delivery in HDP patients.

Bibliometrics and Bioinformatics Exploration of Research Hotspots and Key Targets in Comorbidity: Interstitial Lung Disease and Pulmonary Hypertension.

Revue : Journal of visualized experiments : JoVE | Tier : NA | IF estime : NA

Date de publication : 2026-08-07

Resultats principaux : This study used a dual approach of bibliometrics and bioinformatics to map research hotspots and identify key molecular targets in the comorbidity of interstitial lung disease (ILD) and pulmonary hypertension (PH), drawing data from Web of Science Core Collection and Scopus with CiteSpace and VOSviewer for trend analysis. Overlapping genetic targets for both diseases were identified from GeneCards, and protein-protein interaction networks were constructed using STRING to identify hub genes; KEGG pathway enrichment analysis was performed using R. FN1, IL6, and TNF emerged as potential core comorbidity targets with biological plausibility in both conditions. Limitations include the retrospective bibliometric nature, reliance on publicly available genomic databases, and lack of experimental validation of the identified targets.

Implications Medical Affairs : The identification of FN1, IL6, and TNF as shared molecular targets in ILD-PH comorbidity offers mechanistic rationale for exploring anti-inflammatory and anti-fibrotic therapies in this high-mortality population, informing pipeline evaluation for compounds addressing PI3K-Akt or MAPK signaling. While primarily hypothesis-generating, this work provides a reproducible computational methodology applicable to other complex disease comorbidities, relevant for target assessment and cross-indication research planning. Medical Affairs teams should monitor the clinical translation of these targets to anticipate guideline evolution and position emerging therapies in this underserved patient population.

Distinctive mortality patterns and the survival paradox in intracerebral haemorrhage subtypes: a 15-year longitudinal study.

Revue : European stroke journal | Tier : NA | IF estime : NA

Date de publication : 2026-08-05

Resultats principaux : This large retrospective cohort study analyzed 15,186 non-traumatic intracerebral haemorrhage (ICH) patients from the Stroke Registry of the Chang-Gung Healthcare System in Taiwan (2007-2018), linked to national health insurance, clinical, and death registry data through 2022, stratifying patients into hypertensive deep ICH (first-ever: HICH-F; recurrent: HICH-R) and non-hypertensive lobar ICH (first-ever: NHICH-F; recurrent: NHICH-R) subtypes. Hypertensive ICH patients carried a higher burden of metabolic risk factors compared to non-hypertensive cases. A striking survival paradox was identified: NHICH-F patients had the highest initial 30-day mortality (22.9%) yet the best long-term survival probability among all subtypes, whereas HICH-F patients had the highest rate of subsequent ICH recurrence at 50.5%. Overall mortality in recurrent ICH groups approached 66%, driven primarily by pneumonia and diabetes-related complications rather than stroke recurrence alone. Limitations include the retrospective single-system design in Taiwan and potential inaccuracies in administrative coding of ICH subtypes and causes of death.

Implications Medical Affairs : This study demonstrates that hypertension-driven ICH carries a distinct long-term risk profile characterized by a 50.5% recurrence rate, emphasizing the critical unmet need for sustained and optimized blood pressure control after first-ever hypertensive stroke. From a Medical Affairs perspective, these long-term outcome data provide a compelling evidence base for KOL engagement and guideline advocacy targeting neurologists and cardiologists on the importance of tight blood pressure management in post-ICH patients. Educational initiatives should leverage this recurrence and mortality burden data to drive adoption of more aggressive antihypertensive secondary prevention strategies in the post-hypertensive ICH population.

Association of N-butylphthalide treatment with 1-year activities-of-daily-living recovery in convalescent ischemic stroke patients with chronic disease burden: a post-marketing real-world non-randomized controlled study.

Revue : Frontiers in medicine | Tier : NA | IF estime : NA

Date de publication : 2026-08-03

Resultats principaux : This post-marketing, real-world, prospective, non-randomized controlled study enrolled 1,190 convalescent ischemic stroke patients in Tianjin, China, of whom 1,095 completed 1-year functional assessment (NBP group: n=533; control: n=562); chronic disease burden was defined by cumulative comorbidities including hypertension, diabetes, hyperlipidemia, obesity, and coronary heart disease. The primary endpoint was change in Barthel Index (BI) score from baseline to 1 year, with secondary endpoints including mRS score change and item-level BI changes. At 1 year, the NBP group showed significantly greater BI improvement versus controls (1.31+/-13.99 vs. -1.11+/-15.56; p=0.0067), while mRS change did not differ significantly (p=0.877). Fully adjusted multivariable regression confirmed the association between NBP treatment and greater BI improvement (beta=2.40, 95% CI: 0.77-4.03; p=0.0038), with IPTW and conservative death-imputation analyses yielding consistent results. Limitations include the non-randomized design, potential selection bias, and inability to establish causality in a post-marketing observational framework.

Implications Medical Affairs : This real-world study suggests N-butylphthalide may provide functional rehabilitation benefits in hypertensive stroke survivors beyond its established acute indication, representing a potentially relevant post-stroke management option in a population with high chronic disease burden. From a Medical Affairs perspective, the BI-centered improvement in self-care and mobility provides actionable insights for product positioning around long-term quality-of-life outcomes in stroke rehabilitation, a dimension distinct from standard recurrence-prevention messaging. These findings highlight a research opportunity for a prospective randomized controlled trial focusing on ADL recovery as a primary endpoint in the post-stroke hypertensive patient population.

Global and regional progress towards reduced burden of oral conditions between 2019 and 2023: a systematic analysis for the Global Burden of Disease Study 2023.

Revue : The Lancet. Public health | Tier : NA | IF estime : NA

Date de publication : 2026-09

Resultats principaux : This systematic GBD 2023 analysis evaluated global and regional trends in the prevalence of major oral conditions across 204 countries and territories from 2019 to 2023, targeting the WHO Global Oral Health Action Plan goal of a 10% relative reduction in combined oral condition prevalence by 2030. Globally, age-standardised prevalence of main oral conditions declined modestly (-1.55%, 95% UI: -2.32 to -0.82) while total cases increased by 3.07% from 3.62 to 3.73 billion due to population growth. Progress was heterogeneous: South Asia achieved the largest reductions (-3.34% in age-standardised prevalence), while Sub-Saharan Africa showed the largest increase in prevalent cases (+9.51%) and the highest rise in lip/oral cavity cancer incidence (+14.86%). The age-standardised prevalence of orofacial clefts increased (+2.25%), high-income countries paradoxically showed an increase in oral condition prevalence (+1.24%), and at current pace global targets for 2030 are unlikely to be met. The study's principal limitation is reliance on modelled estimates from heterogeneous epidemiological surveys and registries across 204 countries.

Implications Medical Affairs : Although this GBD analysis focuses on oral health, its documentation of persistent global NCD burden including cardiovascular-metabolic risk connections (oral-systemic disease links) is increasingly relevant to integrated cardiometabolic Medical Affairs strategies. From a positioning perspective, the persistent global burden of untreated oral conditions in lower-income regions highlights socioeconomic barriers to comprehensive preventive care that equally affect hypertension management access, relevant for multi-morbidity burden analyses. Medical Affairs teams should consider cross-disease GBD data to contextualize multi-morbidity frameworks and engage KOLs in integrated NCD prevention strategies targeting shared social determinants.

Date de publication : 2026-09

The principal limitation is reliance on modelled spatiotemporal Gaussian process regression estimates, which may mask local granularity and sub-national variation.

Implications Medical Affairs : The global health workforce deficit has direct implications for hypertension management access in low- and middle-income countries, where physician and nurse shortages restrict diagnosis, treatment initiation, and long-term blood pressure follow-up. Global, regional, and national burden of HIV/AIDS, 1990-2023, with the estimated burden attributable to intimate partner violence and forecasted impacts of funding cuts through 2030: results from the Global Burden of Disease Study 2023.

Revue : The lancet. HIV | Tier : NA | IF estime : NA

Date de publication : 2026-08-18

Resultats principaux : This GBD 2023 systematic analysis estimated annual HIV incidence, prevalence, and mortality for 204 countries and territories from 1990 to 2023, disaggregated by age and sex, and quantified the proportion of HIV burden attributable to intimate partner violence (IPV) and projected impacts of funding reductions through 2030. Global HIV incidence declined from 2.85 million (95% UI: 2.76-2.96) in 2003 to 2.06 million (1.88-2.29) in 2023, while HIV-related deaths fell from 1.71 million (2004 peak) to 0.83 million in 2023. IPV was associated with an estimated 10.7% (1.6-20.4) of HIV-related deaths among women aged 15 years and older globally in 2023, corresponding to approximately 40,700 deaths, with the highest attributable fractions in Oceania (17.4%), Central sub-Saharan Africa (14.2%), and Eastern sub-Saharan Africa (13.2%). Forecasts indicate a projected 8.1% global reduction in ART coverage from funding cuts could result in 1.6 million additional HIV infections and approximately 790,600 additional HIV-related deaths among females by 2030. Limitations include modelling uncertainty in ART coverage projections and reliance on administrative data for antiretroviral therapy estimates across diverse health systems.

Implications Medical Affairs : HIV-associated hypertension and cardiovascular risk are increasingly recognized as critical comorbidities in people living with HIV, particularly as long-term ART use is associated with metabolic and vascular side effects including elevated blood pressure requiring antihypertensive management. From a Medical Affairs perspective, the projected loss of ART coverage could increase the burden of untreated HIV-associated comorbidities including hypertension in sub-Saharan Africa, making cross-disease management frameworks increasingly strategic for pharmaceutical companies with both HIV and cardiovascular portfolios. Engagement with global health organizations and KOLs on integrated NCD-HIV care pathways, particularly in sub-Saharan Africa, presents a tangible opportunity aligned with these GBD projections.

Revue : Internal and emergency medicine | Tier : NA | IF estime : NA

Date de publication : 2026-08-18

Resultats principaux : This comprehensive literature review examined how gender identity influences cardiovascular health in the transgender population, focusing on emerging risk factors, biological mechanisms, psychosocial determinants, and the role of gender-affirming hormone therapy (GAHT), drawing from PubMed, Scopus, and Embase. Key cardiovascular risk factors identified in this population include social stress, discrimination, limited healthcare access, poor lifestyle behaviors (smoking, poor diet, physical inactivity), and clinical conditions such as hypertension, all of which act synergistically to elevate cardiovascular risk. Implications Medical Affairs : This review highlights hypertension and cardiovascular risk as a major but underaddressed concern in transgender individuals, a population historically excluded from clinical hypertension trials, representing a gap in evidence-based antihypertensive guidelines that Medical Affairs should actively address. From a Medical Affairs perspective, the interaction between GAHT and cardiovascular risk factors including hypertension creates a niche but growing area for evidence generation and KOL engagement at the intersection of gender medicine and cardiology. Morphological subtypes and clinicopathological features of podocyte infolding glomerulopathy: retrospective nationwide Japanese cohort study of 93 cases.

Revue : Clinical and experimental nephrology | Tier : NA | IF estime : NA

Date de publication : 2026-08-18

Resultats principaux : This retrospective nationwide cohort study conducted by the PIG Working Group of the Japanese Society of Nephrology enrolled 103 registered cases across 69 institutions (1991-2025), ultimately analyzing 93 confirmed cases of podocyte infolding glomerulopathy (PIG) using novel transmission electron microscopy (TEM)-based diagnostic criteria. Four morphological subtypes were identified: Type A (primary podocyte infolding without prominent microspheres/microtubules, n=21), Type B (predominant microspheres, n=34), Type C (microtubules with/without microspheres, n=38), and Type D (dense microsphere clusters, n=10). Types B and C were more common in younger females and frequently associated with systemic lupus erythematosus (SLE; 58% and 71% respectively), while Types A and D occurred mainly in older males without SLE association, suggesting two distinct clinicopathological phenotypes. Secondary hypertension from glomerular disease is an important downstream complication in this population; the study did not directly assess hypertension as a primary endpoint but its presence as a disease complication is clinically relevant. The rare nature of PIG with only 93 cases nationwide across 34 years represents the primary study limitation, alongside retrospective design and potential selection bias.

Implications Medical Affairs : Although PIG is a rare glomerular disease, its classification into SLE-associated and non-SLE phenotypes has important implications for diagnosing and managing secondary hypertension and renal complications in affected patients, a population where blood pressure control is critical to slowing CKD progression. From a Medical Affairs perspective, this study underscores the importance of TEM-based glomerular diagnostics in rare nephropathies and may inform positioning of immunosuppressive or biologic therapies in SLE-associated glomerular disease where blood pressure management is a co-objective. The establishment of standardized TEM diagnostic criteria in this nationwide cohort provides a model for future multinational rare disease registries and supports the Medical Affairs rationale for rare nephropathy research investment.

Date de publication : 2026-08-17

Resultats principaux : This retrospective cohort study analyzed 11,394 patients with alcohol-associated liver disease (ALD) in the Banner Health System across five US states (January 2012 to October 2024), using the Social Deprivation Index (SDI) to stratify patients into four neighbourhood deprivation quartiles; 6,747 had at least 365 days of follow-up, with mean age 51 years and 65.6% female. Hypertension was included as a covariate in multivariable competing risk analysis alongside other cardiovascular and metabolic comorbidities, reflecting its high prevalence in this population. Patients in the most socioeconomically deprived neighbourhoods (Quartile 4) had significantly increased adjusted mortality (aHR: 1.48, 95% CI: 1.09-2.01) compared to those in the least deprived areas (Quartile 1), after adjustment for age, sex, race/ethnicity, insurance status, and comorbidities including hypertension. Quartile 4 patients were more likely to be Hispanic, Black, or Native American/Alaska Native, uninsured or Medicaid-reliant, and to carry higher BMI and greater comorbid burden including cardiovascular risk factors. Limitations include retrospective ICD code-based ascertainment, restriction to a single health system, and lack of longitudinal socioeconomic data.

Implications Medical Affairs : This study demonstrates that neighbourhood-level social determinants of health independently predict mortality in ALD patients with hypertension as a concurrent cardiovascular comorbidity, reinforcing the framework linking environmental exposures and socioeconomic conditions to cardiovascular and hepatic outcomes. From a Medical Affairs standpoint, these data support the inclusion of social determinants screening in holistic patient management programs, particularly relevant for pharmaceutical companies developing therapies for cardiometabolic comorbidities in socially disadvantaged populations. Resultats principaux : This large nationwide retrospective cohort study analyzed 2,989,648 individuals from Japanese claims data linked to health checkup records (April 2008 to July 2024), of whom 10.1% had at least one mental disorder; median follow-up was 1,252 days (IQR: 728-1,805). The composite primary kidney outcome (greater than or equal to 30% eGFR decline, incident ESRD, or initiation of kidney replacement therapy) occurred in 31,996 individuals over follow-up. Implications Medical Affairs : This large nationwide study identifies mental disorders as independent contributors to kidney function decline, directly relevant to hypertension management given that CKD, mental illness, and elevated blood pressure frequently co-exist as a triad driving cardiorenal risk. From a Medical Affairs perspective, the dose-response relationship between psychiatric multimorbidity and kidney outcomes supports the clinical rationale for comprehensive cardiorenal-psychiatric risk assessment including routine blood pressure monitoring in patients with mental disorders. Resultats principaux : This review, based on clinical cohort data, summarized evidence linking behavioral risk factors—including smoking, alcohol intake, sedentary lifestyle, and poor dietary quality—with altered metabolic profiles, focusing on mechanistic pathways, biomarker changes, and interaction effects across multiple cohorts. Key metabolic outcomes associated with poor health behaviors included impaired insulin sensitivity, dyslipidemia, increased visceral adiposity, hypertension, and chronic low-grade inflammation, with behaviors demonstrating additive and in some cases synergistic metabolic effects. Primary limitations include the review's observational and retrospective nature, heterogeneous cohort compositions, variable adjustment for confounders, and absence of novel primary data generation.

Implications Medical Affairs : This review reinforces the clinically underutilized concept that behavioral risk factor clustering—including sedentary behavior, poor diet, and psychosocial stress—synergistically elevates blood pressure, underscoring the importance of behavioral intervention programs as adjuncts in hypertension prevention strategies. From a Medical Affairs perspective, evidence that psychological stress and sleep disruption act as metabolic amplifiers provides scientific grounding for holistic hypertension management programs combining pharmacotherapy with behavioral interventions, which can be incorporated into KOL messaging and patient education frameworks. Gut-brain axis: a revolutionary paradigm in the pathogenesis and treatment of ischemic stroke.

Revue : Frontiers in neurology | Tier : NA | IF estime : NA

Date de publication : 2026-08-03

Resultats principaux : This systematic review examined the bidirectional communication network between the brain, gut, and gut microbiota (Gut-Brain Axis) in ischemic stroke pathophysiology and treatment, encompassing human observational data and preclinical animal models across the published literature. The review specifically discusses hypertension and atherosclerosis as major stroke risk factors whose progression correlates with gut microbiota composition, noting that most human data are correlative while causal evidence remains largely preclinical. Cerebral ischemia was shown to impair intestinal barrier function and cause microbial dysbiosis via autonomic and inflammatory pathways, which in turn promotes neuroinflammation and hinders brain repair through metabolites such as short-chain fatty acids (SCFAs) and trimethylamine N-oxide (TMAO), a pro-hypertensive and pro-atherosclerotic gut-derived metabolite. Clinical evidence for gut-targeted interventions including dietary modification, probiotics, and fecal microbiota transplantation was reviewed, with the authors emphasizing that most clinical trials are small, single-center, and inconsistent, necessitating large multicenter RCTs before any clinical recommendation. Key limitations include the predominantly preclinical evidence base, inter-individual microbiome heterogeneity, and significant translational gaps between animal models and human stroke patients.

Implications Medical Affairs : This review positions gut microbiota modulation as an emerging adjunct strategy in hypertension-driven stroke prevention and rehabilitation, with potential implications for novel therapeutic targets beyond traditional antihypertensive pharmacotherapy. The role of TMAO as a gut-derived pro-hypertensive and pro-atherosclerotic metabolite provides scientific rationale for exploring dietary and microbiome-targeted interventions alongside antihypertensive therapy, potentially informing pipeline positioning in cardiometabolic and stroke prevention research. KOL engagement initiatives targeting neurologists, cardiologists, and gastroenterologists should incorporate these emerging gut-brain findings to foster cross-specialty dialogue on integrated hypertension and stroke prevention strategies.
