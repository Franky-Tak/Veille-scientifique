Association between the Dietary Approaches to Stop Hypertension (DASH) diet and risk of incident heart failure and heart failure-related death: a UK Biobank cohort study.

Revue : Open Heart | Tier : NA | IF estime : NA

Date de publication : 2026-08-10

Resultats principaux : This prospective cohort study analyzed 124,777 UK Biobank participants (mean age 56.1 plus or minus 7.8 years; 56% female) free of heart failure at baseline (2006-2010), followed for a mean of 12 years to 2024. The primary composite outcome was incident heart failure or HF-related death, with secondary outcomes including incident hypertension and type 2 diabetes mellitus; DASH adherence was assessed via at least two 24-hour dietary recalls and categorized into quintiles using Fine-Gray competing risks Cox models. Compared with the lowest DASH quintile, the highest adherence quintile was associated with a 21% lower risk of HF after sociodemographic adjustment (aHR 0.79, 95% CI 0.70-0.89, p less than 0.001) and an 11% reduction after full comorbidity adjustment (aHR 0.89, 95% CI 0.79-1.00, p less than 0.05), with 2,931 HF events and 441 HF-related deaths observed during follow-up. Greater DASH adherence also significantly reduced risk of hypertension (aHR 0.91, 95% CI 0.86-0.96, p less than 0.001) and T2DM (aHR 0.82, 95% CI 0.75-0.90, p less than 0.001), indicating that DASH modifies multiple cardiovascular risk pathways simultaneously. The attenuation of the heart failure risk reduction after comorbidity adjustment suggests that part of the cardioprotective effect of DASH is mediated through hypertension and diabetes prevention rather than a direct independent effect on HF, which is an important mechanistic limitation for interpreting causality.

Implications Medical Affairs : These results strengthen the evidence base for dietary intervention recommendations in hypertension management guidelines, particularly as DASH's protective effect on incident hypertension may reduce downstream cardiovascular events across the prevention continuum. This large population-based finding supports Medical Affairs messaging around integrated lifestyle modification as a complement to antihypertensive pharmacotherapy, particularly in primary prevention communications and patient education materials. The UK Biobank dataset represents a platform for collaborative real-world evidence initiatives, offering KOL engagement opportunities around diet-cardiovascular risk interactions that can reinforce product positioning within comprehensive cardiovascular risk reduction strategies.

Genetic predisposition to high blood pressure and out-of-office hypertension: Insights from a population sample in Liechtenstein.

Revue : PloS one | Tier : NA | IF estime : NA

Date de publication : 2026-08-10

Resultats principaux : This study enrolled 1,444 participants aged 25-41 years from the GAPP (Genetic and Phenotypic Determinants of Blood Pressure) study in Liechtenstein to evaluate whether polygenic risk scores (PRS) and family history predict ambulatory blood pressure measures in a predominantly healthy young adult population. Strikingly, 12% of participants had office hypertension while 37% had out-of-office (ambulatory daytime) hypertension, revealing a substantial masked hypertension burden in this young European cohort. Both PRS (2.2 mmHg per 1 SD increase; 95% CI 1.6-2.7) and positive family history (2.4 mmHg increase; 95% CI 1.3-3.4) were strongly associated with ambulatory systolic blood pressure, but only PRS provided incremental predictive improvement beyond a validated BP prediction model (delta-AIC = -33 versus delta-AIC = +1 for family history). The improvement in positive likelihood ratio with PRS was modest (7.69 versus 6.87 baseline), indicating that while statistically significant, the incremental clinical utility of PRS at the point of care requires further development before routine implementation. The study was conducted exclusively in a white European population from Liechtenstein, significantly limiting generalizability to ethnically diverse populations where PRS performance may differ substantially.

Implications Medical Affairs : This study contributes to the evolving literature on precision medicine approaches to hypertension screening, suggesting that PRS tools may eventually complement clinical workflows for early identification of masked hypertension — a condition often missed with standard office measurement alone. For Medical Affairs, these data support ongoing monitoring of companion diagnostic developments and genetic risk stratification tools that could be co-developed alongside antihypertensive therapies as part of personalized treatment pathways in precision cardiovascular medicine. The finding that 37% of this young adult cohort had ambulatory hypertension underscores a significant unmet diagnostic need and creates opportunities for engaging KOLs in cardiovascular genomics, particularly in advocacy for expanded ambulatory blood pressure monitoring in primary prevention.

A Case Report of Myocardial Infarction Complicated With Blood Pressure Fluctuations Caused by Pheochromocytoma.

Revue : Journal of clinical hypertension (Greenwich, Conn.) | Tier : Tier 3 | IF estime : 3-10

Date de publication : 2026-08

Resultats principaux : This case report describes a 51-year-old male presenting with 17-day history of chest tightness who was initially diagnosed with NSTEMI based on elevated troponin and multivessel coronary artery disease with LAD occlusion on angiography, without initial suspicion of secondary hypertension. During percutaneous coronary intervention, blood pressure surged catastrophically to 240/130 mmHg, then dropped to 95/55 mmHg with intravenous nitroprusside and rebounded above 200 mmHg upon withdrawal in an "all-or-none" pattern atypical of pharmacological management, prompting further evaluation. Biochemical workup revealed markedly elevated plasma catecholamines — dopamine 77.58 pg/mL (normal 0-20), normetanephrine 1881.63 pg/mL (normal 0-145), and norepinephrine 5,724.04 pg/mL (normal 217-1,109) — and CT plus PET-CT identified a left retroperitoneal mass (59x34x44 mm, SUVmax 14.6) consistent with pheochromocytoma. Surgical resection confirmed by histopathology and immunohistochemistry resulted in complete normalization of blood pressure without antihypertensive medications. The primary limitation is the single-case nature of this report, which precludes establishing prevalence or generalizability of this rare atypical pheochromocytoma presentation as an acute cardiovascular event.

Implications Medical Affairs : This case reinforces the critical importance of including secondary hypertension in the differential diagnosis of patients presenting with acute cardiovascular events complicated by unusual hemodynamic instability, particularly when standard antihypertensive responses are paradoxical. From a Medical Affairs perspective, this finding supports educational initiatives on secondary hypertension diagnosis, particularly targeting interventional cardiologists and intensivists who may encounter this rare but life-threatening presentation in catheterization laboratory settings. The curative outcome following surgical resection without residual antihypertensive therapy illustrates the value of thorough etiological workup and creates a compelling clinical narrative for KOL-led educational programs on differentiating primary from secondary hypertension.

Association between hypertension prevalence and neighborhood characteristics in Japan using combined claims data and geographic information systems: a neighborhood correlation study from LIFE Study.

Revue : Environmental health and preventive medicine | Tier : NA | IF estime : NA

Date de publication : 2026

Resultats principaux : This neighborhood correlation study examined 424 elementary school districts across 14 municipalities in five prefectures in western Japan, using 2021 medical claims and geographic information system data with a mean of 2,248 participants per district, focusing on individuals enrolled in the National Health Insurance or Medical Care System for the Elderly. The mean hypertension prevalence across districts was 51% (53% in men, 50% in women), confirming Japan's substantial and well-documented hypertension burden at the community level. In quasi-Poisson regression analyses with Bonferroni correction, higher neighborhood proportion of university graduates was significantly and negatively associated with hypertension prevalence in combined, male-only, and female-only analyses, suggesting that educational attainment operates as a community-level protective factor. Green space coverage was also significantly inversely associated with hypertension prevalence in all population groups, and park density showed an additional significant negative association in male-only models, supporting the role of built environment access in cardiovascular health. This ecological study design precludes individual-level causal inference, and unmeasured community confounders including healthcare access density, dietary patterns, and average salt intake cannot be excluded as explanatory variables.

Implications Medical Affairs : This study adds important ecological evidence linking social determinants of health and built environment factors with population-level hypertension burden, complementing pharmacological approaches with a structural prevention perspective particularly relevant in aging Japanese and Asia-Pacific markets. For Medical Affairs teams, these findings can support advocacy for integrated hypertension prevention programs combining antihypertensive pharmacotherapy with community-level interventions, strengthening health economic arguments around population-wide blood pressure reduction. The Japanese context, with a mean district hypertension prevalence of 51%, highlights the scale of the treatment opportunity and provides quantitative grounding for market access arguments centered on unmet need in high-prevalence aging populations.

Clustering patterns of behavioral and metabolic risk factors for noncommunicable diseases in Iran: findings from a national STEPS survey.

Revue : Global health action | Tier : NA | IF estime : NA

Date de publication : 2026-08-10

Resultats principaux : This cross-sectional study analyzed 16,618 Iranian adults aged 25 years and older from the nationally representative 2021 STEPS survey, applying k-means clustering to 13 behavioral and metabolic variables including blood pressure, BMI, fasting glucose, lipid markers, physical activity, smoking, alcohol, and salt intake. Five distinct co-occurrence clusters were identified: smokers-drinkers (3.1%), healthy-low-risk (40.3%), physically active (6.6%), dyslipidemic (26.0%), and hypertensive-diabetic (24.0%), with the latter two clusters collectively representing approximately 50% of the adult Iranian population. The hypertensive-diabetic cluster, constituting nearly a quarter of adults, was characterized by the highest rates of obesity, hypertension, and diabetes and was predominantly composed of older urban adults, highlighting the concentration of cardiometabolic risk in aging urban populations. The dyslipidemic cluster showed high rates of overweight and dyslipidemia without the full hypertensive-diabetic profile, suggesting a potential progression pathway toward the high-risk cluster if untreated. A key limitation is the cross-sectional design, which precludes tracking of risk factor cluster transitions over time, and self-reported dietary and behavioral data may underestimate true prevalence due to social desirability bias, particularly for alcohol and salt intake.

Implications Medical Affairs : The finding that nearly half of Iranian adults belong to high-risk metabolic clusters with co-occurring hypertension reinforces the need for comprehensive multi-target therapeutic strategies that address hypertension, diabetes, and dyslipidemia simultaneously, which is directly relevant to combination antihypertensive therapy positioning. From a Medical Affairs standpoint, these quantitative cluster data support building health economic models demonstrating the value of early antihypertensive intervention in preventing cluster progression toward the highest-risk hypertensive-diabetic phenotype. The national representativeness of this dataset makes it a strong evidence anchor for KOL-driven educational initiatives in Iran and the broader MENA region, where rapid urbanization is amplifying metabolic risk burden.

Type 2 Diabetes Mellitus and Chronic Venous Disease of the Lower Extremities: A Narrative Review.

Revue : Diabetes therapy : research, treatment and education of diabetes and related disorders | Tier : NA | IF estime : NA

Date de publication : 2026-08-10

Resultats principaux : This narrative review examined the epidemiological overlap, shared pathophysiological mechanisms, and management strategies for type 2 diabetes mellitus and chronic venous disease (CVD) of the lower extremities, integrating evidence from clinical studies, mechanistic research, and current guidelines. Epidemiological data showed that diabetes is present in 17.8% of CVD patients compared with 8.6% in the general population (p less than 0.0001), confirming a significantly higher CVD burden in diabetic patients and pointing to shared risk factors including obesity, sedentary lifestyle, and hypertension. Diabetes contributes to CVD progression through endothelial dysfunction, chronic inflammation, oxidative stress, and microangiopathy, resulting in structural changes such as valvular incompetence, venous reflux, and venous hypertension exceeding 60 mmHg at the capillary level in advanced disease stages. Patients with diabetes are disproportionately prone to advanced CVD manifestations including edema, skin changes, and venous ulceration, with impaired wound healing from microangiopathy and immune dysfunction increasing the risk of infection and ulcer chronicity requiring multidisciplinary management including glycemic control, compression therapy, and lifestyle modification. As a narrative review, this article is inherently susceptible to selection bias in literature inclusion, and the heterogeneity of CVD definitions and outcome measures across included studies limits direct comparisons and meta-analytic synthesis.

Implications Medical Affairs : While primarily focused on the diabetes-venous disease interface, this review highlights the compounding role of hypertension as a shared risk factor in the metabolic syndrome triad of obesity, diabetes, and hypertension, which co-drive venous hypertension and limb complications requiring integrated vascular management. For Medical Affairs, the overlapping clinical profiles of hypertensive, diabetic, and venous disease patients creates an opportunity to position antihypertensive agents with additional metabolic or vascular protective profiles within multidisciplinary care pathways involving endocrinologists and vascular surgeons. Targeted research into the specific contribution of hypertension to chronic venous disease progression would strengthen the evidence base and support KOL engagement in vascular medicine and endocrinology, particularly around cardiometabolic risk reduction in patients with multiple comorbidities.

Date de publication : 2026-08-10

Resultats principaux : This narrative review synthesized recent epidemiological evidence on the association between major dietary patterns — including the Mediterranean Diet, DASH, MIND, Alternative Healthy Eating Index, and plant-based diets — and age-related cognitive decline, with a specific focus on the dual cardiovascular and neurocognitive protective mechanisms of these patterns. The DASH diet, originally developed to lower blood pressure, emerged as having dual benefits by simultaneously reducing hypertension risk and preserving neurocognitive function across multiple cohort studies, supporting its role in integrated cardiovascular and brain health prevention. Key findings identified significant research gaps, including limited long-term longitudinal data, inadequate understanding of underlying molecular and neurophysiological mechanisms linking specific nutrients to brain structure and function, and insufficient culturally diverse studies that would allow cross-population generalization of these dietary effects. The review calls for well-powered longitudinal studies and culturally adaptable dietary interventions to establish definitive causal relationships, noting that current observational evidence is insufficient for causal conclusions. As a narrative review, this article is susceptible to selection bias and publication bias in literature inclusion, and heterogeneity of cognitive outcome measures across studies significantly limits direct comparison of effect sizes.

Coronary heart disease risk factors in taxi drivers in Medellin.

Revue : Occupational medicine (Oxford, England) | Tier : NA | IF estime : NA

Date de publication : 2026-08-10

Resultats principaux : This cross-sectional study recruited 484 taxi drivers citywide in Medellin, Colombia to characterize the prevalence of coronary heart disease risk factors in this high-risk occupational group exposed to prolonged sedentary work, extended shifts, and chronic occupational stress beyond traditional cardiovascular risk factors. Prevalences were high across multiple domains: sedentary lifestyle 71%, hypertension 30%, dyslipidemia 24%, hazardous alcohol use 25%, hazardous tobacco use 14%, and diabetes 10%, with 52% of participants carrying two or more simultaneous CHD risk factors. In adjusted Poisson regression models, significantly higher prevalence of multiple risk factors was observed in drivers with primary education (aPR=1.71; 95% CI 1.26-2.32), secondary education (aPR=1.43; 95% CI 1.06-1.93), more than 10 years of driving experience (aPR=1.39; 95% CI 1.09-1.75), and mixed day-night shift schedules (aPR=1.18; 95% CI 1.01-1.40) after adjustment for sociodemographic and occupational variables. These findings confirm the interacting roles of sedentary occupation, circadian disruption, low educational attainment, and prolonged occupational exposure in amplifying cardiovascular risk in this vulnerable occupational group in Latin America. The cross-sectional design limits causal inference, and self-reported diagnoses of hypertension, dyslipidemia, and diabetes likely underestimate true prevalence in a population with limited healthcare access and low rates of regular medical screening.

Implications Medical Affairs : This study illustrates the disproportionate cardiovascular burden in occupational groups combining high sedentary exposure with chronic psychosocial stress — two major modifiable hypertension risk factors — underscoring the importance of workplace health promotion as an underutilized prevention strategy in Latin American markets. From a Medical Affairs perspective, the 30% hypertension prevalence in this occupational cohort supports advocacy for occupational cardiovascular screening programs that could improve early diagnosis rates and treatment initiation in hard-to-reach working-age populations, an important segment for pharmacotherapy uptake. Engaging KOLs in occupational medicine and preventive cardiology around workplace-based hypertension interventions represents an underexplored avenue for awareness campaigns and health system partnerships, particularly in emerging markets in Latin America where cardiovascular disease burden is rising.

Revue : Vascular health and risk management | Tier : NA | IF estime : NA

Date de publication : 2026-08-05

Resultats principaux : This cross-sectional analytical study used data from the Jordan SMuRF-Less study to analyze 5,540 ASCVD patients (mean age 57.13 plus or minus 12.31 years), of whom 1,016 had available metabolic syndrome status assessed using a novel six-risk-factor definition encompassing hypertension, T2DM, dyslipidemia, obesity, smoking, and physical inactivity; 434 patients (42.7%) met MetS criteria. Patients with MetS were more likely to be in the 46-65 age group and showed higher prevalence of hypertension, hypertriglyceridemia, low HDL cholesterol, T2DM, obesity, and physical inactivity compared with ASCVD patients without MetS. Additional comorbidities concentrated in the MetS group included heart failure, chronic kidney disease, obstructive sleep apnea, lower educational attainment, higher smoking prevalence, and lack of health insurance, painting a picture of significant social vulnerability alongside metabolic risk. The study authors recommend establishing dedicated MetS clinics with early detection and comprehensive modifiable risk factor management as a priority strategy for ASCVD prevention in the MENA region. Key limitations include the cross-sectional design precluding causal inference, potential hospital-based selection bias, and the fact that MetS status was available for only 18% of the total cohort, raising questions about the representativeness of the analyzed subset.

Implications Medical Affairs : The 42.7% MetS prevalence with co-occurring hypertension in this Middle Eastern ASCVD cohort underlines a substantial unmet need for integrated cardiovascular risk management in the MENA region, representing a strategically important and growing market for antihypertensive therapies. From a Medical Affairs perspective, this data supports positioning antihypertensive agents as part of comprehensive metabolic management protocols in high-risk MENA populations, where risk factors cluster at high frequency and dedicated multidisciplinary management infrastructure remains limited. Reproductive factors and brain volume in community-dwelling individuals: the Ohasama Study.

Revue : Environmental health and preventive medicine | Tier : NA | IF estime : NA

Date de publication : 2026

Resultats principaux : This cross-sectional study enrolled 361 postmenopausal women aged 55 years and older from the Ohasama Study — a long-running Japanese community-based blood pressure cohort — examining associations between reproductive factors (age at menarche, age at menopause, estrogen exposure duration, parity) and MRI-assessed brain volumes using voxel-based morphometry from imaging acquired between 2009 and 2019. After adjustment for intracranial volume, age, birth cohort, and BMI, most reproductive-brain volume associations were substantially attenuated, with only earlier age at menopause showing a statistically significant association with smaller occipital lobe volume (beta = -0.52 cm3, 95% CI -1.01 to -0.03; p=0.04), representing a -1.08% change exceeding the pre-specified smallest effect size of interest threshold of plus or minus 0.64%. Earlier age at menarche showed a non-significant trend toward larger temporal lobe volume (beta=0.59 cm3, 95% CI -0.03 to 1.21; p=0.06), and no significant associations were found for estrogen exposure duration or parity, suggesting limited overall impact of reproductive history on regional brain volumes. The authors conclude that while statistically detectable, the observed effect sizes are small and comparable in magnitude to short-term age-related brain volume change, limiting their individual clinical significance. Key limitations include the cross-sectional design, relatively small sample (n=361) for MRI analysis, potential healthy survivor bias, and limited generalizability from a monoethnic elderly Japanese female cohort.

Implications Medical Affairs : While primarily a neuroimaging study, the Ohasama Study context — a blood pressure cohort — connects this research to questions about how blood pressure management across the reproductive lifespan may influence long-term brain outcomes, an area of increasing scientific and regulatory interest in hypertension guidelines specifically addressing women. For Medical Affairs, this study highlights the growing importance of sex-specific and reproductive-history-based approaches to cardiovascular and neurological risk stratification, which may support development of differentiated treatment narratives for antihypertensive agents with neuroprotective profiles in postmenopausal women. Investment in sex-disaggregated hypertension research and engagement with KOLs specializing in women's cardiovascular health, particularly in Japan and the Asia-Pacific region, could yield clinically relevant insights for guideline development and product positioning in a high-priority demographic segment.
