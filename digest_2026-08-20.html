Association of 10-year Cardiovascular Risk with Left Ventricular Hypertrophy in a Population of Nigerian Hypertensives.

Revue : The Nigerian Postgraduate Medical Journal | Tier : NA | IF estime : NA

Date de publication : 2026-08-17

Resultats principaux : This cross-sectional study analyzed 934 hypertensive patients from the Federal Medical Centre Abuja Hypertension Registry in Nigeria, stratified by 10-year ASCVD risk using the Pooled Cohort Equation (PCE): low risk (<7.5%) and elevated risk (≥7.5%). In the elevated ASCVD risk group, 42.1% had echocardiographic left ventricular hypertrophy (LVH) and 26.3% had ECG LVH, compared to 32.3% and 19.6%, respectively, in the low-risk group (p<0.05). Patients with elevated ASCVD risk had approximately twice the odds of both echocardiographic LVH (OR: 2.02, 95% CI 1.39–2.93) and ECG LVH (OR: 2.00, 95% CI 1.29–3.08) relative to those in the low-risk group. Participants in the elevated ASCVD group were significantly older (59.2±9.0 years vs. low risk group, p<0.001), confirming age as a confounding contributor to both risk and LVH. Limitations include the cross-sectional design precluding causal inference, potential selection bias from a single-center registry, and the use of the PCE, which was developed in Western populations and may not be optimally calibrated for Nigerian patients.

Implications Medical Affairs : The PCE-derived ASCVD risk score represents a low-cost, widely accessible tool with demonstrated clinical utility in Nigerian Black populations, offering actionable risk stratification for LVH detection in resource-limited settings where echocardiography may not be universally available. From a Medical Affairs standpoint, these data support advocacy for antihypertensive regimens capable of inducing LVH regression — particularly RAAS inhibitors — in high-cardiovascular-risk African patients, and can underpin medical education initiatives with cardiologists and hypertension specialists in Sub-Saharan Africa. These findings offer a platform for KOL engagement and disease burden communication in Nigeria and West Africa, where hypertension complications remain chronically underdiagnosed and undertreated.

Clinical Characteristics of Patients Undergoing Major Orthopaedic Surgery in a Real-World Clinical Practice: a Physician's Perspective.

Revue : Terapevticheskii arkhiv | Tier : NA | IF estime : NA

Date de publication : 2026-07-31

Resultats principaux : This real-world cross-sectional study conducted at Sechenov First Moscow State Medical University enrolled 1,733 patients (mean age 64.7±10.4 years, 64.4% female) scheduled for large joint arthroplasty of the lower extremities. A high comorbidity burden was documented: 77.6% had hypertension and other cardiovascular diseases, and 76.7% had chronic kidney disease stages II–V — all of whom also had obesity and hypertension. Glycemic disorders were present in 21.4% of patients, with inadequate glycemic control in 14 (3.8%) of this subgroup. Medication use was frequent perioperatively, with 15.1% on anticoagulants and 30.9% on antiplatelet agents, raising significant perioperative bleeding and cardiovascular risk management challenges. The study highlights a critical need for structured preoperative cardiovascular and metabolic assessment protocols in this high-risk surgical population, as current literature provides limited evidence-based guidance for perioperative management of patients with multiple cardiometabolic comorbidities.

Implications Medical Affairs : These real-world data underscore the extremely high cardiovascular comorbidity burden — particularly hypertension and CKD — in orthopedic surgery candidates, reinforcing the critical importance of pre-operative blood pressure optimization and RAAS inhibitor management decisions in the perioperative window. From a Medical Affairs perspective, this represents an opportunity to position antihypertensives with established renal-protective profiles as part of comprehensive perioperative risk reduction protocols, to be communicated with surgical, anesthesiology, and internal medicine KOLs. The authors' explicit call for large-scale prospective studies in this population opens potential for investigator-initiated research partnerships to strengthen evidence-based perioperative cardiovascular guidelines in Eastern Europe and beyond.

Aldosterone: Mechanisms of Normal and Impaired Regulation and the Possibility of Their Correction in Arterial Hypertension. Consensus of Russian Medical Society on Arterial Hypertension (RSH) Experts.

Revue : Terapevticheskii arkhiv | Tier : NA | IF estime : NA

Date de publication : 2026-07-31

Resultats principaux : This expert consensus document from the Russian Medical Society on Arterial Hypertension reviews the pathophysiological mechanisms underlying aldosterone dysregulation and mineralocorticoid receptor (MR) activation in arterial hypertension, presenting a comprehensive synthesis of recent research on this RAAS axis. The document evaluates the efficacy and safety of nonsteroidal MR antagonists (ns-MRAs), a novel class with a more selective receptor binding profile and reduced off-target hormonal side effects compared to steroidal predecessors (spironolactone, eplerenone), emphasizing their potential for preventing target organ damage in hypertension. A key focus is the emerging class of aldosterone synthase inhibitors (ASIs), which suppress both genomic and non-genomic effects of aldosterone by inhibiting its synthesis at the CYP11B2 enzyme level, addressing mechanistic pathways not covered by MR antagonists alone. The consensus specifically addresses the high-risk subgroup of patients with hypertension secondary to primary hyperaldosteronism, where the combination of ASIs and ns-MRAs may offer superior cardiovascular and renal risk reduction. Limitations of this consensus include reliance on expert opinion in areas where randomized clinical trial data remain limited, particularly for ASIs, and the Russian-language publication restricts immediate international dissemination.

Implications Medical Affairs : This consensus document positions nonsteroidal MR antagonists and aldosterone synthase inhibitors as pivotal additions to the antihypertensive armamentarium — particularly for resistant hypertension and primary hyperaldosteronism — two segments with high unmet medical need where treatment optimization represents a significant competitive opportunity. From a Medical Affairs standpoint, these developments represent a strategic inflection point to update medical education materials, congress symposia curricula, and KOL engagement strategies to reflect the mechanistic evolution of aldosterone-targeted therapy beyond traditional RAAS inhibitor classes. The growing evidence base for ASIs warrants close pipeline monitoring, with a strategic view toward positioning within hypertension treatment algorithms and upcoming European and international guideline revision cycles.

Effects of Acupuncture on Arterial Hypertension and Endothelial Dysfunction: The AHEaD Trial.

Revue : Arquivos Brasileiros de Cardiologia | Tier : NA | IF estime : NA

Date de publication : 2026-08-17

Resultats principaux : The AHEaD Trial was a prospective, single-blind, placebo-controlled, single-center randomized clinical trial conducted at the Federal University of Goias in Brazil, enrolling 30 adults with prehypertension or stage 1 hypertension who were not receiving antihypertensive therapy (acupuncture n=14; sham n=16). Participants received manual acupuncture or nonpenetrating sham acupuncture in two 30-minute sessions per week for 12 weeks, followed by a 30-day post-treatment observation visit. At 12 weeks, acupuncture significantly reduced peripheral systolic blood pressure by 13.2 mmHg (p=0.003) versus 4.1 mmHg in the sham group, producing a between-group difference of -9.1 mmHg (p=0.006) that persisted at 30 days (-8.4 mmHg, p=0.038). Flow-mediated dilation — a marker of endothelial function — improved by 2.7% in the acupuncture group versus 1.6% in sham (between-group difference +3.0%, p=0.037 at 12 weeks; +2.9%, p=0.028 at 30 days), indicating sustained endothelial benefit. Major limitations include the very small sample size (n=30), single-center Brazilian design limiting generalizability, absence of 24-hour ambulatory blood pressure monitoring as a primary endpoint, and insufficient statistical power to evaluate cardiovascular event outcomes.

Implications Medical Affairs : Although limited by small sample size, the AHEaD Trial provides preliminary randomized evidence that acupuncture may offer a clinically meaningful adjunctive strategy for blood pressure reduction and endothelial protection in early-stage hypertension, representing a potential non-pharmacological complement to lifestyle interventions prior to drug initiation. From a Medical Affairs perspective, this study highlights the landscape of non-pharmacological interventions in hypertension guidelines and reinforces the need for pharmacological treatment to be embedded within a comprehensive lifestyle and preventive medicine framework — an important narrative for patient education materials and value-demonstration programs. Future larger-scale RCTs on acupuncture or other integrative medicine approaches in hypertension offer an opportunity for joint research collaboration with leading hypertension centers, potentially informing future guideline amendments on non-pharmacological BP management strategies.

Prevalence and Factors Associated With Poor Glycaemic Control in Type 2 Diabetes Mellitus Patients: A Single-Centre, Hospital-Based Cross-Sectional Study in Jhenaidah District, Bangladesh.

Revue : Endocrinology, Diabetes & Metabolism | Tier : NA | IF estime : NA

Date de publication : 2026-09 (Epub ahead of print)

Resultats principaux : This hospital-based cross-sectional study enrolled 497 patients with type 2 diabetes mellitus attending an outpatient department in Jhenaidah, Bangladesh, from October 2023 to April 2024, using Modified Poisson regression with robust variance to identify factors associated with poor glycemic control. The prevalence of poor glycemic control (HbA1c above target) was 79.1% (95% CI: 75.3%–82.4%), reflecting an alarming unmet need in diabetes management in this low-resource setting. A high comorbidity burden was documented: 72.4% had at least one comorbidity — most frequently overweight/obesity (63.4%), with hypertension co-occurring as a major comorbidity — and 81.1% had at least one diabetes-related complication, most commonly neuropathy (59.6%). Longer diabetes duration (6–10 years) was significantly associated with poor control (aPR=1.13, 95% CI 1.01–1.27, p=0.037), and having multiple comorbidities (≥2) was associated with a 25% higher prevalence of poor glycemic control (aPR=1.25, 95% CI 1.09–1.43, p=0.002). Key limitations include the single-center, clinic-based design which limits population representativeness, and the cross-sectional methodology precluding causal inference between hypertension burden and glycemic outcomes.

Implications Medical Affairs : The high co-occurrence of hypertension and poor glycemic control in this Bangladeshi diabetic cohort — with nearly 4 in 5 patients failing glycemic targets — underlines the critical importance of integrated cardiometabolic risk management in South Asian populations, where both conditions frequently cluster and interact synergistically to accelerate vascular damage. From a Medical Affairs perspective, this evidence reinforces the need for combination cardiovascular risk reduction strategies — including antihypertensive therapy with proven cardiorenal benefits — in patients with T2DM and concurrent hypertension in low-to-middle income countries, supporting patient-centric care models. These findings support targeted educational programs for primary care providers in South Asia on early and aggressive hypertension management within the T2DM care pathway, a territory of strategic importance for KOL partnerships and medical education initiatives.

Patterns in Cardiovascular Risk Factors Stratified by Age and Sex in a Large German Population.

Revue : JACC Advances | Tier : NA | IF estime : NA

Date de publication : 2026-08-19

Resultats principaux : This large observational study analyzed 40,704 occupational health checkup records from German working adults (mean age 49 years, 25% women) provided by ias PREVENT, a corporate medical checkup provider, stratifying cardiovascular risk factors by sex and three age groups (<45, 45–55, and >55 years). Hypertension prevalence was markedly higher in men (30.7%) than women (18.4%), and elevated LDL was similarly sex-skewed (58.7% vs. 39.5%), reflecting known hormonal protective effects of estrogen in pre-menopausal women. A critical inflection was identified in the 45–55 age window, where smoking was more prevalent in women than men, and obesity prevalence began to exceed that of men post-age 50, coinciding with the menopausal transition. Diabetes prevalence was higher in women under 45 years, but reversed above 55 years when men showed greater burden, highlighting distinct age-sex interaction patterns in cardiometabolic risk trajectories. Limitations include the healthy worker bias inherent to an occupational cohort, cross-sectional design, potential self-reporting inaccuracies, and the predominantly German working-age population limiting ethnic and socioeconomic generalizability.

Implications Medical Affairs : The identification of the menopausal transition (45–55 years) as a key cardiovascular risk inflection point in women — evidenced by converging hypertension, obesity, and smoking risk — strengthens the scientific case for sex- and age-stratified hypertension screening and treatment guidelines, an area of growing regulatory and clinical interest globally. From a Medical Affairs perspective, these data provide compelling scientific ammunition for targeted educational campaigns around female cardiovascular health in the peri- and post-menopausal period, supporting the clinical value of antihypertensives with favorable metabolic profiles in this high-priority demographic. The occupational health setting represents a novel and underutilized channel for KOL partnerships with occupational medicine societies and employer wellness programs, expanding the medical affairs reach beyond traditional cardiology KOL networks.

Association of Air Pollution with Diabetic Microvascular Complications: A Prospective Cohort Study.

Revue : PLoS One | Tier : NA | IF estime : NA

Date de publication : 2026-08-19

Resultats principaux : This prospective cohort study leveraged data from 9,671 UK Biobank participants with diabetes, followed for a median of 12.4 years, to examine the association between long-term air pollution exposure and the development of diabetic microvascular complications. A total of 2,104 participants (21.8%) developed microvascular complications over follow-up, with Kaplan-Meier analysis demonstrating early divergence in cumulative incidence curves by pollution exposure tertile. After multivariable adjustment, higher NO2 exposure (highest vs. lowest quartile) was associated with a 27% increased risk of microvascular complications (HR=1.27, 95% CI 1.12–1.45), while PM10 exposure carried a 44% increased risk (HR=1.44, 95% CI 1.27–1.64). Stratified and sensitivity analyses confirmed the consistency of these associations, and restricted cubic spline analyses revealed potential nonlinear concentration-response relationships with risk elevation evident even at relatively low exposure levels. Limitations include potential residual confounding from unmeasured lifestyle factors, reliance on area-level rather than individual air pollution estimates, and the predominantly white UK Biobank population limiting ethnic generalizability including to hypertensive populations in non-Western countries.

Implications Medical Affairs : These findings provide large-scale prospective evidence linking chronic air pollution exposure to vascular complications in diabetic patients — a population highly enriched with hypertension — reinforcing environmental risk factors as modifiable determinants of cardiometabolic disease and underlining the insufficiency of pharmacological treatment alone in addressing total vascular risk burden. From a Medical Affairs standpoint, air pollution exposure may explain part of the residual cardiovascular and microvascular risk observed in treated hypertensive-diabetic patients, strengthening advocacy for comprehensive risk factor management including environmental health policy engagement as a complement to pharmacotherapy. These data may reinforce the scientific rationale for vascular-protective antihypertensive strategies in environmentally exposed populations and support KOL education initiatives around cardiovascular-renal-metabolic risk and environmental determinants of disease.

Prevalence of Cognitive Impairment Among Older Adult Palestinians in Community and Nursing Home Settings.

Revue : PLoS One | Tier : NA | IF estime : NA

Date de publication : 2026-08-19

Resultats principaux : This cross-sectional study enrolled 441 Palestinian adults aged 55 years and older from 10 nursing homes and primary healthcare centers across the West Bank from May to October 2024, assessing cognitive function using the validated Arabic A-MMSE (GTD-USJ) scale. In the primary healthcare cohort (n=305, median age 65 years), hypertension was the most common condition at 67.9%, followed by dyslipidemia (59%) and diabetes (50.2%), documenting a very high hypertension prevalence in this Middle Eastern population. In the nursing home cohort (n=136, median age 73 years), hypertension was similarly the leading illness at 47.8%, co-occurring with obesity and diabetes. Cognitive impairment was significantly associated with gender, smoking status, educational attainment, and monolingualism in both care settings (all p<0.05), confirming well-established modifiable and non-modifiable determinants of cognitive decline. This study provides the first baseline prevalence data on cognitive impairment in both community and institutional care settings in Palestine, though cross-sectional design and convenience sampling from specific institutions limit representativeness and causal inference.

Implications Medical Affairs : The very high prevalence of hypertension in this Middle Eastern population — exceeding 67% in primary care — confirms hypertension as a leading modifiable vascular risk factor for cognitive impairment in this region, reinforcing the scientific narrative linking blood pressure control to long-term brain health and dementia prevention. From a Medical Affairs perspective, these findings underscore the dual public health burden of hypertension and cognitive impairment in aging populations, providing scientific rationale for positioning antihypertensive regimens with demonstrated neuroprotective or cognitively neutral profiles as part of holistic prevention strategies for older patients. KOL engagement strategies in the Eastern Mediterranean and Middle Eastern region can leverage these prevalence data to advocate for integrated cardiometabolic-cognitive health screening programs and population-specific treatment guideline development.

Cardiovascular disease — principally ischemic heart disease — remains the leading cause of death among women in LAC, yet has been systematically overshadowed by cancer prevention in regional women's health programs, creating a critical gap in sex-specific cardiovascular care. In 2021, COVID-19 temporarily surpassed ischemic heart disease as the leading cause of death in the Americas (176.7 per 100,000), highlighting the multiplicative vulnerability of women with underlying cardiometabolic risk — including uncontrolled hypertension.

Proteomic Signature of Healthy Dietary Patterns and Risk of Cardiovascular Disease in the Framingham Heart Study.

Revue : Clinical Nutrition | Tier : NA | IF estime : NA

Date de publication : 2026-08-13

Resultats principaux : This prospective study examined data from the multi-generational Framingham Heart Study — Generation 2 (n=1,611, mean age 55±10 years, 55.4% women) and Generation 3 (n=862, mean age 46±8 years, 56.6% women) — using DNA aptamer-based plasma proteomic profiling and elastic net regression to identify an 11-protein dietary proteomic score (DPS). The DPS showed its strongest correlation with the DASH (Dietary Approaches to Stop Hypertension) diet score (r=0.30, p<0.0001), underscoring the mechanistic alignment between a proteomic health signature and an evidence-based anti-hypertensive dietary pattern. Over medians of 25 and 13 follow-up years respectively, 533 CVD events occurred; a 1-SD increase in DPS was prospectively associated with a 20% lower CVD risk (HR=0.80, 95% CI 0.73–0.88), and highest versus lowest DPS tertile had a 38% reduced risk (HR=0.62, 95% CI 0.49–0.78). After further adjustment for established cardiometabolic risk factors including hypertension, these associations were substantially attenuated (HR=0.94, 95% CI 0.85–1.04), suggesting that traditional risk pathways — including blood pressure — mediate much of the diet-CVD association. Limitations include a predominantly white community-based population limiting ethnic generalizability, reliance on food frequency questionnaires subject to reporting bias, and the observational design precluding causal inference.

Implications Medical Affairs : The strong mechanistic link between the DASH diet proteomic signature and reduced cardiovascular risk provides molecular-level support for dietary interventions as a complement to antihypertensive pharmacotherapy, reinforcing the scientific basis for lifestyle modification recommendations in comprehensive hypertension management programs. From a Medical Affairs perspective, these proteomic findings may open avenues for precision nutrition and precision prevention strategies in high-risk hypertensive populations, with potential applications in personalized cardiovascular risk reduction programs that pharmaceutical companies can support as value-beyond-the-pill initiatives. KOL engagement with nutrition scientists, cardiologists, and biomarker researchers around proteomic dietary quality indices can enhance scientific credibility and support investigator-initiated studies linking DASH diet adherence, blood pressure control, and cardiovascular outcomes in hypertensive populations.

The Lancet Commission Obesity Categories, Excess Adiposity, and BMI Discordance in Urban Chinese Adults: A Cross-Sectional Survey in Beijing.

Revue : Frontiers in Nutrition | Tier : NA | IF estime : NA

Date de publication : 2026-08-04

Resultats principaux : This cross-sectional study analyzed 8,130 adults aged 18–79 years from the 2024 Haidian Chronic Disease and Risk Factor Surveillance, a stratified multistage probability cluster survey in urban Beijing, applying the Lancet Commission obesity framework using BMI, waist circumference, and waist-to-height ratio with China-specific anthropometric cut points. The weighted prevalence of excess adiposity was 41.2%, with 37.3% classified as clinical obesity and 3.9% as preclinical obesity; hypertension and metabolic impairment were the most frequent obesity-related complications among participants with clinical obesity. Substantial discordance between BMI and Lancet obesity categories was observed: 41.6% of individuals with BMI 24.0–27.9 kg/m2 had clinical obesity, while 5.3% with BMI <24 kg/m2 also met clinical obesity criteria, demonstrating the critical inadequacy of BMI alone for hypertension risk stratification in Chinese adults. Only 3.4% of participants with BMI ≥28 kg/m2 had no obesity under the Lancet framework, confirming that the highest BMI stratum reliably identifies clinical obesity in this population. Female sex was associated with significantly lower odds of being in a higher Lancet obesity category (OR=0.46, 95% CI 0.40–0.53), highlighting sex-specific adiposity distribution patterns that may differentially affect hypertension risk.

Implications Medical Affairs : The high prevalence of clinical obesity with concurrent hypertension in urban Beijing — combined with the demonstrated limitations of BMI as a sole screening tool — provides strong evidence for adopting complementary anthropometric assessment (waist circumference, waist-to-height ratio) in hypertension risk stratification algorithms for Chinese and Asian populations, where standard WHO BMI thresholds may underestimate cardiometabolic risk. From a Medical Affairs perspective, these findings support engagement with Chinese health authorities and cardiologists to advocate for updated obesity-hypertension risk stratification tools in Chinese clinical guidelines, and underpin the positioning of antihypertensive therapies with favorable or weight-neutral metabolic profiles in the growing obese hypertensive population of urban China. KOL engagement with Chinese cardiologists, endocrinologists, and public health leaders can leverage these surveillance data to advance comprehensive cardiometabolic management strategies — integrating blood pressure control with obesity management — as the evolving standard of care in urban China.
