Safety and Efficacy of Remimazolam Combined with Esketamine for Anesthesia in Elderly Patients Undergoing ERCP: A Randomized Controlled Trial.

Revue : Drug Design, Development and Therapy | Tier : NA | IF estime : NA

Date de publication : 2026-08-25

Resultats principaux : This single-center, prospective, randomized parallel-group superiority trial enrolled 160 elderly patients (aged 60-80 years, ASA physical status I-III) scheduled for elective endoscopic retrograde cholangiopancreatography (ERCP) at a Chinese hospital, comparing remimazolam-esketamine (RE, n=80) against propofol-fentanyl (PF, n=80) under identical standardized nasopharyngeal airway-assisted respiratory management. The primary endpoint was a composite of sedation-related adverse events (SRAEs) comprising hypotension, bradycardia, hypoxemia, or respiratory depression from anesthesia induction to endoscope withdrawal, analyzed by intention to treat. The RE regimen was associated with significantly fewer composite SRAEs compared to PF: 17.5% vs. 35.0% (RR 0.50; 95% CI 0.29-0.88; risk difference -17.5 percentage points; P=0.016), corroborated by adjusted analysis (RR 0.49; 95% CI 0.31-0.78; P=0.003). Anesthetic success was similar between groups (97.5% vs. 98.8%), induction time was modestly longer with RE, and recovery metrics (eye-opening, modified Aldrete score time) were slightly shorter with RE; notably, tachycardia, hypertension, and psychotomimetic symptoms were numerically more frequent in the RE group, though event counts were small and estimates imprecise. The primary limitation is the single-center Chinese design with a selected elderly population, precluding generalizability to broader or more diverse clinical settings, and multicenter studies are explicitly recommended by the authors.

Implications Medical Affairs : This RCT provides clinically meaningful evidence that remimazolam-esketamine may reduce hemodynamic and respiratory adverse events compared to propofol-fentanyl in elderly procedural patients, an important finding for anesthesia guidelines applicable to hypertensive elderly individuals undergoing endoscopic procedures. The observed numerical increase in hypertensive side effects within the RE group warrants careful consideration for patients with pre-existing arterial hypertension, potentially informing prescribing guidance and label updates in this vulnerable cardiovascular population. Medical Affairs teams should monitor upcoming multicenter trials to confirm generalizability of these findings and leverage this evidence for KOL engagement in the fields of procedural sedation and geriatric cardiovascular medicine.

Population reach and participation rate in opportunistic vs. systematic atrial fibrillation screening: STROKESTOP III.

Revue : Europace | Tier : NA | IF estime : NA

Date de publication : 2026-08-04

Resultats principaux : STROKESTOP III is a cluster-randomized trial conducted across 16 primary care centers in Region Varmland, Sweden, enrolling individuals aged 75-76 years to compare opportunistic AF screening (invitations during routine healthcare visits) versus systematic AF screening (mailed invitations), with a primary focus on participation rates and population reach. In the systematic arm, 1,312 eligible individuals were invited and 607 participated (46.3%), while in the opportunistic arm, 749 of 1,390 potentially eligible individuals were assessed for eligibility, with 374 of 609 assessed-eligible individuals ultimately participating (61.4% among those invited; P<0.005 vs. systematic). However, overall population reach was significantly lower in the opportunistic arm (374/1,479: 25.3% vs. 607/1,437: 42.2%; P<0.005) due to incomplete eligibility assessment during visits. Participants in the opportunistic arm had a higher cardiovascular risk burden, including significantly more prevalent hypertension, diabetes, and a higher mean CHA2DS2-VASc score (3.99 vs. 3.60; P<0.005), and exclusion rates were also higher (18.7% vs. 8.7%; P<0.005) mainly due to previously diagnosed AF and cognitive impairment. The single-region Swedish design and inherent variability in clinical workflow standardization limit the generalizability of these findings to other healthcare systems and geographic settings.

Implications Medical Affairs : This trial demonstrates that opportunistic AF screening captures a higher-risk cardiovascular population with more prevalent hypertension, suggesting that integrating AF detection into routine hypertension management visits may identify undertreated patients who would benefit from anticoagulation, with direct implications for hypertension-focused guidelines to include AF screening recommendations. From a Medical Affairs perspective, this evidence supports advocacy for hybrid screening strategies that combine opportunistic and systematic approaches, creating an opportunity for companies with cardiovascular portfolios to engage guideline committees and KOLs around optimizing detection pathways for the high-risk hypertension-AF overlap population. The finding that population reach was substantially lower in purely opportunistic strategies provides an actionable insight for designing real-world evidence studies and health system partnerships that combine routine hypertension visit data with systematic population outreach to maximize AF detection.

Beyond Proxies: Environment, Neighborhood, and Racial Disparities in Hypertensive Disorders of Pregnancy.

Revue : AJPM Focus | Tier : NA | IF estime : NA

Date de publication : 2026-03-23

Resultats principaux : This retrospective cohort study enrolled 619 pregnant patients receiving care within a Federally Qualified Health Center network in the United States, examining associations between address-linked environmental and neighborhood-level exposures and hypertensive disorders of pregnancy (HDP), defined as gestational hypertension or preeclampsia diagnosed during pregnancy, intrapartum, or postpartum. Among the cohort, 114 patients (18.4%) developed HDP, and multivariate logistic regression demonstrated that high nitrogen dioxide (NO2) pollution (>=5 ppb) conferred the strongest odds of HDP (OR=3.73; 95% CI 2.43-5.79; P<0.001), followed by high neighborhood poverty (>30% of residents below the federal poverty level; OR=2.21; 95% CI 1.45-3.43; P<0.001), with high NO2 exposure retaining significance after adjustment for aspirin prescription (OR=2.88; 95% CI 1.81-4.61; P<0.001). Race-stratified subgroup analyses showed that residence in majority White ZIP codes was independently protective for both Black patients (OR=0.41; 95% CI 0.20-0.81; P=0.012) and non-Black patients (OR=0.37; 95% CI 0.22-0.64; P<0.001), suggesting a neighborhood-level structural effect on HDP risk that operates across racial groups and beyond individual race-based risk factors. Notable limitations include the retrospective single-network design from one urban U.S. health system, reliance on administrative data with potential misclassification, the absence of longitudinal residential exposure data, and possible residual confounding from unmeasured comorbidities and healthcare utilization patterns.

Implications Medical Affairs : This study provides robust evidence that environmental exposures - particularly air pollution - and neighborhood poverty are stronger and more clinically precise predictors of hypertensive disorders of pregnancy than race- or income-based proxy variables, with direct implications for refining prenatal hypertension risk stratification tools in clinical guidelines and aspirin prophylaxis protocols. From a Medical Affairs perspective, these findings represent a significant opportunity to engage maternal-fetal medicine and public health KOLs in advocating for address-linked environmental data integration into hypertension prevention guidelines for pregnant women, positioning pharmaceutical companies with maternal cardiovascular portfolios as partners in evidence-based health equity initiatives. An actionable insight for future research strategy is to design prospective studies using geospatial risk models to identify high-risk pregnant patients in underserved communities, supporting both preventive care access programs and outcomes research that could strengthen product positioning in the hypertensive disorders of pregnancy therapeutic area.
