Systemic Comorbidities in Central Centrifugal Cicatricial Alopecia: A Systematic Review and Meta-analysis

Revue : Acta dermato-venereologica | Tier : NA | IF estime : NA

Date de publication : 2026-08-04

Resultats principaux : This PRISMA 2020-compliant systematic review and meta-analysis, prospectively registered in PROSPERO (CRD420250656067), examined systemic comorbidities in central centrifugal cicatricial alopecia (CCCA) — the most common scarring alopecia in women of African descent — across 23 observational studies enrolling 9,103 patients. The primary comorbidities reported included hypertension (pooled prevalence 51.9%, 95% CI 33.8–69.6%), dyslipidemia (58.4%, 95% CI 29.0–82.8%), obesity (37.1%, 95% CI 10.8–74.2%), and uterine leiomyomas (28.0%, 95% CI 8.0–64.0%), all with substantial heterogeneity. In case-control analyses, CCCA showed statistically significant associations with dyslipidemia (OR 4.46, 95% CI 1.09–18.25), uterine leiomyomas (OR 2.25, 95% CI 1.10–4.58), and anxiety disorders (OR 3.27, 95% CI 1.22–8.79), while hypertension, obesity, and thyroid disease did not achieve consistent statistical significance. The predominantly retrospective design of included studies and their largely U.S.-based sampling constitute key limitations, contributing to methodological heterogeneity and restricting generalizability to other populations. These findings nonetheless support a holistic, individualized clinical assessment for CCCA patients given the cardiometabolic and fibroproliferative burden documented.

Implications Medical Affairs : This meta-analysis reveals a substantial cardiometabolic comorbidity burden — including a hypertension prevalence exceeding 50% — in CCCA patients, a predominantly Black female population historically underrepresented in cardiovascular research, underscoring the need for integrated management pathways that embed cardiovascular risk screening into dermatology practice. From a Medical Affairs perspective, this evidence supports cross-specialty engagement between dermatology and cardiology KOLs to develop joint clinical pathways for earlier hypertension detection and management in this underserved population. As a strategic insight, pharmaceutical companies with antihypertensive portfolios should consider CCCA patient registries and dermatology partnerships as emerging channels for hypertension diagnosis and treatment initiation in women of African descent.

Intermittent exogenous ketosis increases erythropoietin in healthy adults: A randomized, placebo-controlled study with preliminary sex-specific observations

Revue : Physiological reports | Tier : NA | IF estime : NA

Date de publication : 2026-08

Resultats principaux : This randomized, placebo-controlled study investigated the effects of oral beta-hydroxybutyrate ketone monoester (KE) on erythropoietin (EPO), erythropoiesis, and iron metabolism in 16 healthy adults across two phases: an acute single-dose assessment and a 2-week twice-daily KE versus placebo intervention. The primary finding was that intermittent exogenous ketosis was associated with approximately 30% higher EPO levels after 2 weeks compared to placebo (95% CI 6–60%, p=0.01), with a pronounced sex-specific effect: females in the KE group had EPO levels of 9.7 IU/L (95% CI 6.0–13.5) versus 6.4 IU/L with placebo (p=0.008), while males showed no statistically significant difference (10.7 vs. 9.6 IU/L, p=0.46). Ferritin was significantly elevated in females receiving KE compared to placebo (42 vs. 16 µg/L, p<0.001), suggesting a sex-differential effect on iron handling and early erythropoietic stimulation. Hemoglobin and red blood cell count were unaffected at 2 weeks, indicating the effect had not yet translated into measurable changes in red cell mass within the study timeframe. Key limitations include the small sample size (n=16), the short 2-week intervention duration, recruitment of exclusively healthy adults without hypertensive or renal comorbidities, and preliminary sex-specific analyses that require confirmation in larger, adequately powered studies.

Implications Medical Affairs : Although this study does not directly address hypertension management, the sex-specific EPO response to exogenous ketosis has emerging relevance for understanding cardiorenal hematopoietic mechanisms in women, a group with distinct pathophysiology in hypertension-associated chronic kidney disease and anemia. From a Medical Affairs standpoint, this work adds to the evidence on metabolic modulators and their downstream hematopoietic effects, and may inform exploratory research pathways in hypertensive patients with renal impairment where EPO dysregulation and anemia are prevalent. Engagement with nephrology and hematology KOLs could open collaborative research opportunities investigating ketone-based metabolic interventions as potential adjunctive strategies in hypertensive chronic kidney disease patients with renal anemia.

Effects of HIIT and MICT on cardiovascular function in essential hypertension: roles of inflammation, oxidative stress, and RAS axis

Revue : Frontiers in cardiovascular medicine | Tier : NA | IF estime : NA

Date de publication : 2026-07-20

Resultats principaux : This 8-week randomized clinical trial enrolled 56 patients with essential hypertension, allocating them 1:1 to High Intensity Interval Training (HIIT; 1x10 min with 2-min rest intervals) or Moderate Intensity Continuous Training (MICT; 35 min), with 54 patients (96.4%) completing the full protocol. The primary endpoint — change in flow-mediated dilation (FMD), a marker of endothelial function — showed significantly greater improvement with HIIT versus MICT, with a mean difference of 0.85% (95% CI 0.50–1.21). HIIT demonstrated superior effects over MICT on multiple secondary endpoints: nitric oxide, endothelin-1, brachial-ankle pulse wave velocity, peak systolic velocity, corrected QT interval, E/e' ratio (diastolic function), angiotensin-(1-7), and C-reactive protein (all p<0.05). Both training modalities produced equivalent and statistically significant reductions in systolic blood pressure, pulsatility index, resistive index, angiotensin II, and norepinephrine (all p<0.05, no between-group difference). Key limitations include the relatively small sample size of 54 completers, the 8-week duration that may not capture long-term cardiovascular adaptations, the absence of a sedentary control group, and no standardized concurrent antihypertensive medication protocol reported.

Implications Medical Affairs : This trial provides both mechanistic and clinical evidence that HIIT is superior to MICT for improving endothelial function, arterial stiffness, cardiac diastolic performance, and inflammatory markers in essential hypertension through RAS, oxidative stress, and neurohumoral pathways, with direct implications for exercise prescription guidelines in hypertensive patients. From a Medical Affairs perspective, these findings strengthen the scientific basis for positioning structured exercise — particularly HIIT — as a guideline-recommended adjunct to first-line antihypertensive pharmacotherapy, offering a compelling narrative for combination non-pharmacological and pharmacological treatment strategies. Collaborative investigator-initiated trials pairing HIIT with RAAS inhibitors or other antihypertensive drug classes could generate differentiated clinical data supporting product label enhancements and KOL-driven medical education on holistic hypertension management.

Influence of nutritional and behavioral risk factors on blood pressure profiles of the selected marine fishers

Revue : International maritime health | Tier : NA | IF estime : NA

Date de publication : 2026-08-04

Resultats principaux : This cross-sectional study assessed nutritional and behavioral determinants of blood pressure profiles in 550 marine fishers from Ramanathapuram District, Tamil Nadu, India — a high-risk occupational group exposed to harsh environmental conditions that elevate noncommunicable disease risk. Hypertension prevalence was highest in the 46–55 age bracket, and significant associations with weak positive correlations were identified between hypertension and anthropometric indices: BMI (mean 24.74 ± 5.85 kg/m2), waist circumference (86.64 ± 8.01 cm), WHR, and WHtR. Behavioral risk factors including smoking and alcohol consumption were associated with pre-hypertension and hypertension at the 10% significance level. Multiple regression analysis identified BMI (B=0.474 for SBP/0.438 for DBP), WHtR (B=68.414/45.496), meal-skipping (B=7.142/3.759), type of cooking oil used (B=1.610/1.626), and street food consumption (B=5.611/1.348) as significant positive predictors of both systolic and diastolic blood pressure. Key limitations include the cross-sectional design precluding causal inference, single-district geographic sampling limiting generalizability to other South Asian fishing communities, and reliance on self-reported dietary and behavioral data prone to recall bias.

Implications Medical Affairs : This study strengthens the evidence that modifiable dietary and behavioral risk factors — including adiposity, meal patterns, cooking oil type, and street food habits — are significant predictors of elevated blood pressure in an occupationally exposed South Asian male population, highlighting priority targets for hypertension prevention interventions beyond standard pharmacotherapy. From a Medical Affairs standpoint, real-world evidence strategies should expand to capture hypertension risk in occupational and migrant cohorts in emerging markets such as India, representing an underserved and growing patient segment for cardiovascular prevention and treatment programs. KOL engagement in preventive cardiology and occupational medicine across South and Southeast Asia could leverage these findings to advocate for employer-based hypertension screening programs and culturally adapted dietary modification tools to complement antihypertensive therapy.

Community-driven approach to promoting healthy culturally tailored diets and cardiometabolic health among East African immigrants in San Diego: a community case study

Revue : Frontiers in public health | Tier : NA | IF estime : NA

Date de publication : 2026-07-20

Resultats principaux : This community case study describes the HAWAASH (Healthy Eating with Spices and Herbs to Manage Hypertension) community-academic research partnership between United Women of East Africa Support Team (UWEAST) and UC San Diego, employing a Community Based Participatory Research (CBPR) framework to design culturally tailored nutrition interventions targeting cardiometabolic disease prevention among East African immigrants in San Diego. A formative mixed-methods study using convenience sampling engaged 60 community members and 7 key informants, with grounded theory-guided thematic analysis identifying five key themes: cultural food identity, structural barriers to healthy eating, dietary acculturation, community social assets, and readiness for nutrition programming. Background epidemiology from the U.S. Midwest Somali immigrant community documented high rates of diabetes (4.5–21.1%), overweight/obesity, and dyslipidemia, with dietary acculturation in the first decade of migration linked to decreased traditional food intake and increased sodium/sugar exposure — recognized risk factors for hypertension. The study did not report quantitative blood pressure or hypertension endpoints, as it represents a formative design phase preceding a full randomized nutrition intervention; findings are reported as preliminary in companion manuscripts. Key limitations include convenience sampling, qualitative and formative methodology precluding clinical endpoint measurement, and single-site enrollment limiting cultural and geographic generalizability.

Implications Medical Affairs : This study demonstrates the critical importance of culturally responsive, community-driven research design in developing effective hypertension prevention strategies for immigrant populations, who face unique acculturation-related dietary shifts and structural barriers that increase their cardiometabolic risk and reduce guideline-adherent care access. For Medical Affairs, this evidence highlights a significant equity gap in hypertension management among minority immigrant communities and presents a strategic opportunity for pharmaceutical companies to co-invest in community-based research partnerships as part of broader health equity and patient advocacy commitments. KOL engagement with researchers and community health leaders working in underserved urban immigrant populations could support the development of culturally adapted adherence tools and disease awareness programs for antihypertensive therapies, strengthening both reach and real-world outcomes in this growing demographic.

Impact of Dietary Risks on Noncommunicable Disease Mortality between 2000 and 2021: Which Noncommunicable Disease Is Most Affected by Dietary Risks?

Revue : Iranian journal of public health | Tier : NA | IF estime : NA

Date de publication : 2025-12

Resultats principaux : This descriptive ecological study analyzed trends in the population attributable fraction (PAF) of dietary risk-attributed mortality across six noncommunicable diseases (NCDs) over a 22-year period (2000–2021) using age-standardized and sex-specific data from the Global Burden of Disease Study 2021, with temporal trend assessment by joinpoint regression analysis. Hypertensive heart disease emerged as the NCD with the highest mean PAF attributable to dietary risks (PAF 0.63 ± 0.01), followed by ischemic heart disease (PAF 0.45 ± 0.01), underscoring the disproportionate dietary contribution to hypertension-related cardiac mortality at the global level. Sex-specific differences in PAF were statistically significant for four of the six diseases studied, and dietary risk-attributable mortality was documented as initiating at young ages across all six NCDs. Over the 22-year observation period, a statistically significant decreasing trend in the PAF for dietary risks was observed across all six diseases, with the majority of specific dietary risk factors also showing a declining trend. Key limitations include the ecological design precluding individual-level causal inference, reliance on GBD 2021 data subject to modeling assumptions and variable data quality across countries, and the aggregation of diverse dietary risks that may obscure specific high-impact nutritional determinants for targeted intervention.

Implications Medical Affairs : The finding that dietary risks account for the largest attributable fraction of mortality specifically in hypertensive heart disease — exceeding that for ischemic heart disease — constitutes a compelling global epidemiological argument for prioritizing dietary intervention as a primary prevention strategy within hypertension management frameworks, with potential implications for guideline-level emphasis on non-pharmacological approaches. From a Medical Affairs perspective, these GBD-derived data provide a powerful evidence base for patient advocacy messaging and payer education highlighting the modifiable lifestyle burden in hypertension-related mortality, helping contextualize antihypertensive drug therapy within a broader preventive care framework and supporting value-based care narratives. KOL engagement with cardiovascular epidemiologists, global health researchers, and guideline committee members could leverage this mortality-burden evidence to reinforce the scientific narrative for dietary co-management as a standard complement to antihypertensive pharmacotherapy in next-generation international guidelines.
