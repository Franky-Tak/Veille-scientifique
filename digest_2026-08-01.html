Anti-Müllerian Hormone and Metabolic-Hormonal Profiles in Women With and Without Polycystic Ovary Syndrome: A Population-Based Study.

Revue : Endocrinology, Diabetes & Metabolism | Tier : NA | IF estime : NA

Date de publication : Sep 2026

Resultats principaux : This cross-sectional, population-based study enrolled 883 women aged 18–45 years in Iran, categorized into PCOS (n=124, Rotterdam criteria), suspicious (n=274, one PCOS criterion), and healthy controls (n=485). The study assessed anti-Müllerian hormone (AMH) associations with metabolic outcomes including hypertension, type 2 diabetes, metabolic syndrome, and dyslipidemia using age-stratified multivariable logistic regression adjusted for BMI, parity, and physical activity. Age explained approximately 40% of AMH variation in healthy women but only 20% in women with PCOS, demonstrating an altered age-related hormonal decline pattern. Among healthy women aged 35 years or older, higher AMH was associated with a 37% reduced likelihood of type 2 diabetes (OR: 0.63, 95% CI: 0.41–0.97), while younger PCOS women with elevated AMH showed a 2.6-fold higher odds of metabolic syndrome (OR: 2.6, 95% CI: 1.20–6.20). Key limitations include the cross-sectional design preventing causal inference, single-country recruitment limiting external validity, and lack of hormonal cycle standardization at sample collection.

Implications Medical Affairs : These findings suggest that AMH may serve as a sex-specific biomarker for metabolic risk stratification with relevance to early hypertension prevention strategies in reproductive-age women, a population increasingly recognized as under-screened for cardiovascular risk factors. From a Medical Affairs perspective, this evidence opens a potential niche for antihypertensive product positioning in female-specific metabolic risk profiles, where endocrine dysregulation precedes overt hypertension. Prioritizing KOL engagement with endocrinologists and gynecologists could accelerate interest in AMH-guided cardiovascular risk screening pathways in clinical practice.

The Serum Testosterone Level in Male Patients Is Inversely Associated With Hypertension Incidence.

Revue : Journal of Clinical Hypertension (Greenwich) | Tier : 3 | IF estime : 3–10

Date de publication : Aug 2026

Resultats principaux : This observational study consecutively analyzed 3,401 male patients aged 40 years or older from the Cardiovascular Medicine Center of Zhejiang University's First Affiliated Hospital over a four-year period. The primary endpoints were the association between serum testosterone levels and hypertension across age strata (40–60, 60–79, and 80+ years), assessed using linear regression, generalized additive models, multivariate regression, and ROC curve analysis. Multivariate regression demonstrated a statistically significant inverse association between serum testosterone and hypertension in men aged 40 to less than 80 years, with hypertensive patients showing significantly lower testosterone than normotensive counterparts. ROC curve analysis revealed testosterone had modest predictive value for hypertension in men aged 40–60 years, and when combined with age, smoking status, and BMI, the predictive accuracy improved for the 60–79 age group. A principal limitation is the observational single-center design, which prevents causal conclusions about the testosterone-hypertension relationship and limits generalizability beyond this Chinese cardiovascular clinic population.

Implications Medical Affairs : This study adds epidemiological evidence to the growing literature on androgen-cardiovascular interactions, potentially influencing clinical discussions around testosterone replacement therapy risks and monitoring requirements in hypertensive men. Medical Affairs teams should monitor whether this and related data translate into updated guidelines on endocrine evaluation in hypertensive male patients, creating educational opportunities around combined biomarker-based risk stratification. Engaging KOLs specializing in endocrinology and cardiovascular medicine would support development of multi-disciplinary hypertension management programs that address hormonal contributors to blood pressure dysregulation.

Wearable Devices for Monitoring and Management of Comorbid Obstructive Sleep Apnea and Hypertension: Scoping Review.

Revue : JMIR mHealth and uHealth | Tier : NA | IF estime : NA

Date de publication : Jul 31, 2026

Resultats principaux : This scoping review systematically identified 13 eligible studies from PubMed, Web of Science, Embase, and IEEE Xplore published between January 2015 and February 2026, covering 5,596 participants across 9 countries with comorbid obstructive sleep apnea (OSA) and hypertension. Device types evaluated included wrist-worn devices, fingertip contact devices, patch and single-lead devices, and multiparameter portable monitoring systems for simultaneous assessment of respiratory and blood pressure metrics. Clinical applications centered on three domains: screening and risk stratification for comorbid OSA-hypertension, monitoring of abnormal nocturnal blood pressure and hemodynamic changes, and longitudinal cardiovascular risk management. Despite the theoretical appeal of wearable technology for continuous dual monitoring, the review identified major heterogeneity in reference standards, diagnostic thresholds, and validation pathways across devices, severely limiting clinical translation. Primary limitations include the sparse evidence base with mostly observational and device validation studies, single-disease framework designs, and absence of large-scale prospective multicenter validation data in comorbid populations.

Implications Medical Affairs : This review reveals the unmet need for evidence-based digital health tools in managing the clinically significant OSA-hypertension comorbidity, where blood pressure control remains challenging due to nocturnal hemodynamic instability. Medical Affairs should monitor the evolving digital biomarker landscape as wearable devices gain regulatory traction, positioning the company for potential partnerships where companion monitoring tools could enhance antihypertensive therapy adherence and outcome documentation. Cross-disciplinary KOL engagement in sleep medicine and cardiology could support investigator-initiated studies comparing RPM-integrated hypertension management strategies in OSA patients, a population with notoriously poor blood pressure control.

The Prognostic Significance of Stress Hyperglycemic Ratio in Critically Ill Patients With Hypertension: A Study Using the MIMIC-IV Database.

Revue : PLoS ONE | Tier : NA | IF estime : NA

Date de publication : Jul 31, 2026

Resultats principaux : This retrospective database study extracted data on 2,140 critically ill patients with essential hypertension from the MIMIC-IV database, stratified into quartiles based on the Stress Hyperglycemia Ratio (SHR), a composite marker accounting for baseline glycemic status to reflect true acute hyperglycemia. Kaplan-Meier survival analysis demonstrated that elevated SHR was significantly associated with increased all-cause mortality across all follow-up periods (30, 90, 180, and 365 days; log-rank p<0.05). Multivariate Cox proportional hazards regression confirmed SHR as an independent predictor of mortality (p<0.001), while restricted cubic spline analysis revealed a nonlinear, inverse U-shaped association between SHR and all-cause mortality. Subgroup analyses demonstrated statistically significant differential mortality associations across gender, age, and comorbid conditions including diabetes, myocardial infarction, congestive heart failure, cerebrovascular disease, and paraplegia. Key limitations include the retrospective design, single-database US-based source, potential residual confounding, and inability to establish causal relationships between acute glucose dysregulation and outcomes in hypertensive ICU patients.

Implications Medical Affairs : The SHR represents a clinically actionable prognostic marker in hypertensive critically ill patients, potentially enabling more targeted glucose management strategies that could reduce mortality in this high-risk inpatient population. For Medical Affairs, this evidence supports development of educational programs focused on metabolic monitoring in hospitalized hypertensive patients, strengthening the narrative for antihypertensive therapies that also confer glycemic benefits in acute settings. Collaboration with intensivists and diabetologists could support positioning of cardiometabolic management protocols that integrate blood pressure and glucose optimization as synergistic therapeutic targets.

Hypertensive Disorders of Pregnancy and GLP-1 Receptor Agonist Timing: A Systematic Review and Meta-Analysis.

Revue : Endocrine | Tier : NA | IF estime : NA

Date de publication : Jul 31, 2026

Resultats principaux : This systematic review and meta-analysis included 3 retrospective cohort studies with 10,880 pregnancies (4,942 exposed to GLP-1 receptor agonists and 5,938 unexposed) from the United States (2014–2025), evaluating periconceptional or first-trimester exposure to seven agents including semaglutide, liraglutide, dulaglutide, tirzepatide, exenatide, lixisenatide, and albiglutide. The primary endpoint was the incidence of hypertensive disorders of pregnancy (HDP) in GLP-1 RA-exposed versus unexposed pregnancies, pooled using a random-effects Mantel-Haenszel model. The pooled odds ratio for HDP was 0.91 (95% CI: 0.57–1.47), not reaching statistical significance, with two studies showing lower HDP risk and one showing higher risk in exposed pregnancies. Risk of bias was moderate-to-high per the ROBINS-I tool across all three included studies, and significant heterogeneity in drug types, exposure timing, and comparator definitions was noted. Major limitations include the very small number of eligible studies (n=3), diverse drug exposures limiting class-level conclusions, inadequate statistical power to detect clinically meaningful differences, and the exclusively retrospective observational design.

Implications Medical Affairs : This meta-analysis provides inconclusive but early safety signal data on GLP-1 RAs in periconceptional exposure relative to hypertensive disorders of pregnancy, a clinically critical question given the rapidly expanding use of these agents in women of reproductive age who also have metabolic disease risk factors for HDP. Medical Affairs teams should closely monitor this evolving regulatory and clinical space, as future prospective registry data may significantly affect prescribing guidelines, product labeling, and risk communication strategies for GLP-1 RAs in reproductive-age women. Proactive engagement with regulatory affairs, maternal-fetal medicine KOLs, and pharmacovigilance teams could position the company to contribute meaningfully to the emerging evidence base through dedicated pharmacoepidemiological studies.

Hypertension Is Associated With an Increased Risk of Peritoneal Dialysis-Associated Peritonitis: A Retrospective Cohort Study.

Revue : Frontiers in Cellular and Infection Microbiology | Tier : NA | IF estime : NA

Date de publication : Jul 16, 2026

Resultats principaux : This retrospective cohort study enrolled 352 adult peritoneal dialysis (PD) patients followed from January 2007 to December 2024, of whom 194 (55.1%) had hypertension defined by documented diagnosis, antihypertensive medication use, or repeated BP measurements of 140/90 mmHg or higher at baseline. The primary outcome was peritoneal dialysis-associated peritonitis (PDAP), assessed using cumulative incidence function curves with Gray's test and Fine-Gray competing risk regression over a mean follow-up of 46.1 months. After multivariable adjustment, hypertension remained independently associated with a significantly higher risk of PDAP in both Fine-Gray (HR=4.46, 95% CI: 2.49–7.99, p<0.001) and logistic regression models (OR=4.38, 95% CI: 2.11–9.08, p<0.001). Descriptive microbiological analysis revealed that hypertensive patients showed a higher proportion of gram-negative organisms compared to the gram-positive predominance in non-hypertensive patients. Primary limitations include the retrospective single-center Chinese design, relatively small sample (n=352), and inability to account for blood pressure control quality, specific antihypertensive agents used, or immune function differences that may confound the hypertension-PDAP association.

Implications Medical Affairs : This study identifies hypertension as a significant and independent risk factor for peritoneal dialysis-associated peritonitis with a strikingly elevated hazard ratio, with potential implications for PD patient selection criteria and infection risk stratification protocols in nephrology practice. Medical Affairs should consider the clinical significance of blood pressure management in the dialysis population, where antihypertensive optimization may confer benefits extending beyond cardiovascular protection to infection risk reduction in PD patients. Engaging nephrology and infectious disease KOLs to investigate whether specific antihypertensive regimens differentially affect PDAP risk could support differentiated product messaging in the renal hypertension space.

Remote Patient Monitoring Adoption for Hypertension Management Among Medicare Beneficiaries.

Revue : JAMA Network Open | Tier : NA | IF estime : NA

Date de publication : Jul 1, 2026

Resultats principaux : This large cohort study with a difference-in-differences design and propensity score matching analyzed Medicare enrollment and claims data from 2016 to 2022, comparing 281,620 beneficiaries aged 65 years or older with hypertension who switched from Medicare fee-for-service (FFS) to Medicare Advantage (MA) versus those who remained in FFS. The matched sample included 46,833 MA-VBC proxy switchers, 46,833 FFS comparators, 93,977 MA non-VBC switchers, and 93,977 FFS comparators, with common comorbidities including diabetes, chronic kidney disease, and heart failure. Switching to MA was associated with significantly lower remote patient monitoring (RPM) adoption in 2022 (VBC proxy: OR 0.55, 95% CI: 0.42–0.72; non-VBC: OR 0.73, 95% CI: 0.54–0.99), greater clinician loss without replacement (VBC: OR 1.27; non-VBC: OR 1.09), and higher hypertension-related hospitalizations (VBC proxy: OR 1.75; non-VBC: OR 1.94, 1.56 percentage points in both). Event-study analyses showed sustained postswitch divergence through 2022, suggesting persistent rather than transitional effects. Limitations include the observational design with potential residual confounding, reliance on encounter data that may undercount MA plan services, and lack of actual blood pressure control data.

Implications Medical Affairs : These findings highlight major health system discontinuities in hypertension monitoring and care when patients transition between insurance models, with adverse consequences for blood pressure management quality and RPM adoption that may undermine antihypertensive therapy effectiveness. For Medical Affairs, this underscores the importance of advocating with health policy KOLs and payers for consistent RPM coverage incentives that support medication adherence and blood pressure monitoring across insurance plan transitions. This data can be leveraged to position comprehensive hypertension management solutions that integrate therapeutic and digital monitoring components in value-based care contract negotiations with payers and health systems.

Prevalence and Correlates of Erectile Dysfunction: A Comparative Study of Adult Hypertensive Patients in an Outpatient Clinic, South East Nigeria.

Revue : Nigerian Journal of Physiological Sciences | Tier : NA | IF estime : NA

Date de publication : Jun 30, 2026

Resultats principaux : This hospital-based cross-sectional comparative study recruited 236 adult male patients (hypertensive and non-hypertensive) from a tertiary care center in Abakaliki, south east Nigeria using systematic random sampling, with erectile dysfunction (ED) assessed by the Sexual Health Inventory for Men (SHIM). The overall ED prevalence was 44.1%, with significantly higher prevalence in hypertensive patients (54.2%) compared to non-hypertensive patients (33.9%), a statistically significant difference (p<0.05). Logistic regression analysis identified blood pressure (both SBP and DBP) as an independent predictor of ED (OR: 2.026, 95% CI: 1.180–3.479, p=0.010), and BMI also showed a significant association (p=0.048), while antihypertensive drug class and smoking showed no significant independent association. Alcohol use was significantly associated with ED in unadjusted analysis but this association disappeared after elimination of confounders. Key limitations include the small sample size (n=236), single-center tertiary hospital design limiting representativeness of the general Nigerian hypertensive population, and cross-sectional design preventing causal inference.

Implications Medical Affairs : This African study adds important regional data confirming hypertension as a significant independent predictor of erectile dysfunction in a low-resource tertiary care setting, where this comorbidity remains under-screened and undertreated despite its substantial impact on quality of life and treatment adherence. Medical Affairs should consider antihypertensive drug class-specific erectile function profiles in KOL education programs, as certain agents (ARBs, CCBs) carry more favorable sexual function profiles which may improve treatment adherence in men of reproductive age. This data supports the business case for expanded hypertension epidemiology research in sub-Saharan Africa and could inform patient adherence initiatives addressing sexual side effects as a documented barrier to antihypertensive therapy continuation.

Prevalence of (Pre)Hypertension and Correlations Between Blood Pressures and Obesity in a Lean Population of Adults from the Old Eastern Region of Nigeria.

Revue : Nigerian Journal of Physiological Sciences | Tier : NA | IF estime : NA

Date de publication : Jun 30, 2026

Resultats principaux : This large cross-sectional epidemiological study enrolled 1,755 subjects (59% females) from five states in the old Eastern Region of Nigeria using standard clinical measurement protocols and statistical analyses including chi-square, Fisher's test, and logistic regression. Prehypertension was found in 40.0% of males and 27.5% of females, while established hypertension affected 19.1% of males and 18.6% of females, revealing a substantial and nearly sex-equivalent hypertension burden. Urban-rural analyses showed prehypertension was more prevalent among urban females (29.1% vs 24.3%), while hypertension was more prevalent among rural females (21.9% vs 17.1%), with urban-rural male rates being similar at approximately 19%. Systolic blood pressure showed significant positive correlations with weight, waist circumference, hip circumference, waist-to-height ratio, and BMI in the general population, but these correlations varied considerably in strength and direction when disaggregated by sex and blood pressure phenotype. A critical finding was the non-linear relationship between obesity and hypertension, particularly among those already hypertensive, challenging assumptions of a simple dose-response obesity-blood pressure relationship.

Implications Medical Affairs : This study provides important Nigerian epidemiological data documenting a high combined (pre)hypertension burden of approximately 60% in males, highlighting the scale of unaddressed cardiovascular risk in Nigeria's Eastern Region across both urban and rural settings. From a Medical Affairs perspective, this data supports advocacy for hypertension awareness and screening programs in sub-Saharan Africa, representing a significant and growing market for antihypertensive therapies where treatment gaps remain substantial. Strategic engagement with African cardiovascular societies, national health ministries, and global health KOLs could position the company as an active partner in addressing the hypertension epidemic in West Africa, aligning commercial interests with public health impact.

Mortality Related to Different Circulatory Diseases Before, During and After the COVID-19 Pandemic.

Revue : Clinical Cardiology | Tier : NA | IF estime : NA

Date de publication : Aug 2026

Resultats principaux : This population-based retrospective mortality analysis used multiple causes of death data from death certificates of Veneto Region residents aged 40 or older in Italy, covering the period 2008 to 2023, with age-standardized monthly mortality rates computed for ischemic heart disease, cerebrovascular disease, hypertensive diseases, and atrial fibrillation. Seasonal ARIMA and Lee-Carter time-series models were applied to generate 2023 mortality forecasts under two scenarios: using the pre-pandemic baseline (2008–2019) and pandemic-inclusive baseline (2008–2022). In 2020, hypertensive diseases showed the sharpest mortality peak at +29%, while rates decreased in 2021–2022 but remained significantly above pre-pandemic levels. By 2023, observed mortality for all circulatory conditions aligned closely with pre-pandemic forecast trajectories (2008–2019 baseline) but was significantly lower than predicted by the pandemic-inclusive model, suggesting return to pre-pandemic trends. Limitations include the ecological study design, regional (Veneto) focus limiting national and international generalizability, reliance on death certificate coding that may inconsistently attribute hypertensive disease as cause of death, and potential confounding from concurrent lifestyle changes during the pandemic.

Implications Medical Affairs : The 29% excess hypertensive disease mortality peak during the acute COVID-19 pandemic represents a critical signal for Medical Affairs teams regarding the vulnerability of hypertensive patients to health system disruptions, infectious triggers, and care interruptions during emergencies. These findings strengthen the case for resilient, continuous antihypertensive management strategies and digital health solutions that can maintain therapy adherence and blood pressure monitoring even during healthcare access disruptions. KOL engagement with public health epidemiologists, cardiologists, and health systems researchers should focus on pandemic preparedness frameworks that ensure hypertension care continuity, creating opportunities to position monitoring-integrated antihypertensive products as healthcare infrastructure solutions.

Socioeconomic and Behavioral Risk Factors for Cardiovascular Complications Among Hypertensive Adults in Lusaka, Zambia: A Cross-Sectional Study.

Revue : Health Science Reports | Tier : NA | IF estime : NA

Date de publication : Jul 29, 2026

Resultats principaux : This hospital-based cross-sectional study analyzed secondary data from 384 medical records of hypertensive adults aged 18 years or older at the University Teaching Hospital in Lusaka, Zambia, assessing the association between socioeconomic status, lifestyle behaviors, and cardiovascular complications (CVCs) including heart failure and left ventricular hypertrophy. Multivariable Firth's penalized logistic regression was used to address quasi-complete separation, showing that low socioeconomic status was associated with borderline significantly higher CVC odds (aOR=4.09, p=0.05). Smoking (aOR=3.09, p=0.04) and alcohol intake (aOR=1.13, p=0.002) were significantly associated with increased CVC risk, while physical activity was strongly protective (aOR=0.08, p<0.001), and increasing age was associated with incrementally higher CVC odds (aOR=1.097, p<0.001). The study did not find a statistically significant independent association with male sex after adjustment (aOR=1.76, p=0.24), suggesting similar cardiovascular vulnerability across sexes in this hypertensive Zambian cohort. Key limitations include the retrospective secondary data design with potential information bias, single-center tertiary hospital setting, relatively small sample size (n=384), and restriction to Lusaka limiting rural and national generalizability.

Implications Medical Affairs : This Zambian study provides evidence that modifiable behavioral risk factors, particularly smoking and physical inactivity, substantially drive cardiovascular complications in hypertensive patients in sub-Saharan Africa, where pharmacological management access is limited and lifestyle factors dominate preventable risk. Medical Affairs teams can leverage this data to support comprehensive hypertension management narratives that emphasize lifestyle modification as a first-line and adjunctive strategy alongside antihypertensive pharmacotherapy, especially in resource-limited settings where adherence to combined approaches is critical. Strategic KOL engagement with African cardiovascular societies and global health organizations could position the company as an advocate for evidence-based, context-adapted hypertension management guidelines for low-resource environments.

Hypertensive Disorders of Pregnancy and Women's Health Throughout the Life Course: An Update Review 2025–2026.

Revue : Hypertension Research | Tier : NA | IF estime : NA

Date de publication : Jul 31, 2026

Resultats principaux : This comprehensive review summarizes updated 2025–2026 evidence on hypertensive disorders of pregnancy (HDP) and preeclampsia (PE), contextualizing the global rise in HDP incidence against increasing chronic hypertension and obesity prevalence among reproductive-age women. Key emerging evidence includes individualized prevention strategies with tailored low-dose aspirin regimens based on maternal pathophysiology and the clinical use of the sFlt-1/PlGF angiogenic biomarker ratio for PE risk stratification to enable timely targeted intervention in high-risk women. The review identifies the preconception and postpartum periods as critical windows requiring systematic blood pressure monitoring, lifestyle assessment, and cardiovascular risk follow-up, expanding the clinical management framework beyond the pregnancy episode. Growing evidence supports a life-course conceptual model that views HDP not as a transient pregnancy complication but as a sentinel cardiovascular event predicting future hypertension, coronary artery disease, stroke, and heart failure. Limitations inherent to the review format include lack of meta-analytic pooling, potential for publication bias toward positive studies, and the rapidly evolving evidence base in this area making some statements susceptible to rapid obsolescence.

Implications Medical Affairs : This review reinforces the paradigm shift toward viewing HDP as an early indicator of lifelong cardiovascular risk, with significant implications for how antihypertensive and preventive therapies should be positioned across the female life course from preconception through menopause. Medical Affairs should leverage this emerging framework to develop product positioning strategies in the preeclampsia prevention and postpartum hypertension management space, targeting agents with established maternal safety profiles and long-term cardiovascular benefit. Collaboration with maternal-fetal medicine and cardiovascular KOLs, and engagement with guideline committees developing life-course hypertension management frameworks, should be prioritized to incorporate this narrative into actionable clinical practice recommendations.

Association Between GLM7 Glycolipid Metabolism Index and Female Infertility: A Cross-Sectional Analysis of NHANES 2013–2020.

Revue : Medicine (Baltimore) | Tier : NA | IF estime : NA

Date de publication : Jul 31, 2026

Resultats principaux : This cross-sectional study used NHANES 2013–2020 data from 3,792 American women to investigate the association between the GLM7 glycolipid metabolism composite index (incorporating age, triglycerides, fasting glucose, BMI, HDL-C, LDL-C, and insulin) and female infertility risk. Multivariable logistic regression showed each one-unit increase in GLM7 was associated with a 29% higher infertility risk (OR=1.29, 95% CI: 1.10–1.53, p=0.002), with the highest GLM7 tertile associated with 49% increased infertility risk compared to the lowest (OR=1.49, 95% CI: 1.10–2.02, p-trend=0.011). Restricted cubic spline analysis confirmed a nonlinear increasing relationship between GLM7 and infertility (overall p=0.001, nonlinear p=0.009), with a significant inflection point at GLM7=6.84 below which positive association was steepest. Full adjustment for confounders including hypertension, diabetes, and cardiovascular history yielded an ROC area under the curve of 0.68 for infertility discrimination. Limitations include the cross-sectional design preventing causal inference, NHANES-specific sampling potentially limiting generalizability, and the fact that hypertension is included only as an adjusted confounder rather than a primary outcome.

Implications Medical Affairs : This study is peripherally relevant to hypertension as a metabolic comorbidity adjusted for in analyses assessing composite cardiometabolic risk, suggesting that composite glycolipid-metabolic indices may have utility in identifying women who carry multiple cardiovascular risk factors including hypertension alongside reproductive dysfunction. For Medical Affairs, the GLM7 concept could inform future composite risk stratification tools that incorporate blood pressure alongside metabolic parameters in women of reproductive age, potentially identifying hypertension-prone patient segments earlier in the disease course. This emerging area warrants monitoring for convergent evidence linking metabolic composite markers to hypertension outcomes rather than immediate investment in KOL programs specifically around this index.

Sarcopenia, an Independent Predictor for All-Cause Mortality in Rheumatoid Arthritis: Insights from the NHANES Database.

Revue : Medicine (Baltimore) | Tier : NA | IF estime : NA

Date de publication : Jul 31, 2026

Resultats principaux : This study analyzed 910 RA patients identified in NHANES 1999–2018, defining sarcopenia by appendicular skeletal muscle mass index adjusted for BMI (ASM/BMI) and assessing all-cause mortality using Cox proportional hazards regression with adjustment for demographic, clinical, and lifestyle factors. Sarcopenia was independently associated with higher all-cause mortality risk (HR=1.610, 95% CI: 1.192–2.176, p=0.002) in the fully adjusted model. Subgroup analysis revealed particularly significant associations in individuals aged under 60 years, males, and those with comorbid hypertension, diabetes, and cardiovascular disease, indicating that hypertension substantially amplifies the mortality risk conferred by sarcopenia. Sensitivity analyses confirmed the robustness of the primary findings across alternative model specifications. Key limitations include self-reported RA diagnosis without clinical validation, the cross-sectional NHANES design preventing prospective causal interpretation, and inability to assess RA disease activity or specific treatment regimens as effect modifiers on the sarcopenia-mortality relationship.

Implications Medical Affairs : Hypertension was identified as a significant comorbidity that amplifies the sarcopenia-related mortality risk in RA patients, highlighting the compounded cardiovascular risk in this complex multimorbid population where standard antihypertensive care may be under-prioritized relative to immunological management. For Medical Affairs, this reinforces the importance of proactive antihypertensive management in inflammatory disease patients, where comorbidity burden substantially influences mortality outcomes and may represent a distinct high-priority target population for cardiovascular risk reduction strategies. Engaging rheumatology KOLs to discuss integrated cardiovascular risk management including hypertension control in RA patients could support prescribing of antihypertensive agents with favorable inflammatory and muscle-preserving profiles in this specialized population.

[Association Between Occupational Hazardous Factor Exposure and Chronic Disease Multimorbidity in Steel Workers].

Revue : Chinese Journal of Industrial Hygiene and Occupational Diseases | Tier : NA | IF estime : NA

Date de publication : Jul 20, 2026

Resultats principaux : This large cross-sectional study from the Health Effects Cohort of the Beijing-Tianjin-Hebei Region enrolled 6,560 steel workers (6,001 men, 559 women) who underwent occupational health examinations in 2017–2018 at a Tangshan steel company. Chronic disease multimorbidity (including hypertension as one of the composite conditions) prevalence was 62.1% in men and 24.7% in women, with statistically significant associations with age, sex, educational attainment, income, marital status, BMI, DASH diet score, smoking, alcohol use, and family history of multiple diseases. The DASH dietary score, specifically designed to address hypertension, emerged as a modifiable protective factor against multimorbidity in this industrial cohort. Limitations include the cross-sectional design preventing causal inference, single-company workforce limiting broader industrial generalizability, and reliance on self-reported disease history for the multimorbidity composite endpoint.

Implications Medical Affairs : This study identifies occupational environmental exposures—particularly night-shift work, extreme heat, noise, and dust—as potent risk factors for chronic disease multimorbidity including hypertension in a large industrial workforce, underscoring the occupational dimension of hypertension pathogenesis. Medical Affairs teams should consider occupational hypertension as an underaddressed clinical segment where targeted prevention programs and early antihypertensive intervention could substantially reduce the high multimorbidity burden and associated economic costs in industrial settings. Engagement with occupational medicine KOLs and corporate health program administrators could create pathways for workplace hypertension screening initiatives and disease management programs that incorporate antihypertensive therapy alongside occupational risk reduction.

Date de publication : Jul 16, 2026

Resultats principaux : The Prostate Cancer Cardiovascular (PCCV) Risk Expert Panel comprising urologists, oncologists, and cardio-oncologists from India, with international expert guidance, developed practical consensus recommendations using a modified Delphi methodology with consensus defined as 75% or greater agreement among panelists. Implications Medical Affairs : This consensus explicitly positions hypertension monitoring and management as a critical component of cardiovascular risk mitigation in prostate cancer patients receiving ADT and ARPIs, a growing patient population where antihypertensive management is clinically necessary but frequently under-implemented. Micro-Nanoplastics in Cardiovascular Disease: A Critical Update from Environmental Exposure to Clinical Implications.

Revue : Cardiovascular Toxicology | Tier : NA | IF estime : NA

Date de publication : Jun 16, 2026

Resultats principaux : This critical narrative review summarizes current evidence on the cardiovascular effects of micro- and nanoplastics (MNPs), an emerging class of environmental contaminants detected in human patients with myocardial infarction, acute coronary syndrome, coronary artery disease, and carotid artery stenosis. In these observational human studies, higher MNP levels have been associated with elevated inflammatory and coagulation-related biomarkers and greater disease severity. Experimental in vivo and in vitro evidence provides mechanistic biological plausibility, demonstrating that MNP exposure promotes endothelial dysfunction, oxidative stress, systemic and vascular inflammation, platelet activation, coagulation abnormalities, cardiometabolic alterations, myocardial remodeling, fibrosis, and hypertension as experimental endpoints. The review also discusses iatrogenic MNP exposure through cardiovascular medical devices and emerging therapeutic perspectives targeting MNP-mediated cardiovascular pathways. Major uncertainties remain regarding environmentally relevant exposure concentrations, long-term human dose-response relationships, and the translational relevance of current experimental findings to clinically observed cardiovascular disease burden.

Implications Medical Affairs : The identification of hypertension as a potential MNP-mediated experimental endpoint adds an emerging environmental dimension to hypertension pathophysiology that is gaining scientific momentum and may attract regulatory and clinical guideline attention within the next decade.
