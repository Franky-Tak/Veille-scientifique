Revue : Renal failure | Tier : NA | IF estime : NA

Date de publication : 2026-09-14

Resultats principaux : This retrospective longitudinal study analyzed 20 years of NHANES data (1999–2018) in US adults aged ≥20 years to describe temporal trends in CKD prevalence and nine associated comorbidities using the CKD-EPI 2021 equation without the race variable. CKD prevalence remained stable at approximately 20% across the observation period, providing a robust national benchmark. Evaluation of a National Health Service Machine-Learning Model for Hypertension Case-Finding: Retrospective Cohort Study.

Revue : Journal of medical Internet research | Tier : NA | IF estime : NA

Date de publication : 2026-09-15

Resultats principaux : This retrospective cohort study independently evaluated an NHS-commissioned ML model for identifying undiagnosed hypertension in 1,802,920 adults registered with general practices in North West London between May 2023 and May 2024. The model achieved overall sensitivity of 62.7% (95% CI 62.5–62.8) and specificity of 60.7% (95% CI 60.5–60.8), with positive predictive value ranging from 31.5% to 42.9% depending on demographic subgroup. Substantial performance variation was identified: sensitivity was higher in older adults and Black patients, while specificity was higher in younger adults, female patients, and White patients; higher socioeconomic deprivation was associated with higher sensitivity but lower specificity. Age was the dominant model driver, with 96.2% of 70–79-year-olds predicted positive while only 0.08% of 20–39-year-olds received a positive prediction. The ML model performed comparably to a simpler logistic regression model, suggesting limited added benefit from algorithmic complexity and raising questions about the clinical utility of deploying resource-intensive AI tools in this setting.

Implications Medical Affairs : The suboptimal positive predictive value and demographic performance variation of this AI case-finding tool highlight that digital health approaches to hypertension detection require careful validation before deployment, with significant equity implications if used for differential resource allocation. From a Medical Affairs perspective, these data support the development of complementary screening strategies — including pharmacy-based measurement programs and community outreach — to reach populations systematically underdetected by algorithmic approaches. The publication provides a scientific framework for Medical Affairs teams to engage health systems and integrated care boards in designing evidence-based, equitable hypertension detection pipelines that can expand the diagnosed patient population eligible for pharmacotherapy.

Poor adherence to antihypertensive medication in patients with hypertension in eastern Sudan: A cross-sectional study.

Revue : Medicine | Tier : NA | IF estime : NA

Date de publication : 2026-09-11

Resultats principaux : This hospital-based cross-sectional study enrolled 474 hypertensive adults (65.8% female, median age 53 years, IQR 45–60) in Gadarif, eastern Sudan during the ongoing armed conflict, using the validated Morisky Medication Adherence Scale-8 (MMAS-8) to assess adherence. A critically high non-adherence rate of 94.1% (MMAS-8 score <6) was identified, with only 1.7% achieving high adherence and 4.2% medium adherence. Multivariate binary logistic regression identified urban residence as the sole independent predictor of non-adherence (adjusted OR = 2.39, 95% CI 1.06–5.43), while age, sex, education, disease duration, number of medications, income, and comorbidities were not significantly associated. The conflict context likely contributes substantially to the observed non-adherence through disruption of healthcare access, drug supply chains, and psychosocial stress, although the study design cannot quantify these contributions. Limitations include the single-site tertiary hospital design, reliance on self-reported questionnaire data subject to social desirability bias, and restricted generalizability outside conflict-affected settings.

Implications Medical Affairs : The near-total non-adherence rate observed in this conflict-affected population represents one of the most extreme adherence failure scenarios documented, underscoring the vulnerability of hypertension management in fragile states and the catastrophic cardiovascular consequences that will likely follow. For Medical Affairs teams in global health, this evidence strengthens the case for prioritizing simplified once-daily fixed-dose combination antihypertensive regimens in access programs and humanitarian health initiatives in Sub-Saharan Africa and conflict zones. These data can be used to engage WHO, the Global Health Security Agenda, and NGO partners in evidence-based advocacy for essential medicine access policies and to support humanitarian Medical Affairs programs that differentiate corporate responsibility commitments in emerging markets.

Association of advanced lung cancer inflammation index with hypertension in obstructive sleep apnea.

Revue : Medicine | Tier : NA | IF estime : NA

Date de publication : 2026-09-11

Resultats principaux : This cross-sectional study analyzed data from 12,049 adults across four NHANES cycles to evaluate the association between the Advanced Lung Cancer Inflammation Index (ALI) — a composite marker integrating BMI, serum albumin, and the neutrophil-to-lymphocyte ratio — and hypertension prevalence in individuals with obstructive sleep apnea (OSA). Logistic regression revealed that each unit increase in log ALI was independently associated with higher odds of hypertension (OR = 1.44, 95% CI 1.25–1.66, p < 0.001) after adjustment for multiple confounders. Quartile analysis showed that participants in the highest ALI quartile had 70% higher odds of hypertension compared to the lowest quartile (OR = 1.70, 95% CI 1.40–2.07, p < 0.001). Restricted cubic spline analysis confirmed a nonlinear dose-response relationship between ALI and hypertension risk in the OSA population. Study limitations include the cross-sectional design precluding causal inference, the questionnaire-based OSA ascertainment without polysomnography, and the inability to establish temporal relationships between ALI changes and hypertension onset.

Implications Medical Affairs : The robust association between ALI — reflecting systemic inflammation and nutritional status — and hypertension in OSA patients points to shared inflammatory pathways as a mechanistic link, which may represent an actionable therapeutic target beyond CPAP therapy and conventional antihypertensives. For Medical Affairs, positioning the OSA-hypertension comorbidity as a bidirectional inflammatory condition could support the scientific rationale for antihypertensive agents with pleiotropic anti-inflammatory properties in the growing OSA patient segment. The potential clinical utility of ALI as a simple, routine-laboratory-derived screening tool for identifying high-risk OSA patients warranting intensified hypertension management represents a practical KOL discussion point that bridges pulmonology and cardiology Medical Affairs efforts.

Date de publication : 2026-09

Active cancer prevalence, the primary outcome, ranged from 4.22% (95% CI 2.18–5.32) in T2DM to 5.55% (95% CI 3.97–7.01) in AF, with hypertension patients falling within this range as a distinct high-prevalence subgroup. The analysis was conducted using one-step generalized linear mixed models, providing robust pooled estimates despite heterogeneous ascertainment methods across included studies. Important limitations include heterogeneity in how cancer was defined and ascertained across studies and variable follow-up periods, which may affect the precision of pooled estimates.

Implications Medical Affairs : The quantification of a substantial concurrent cancer burden in hypertensive patients — within the broader cardiovascular comorbidity landscape — has direct implications for Medical Affairs teams positioning antihypertensive agents in the context of cardio-oncology care, where drug-drug interactions, cardiovascular toxicity monitoring, and blood pressure management in cancer patients represent a growing clinical priority. Effect modification by fatty liver in the association between triglyceride-Glucose index and new-onset hypertension: a large health-examination cohort study.

Revue : Frontiers in endocrinology | Tier : NA | IF estime : NA

Date de publication : 2026-08-31

Resultats principaux : This large retrospective cohort study followed 66,664 adults without baseline hypertension who underwent routine health examinations (median follow-up 2.37 years, IQR 1.69–3.21), using Cox proportional hazards models to evaluate the association between the TyG index — a validated surrogate of insulin resistance calculated from fasting triglycerides and glucose — and new-onset hypertension. Higher TyG was independently associated with incident hypertension as a continuous variable (HR 1.37, 95% CI 1.30–1.45) and in the highest versus lowest quartile (HR 1.61, 95% CI 1.49–1.75). Fatty liver status, assessed by abdominal ultrasound, significantly modified the TyG-hypertension relationship (P for interaction <0.001), with participants having both elevated TyG and fatty liver exhibiting the highest risk (HR 1.63, 95% CI 1.52–1.76). Results were robust to sensitivity analyses adjusting for family history of hypertension, diabetes, renal function, and baseline blood pressure. Study limitations include the retrospective design, shorter median follow-up, and reliance on ultrasound rather than liver biopsy for fatty liver diagnosis, which may result in underclassification.

Implications Medical Affairs : The synergistic interaction between insulin resistance (TyG) and fatty liver in driving new-onset hypertension positions metabolic-associated steatotic liver disease (MASLD) as a critical upstream risk factor for hypertension, strengthening the scientific case for early antihypertensive intervention in patients with insulin resistance and hepatic steatosis. For Medical Affairs, agents with dual metabolic and blood pressure benefits — including RAAS inhibitors with renoprotective and insulin-sensitizing properties — gain additional scientific differentiation from these data, which can be incorporated into scientific exchange materials for endocrinology, hepatology, and cardiology KOLs. The identification of fatty liver as an effect modifier provides a novel patient segmentation strategy for hypertension prevention clinical trials and for Medical Affairs-supported real-world evidence studies in the growing MASLD patient population.

Cross-Sectional Associations of Dietary Pattern, Physical Activity with Hypertension Among Adults in Southwestern Nigeria.

Revue : Nutrients | Tier : NA | IF estime : NA

Date de publication : 2026-08-28

Resultats principaux : This cross-sectional study examined 608 adults from Oyo and Kwara States, southwestern Nigeria using a culturally validated 7-day Food Frequency Questionnaire (FFQ) and the Global Physical Activity Questionnaire (GPAQ), with hypertension defined by WHO criteria on standardized single-visit blood pressure measurement. Overall hypertension prevalence was 24.34%, with significantly higher rates in males (29.78%) versus females (16.67%), and four dietary patterns were extracted using Varimax-rotated principal component analysis. After full covariate adjustment, the typical traditional dietary pattern (high in starchy roots and processed foods) was associated with significantly higher hypertension prevalence (PR = 1.642, 95% CI 1.069–2.252, p = 0.024), while the fruit and vegetable pattern was protective (PR = 0.408, 95% CI 0.258–0.646, p < 0.001). Physical activity showed no independent association with hypertension after controlling for dietary pattern, underscoring the primacy of diet in this population. Limitations include the cross-sectional design with single blood pressure measurement, potential FFQ recall bias, geographic restriction to two southwestern states, and inability to establish causality.

Implications Medical Affairs : These findings add to the growing evidence base on dietary determinants of hypertension in Sub-Saharan Africa, a region experiencing rapid epidemiological transition with surging cardiovascular disease incidence but where culturally tailored lifestyle interventions are more feasible and scalable than pharmacotherapy alone. From a Medical Affairs perspective, the data support the development of locally relevant patient education and lifestyle modification materials that complement antihypertensive pharmacotherapy in Nigerian and broader West African markets, where fruit and vegetable promotion could be integrated into product-support programs. Engagement with African hypertension societies, nutritional scientists, and regional KOLs on diet-disease interactions provides Medical Affairs teams with an evidence-based platform to support access initiatives that position antihypertensives as part of holistic cardiovascular risk management in emerging markets.

Revue : Medicine | Tier : NA | IF estime : NA

Date de publication : 2026-09-11

Resultats principaux : Using NHANES data from 1999–2004, this cross-sectional study of 6,018 US adults examined the relationship between the Composite Dietary Antioxidant Index (CDAI) — integrating intake of vitamins A, C, E, zinc, selenium, and carotenoids — and peripheral artery disease (PAD) prevalence (5.9%), with hypertension as one of the key covariates in the fully adjusted models. After comprehensive adjustment for demographic, lifestyle, and clinical variables including hypertension history, a significant inverse association was found between CDAI and PAD prevalence (OR = 0.96, 95% CI 0.92–1.00). Limitations include the cross-sectional design precluding causal inference, potential dietary recall bias from the 24-hour recall methodology, and the temporal limitation of 1999–2004 NHANES data which may not reflect current dietary patterns.

Implications Medical Affairs : This cross-sectional evidence linking dietary antioxidant intake to reduced PAD risk — in a population with substantial hypertension comorbidity — supports an integrated vascular risk reduction narrative that positions antihypertensive therapy alongside dietary antioxidant optimization, reinforcing the value of comprehensive cardiovascular risk management programs. For Medical Affairs teams, these data can inform the scientific content of patient education programs emphasizing lifestyle modification as an adjunct to pharmacotherapy in hypertensive patients with high peripheral vascular risk. Elevated Prorenin Induces Podocyte Injury and Glomerular Fibrosis in cyp1a1-Prorenin Transgenic Rats.

Revue : International journal of molecular sciences | Tier : NA | IF estime : NA

Date de publication : 2026-09-03

Resultats principaux : This experimental study used cyp1a1-prorenin transgenic rats with dose-dependent indole-3-carbinol (I3C) induction at 0.05%, 0.15%, and 0.3% dietary concentrations to achieve graded plasma prorenin overexpression, modeling the elevated prorenin commonly observed in diabetic nephropathy. Elevated prorenin caused dose-dependent increases in mean arterial pressure and urinary albumin excretion, alongside reduced podocyte number, diminished slit diaphragm protein expression, segmental foot process effacement, and podocyte hypertrophy, collectively indicating progressive podocyte injury. Increased prorenin further stimulated renal expression of profibrotic factors and promoted glomerular fibrosis in a dose-dependent manner. Treatment with either amlodipine or enalapril for 6 weeks prevented hypertension development and partially attenuated podocyte injury, albuminuria, and renal fibrosis without fully reversing these changes, with protective effects linked to NF-kB and Nox2 pathway suppression. Limitations include the use of an artificial transgenic induction system that may not fully replicate clinical hypertensive nephropathy, and the inability to distinguish angiotensin II-dependent from II-independent prorenin effects with precision in vivo.

Implications Medical Affairs : These preclinical data provide mechanistic evidence that prorenin contributes to hypertensive nephropathy through both angiotensin II-dependent and -independent pathways, highlighting prorenin receptor signaling as a potential target for next-generation renoprotective therapies beyond current RAAS inhibitors. The partial but incomplete renoprotection achieved with either amlodipine or enalapril alone demonstrates residual renal injury despite blood pressure normalization, providing experimental justification for combination antihypertensive strategies and for investigating agents with direct anti-prorenin or anti-inflammatory renal mechanisms. For Medical Affairs educational purposes, these findings can be used in nephrology KOL discussions to explain the mechanistic limitations of current standard-of-care RAAS inhibition in preventing progressive CKD in patients with metabolic syndrome or diabetes, supporting scientific exchange around combination or novel renoprotective approaches.

Association between ambient fine particulate matter and hemorrhagic stroke in Yancheng, China.

Revue : Frontiers in public health | Tier : NA | IF estime : NA

Date de publication : 2026-08-31

Resultats principaux : This time-stratified case-crossover study included 2,312 patients hospitalized for hemorrhagic stroke (HS) across four major hospitals in Yancheng, China between 2022 and 2024, using conditional logistic regression models to assess the short-term effects of PM2.5 on HS hospitalization risk at multiple lag structures. The strongest association was observed at lag01 (two-day moving average), where each 10 µg/m³ increase in PM2.5 concentration was associated with a 3.24% increase in HS hospitalization risk (95% CI 0.55%–6.00%), with significant effects also seen at lag0 (2.50%, 95% CI 0.11%–4.95%) and lag1 (2.76%, 95% CI 0.32%–5.27%). Stratified analyses revealed stronger effect estimates among females, older adults, individuals with hypertension or diabetes, smokers, alcohol consumers, and during the cold season. The PM2.5-HS association was attenuated after mutual adjustment for SO₂, NO₂, and CO, suggesting co-pollutant confounding may modify the observed effects. Key limitations include inability to fully control for individual-level exposures, potential HS subtype misclassification, and limited generalizability to other geographic or pollution profiles.

Implications Medical Affairs : The finding that pre-existing hypertension significantly amplifies PM2.5-related hemorrhagic stroke risk provides strong evidence that effective blood pressure control is a critical modifier of air pollution cardiovascular impact, reinforcing the public health value of optimized antihypertensive treatment in high-pollution environments. For Medical Affairs teams, these data can be used in scientific communications to environmental health advocates, urban health policymakers, and stroke neurologists, positioning aggressive hypertension management as a targeted protective strategy against pollution-related cerebrovascular events in cities with high particulate burden. Date de publication : 2026-08-28

Resultats principaux : This prospective single-center imaging study compared 18F-FDG PET/MRI findings in 28 stable pulmonary arterial hypertension (PAH) patients versus 12 age-matched healthy controls, with 20 PAH patients undergoing repeat imaging after 24 months of targeted PAH therapy. PAH patients demonstrated markedly elevated lung parenchymal SUV (median 0.405 vs. 0.225, p < 0.001) and proximal pulmonary artery SUV (3.46 vs. 1.48, p < 0.001) compared to controls, though the two metabolic metrics were uncorrelated with each other (r = +0.105). Implications Medical Affairs : The failure of FDG-PET/MRI metabolic metrics to independently predict PAH outcomes — despite markedly elevated uptake — clarifies that hemodynamic parameters remain the gold standard for PAH risk stratification, limiting the near-term use of metabolic imaging as a standalone treatment response biomarker in clinical trials. Insights into the relationship between menopausal timing and risk of cardiovascular disease: a systematic review and meta-analysis of Mendelian randomization analyses.

Revue : Systematic reviews | Tier : NA | IF estime : NA

Date de publication : 2026-08-18

Resultats principaux : This PROSPERO-registered systematic review and meta-analysis (CRD420251058908) synthesized evidence from 17 Mendelian randomization (MR) studies examining whether genetically determined menopausal timing causally influences cardiovascular disease risk, predominantly in European ancestry populations. Meta-analyses using random-effects inverse-variance weighted models found no significant causal association between menopausal timing and coronary heart disease (OR = 0.97, 95% CI 0.87–1.07, I² = 48.08%, p = 0.52) or stroke (OR = 1.00, 95% CI 0.95–1.05, I² = 0.01%, p = 0.93). Cardiovascular outcomes evaluated across included studies encompassed CAD, ischemic and hemorrhagic stroke, AF, HF, hypertension, ventricular structure and function, and cardiometabolic traits. While most studies adequately addressed the three core MR assumptions, more than half were assessed as having unclear or high risk of bias from other potential sources, particularly related to population diversity and incomplete confounder adjustment. Key limitations include the predominance of European ancestry participants (limiting cross-ethnic generalizability), the inherent assumptions of MR designs, and the absence of gene-environment interaction analyses in most included studies.

Implications Medical Affairs : The absence of a statistically supported causal relationship between menopausal timing and CVD risk — including hypertension — challenges the traditional framing of early menopause as a primary cardiovascular risk driver, and suggests that modifiable risk factors such as hypertension, dyslipidemia, and lifestyle should remain the central focus of cardiovascular prevention in perimenopausal women. For Medical Affairs teams developing women's cardiovascular health programs or labeling strategies for antihypertensives in perimenopausal and postmenopausal women, these MR findings shift the evidence base away from hormone timing toward risk factor optimization, potentially strengthening the indication for early antihypertensive therapy irrespective of menopausal status. KOL engagement in women's cardiology and menopause medicine should be updated to reflect this evidence, recommending that Medical Affairs educational programs emphasize evidence-based blood pressure management over menopausal timing in cardiovascular risk counseling.
