[Primary aldosteronism as an independent factor of kidney damage. Potential therapeutic strategies and research prospects].

Revue : Problemy endokrinologii | Tier : NA | IF estime : NA

Date de publication : 2026-07-22

Resultats principaux : This is a comprehensive narrative review examining the pathophysiological mechanisms by which primary aldosteronism (PA), the most common curable cause of secondary hypertension, induces chronic kidney disease (CKD) progression. The review synthesizes evidence from experimental and clinical studies showing that chronic aldosterone excess drives renal injury through mineralocorticoid receptor (MR) activation, nongenomic signaling via GPER, oxidative stress, and upregulation of profibrotic/inflammatory mediators including TGF-beta1, NF-kappaB, and NLRP3 inflammasome. Key quantitative findings highlight that somatic mutations in KCNJ5, ATP1A1, and CACNA1D are identified as major drivers of PA-related adenoma formation, while tubular stress biomarkers such as L-FABP, KIM-1, and microalbuminuria serve as early markers of nephron injury before GFR decline becomes measurable. Comparative clinical evidence demonstrates that adrenalectomy provides superior nephroprotection versus pharmacological MR blockade alone, with greater GFR preservation at 12-month follow-up; emerging agents, including nonsteroidal MR antagonists (finerenone-class) and the aldosterone synthase inhibitor baxdrostat, are presented as promising targeted nephroprotective strategies. Notable limitations include the heterogeneity of cited studies, the predominantly Russian-language institutional context of contributing centers, and the absence of prospective randomized data directly comparing novel agents head-to-head in PA-related nephropathy; the article is written in Russian with an English abstract, which may limit broader dissemination.

Implications Medical Affairs : This review reinforces the clinical and regulatory relevance of mineralocorticoid receptor antagonism as a therapeutic pillar in secondary hypertension, lending scientific credibility to the growing portfolio of nonsteroidal MR antagonists and aldosterone synthase inhibitors entering late-stage clinical development. From a product positioning standpoint, the identification of early biomarkers (L-FABP, KIM-1, microalbuminuria) for PA-related renal injury opens a window for companion diagnostics strategies and could support labeling differentiation of next-generation MR antagonists with nephroprotective claims. For KOL engagement, the article points to a need for international prospective trials comparing baxdrostat and nonsteroidal MR antagonists in PA patients with CKD, representing a high-value unmet need that could anchor Medical Affairs-sponsored investigator-initiated studies.

Socioeconomic deprivation and dementia risk in New Zealand: A modelling study.

Revue : The Australian and New Zealand journal of psychiatry | Tier : NA | IF estime : NA

Date de publication : 2026-08-02

Resultats principaux : This population-level modelling study from Aotearoa New Zealand quantified the population attributable fraction (PAF) for dementia associated with 13 modifiable risk factors (including hypertension, obesity, smoking, physical inactivity, air pollution, and social isolation) stratified by neighbourhood deprivation quintile and ethnicity. The primary endpoint was the PAF for dementia across deprivation strata, projected over a 30-year horizon; secondary analyses estimated the absolute impact of hypothetical deprivation reduction on future dementia prevalence in Maori, Pacific, and European populations. The overall PAF increased from 36.0% in the least deprived quintile to 45.8% in the most deprived quintile, demonstrating a dose-response relationship between socioeconomic disadvantage and modifiable dementia risk, with hypertension featuring as a consistent risk factor across all strata. Notably, modelled reductions in deprivation produced disproportionately greater dementia prevalence reductions for Maori and Pacific peoples compared to Europeans at all 5-, 10-, and 30-year time points, underscoring the role of structural determinants as multiplicative risk amplifiers. Key limitations include the ecological and modelling nature of the study (no individual-level longitudinal data), reliance on deprivation indices that may not fully capture individual circumstances, and uncertainty in projection assumptions over 30 years.

Implications Medical Affairs : The finding that hypertension control is a major lever for dementia prevention across all socioeconomic strata strengthens the guideline rationale for aggressive blood pressure management as a cross-disease prevention strategy, reinforcing antihypertensive agents' value beyond cardiovascular outcomes. For product positioning, this evidence supports messaging that antihypertensive therapy contributes to cognitive resilience, particularly in high-deprivation and ethnically diverse populations, which could differentiate products with demonstrated central blood pressure or cerebrovascular benefits in Medical Affairs-sponsored educational initiatives. A KOL-actionable insight is the urgent need for equity-focused clinical trials and real-world evidence studies examining whether intensive hypertension management in deprived populations reduces dementia incidence, providing a clear framing for health economics and outcomes research partnerships.

Dietary intakes of anthocyanins and cardiometabolic health: a systematic review and meta-analysis of randomized trials and prospective cohort studies in healthy participants.

Revue : The American journal of clinical nutrition | Tier : NA | IF estime : NA

Date de publication : 2026-06-18

Resultats principaux : This pre-registered PROSPERO systematic review and meta-analysis (CRD420251015416) synthesised evidence from 18 prospective cohort studies (27 publications) and 65 randomized controlled trials (RCTs) examining the effect of dietary anthocyanin intake on cardiometabolic biomarkers and clinical endpoints exclusively in healthy participants. Across cohort studies, the highest versus lowest habitual anthocyanin intake was associated with statistically significant reductions in CVD incidence (RR -26%, 4 studies), myocardial infarction risk (-18%, 5 studies), type 2 diabetes incidence (-11%, 8 studies), CVD mortality (-9%, 10 studies), and hypertension risk (-8%, 6 studies), with GRADE evidence rated as moderate for CVD and MI. In RCTs, chronic anthocyanin supplementation (>=50 mg/d for >=1 day) significantly improved flow-mediated dilation (FMD) by 1.41% (95% CI: 0.90-1.91%), and acute administration improved FMD by 1.50% (95% CI: 1.16-1.84%) and reduced insulin concentrations by -2.24 pmol/L (-4.22 to -0.27); blood pressure, lipids, glucose, and HOMA-IR were not significantly affected in the overall RCT pool. Subgroup analyses by anthocyanin type revealed that cyanidin improved chronic FMD (1.62%), while delphinidin improved both acute and chronic FMD, reduced pulse wave velocity by -0.33 m/s (-0.62 to -0.05), lowered insulin by -2.32 pmol/L, and reduced triglycerides by -0.10 mmol/L (-0.17 to -0.03), suggesting differential effects by molecular subtype. Limitations include the heterogeneity of anthocyanin sources and doses across studies, the predominance of short-duration RCTs, the inability to isolate anthocyanin effects from overall dietary patterns in cohort studies, and possible publication bias.

Implications Medical Affairs : The consistent 8% hypertension risk reduction associated with higher anthocyanin intake, combined with demonstrated arterial elasticity improvements (FMD, pulse wave velocity), provides a scientific basis for positioning dietary anthocyanin-rich foods or supplements as adjuncts to pharmacological antihypertensive regimens, supporting guideline language around lifestyle co-interventions. From a Medical Affairs perspective, the differential molecular effects of cyanidin versus delphinidin offer a framework for precision nutrition messaging and could support partnerships with dietary supplement stakeholders or co-branding of physician guidance materials on food-drug synergies in hypertension management. Future investigator-initiated studies examining whether anthocyanin-rich dietary patterns enhance blood pressure response to first-line antihypertensives would provide clinically actionable evidence and create KOL engagement opportunities at cardiovascular nutrition intersections.

Prevalence of dyslipidemia in India: The ICMR-INDIAB National Study (ICMR-INDIAB-25).

Revue : Journal of clinical lipidology | Tier : NA | IF estime : NA

Date de publication : 2026-07-14

Resultats principaux : The ICMR-INDIAB-25 study is a nationally representative cross-sectional survey employing stratified multistage cluster sampling across all Indian states and union territories, involving 23,665 adults aged >=20 years with fasting lipid analysis, as a subsample of the broader 121,078-participant ICMR-INDIAB cohort. The primary endpoint was the weighted prevalence of dyslipidemia (defined per Lipid Association of India guidelines) and its components; secondary analyses assessed associated risk factors including hypertension, diabetes, obesity, age, sex, and lifestyle variables. The overall weighted prevalence of dyslipidemia was 87.3%, predominantly driven by low HDL-C (66.8%), with prevalence significantly higher in urban areas and among women; critically, 40% of dyslipidemic individuals had no concurrent diabetes, hypertension, or obesity, highlighting a large unscreened 'metabolically normal' population. Hypertension emerged as a significant independent predictor of dyslipidemia, with prevalence increasing consistently with worsening glycemic status, rising BMI, and blood pressure elevation; younger age, female sex, sedentary lifestyle, obesity, hypertension, and diabetes were all independently associated with dyslipidemia in multivariate models. Key limitations include the cross-sectional design precluding causal inference, reliance on fasting samples (precluding non-fasting lipid profiling), and potential underrepresentation of remote tribal populations despite the broad national sampling frame.

Implications Medical Affairs : The documentation of extreme dyslipidemia prevalence (87.3%) in a population where hypertension is a major co-driver establishes a compelling epidemiological case for combined cardiovascular risk management strategies in India, reinforcing the value proposition of antihypertensive agents with favorable lipid metabolic profiles. For product positioning, the finding that 40% of dyslipidemic individuals are metabolically 'normal' — lacking diagnosed hypertension, diabetes, or obesity — underscores the importance of broader cardiovascular risk screening campaigns, which Medical Affairs teams can support through KOL-driven public health awareness initiatives in partnership with Indian cardiology and endocrinology societies. This dataset provides a strategic opportunity to develop India-specific real-world evidence supporting earlier, combination antihypertensive-lipid-lowering therapy in younger adults, which aligns with global guideline trends toward aggressive early risk reduction.

The model incorporates noninvasive fibrosis scoring tools (FIB-4, APRI), nurse-led FibroScan services, lifestyle modification protocols, and digital follow-up systems embedded within existing maternal health and NCD (including hypertension and diabetes) platforms, drawing comparisons to the Gwent model in Wales. A vignette illustrating a high-risk patient undiagnosed for MASLD despite multiple hypertension and diabetes care encounters reinforces the systemic diagnostic gap; the article advocates for task-sharing and public-private partnerships to scale the model nationally. Predicting short-term blood pressure trajectories using lifestyle and behavioural factors: a prospective cohort study.

Revue : Global cardiology science & practice | Tier : NA | IF estime : NA

Date de publication : 2026-06-30

Resultats principaux : This 12-week prospective cohort study enrolled 75 adults (67 completing follow-up; mean age 44.6 +/- 10.8 years; 52.2% male) attending a preventive cardiology clinic, with systolic blood pressure (SBP) measured at baseline, 6 weeks, and 12 weeks using validated oscillometric devices, to evaluate the influence of lifestyle and behavioural factors on blood pressure trajectories. Primary endpoints were predictors of SBP change (assessed by linear mixed-effects regression) and 12-week SBP prediction accuracy (Elastic Net regression model); lifestyle exposures were quantified using validated instruments: sodium intake and DASH adherence (Food Frequency Questionnaire), physical activity (IPAQ-SF), and psychological stress (PSS-10). Mean SBP declined from 134.2 +/- 12.6 mmHg at baseline to 131.4 +/- 11.5 mmHg at 12 weeks; higher physical activity (p<0.05) and greater DASH adherence (p<0.05) were independently associated with SBP reduction, while higher sodium intake and elevated stress scores predicted SBP elevation (both p<0.05). Subgroup analysis revealed that physical activity had a significantly stronger SBP-lowering effect in participants under 45 years, and that psychological stress amplified the hypertensive effect of high sodium intake, suggesting synergistic interaction between behavioural factors. The Elastic Net model achieved strong predictive performance (R^2 = 0.78; RMSE = 5.9 mmHg), though notable limitations include the small sample size (n=67 completers), single-center design, short 12-week observation window, absence of a control group, and potential confounding by concurrent pharmacotherapy not fully characterized.

Implications Medical Affairs : The demonstration that modifiable behavioural factors (physical activity, DASH adherence, sodium intake, stress) account for approximately 78% of SBP variance over 12 weeks reinforces existing guideline recommendations for lifestyle intervention as first-line hypertension prevention, and provides quantitative evidence that can be incorporated into Medical Affairs educational content for prescribers. The age-stratified effect of physical activity (stronger in <45 years) and the stress-sodium synergy represent clinically actionable findings for patient stratification tools, supporting the development of digital health or adherence support programs for younger hypertension-risk patients that could be co-promoted alongside antihypertensive product portfolios. This study suggests that a future adequately powered randomized trial integrating structured lifestyle intervention with antihypertensive initiation could generate compelling composite endpoint data, representing an unmet evidence gap that Medical Affairs could champion through investigator-initiated study partnerships.

Spatial analysis of environmental and socioeconomic factors impacting maternal and infant health outcomes in North Carolina.

Revue : Journal of environmental studies and sciences | Tier : NA | IF estime : NA

Date de publication : 2025-11-06

Resultats principaux : This retrospective ecological study analysed spatial relationships between environmental stressors, socioeconomic factors (including racial and income segregation), and adverse maternal and infant health outcomes — including pregnancy-induced hypertension, preterm birth, low birth weight, and gestational diabetes mellitus — across North Carolina counties from 2011 to 2019. Bivariate Local Indicators of Spatial Association (LISA) and spatial regression models were applied to county-level data, using Risk-Screening Environmental Indicator (RSEI) toxicity-weighted concentrations as the primary environmental exposure metric and Index of Concentration of Extremes (ICE) as the socioeconomic determinant. A statistically significant association was identified between RSEI hazardous pollutant concentrations and adverse maternal outcomes including pregnancy-induced hypertension, persisting even after adjustment for racial segregation and income inequality, indicating an independent environmental toxicity pathway. Heatwaves (defined as any heatwave event during pregnancy) showed varied and largely non-significant associations with birth and maternal outcomes, suggesting that acute heat exposure may not be the primary environmental driver of adverse perinatal outcomes in this region. Key limitations include the ecological design precluding individual-level causal inference, the aggregation of diverse pollutants into a single RSEI index, limited temporal granularity of outcome data, and the inability to control for individual-level confounders such as prenatal care utilization or pre-pregnancy hypertension.

Implications Medical Affairs : The independent association between environmental chemical exposures and pregnancy-induced hypertension, even after adjusting for socioeconomic factors, highlights a significant environmental contribution to gestational hypertensive disorders, extending the Medical Affairs evidence base for antihypertensives into the maternal health domain. This spatial evidence supports advocacy for environmental health regulation as a complementary blood pressure prevention strategy, and Medical Affairs teams can leverage such data in policy-level engagements with public health authorities and regulatory agencies to broaden hypertension prevention frameworks beyond pharmacological intervention. The documented spatial clustering of pregnancy-induced hypertension in pollutant-exposed, socioeconomically deprived communities identifies priority geographies for targeted clinical research and real-world evidence generation, providing a roadmap for KOL engagement in environmental cardiology and obstetric hypertension.
