Trends in Modifiable Risk Factors Contributing to Ischemic Heart Disease Mortality in Afghanistan: Estimates From the Global Burden of Disease Study 2023 (1999-2023).

Revue : Cureus | Tier : NA | IF estime : NA

Date de publication : 2026-10-10

Resultats principaux : This retrospective analysis used Global Burden of Disease (GBD) 2023 estimates to examine trends in 14 modifiable risk factors contributing to ischemic heart disease (IHD) mortality in Afghanistan from 1999 to 2023, covering both sexes and all age groups using segmented log-linear trend analysis and multiple linear regression. The age-standardized IHD death rate attributable to all risk factors combined declined from 275.94 to 230.49 per 100,000 population, representing a 16.5% reduction over the study period (AAPC: -0.77%, 95% CI: -0.90 to -0.63; p < 0.001). High systolic blood pressure remained the dominant risk factor, accounting for 11,969.1 deaths (55.17% of all IHD deaths) in 2023, followed by high LDL cholesterol (5,875.8 deaths, 27.12%), both showing declining trends (AAPC: -0.95% and -1.14%, respectively; both p < 0.001). Conversely, IHD death rates attributable to sugar-sweetened beverage consumption increased significantly (AAPC: +1.28%, 95% CI: +0.94 to +1.63; p < 0.001), as did smoking-attributable rates (AAPC: +0.37%; p < 0.001), signaling an emerging dual burden of metabolic and behavioral risk factors. Female sex was associated with higher metabolic risk rates while males bore a higher smoking-attributable burden, with a notable methodological limitation that the trend analysis used a permutation-based approximation rather than the validated NCI Joinpoint software, warranting confirmatory analysis.

Implications Medical Affairs : The persistence of hypertension as the leading contributor to IHD mortality in Afghanistan despite an overall declining trend underscores the critical need for intensified blood pressure management in low-resource settings and strengthens the evidence base for global guideline recommendations prioritizing antihypertensive therapy in emerging markets. These GBD-derived data provide compelling KOL engagement material for positioning antihypertensive agents in regions where cardiovascular mortality remains predominantly driven by uncontrolled hypertension, particularly among female populations with high metabolic risk. The rising burden attributable to dietary and tobacco risk factors alongside infrastructure constraints presents an actionable research opportunity to investigate fixed-dose combination therapies addressing multiple cardiovascular risk factors simultaneously in resource-limited environments.

Health behavior profiles in early pregnancy are associated with adverse pregnancy outcomes and maternal blood pressure 2-7 years after delivery.

Revue : American Journal of Preventive Cardiology | Tier : NA | IF estime : NA

Date de publication : 2026-02-17

Resultats principaux : This prospective cohort study from the nuMoM2b Heart Health Study analyzed 8,700 nulliparous women to examine associations between first-trimester health behavior profiles—scored using the Life's Essential 8 framework for physical activity, diet, sleep duration, and smoking—and adverse pregnancy outcomes (APOs) and blood pressure measured 2-7 years postpartum. Researchers identified four latent behavioral profiles using latent profile analysis: Healthiest Behaviors (36%), Healthy Activity/Sleep with Poor Diet/Smoking (21%), Healthy Sleep Only (32%), and Least Healthy Behaviors (11%). Adjusted risk of APOs—including hypertensive disorders of pregnancy, preterm birth, gestational diabetes, small-for-gestational-age birth, and stillbirth—was 9-18% higher across less healthy behavioral profiles compared to the Healthiest Behaviors reference group. Among women without first-trimester obesity, adjusted systolic and diastolic blood pressure were 0.93 and 0.85 mmHg higher, respectively, 2-7 years after delivery in those with less healthy behavioral patterns, though associations were not significant among women with first-trimester obesity. Mediation analysis demonstrated that APOs did not mediate a significant proportion of the behavioral profile-blood pressure association, indicating predominantly direct pathways from early pregnancy health behaviors to long-term cardiovascular risk.

Implications Medical Affairs : These findings reinforce early pregnancy as a critical window for cardiovascular risk stratification, supporting guideline integration of structured behavioral screening using validated frameworks such as Life's Essential 8 during prenatal care and building a Medical Affairs case for earlier antihypertensive risk monitoring in women with poor periconceptional health profiles. The identification of first-trimester obesity as a significant effect modifier—nullifying the behavioral-BP association—has direct product positioning implications, suggesting that women with comorbid obesity may require distinct therapeutic strategies beyond behavioral modification alone for long-term blood pressure management. The absence of APO-mediated pathways points to independent biological mechanisms linking poor periconceptional health behaviors to hypertension risk, providing a compelling basis for KOL-driven advocacy around integrated cardiovascular prevention programs targeting reproductive-age women as an underserved patient segment.

Vaccination as a Strategy for Cardiovascular Prevention: Closing a Gap in Clinical Practice.

Revue : Reviews in Cardiovascular Medicine | Tier : NA | IF estime : NA

Date de publication : 2026-09-11

Resultats principaux : This narrative review synthesizes evidence on the role of vaccination as an evidence-based cardiovascular prevention strategy, examining pathophysiological links between respiratory infections—particularly influenza, COVID-19, Streptococcus pneumoniae, herpes zoster, and respiratory syncytial viruses—and acute ischemic events, heart failure destabilization, and cardiovascular hospitalizations in the context of classical risk factors including hypertension, dyslipidemia, and diabetes. The review highlights that environmental factors such as temperature, air pollution, and infectious diseases may explain substantial seasonal fluctuations in cardiovascular hospital admissions and contribute to "residual cardiovascular risk" persisting despite optimal management of traditional risk factors. Evidence presented indicates that respiratory infections can act as triggers for acute ischemic events and as destabilizing factors in stable patients with cardiovascular disease, with vaccination demonstrably reducing the frequency of these events, hospitalizations, and mortality. Leading cardiology societies now formally endorse vaccination against influenza, COVID-19, and other respiratory pathogens as an evidence-based cardiovascular prevention intervention, yet vaccination coverage remains suboptimal partly due to persistent anti-vaccination misinformation campaigns. A key limitation is the narrative, non-systematic design, which precludes quantitative pooling of vaccination efficacy estimates and may introduce selection bias in literature cited.

Implications Medical Affairs : The formal endorsement of vaccination as a cardiovascular prevention strategy by major cardiology societies creates a framework for Medical Affairs teams to incorporate infection risk into cardiovascular risk reduction messaging, potentially strengthening product differentiation narratives for antihypertensive agents by situating them within a broader residual-risk context that includes infectious triggers. The intersection of hypertension management and infection-triggered cardiovascular events represents an emerging opportunity to develop integrated KOL engagement strategies spanning cardiology and infectious disease specialties, supporting dual-audience educational programs on comprehensive cardiovascular risk reduction. Healthcare professional education initiatives addressing the mechanistic and clinical links between infections, inflammation, and hypertension-related cardiovascular events could enhance prescriber appreciation for multidimensional risk management and create advocacy opportunities around guideline adherence to vaccination recommendations as adjunct therapy.

The Role of Autoimmune Diseases in the Development of Aortic Aneurysms.

Revue : Reviews in Cardiovascular Medicine | Tier : NA | IF estime : NA

Date de publication : 2026-09-04

Resultats principaux : This narrative review examines pathogenic relationships between autoimmune diseases and aortic aneurysm development, synthesizing evidence on how autoimmune hallmarks—chronic systemic inflammation, immune dysregulation, and vascular tissue remodeling—create a pro-aneurysmal vascular environment across thoracic and abdominal aortic segments. The review explores specific associations between aortic aneurysms and autoimmune disorders including rheumatoid arthritis, systemic lupus erythematosus, and related inflammatory conditions, with particular emphasis on the thoracic aorta where sudden death is frequently the first and only clinical manifestation. Traditional risk factors for aortic aneurysms—advanced age, hypertension, and atherosclerosis—are contextualized alongside autoimmune-mediated vascular pathology, with evidence suggesting that immune pathways may amplify classical hemodynamic and metabolic risk factors synergistically. The review addresses diagnostic and management implications, including potentially more aggressive aneurysm surveillance in patients with combined hypertension and autoimmune comorbidities, and explores therapeutic strategies targeting both hemodynamic control and immunological mechanisms. A central limitation is the narrative review design relying on heterogeneous published literature, which limits the capacity to establish causal relationships or quantify relative contributions of autoimmune versus classical risk factors to aneurysm formation or progression.

Implications Medical Affairs : The convergence of hypertension and autoimmune-mediated vascular inflammation as co-pathogenic drivers of aortic aneurysm development highlights an underrecognized high-risk patient subgroup where targeted blood pressure management may carry additional vascular protective benefit beyond standard antihypertensive indications, strengthening the clinical rationale for rigorous BP control in patients with autoimmune comorbidities. Medical Affairs teams could leverage these insights to develop differentiated educational messaging around the dual cardiovascular-immunological risk profile in hypertensive patients with autoimmune diseases, enabling cross-specialty KOL engagement across cardiology and rheumatology audiences for a broader appreciation of antihypertensive therapy value. The emerging mechanistic understanding of immune-driven vascular remodeling as an aneurysm risk pathway may support future investigator-initiated research proposals exploring the protective role of anti-inflammatory antihypertensive combination strategies, providing a scientific foundation for innovative trial design in this high-risk population.
