[An interdisciplinary approach to hypertension management]

Revue : Revue medicale suisse | Tier : NA | IF estime : NA

Date de publication : 2026-09-09

Resultats principaux : This review article examines the rationale for an interdisciplinary, patient-centered approach to hypertension management within the Swiss healthcare context, where approximately 1.5 million people live with hypertension out of nearly one billion affected worldwide. The article highlights that despite the availability of effective antihypertensive treatments, blood pressure control remains suboptimal due to therapeutic inertia, poor medication adherence, and limited health literacy among patients. The authors review the complementary roles of healthcare professionals including primary care physicians, pharmacists, and specialized nurses in achieving blood pressure targets, drawing on evidence from international scientific societies advocating for comprehensive care models. No original quantitative data are reported; however, the authors reference established benchmarks suggesting that a substantial proportion of treated hypertensive patients fail to achieve guideline-recommended BP targets. Limitations include the narrative review format without systematic methodology, potential lack of generalizability outside the Swiss context, and absence of comparative effectiveness data between interdisciplinary and standard care models.

Implications Medical Affairs : This review reinforces that pharmaceutical companies must engage beyond prescribers to support adherence and patient education programs across the full care continuum, as interdisciplinary team models are increasingly endorsed by major scientific societies. From a product-positioning standpoint, the article highlights the strategic opportunity to develop KOL partnerships with pharmacists and specialized nurses as key influencers for antihypertensive therapy optimization and persistence. Medical Affairs teams should leverage this framework to design multi-stakeholder educational initiatives targeting the interdisciplinary team model now advocated by ESC and Swiss hypertension society guidelines.

Extreme Heat Exposure and Blood Pressure in Community Health Center Patients

Revue : JAMA Network Open | Tier : NA | IF estime : NA

Date de publication : 2026-09-01

Resultats principaux : This large cross-sectional study analyzed 4,221,866 ambulatory encounters from 2,554 community health centers across 40 US states during summer months (June-August) of 2019 to 2023, representing one of the largest investigations of acute heat exposure effects on blood pressure in routine outpatient settings. The primary finding was that same-day extreme heat exposure was associated with modest but statistically significant reductions in systolic blood pressure (-0.7 mmHg; 95% CI -0.8 to -0.6) and diastolic blood pressure (-0.3 mmHg; 95% CI -0.3 to -0.2), with effects peaking on the day of exposure and attenuating by day 3 in distributed lag nonlinear models across an 8-day window. Subgroup analyses revealed that older adults (age ≥65 years) experienced larger BP reductions under extreme heat (systolic: -1.1 mmHg vs -0.6 mmHg in those aged under 65; diastolic: -0.8 mmHg vs -0.3 mmHg), and hypertension status significantly modified the association with systolic but not diastolic BP (omnibus interaction P=0.003). The study population was predominantly female (63%), mostly without a hypertension diagnosis (81%), and demonstrated significant racial, ethnic, and geographic diversity, enhancing external generalizability. Limitations include the cross-sectional design precluding causal inference, potential residual confounding from unmeasured variables such as hydration status and indoor temperature, and ecological assignment of heat exposure at the clinic level rather than the individual patient level.

Implications Medical Affairs : These findings have direct regulatory relevance, as heat-related blood pressure variability may confound antihypertensive efficacy assessments in real-world evidence studies conducted during summer months, necessitating season-adjusted analyses in post-marketing surveillance and registry designs. From a product-positioning perspective, this research underscores the need to communicate to prescribers that BP measurements taken during extreme heat events may systematically underestimate true blood pressure control, potentially leading to inappropriate decisions about treatment escalation or modification. Medical Affairs teams should engage KOLs at the intersection of climate health and cardiovascular medicine and consider developing patient education materials that address consistent BP monitoring across seasonal climate variations, particularly for vulnerable older patients.

Chronic conditions and aortic disease risk: a prospective cohort study with predictive and etiological analyses

Revue : Nature Communications | Tier : NA | IF estime : NA

Date de publication : 2026-08-11

Resultats principaux : This prospective cohort study investigated associations between 42 chronic conditions and multimorbidity with incident aortic disease using UK Biobank (UKB) as the primary cohort and China Kadoorie Biobank (CKB) for external validation, representing a comprehensive multinational approach to characterizing aortic disease risk determinants. After false discovery rate correction, 21 chronic conditions were significantly associated with overall aortic disease in UKB, including coronary heart disease, hypertension, atrial fibrillation, peripheral vascular disease, heart failure, and COPD, with consistent patterns reproduced in CKB. Aortic atherosclerosis showed the broadest comorbidity profile across conditions, while dissection and aneurysm subtypes showed narrower comorbidity associations, suggesting heterogeneous underlying pathophysiology across aortic disease subtypes. Critically, hypertension demonstrated the largest estimated population-attributable fraction for aortic disease overall, with additional contributions from COPD and chronic kidney disease; Mendelian randomization provided causal support for selected associations, and machine-learning models demonstrated acceptable internal discriminatory performance for risk stratification. Limitations include potential selection bias toward healthier populations in biobank cohorts, incomplete capture of aortic disease subtypes in some participants, and restriction of machine-learning validation to the internal dataset, warranting caution in clinical implementation.

Implications Medical Affairs : The identification of hypertension as carrying the largest population-attributable fraction for aortic disease strengthens the clinical argument that optimizing blood pressure control may reduce not only classical cardiovascular endpoints but also aortic complications, supporting potential label expansion or indication broadening discussions for antihypertensive agents. This study provides Medical Affairs with compelling data to position hypertension treatment within a broader aortic disease prevention framework, an underexplored clinical niche that may resonate with cardiovascular KOLs, vascular surgeons, and cardiologists managing patients with multimorbidity. Future real-world evidence initiatives should consider incorporating aortic events as secondary cardiovascular outcomes in hypertension registries, and Medical Affairs should coordinate with clinical development teams to explore retrospective analyses of whether existing trial datasets can quantify aortic protection benefits of specific antihypertensive drug classes.

Profile, follow-up adherence and progression of people with advanced chronic kidney disease in specialized care centers: a retrospective study, Santa Catarina state, Brazil, 2021-2024

Revue : Epidemiologia e Servicos de Saude | Tier : NA | IF estime : NA

Date de publication : 2026-04-10

Resultats principaux : This retrospective multicenter study characterized 547 patients with stage 4 and 5 chronic kidney disease managed at five specialized outpatient centers in Santa Catarina state, Brazil between November 2021 and October 2024, with 83.0% of patients at CKD stage 4. The study population was predominantly elderly (80.6% aged over 60 years) with a high burden of cardiovascular risk factors: 91.8% had hypertension, 51.6% had diabetes, and 65.6% were overweight or obese, reflecting the typical multimorbid advanced CKD population seen in Brazilian public health settings. Among the 237 patients who started follow-up by October 2023, 90.2% completed one year of follow-up, and the median estimated glomerular filtration rate remained stable over 12 months (22 [IQR 18-26] versus 22 [IQR 18-27] ml/min/1.73m2; p=0.300), suggesting that specialized care may attenuate disease progression in this population. Only 2.1% progressed to renal replacement therapy during the observation period, indicating that structured nephrology follow-up may delay dialysis initiation in this very high-risk group. Limitations include the retrospective single-state design limiting generalizability, absence of a control group receiving non-specialized care, and incomplete data on medication use and adherence to nephroprotective treatments including renin-angiotensin-aldosterone system blockers.

Implications Medical Affairs : The extremely high prevalence of hypertension (91.8%) in advanced CKD reinforces the importance of rigorous blood pressure management as a cornerstone of nephroprotection, creating strategic opportunities for Medical Affairs to engage nephrologists with positioning data on RAAS-based therapies at the intersection of CKD and hypertension. The observed eGFR stabilization under specialized follow-up care provides indirect evidence that comprehensive disease management including optimized antihypertensive therapy may meaningfully delay CKD progression and renal replacement therapy initiation, supporting value-based healthcare narratives for premium antihypertensives. Medical Affairs teams targeting Latin American markets with high uncontrolled hypertension burdens should consider investing in KOL development at nephrology centers and developing materials positioning antihypertensives within cardiorenal syndrome prevention frameworks aligned with emerging guidelines.

[Primary hyperaldosteronism: updates]

Revue : Revue medicale suisse | Tier : NA | IF estime : NA

Date de publication : 2026-09-09

Resultats principaux : This review article summarizes recent advances in the diagnosis and management of primary hyperaldosteronism (PA), a common yet underdiagnosed cause of hypertension estimated to affect 10 to 15% of all hypertensive patients and associated with higher cardiovascular risk than essential hypertension. The authors detail updated recommendations from the Endocrine Society (2025) and the European Society of Cardiology (2024), which both advocate for systematic screening of all patients with newly diagnosed hypertension using the aldosterone-to-renin ratio as the primary diagnostic test, a significant widening of prior screening criteria. A new severity classification system for PA is presented to assist clinicians in assessing disease severity and estimating the probability of a unilateral versus bilateral form, with direct implications for treatment selection between mineralocorticoid receptor antagonists and surgical adrenalectomy. Current treatment recommendations reinforce MRAs (spironolactone or eplerenone) for bilateral disease and surgery for lateralized PA aligned with patient preference, while also highlighting emerging aldosterone synthase inhibitors currently in clinical development. Limitations of this review include the narrative rather than systematic format, reliance on expert consensus for the new classification system, and the absence of head-to-head efficacy or safety comparisons between aldosterone synthase inhibitors and established MRA therapies.

Implications Medical Affairs : The expansion of PA screening recommendations to all newly diagnosed hypertensive patients by both ESC 2024 and Endocrine Society 2025 guidelines represents a landmark broadening of the relevant patient population, providing Medical Affairs an immediate opportunity to educate primary care physicians, cardiologists, and endocrinologists on the revised diagnostic algorithm and its downstream treatment implications. The clinical development of aldosterone synthase inhibitors represents both a potential competitive landscape shift and an opportunity for pipeline positioning, making it essential for Medical Affairs to closely monitor emerging data and prepare comparative effectiveness narratives relative to established MRA therapy. PA should be elevated as a high-priority sub-topic in hypertension KOL engagement strategy, particularly at the primary care-specialist interface where detection and referral rates remain suboptimal despite strengthened guideline endorsements.

High-intensity interval training in aged female mice preserves physical, cognitive, and cardiovascular function

Revue : GeroScience | Tier : NA | IF estime : NA

Date de publication : 2026-09-10

Resultats principaux : This preclinical study investigated the effects of 14 weeks of high-intensity interval training (HIIT) versus sedentary control on physical, cognitive, and cardiovascular function in 24-month-old aged female C57BL/6J mice (n=9 per group), extending prior work that had demonstrated HIIT benefits in male mice to the female sex. HIIT significantly improved multiple functional domains: aerobic capacity increased by 71% in trained mice versus no change in sedentary controls; four-limb strength and endurance declined by 67% in sedentary versus only 28% in HIIT; overall motor function improved by 39% and executive function by 73% with HIIT compared to no change in sedentary animals. Of greatest cardiovascular relevance, HIIT produced significant reductions in systolic blood pressure (-17 mmHg, a 12% reduction) and mean arterial pressure (-16 mmHg), while also markedly reducing cardiac fibrosis and mitigating frailty onset. Neuroinflammatory benefits were evidenced by reduced IL-1beta expression in the hippocampus, suggesting pleiotropic cardiometabolic and neuroprotective effects of structured exercise in aged female animals. Limitations include the purely preclinical nature restricting direct clinical translation, small sample size (n=9 per group), use of a single inbred mouse strain (C57BL/6J), and the absence of mechanistic investigation into specific pathways linking HIIT to observed blood pressure reductions.

Implications Medical Affairs : Although preclinical, this study adds to a growing evidence base supporting high-intensity exercise as an effective non-pharmacological antihypertensive intervention, reinforcing Medical Affairs messaging around lifestyle modification as a validated complement to pharmacotherapy in hypertension management guidelines. From a product-positioning perspective, demonstrating that exercise and antihypertensive drug therapy can be synergistic rather than competing approaches is clinically relevant for the growing older female population, where both frailty prevention and cardiovascular risk reduction are dual priorities in aging medicine. Medical Affairs teams should monitor the translational pipeline of HIIT clinical trials, as human data confirming BP reductions of the magnitude seen here could reshape guideline recommendations for non-pharmacological management and inform future combination therapy strategy narratives.

Empowering and supporting Latino immigrants to manage chronic disease through a culturally relevant nutrition and physical activity intervention

Revue : Ethnicity & Health | Tier : NA | IF estime : NA

Date de publication : 2026-09-10

Resultats principaux : This mixed-methods study evaluated a culturally responsive lifestyle intervention combining nutrition and physical activity counseling among 243 Latino immigrant participants at a community health clinic partnered with the University of North Carolina at Charlotte, assessing clinical, anthropometric, and behavioral outcomes at baseline, 6 months, and one year. Significant improvements were observed across multiple cardiometabolic risk parameters including HbA1c, total cholesterol, triglycerides, LDL cholesterol, BMI, percent body fat, body weight, waist circumference, functional capacity, and symptoms of depression, anxiety, and stress, indicating broad multidimensional benefits from the program. Qualitative focus group data identified perceived social support as the most prominent motivator for sustaining healthy lifestyle changes, underscoring the critical role of community-based delivery mechanisms in underserved minority populations managing chronic disease. The intervention targeted participants with hypertension and diabetes, populations that carry a disproportionate cardiovascular risk burden in the Latino immigrant community in North Carolina, reflecting a broader unmet need in ethnic minority preventive care. Limitations include the absence of a randomized control group, potential selection bias toward motivated participants at a community clinic, limited reporting on specific hypertension endpoints such as blood pressure values, and restricted generalizability to Latino immigrant populations in other geographic or sociocultural contexts.

Implications Medical Affairs : This study demonstrates that culturally tailored, community-based interventions can achieve clinically meaningful cardiometabolic improvements in high-risk minority populations, providing actionable evidence that Medical Affairs teams should factor ethnic and cultural sensitivity into patient support programs and disease management materials accompanying antihypertensive therapies. The emphasis on social support as a key motivator for behavior change aligns directly with emerging frameworks for improving medication adherence in minority populations, suggesting that pharmaceutical companies designing companion support programs should integrate culturally responsive, community-anchored elements to maximize treatment persistence. Engaging KOLs from Latino and minority health research communities represents a strategically underutilized avenue for Medical Affairs to build credibility, demonstrate health equity commitment, and align with payer, regulatory, and guideline-setting bodies that are increasingly centering health equity and social determinants of health in their decision frameworks.
