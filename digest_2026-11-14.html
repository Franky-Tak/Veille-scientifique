TFAP2C Attenuates Hypertensive Endothelial Dysfunction by Transcriptionally Activating SPRY4: A Novel Axis Regulated by miR-3656.

Revue : FASEB Journal | Tier : NA | IF estime : NA

Date de publication : 2026-11-30

Resultats principaux : This translational study combined a clinical cohort of 91 hypertensive patients and 101 healthy controls with in vitro work in HUVECs and HAECs to investigate the role of TFAP2C in hypertensive endothelial dysfunction. Plasma TFAP2C levels were significantly lower in hypertensive patients compared with healthy controls (7.40 +/- 6.28 vs. 10.04 +/- 5.90, p = 0.003) and correlated inversely with blood pressure. TFAP2C overexpression enhanced endothelial proliferation, migration, and tube formation while reducing apoptosis; knockdown produced the opposite effects (all p < 0.05). SPRY4 was identified as a direct transcriptional target of TFAP2C via bioinformatics prediction, ChIP-qPCR, and dual-luciferase reporter assay, and SPRY4 overexpression partially reversed TFAP2C knockdown-induced endothelial dysfunction. Mechanistically, miR-3656 negatively regulated the TFAP2C/SPRY4 axis, establishing a novel miR-3656/TFAP2C/SPRY4 regulatory cascade. Limitations include the relatively small clinical cohort, the cross-sectional design for the biomarker arm, and the absence of in vivo validation in an animal hypertension model.

Implications Medical Affairs : The identification of TFAP2C as a circulating biomarker of endothelial dysfunction offers a potential monitoring tool that Medical Affairs teams could incorporate into biomarker-stratified clinical trial designs for antihypertensive agents targeting the endothelium. The miR-3656/TFAP2C/SPRY4 axis represents a mechanistically novel pathway that may be of interest for pharmaceutical pipeline positioning vis-a-vis emerging endothelium-protective therapies. KOL engagement in translational hypertension research should consider investigators exploring microRNA-driven endothelial pathways, as this study may catalyze follow-on grant activity and international collaborations.

First recorded hypertension and subsequent cardiovascular and kidney outcomes after newly diagnosed type 2 diabetes: a population-based cohort study.

Revue : Frontiers in Endocrinology | Tier : NA | IF estime : NA

Date de publication : 2026-10-29

Resultats principaux : This population-based cohort study used regional electronic health records from Ningbo, China, enrolling 256,221 adults with newly diagnosed type 2 diabetes (T2D) and no prior hypertension or major cardiorenal disease between 2012 and 2024. During a median follow-up of 4.11 years, hypertension was first recorded in 107,946 participants (42.13%), and 34,371 primary outcomes (myocardial infarction, stroke, heart failure, chronic kidney disease, or all-cause death) occurred. Cox models adjusting for a broad set of covariates showed that newly recorded hypertension was associated with an adjusted hazard ratio of 2.49 (95% CI 2.43-2.55) for the primary composite outcome, and 2.47 (95% CI 2.40-2.55) for modified 3P-MACE. The risk was highest in the first 90 days after hypertension recognition (HR 3.37; 95% CI 3.10-3.65) and remained elevated beyond 3 years (HR 2.15; 95% CI 2.07-2.23). Despite this risk transition, recorded assessments such as urinary albumin-to-creatinine ratio testing increased only marginally (3.35% to 4.15%), suggesting a major gap in cardiorenal surveillance around hypertension recognition in T2D patients; a key limitation is the reliance on recorded rather than confirmed diagnoses.

Implications Medical Affairs : These findings underscore that the moment of hypertension recognition in T2D patients constitutes a critical cardiovascular risk inflection point, which has direct implications for guideline-based treatment initiation thresholds and the value proposition of early, aggressive antihypertensive therapy in this dual-morbidity population. For products positioned in the cardiorenal continuum, this study provides real-world epidemiological ammunition to support earlier combination therapy use and reinforces the unmet need in integrated T2D-hypertension management algorithms. Medical Affairs should leverage this dataset in KOL discussions around structured reassessment protocols at hypertension diagnosis in T2D, potentially as a framework for future investigator-initiated studies.

The mediation and interaction of body mass index between night shift frequency and hypertension among nursing staff.

Revue : BMC Health Services Research | Tier : NA | IF estime : NA

Date de publication : 2026-08-20

Resultats principaux : This large cross-sectional survey included 127,715 nursing staff from 512 hospitals across 11 cities in Hebei Province, China, collected between 2016 and 2018, using the four-way decomposition method to disentangle mediation and interaction components of BMI in the night-shift-hypertension relationship. Night shift frequency was significantly associated with hypertension prevalence in a dose-response manner: estimates were 1.09 (95% CI: 1.06-1.11) for >0 to <=5 shifts/month up to 1.38 (95% CI: 1.27-1.51) for irregular shifts (all p < 0.001). The direct effect of night shift exposure (controlled direct effect) accounted for the majority of the observed association, with BMI-mediated and interaction components being significant mainly among females and in primary-level hospitals with frequent or irregular shifts. Gender modified the effect: larger CDEs were observed in males, while BMI-related interaction components were prominent in females. Parental hypertension history amplified both the CDE and interaction components among nursing staff with frequent night shifts, indicating a gene-environment interaction; the cross-sectional design precludes causal inference.

Implications Medical Affairs : The dose-response relationship between shift work frequency and hypertension risk in a large healthcare worker cohort adds to the occupational risk factor literature, and may support arguments for structured blood pressure monitoring programs in healthcare institutions, potentially creating a new institutional market for digital hypertension management solutions. The finding that most of the night shift effect bypasses BMI as a mediator suggests that mechanisms beyond obesity -- such as circadian disruption and sleep deprivation -- may need to be addressed by complementary pharmacological or behavioral interventions. Medical Affairs can engage occupational health KOLs with this dataset to explore workplace cardiovascular prevention programs as a novel promotional context.

Combined association of drinking water salinity and psychosocial distress with hypertension among reproductive-aged women in coastal Bangladesh: a population-based cross-sectional study.

Revue : BMC Women's Health | Tier : NA | IF estime : NA

Date de publication : 2026-08-19

Resultats principaux : This population-based cross-sectional study surveyed 4,693 ever-married women aged 18 and older from coastal Bangladesh, sampling both high- and low-salinity areas using probability proportionate to size methodology, and defined hypertension as SBP >=140 mmHg, DBP >=90 mmHg, or antihypertensive use. Hypertension prevalence was 24.9%, and moderate/severe psychosocial distress (PHQ-ADS >=20) was present in 75.4% of participants. Independent predictors of hypertension included moderate distress (AOR 1.86; 95% CI 1.51-2.28), severe distress (AOR 1.88; 95% CI 1.51-2.32), and elevated drinking water sodium (AOR 1.10 per 100 mg/L; 95% CI 1.02-1.35). A significant multiplicative interaction was identified between high sodium (>2000 mg/L) and severe distress (interaction AOR 1.29; 95% CI 1.01-1.56; p = 0.011), yielding a combined stratified AOR of 2.45 (95% CI 1.79-3.36). These findings point to a novel environmental-psychosocial synergy in hypertension risk among climate-vulnerable women; limitations include the cross-sectional design, potential self-report bias, and the inability to establish temporality.

Implications Medical Affairs : This study identifies climate change as a structural hypertension risk factor through saline water ingestion combined with psychosocial distress, raising the profile of environmental determinants of hypertension that extend well beyond classical modifiable risk factors. For Medical Affairs, this expands the epidemiological rationale for hypertension treatment in underserved, climate-exposed populations, which can support health equity narratives in product positioning and CSR-linked access programs. KOL strategy should consider partnerships with global health researchers working at the climate-hypertension interface, an emerging field likely to attract substantial research funding in the coming years.

Multidimensional risk factors for hypertension among Moroccan women: a case-control study integrating lifestyle, reproductive, anthropometric, biological, and quality-of-life indicators.

Revue : BMC Women's Health | Tier : NA | IF estime : NA

Date de publication : 2026-08-18

Resultats principaux : This case-control study enrolled 430 Moroccan women (215 hypertensive cases and 215 normotensive controls) and collected sociodemographic, reproductive, clinical, lifestyle, dietary, anthropometric, body composition, biological, and health-related quality-of-life (HRQoL) data to identify multidimensional risk factors for hypertension. The strongest independent predictor was elevated waist-to-height ratio (AOR 2.83; 95% CI 1.61-4.98), followed by family history of hypertension (AOR 2.47; 95% CI 1.49-4.09), elevated visceral fat level (AOR 2.40; 95% CI 1.38-4.18), low physical activity (AOR 2.22; 95% CI 1.31-3.76), low SF-12 physical component score (AOR 2.18; 95% CI 1.29-3.68), low Mediterranean diet adherence (AOR 2.08; 95% CI 1.20-3.59), and hyperuricemia (AOR 2.01; 95% CI 1.15-3.50). The simultaneous integration of physical, metabolic, lifestyle, and QoL dimensions in a single model is a methodological strength allowing holistic risk profiling. Central adiposity and sedentary behavior emerged as the top modifiable targets in this North African population; the case-control design limits causal inference and reverse causation cannot be excluded.

Implications Medical Affairs : The finding that central adiposity, low physical activity, and poor diet quality are the leading modifiable risk factors in Moroccan hypertensive women aligns with and extends current international guidelines, reinforcing the importance of lifestyle intervention as a first-line prevention strategy in the MENA region. This study may strengthen the evidence base for combination antihypertensive therapy in patients presenting with clustered cardiometabolic risk, supporting a Medical Affairs narrative around comprehensive risk management rather than isolated blood pressure control. The focus on women and North African populations represents an underexplored KOL segment that Medical Affairs could cultivate for regional advisory boards and investigator-initiated research grants.

Cumulative exposure of the combined triglyceride-glucose and frailty index for incident hypertension prediction in middle-aged and older adults: a national cohort study.

Revue : BMC Cardiovascular Disorders | Tier : NA | IF estime : NA

Date de publication : 2026-08-15

Resultats principaux : This national cohort study derived from the China Health and Retirement Longitudinal Study (CHARLS) included 3,846 Chinese adults aged 45 or older without baseline hypertension, using cumulative TyGFI (a composite of triglyceride-glucose index and frailty index measured at two time points) to predict incident hypertension ascertained at 7- and 9-year follow-up. In fully adjusted Cox models, each 1-SD increase in cumulative TyGFI was associated with an 18% higher risk of incident hypertension (HR 1.18; 95% CI 1.10-1.27; p < 0.001), and participants in the highest quartile had a 45% higher risk compared to the lowest quartile (HR 1.45; 95% CI 1.16-1.80; p < 0.001), with a significant dose-response trend. Restricted cubic spline analyses supported a linear positive association, and subgroup analyses showed consistent results across strata (all p for interaction > 0.05). Cumulative TyGFI provided statistically significant but modest incremental predictive value over conventional risk factors at 9-year follow-up (Delta C-index 0.008; NRI 13.53%; IDI 0.43%). A key limitation is that the study population was restricted to Chinese middle-aged and older adults, limiting generalizability, and external validation has not yet been performed.

Implications Medical Affairs : The cumulative TyGFI index captures the temporal co-evolution of insulin resistance and frailty, two emerging risk dimensions not fully addressed by current hypertension risk scores, which may justify Medical Affairs investment in research supporting their integration into cardiovascular risk stratification tools. While the incremental predictive improvement is modest, the clinical relevance of identifying high-risk patients earlier -- particularly in an aging, metabolically complex population -- is significant for positioning antihypertensive agents in prevention rather than management. Medical Affairs and commercial teams should monitor this area as cumulative biomarker indices gain traction in guideline discussions, and consider sponsoring validation studies in Western cohorts to broaden the evidence base.

Cadonilimab combined with lenvatinib and transarterial chemoembolization for non-metastatic unresectable hepatocellular carcinoma: a multicenter, open-label, phase 2 study.

Revue : BMC Medicine | Tier : NA | IF estime : NA

Date de publication : 2026-08-13

Resultats principaux : This multicenter, open-label, single-arm phase 2 study conducted at 8 Chinese hospitals enrolled 62 patients with non-metastatic unresectable hepatocellular carcinoma (HCC) between June 2022 and May 2023, evaluating cadonilimab (anti-PD-1/CTLA-4 bispecific antibody, 10 mg/kg Q3W IV) combined with lenvatinib (8 or 12 mg/day PO) and up to 4 cycles of TACE, with progression-free survival per RECIST v1.1 as the primary endpoint. At a median follow-up of 25.7 months, the objective response rate was 38.3% (95% CI 26.1-51.8%), disease control rate was 96.7% (95% CI 88.5-99.6%), and median PFS was 11.20 months (95% CI 8.8-15.6). Median overall survival was not reached (95% CI 25.5-NE), with a 24-month OS rate of 68.0%. Treatment-related adverse events grade >=3 included hypertension in 19.4% of patients (along with elevated liver enzymes and thrombocytopenia), all described as manageable. Limitations include the single-arm design without a randomized comparator, the modest sample size, and the Chinese-only population, which limits generalizability. Note: this article was indexed under hypertension due to hypertension appearing as a treatment-related adverse event, not as the primary focus of the study.

Implications Medical Affairs : While this study primarily addresses HCC oncology, hypertension as a grade 3 adverse event in 19.4% of patients receiving lenvatinib-based regimens highlights an important pharmacovigilance consideration for antihypertensive product positioning in the oncology supportive care market. Medical Affairs teams focused on hypertension can engage oncology KOLs around treatment-induced hypertension management, as anti-angiogenic therapy-associated hypertension is an underserved therapeutic niche with growing clinical significance. This case illustrates the importance of cross-specialty collaboration between oncology and cardiology-hypertension teams in managing cardiovascular toxicities of cancer treatments.

Molecular to Clinical Perspectives in Arrhythmogenic Cardiomyopathy: Current Understanding and Future Therapeutic Directions.

Revue : FASEB Journal | Tier : NA | IF estime : NA

Date de publication : 2026-11-30

Resultats principaux : This comprehensive review article systematically covers the genetics, histological features, molecular mechanisms, clinical presentation, diagnosis, biomarkers, and treatment advances of arrhythmogenic cardiomyopathy (ACM), a genetic heart muscle disease affecting primarily the right ventricle that can lead to sudden cardiac death. ACM is predominantly caused by mutations in desmosomal genes, with key pathogenic pathways including cardiomyocyte loss, fibrosis, and adipogenesis operating through Wnt signaling, PPARgamma signaling, and the Hippo-YAP pathway, as well as an inflammatory component. The International Task Force Criteria distinguish minor and major ACM criteria covering family history, conduction defects, morphological changes, repolarization defects, and arrhythmias. Therapeutic modalities discussed include epicardial and endocardial ablation with electromagnetic mapping, implantable cardiac defibrillators, gene therapy, small-molecule therapies, radiofrequency ablation, and heart transplantation. Lifestyle interventions are highlighted as important adjuncts, particularly restriction of competitive sport. Note: this article was indexed under the hypertension risk factors search due to the cardiovascular department affiliation of the corresponding authors, not as a primary hypertension study.

Implications Medical Affairs : While this review focuses on ACM rather than hypertension, it underscores the broader cardiovascular genetics landscape in which hypertension co-occurs and may exacerbate desmosomal cardiomyopathies, suggesting a potential niche for antihypertensive therapy as a co-management strategy in ACM patients. The therapeutic innovation pipeline described -- including gene therapy and small-molecule approaches -- reflects the broader trend toward precision medicine in cardiovascular disease that Medical Affairs teams tracking hypertension should monitor for competitive intelligence. KOL engagement in inherited cardiovascular disease may reveal opportunities for collaborative research on hypertension as a modifier of outcomes in cardiomyopathy patients.

Advancing Health Equity Through Community Ownership, Small-Scale Built Modification, and Meaningful Engagement: the JUMP Initiative.

Revue : Journal of Racial and Ethnic Health Disparities | Tier : NA | IF estime : NA

Date de publication : 2026-11-12

Resultats principaux : This descriptive paper introduces the Just Us Moving Program (JUMP), a community-engaged, lifestyle-focused intervention designed to reduce chronic disease burden -- including hypertension, diabetes, and cardiovascular disease -- in food-insecure Black and Hispanic communities in the United States. The conceptual framework integrates community-based participatory research (CBPR), the Consolidated Framework for Implementation Research (CFIR), and the Assessing Community Engagement (ACE) framework, operationalized through aeroponic community gardens, experiential learning, SMART goal-setting, motivational interviewing, and iterative community feedback. Current evidence is formative, derived from early pilot studies and community engagement activities rather than a formal effectiveness trial, and the authors acknowledge that findings are hypothesis-generating. Pilot data suggested participant interest in lifestyle change, relevance of structural food access supports, and the critical importance of trust-building, cultural responsiveness, and long-term partnership in sustaining community engagement. Major limitations are the absence of a comparison group and randomization, modest pilot sample sizes, and the unproven sustainability of community ownership; a full effectiveness trial is pending.

Implications Medical Affairs : The JUMP initiative illustrates the growing body of evidence linking food insecurity and built environment deficits to hypertension risk in minority populations, reinforcing the social determinants of health framework that is increasingly shaping guideline narratives and regulatory expectations for inclusive clinical trial enrollment. Medical Affairs teams can reference community-engaged lifestyle intervention data to position antihypertensive agents not as standalone solutions but as components of a comprehensive care model addressing structural barriers, particularly in public health advocacy and payer engagement. Partnering with academic institutions conducting CBPR-based programs may offer access to underrepresented populations for clinical trial recruitment and generate health equity data supportive of label extensions or real-world evidence studies.

Beyond Vascular Territories: The Dual Pathology of Posterior Cerebral Artery Stroke With Reversible Cytotoxic Splenial Lesion.

Revue : Cureus | Tier : NA | IF estime : NA

Date de publication : 2026-11-11

Resultats principaux : This case report describes a 28-year-old male with pre-existing hypertension, poorly controlled type 2 diabetes, high BMI, and multiple atherosclerotic risk factors including polysubstance use (cannabis, cigarettes, vaping) who presented with acute-onset left-sided weakness, blurred vision, and paraesthesia. Initial non-contrast CT head was unremarkable; subsequent MRI demonstrated acute signal changes in the splenium of the corpus callosum and right hippocampus, with CT angiography revealing right posterior cerebral artery (PCA) occlusion at the P2 segment. The atypical location prompted consideration of a concurrent cytotoxic lesion of the corpus callosum (CLOCC) superimposed on ischemia, strongly suspected to be metabolically driven by severe hyperglycemia. Serial MRI showed near-complete regression of the splenial signal abnormality following glycemic optimization, while PCA stroke changes persisted, confirming dual pathology. Management included dual antiplatelet therapy, strict glucose and blood pressure control, and lifestyle modification; as a single case report, it has very limited generalizability and cannot establish causal relationships.

Implications Medical Affairs : This case highlights that hypertension, in combination with other vascular risk factors such as uncontrolled diabetes and polysubstance use, dramatically increases the complexity of acute neurological presentations and the diagnostic workup burden, reinforcing the importance of comprehensive vascular risk factor control. For Medical Affairs, this case exemplifies the multimorbidity context in which antihypertensive therapy operates and strengthens the rationale for holistic risk reduction strategies including combination cardiometabolic management. KOL engagement in stroke neurology and vascular medicine may surface opportunities to highlight the cerebrovascular consequences of uncontrolled hypertension in combination with metabolic risk factors.

Diversity and Functionality of the Gut Microbiota in Hypertensive Patients from Different Regions of Peru.

Revue : F1000Research | Tier : NA | IF estime : NA

Date de publication : 2026-11-06

Resultats principaux : This observational cross-sectional study analyzed gut microbiota composition via 16S rRNA gene amplicon sequencing (V3-V4 region) in 84 adults from nine Peruvian regions: 46 hypertensive and 38 normotensive, examining alpha and beta diversity, and functional metabolic pathway profiles. The dominant phyla across all groups were Bacillota and Bacteroidota; at genus level, one genus was associated with hypertensive patients with BMI >=25, while another appeared exclusively in normotensive individuals with BMI <25. Alpha diversity was greater in hypertensive participants under 50 years with BMI <25, while lower diversity was found in normotensive adults over 50 with BMI >=25. Beta diversity analyses showed substantial overlap across hypertensive status, age, and regional groups, and adjusted multivariable models confirmed no significant independent association between hypertension status and either alpha or beta diversity after accounting for age, BMI, and region. Geographic region was the strongest independent correlate of community composition (R2 = 16.3%; p = 0.001), suggesting that environmental and nutritional context exerts greater influence on microbiota than hypertension status alone. Functional profiles were similar across groups, with main involvement in carbohydrate, amino acid, and lipid metabolism; limitations include the small sample size, cross-sectional design, and lack of dietary data.

Implications Medical Affairs : The finding that geographic region -- a proxy for diet and environmental exposures -- drives gut microbiota composition more strongly than hypertension status per se suggests that microbiome-based biomarkers for hypertension will need to be population- and region-specific, with important implications for precision medicine approaches to antihypertensive therapy development. Medical Affairs can leverage the growing microbiome-hypertension literature to position products that may favorably modulate gut-mediated blood pressure pathways, though this study cautions against over-extrapolating microbiome findings across populations. KOL strategy should include Latin American academic researchers at the intersection of environmental health, nutrition, and hypertension, as this region represents an underrepresented but scientifically active frontier in cardiovascular epidemiology.
