Hypertensive disorders in pregnancies with early-onset fetal growth restriction: a retrospective cohort study.

Revue : The Journal of Maternal-Fetal & Neonatal Medicine | Tier : NA | IF estime : NA

Date de publication : 2026-07-26

Resultats principaux : This retrospective cohort study enrolled 774 pregnant individuals with suspected early-onset fetal growth restriction (FGR), defined as onset before 32 weeks of gestation, presenting to a tertiary university-affiliated medical center in Israel between 2011 and 2024. The primary endpoint was the subsequent development of hypertensive disorder of pregnancy (HDP), and 79 individuals (10.2%) of the cohort ultimately developed HDP. Multivariate logistic regression identified three independent predictors of HDP: umbilical artery Doppler pulsatility index (UAPI) above the 95th percentile (OR 3.35, 95% CI 1.55-7.20, p < 0.01), gestational age at FGR diagnosis of 24-28 weeks (OR 2.54, 95% CI 1.20-5.36, p = 0.01), and abdominal circumference below the 5th percentile (OR 2.31, 95% CI 1.10-4.86, p = 0.02), with elevated UAPI being the strongest single predictor. No significant subgroup analyses by parity, mode of conception, or comorbidity (thrombophilia, chronic renal disease) were reported in the multivariate model, though these variables were examined in univariate analyses. Limitations include the retrospective, single-center design at a tertiary referral center, reliance on hospital records which may introduce information bias, and a potential case-mix that may not be generalizable to lower-acuity obstetric settings.

Implications Medical Affairs : This study provides clinically actionable risk stratification criteria for HDP in pregnancies complicated by early-onset FGR, aligning with broader guideline trends toward earlier and more individualized surveillance of high-risk pregnancies, which may reinforce recommendations for antihypertensive readiness in this obstetric population. From a Medical Affairs perspective, the identification of Doppler and biometric predictors of HDP creates a strategic opportunity to position antihypertensive agents within an evidence-based clinical pathway targeting this vulnerable obstetric subgroup, particularly in tertiary maternal-fetal medicine centers. Engaging maternal-fetal medicine KOLs to develop research integrating these clinical predictors with angiogenic biomarkers such as sFlt-1/PlGF ratios could advance risk stratification science while supporting product differentiation narratives for hypertensive complications of pregnancy.

Metabolic Dysfunction in Psychiatric Disorders: A Narrative Review of Shared Pathways Between Mental Illness and Cardiometabolic Disease.

Revue : Cureus | Tier : NA | IF estime : NA

Date de publication : 2026-06-25

Resultats principaux : This narrative review synthesized recent evidence on the bidirectional and overlapping biological pathways connecting psychiatric disorders (depression, anxiety, schizophrenia, and severe mental illness) with cardiometabolic diseases including hypertension, diabetes, dyslipidemia, and cardiovascular disease, drawing from major biomedical databases with literature prioritized by relevance, clinical significance, recency, and methodological quality. The review identified key shared mechanistic pathways including chronic low-grade inflammation, hypothalamic-pituitary-adrenal (HPA) axis dysregulation, insulin resistance, oxidative stress, mitochondrial dysfunction, gut microbiome disruption, sleep and circadian disturbance, and psychotropic medication effects that collectively increase cardiometabolic risk (CMR) in psychiatric populations. Antipsychotic-associated weight gain and glucose-lipid abnormalities were highlighted as clinically significant contributors, though metabolic changes were noted to precede long-term treatment exposure particularly in psychotic disorders, indicating intrinsic disease-related metabolic vulnerability independent of medication effects. No formal subgroup analyses or pooled quantitative estimates were performed given the narrative rather than systematic methodology, and the directionality of observed associations remains uncertain given the predominantly cross-sectional evidence base. Limitations include the narrative review methodology subject to selection bias, absence of meta-analytic synthesis, heterogeneity of study populations and psychiatric diagnoses across reviewed studies, and limited longitudinal data clarifying the temporal sequence and causal mechanisms underlying psychiatric-cardiometabolic comorbidity.

Implications Medical Affairs : This review reinforces the scientific basis for treating hypertension and broader cardiometabolic risk as core components of psychiatric care, which has direct implications for integrated guideline development spanning psychiatry, cardiology, and primary care, and supports calls for routine blood pressure screening in patients with severe mental illness. From a Medical Affairs perspective, the demonstrated association between psychosocial stress, psychiatric comorbidity, and hypertension risk creates a compelling narrative for positioning antihypertensive therapies within integrated cardiometabolic care models targeting patients with dual psychiatric-cardiometabolic burden, a growing patient segment with high unmet need. KOL engagement strategies targeting psychiatrists, primary care physicians, and cardiologists around shared care models and metabolic screening protocols could amplify medical education initiatives, drive earlier diagnosis of hypertension in psychiatric populations, and open new pathways for product uptake in this underserved group.
