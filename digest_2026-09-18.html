Intensification of blood pressure lowering therapeutics based on diuretics versus usual management for uncontrolled hypertension in patients with moderate to severe chronic kidney disease (THINK): protocol for an open label, two-parallel group, cluster randomised controlled, superiority, phase 3 trial.

Revue : BMJ Open | Tier : NA | IF estime : NA

Date de publication : 2026-09-17

Resultats principaux : The THINK trial is a multicenter open-label cluster randomised superiority phase 3 trial enrolling 720 patients with moderate to severe chronic kidney disease (CKD) across 40 centres in France to test the hypothesis that these patients are chronically fluid-overloaded and therefore that a diuretic should be the preferred second-line antihypertensive agent after ACEi or ARB. The primary composite endpoint combines kidney failure requiring replacement therapy, eGFR decline of 40% or more, major cardiovascular events (myocardial infarction, heart failure hospitalisation, stroke), and all-cause mortality. Secondary endpoints include blood pressure control rates at 2 years, changes in eGFR and proteinuria at 3 and 6 months and every 6 months thereafter, proportion of patients using at least one diuretic during the trial, quality of life, and safety outcomes. The trial was approved by ethics committee CPP Ile-de-France VII in January 2023 (ref: 2022-5 01 494-39-00) and is registered as NCT05732727. Key methodological limitations include the open-label design which may introduce performance and assessment bias, and the cluster randomisation structure which may reduce individual-level precision compared to individual randomisation.

Implications Medical Affairs : The THINK trial directly addresses an unresolved clinical question highlighted in current hypertension and CKD guidelines — what antihypertensive agent to add after first-line ACEi/ARB in uncontrolled CKD hypertension — and a positive result could drive a major shift in guideline recommendations towards diuretic-first intensification strategies in this population. For pharmaceutical companies with diuretic or cardiorenal portfolios, this trial represents a high-priority asset to monitor, as positive outcomes would provide the strongest available evidence to support product positioning of loop or thiazide-like diuretics as standard-of-care second-line agents in CKD-associated hypertension. Medical Affairs teams should proactively engage with nephrology and hypertension KOLs at investigator sites and at congresses such as ERA and ESH to build scientific exchange around cardiorenal treatment algorithms before the trial results are published.

Machine learning and deep learning-based prediction of hypertension and analysis of its major risk factors in Bangladesh.

Revue : PloS One | Tier : NA | IF estime : NA

Date de publication : 2026-09-17

Resultats principaux : This cross-sectional study analyzed data from 14,283 adults (18 years and older) derived from the 2022 Bangladesh Demographic and Health Survey to estimate hypertension prevalence and develop predictive models using four machine learning (ML) and two deep learning (DL) algorithms. Overall hypertension prevalence was 18.04% (95% CI: 17.2%–18.9%), with higher prevalence in women (18.87%) than men (16.97%), and significant associations were identified with age, BMI, diabetes, wealth index, education level, household size, and geographic region (p<0.05 for all). Among all models tested, weighted logistic regression (WLR) achieved the highest accuracy (0.817), precision (0.444), specificity (0.981), AUC-ROC (0.751), and AUC-PR (0.357), but its critically low recall (0.070) limits its clinical utility for population screening. The random forest (RF) model achieved the highest recall (0.687) and F1-score (0.460), demonstrating superior sensitivity for identifying hypertensive individuals and making it more appropriate for public health screening applications. Principal limitations include the cross-sectional design preventing causal inference, potential recall bias in self-reported measures, and absence of external validation of the predictive models in other low- and middle-income country (LMIC) settings.

Implications Medical Affairs : The 18% hypertension prevalence in Bangladesh, together with its socio-demographic determinants (age, BMI, diabetes, low educational attainment), documents a substantial and growing unmet treatment need in South Asian LMICs, representing a commercially significant opportunity for pharmaceutical companies with established or generic antihypertensive portfolios seeking market expansion. The superior recall performance of the random forest model supports the integration of AI-based screening tools in public health programs, a trend that Medical Affairs could leverage to establish disease awareness partnerships with local health ministries and NGOs to facilitate earlier hypertension diagnosis and treatment initiation at scale. Strategic KOL engagement should target Bangladeshi and South Asian epidemiologists, digital health researchers, and public health policymakers who can amplify real-world evidence generation around AI-driven hypertension screening programs in resource-limited settings.

Association between antenatal depression and quality of life in pregnant women with hypertensive disorders of pregnancy: Mediating effects of pregnancy stress and psychological resilience.

Revue : African Journal of Reproductive Health | Tier : NA | IF estime : NA

Date de publication : 2026-09-17

Resultats principaux : This single-centre retrospective study enrolled 301 pregnant women with hypertensive disorders of pregnancy (HDP) at a Chinese hospital, who completed validated scales including the Edinburgh Postnatal Depression Scale (EPDS), Perceived Stress Scale, Resilience Scale, and the SF-36 Health Survey. The mean EPDS score was 7.17 plus or minus 3.48, and 26.25% of women exceeded the clinical cut-off of greater than 9.5, indicating clinically significant antenatal depression (AD). Antenatal depression was significantly associated with poorer quality of life (total effect = -0.788, P<0.001), with pregnancy stress and psychological resilience jointly mediating this association through a chain pathway accounting for 53.7% of the total effect (stress alone: 30.0%, resilience alone: 17.1%, stress-to-resilience chain: 6.7%). In the mild preeclampsia subgroup (n=123), the total indirect mediation effect increased to 70.0% of the total effect, suggesting that stress and resilience mechanisms are especially dominant in this clinical subgroup. Major limitations include the single-centre retrospective design, potential selection bias, and reliance on self-report instruments without objective psychological measurements.

Implications Medical Affairs : This study documents a high prevalence of antenatal depression in women with HDP (26.25% above clinical threshold) with quality-of-life impairment predominantly mediated through psychological pathways, reinforcing the need for integrated mental health and antihypertensive management protocols in obstetric care that address both biological and psychological dimensions of HDP. For Medical Affairs teams, these findings support advocacy initiatives for holistic maternity care pathways that combine antihypertensive pharmacotherapy with structured psychological resilience and stress-management interventions, which can differentiate product value propositions through patient-reported outcome endpoints in future clinical trials. KOL engagement with maternal-fetal medicine specialists, obstetricians, and perinatal psychiatrists in Asia-Pacific markets should be prioritised to build scientific exchange on HDP-related mental health outcomes as emerging patient-centered secondary endpoints in hypertension trials.

Neonatal outcomes of hypertensive pregnancy subtypes in very preterm infants: a multicenter cohort study.

Revue : BMC Pediatrics | Tier : NA | IF estime : NA

Date de publication : 2026-08-01

Resultats principaux : This multicenter retrospective cohort study used prospectively collected data from the Brazilian Neonatal Research Network, enrolling 9,689 very preterm infants (birthweight 401–1500 g, gestational age 22+0 to 32+6 weeks) born between 2013 and 2020, of whom 41.4% (n=4,011) were exposed to maternal hypertensive disorders of pregnancy (HDP). Three HDP subtypes were analysed: chronic hypertension (CH), pregnancy-specific hypertensive disorders (PSHD), and superimposed preeclampsia (CH+PE), using Poisson regression with robust variance adjusted for gestational age, birthweight, sex, antenatal corticosteroids and other perinatal variables. Both PSHD and CH+PE were associated with higher risks of respiratory distress syndrome and surfactant use; CH+PE was additionally linked to higher in-hospital mortality, particularly pronounced in infants at or below 28 weeks gestation. In contrast, CH was associated with lower observed risks of late-onset sepsis and, among infants older than 28 weeks, lower risk of severe intraventricular haemorrhage, suggesting a potentially protective vascular adaptation effect. Key limitations include the retrospective design, strong residual confounding by gestational age that attenuated multiple crude associations after adjustment, and the geographic restriction to Brazilian tertiary centres limiting global generalisability.

Implications Medical Affairs : The demonstration of distinct neonatal outcome profiles across HDP subtypes — with CH+PE carrying the highest mortality risk and CH showing a relatively neutral or protective pattern — supports subtype-specific clinical risk stratification and management, with potential implications for guideline recommendations on antihypertensive intensification strategies in pregnancy. For Medical Affairs, this evidence base reinforces the importance of differentiating antihypertensive product indications and safety profiles across chronic hypertension, preeclampsia, and superimposed preeclampsia contexts, particularly for agents being evaluated or positioned in the obstetric hypertension space. Collaboration with Brazilian and Latin American neonatology and maternal-fetal medicine KOLs should be pursued to generate regional registry data replicating these findings, strengthening local evidence for market positioning and regulatory submissions in the region.

Biochemical characteristics associated with spironolactone use in hospitalized patients with hypotonic hyponatraemia.

Revue : Annals of Medicine | Tier : NA | IF estime : NA

Date de publication : 2026-09-17

Resultats principaux : This single-centre retrospective cohort study characterised the biochemical profiles of 534 hospitalised patients with hypotonic hyponatraemia comparing spironolactone users (n=107) versus non-users (n=427), excluding thiazide-treated patients to isolate the specific aldosterone antagonist effect. After eGFR adjustment, spironolactone users showed significantly higher serum potassium (p<0.001), serum urea (p<0.001), and serum uric acid (p=0.01) compared to non-users; multivariable regression confirmed independent associations with higher serum potassium (beta=0.33, p=0.001) and a 24.5% increase in serum urea (p<0.001). Among the spironolactone-alone subgroup (n=27) compared to the combined spironolactone-plus-furosemide group (n=80), lower dose, serum urea, creatinine, and uric acid were observed along with higher eGFR and hypertension prevalence (p=0.048). Importantly, no clear evidence was found that concomitant furosemide materially modified the spironolactone-associated biochemical pattern, suggesting this phenotype is driven primarily by aldosterone antagonism rather than loop diuretic co-exposure. Limitations include the retrospective single-centre design, absence of urinary potassium excretion data in several subgroups, and the need for prospective validation of the described biochemical phenotype in a broader hyponatraemic cohort.

Implications Medical Affairs : This pharmacovigilance study characterises a reproducible biochemical phenotype of elevated serum potassium and urea associated with spironolactone in hospitalised patients, providing clinically actionable safety information for prescribers managing hypertensive patients on mineralocorticoid receptor antagonist (MRA) therapy — particularly those with co-existing CKD or heart failure where electrolyte dysregulation is already a concern. For Medical Affairs teams responsible for MRA portfolios or differentiating newer selective MRAs such as finerenone from older non-selective agents like spironolactone, this safety characterisation supports the development of targeted prescriber education materials on electrolyte and renal function monitoring protocols that can highlight differentiated safety profiles. High-value KOL engagement should focus on hospital pharmacists, nephrologists, and cardiologists through scientific symposia or webinars dedicated to drug-induced electrolyte disorders, positioning such educational initiatives as part of a broader risk management and product differentiation strategy.

Deep learning-derived retinal age gap and its associations with lifestyle, systemic, and ocular health in a health screening cohort.

Revue : GeroScience | Tier : NA | IF estime : NA

Date de publication : 2026-09-17

Resultats principaux : This cross-sectional cohort study developed a multi-task deep learning model trained on 29,530 fundus images from 7,535 participants in a Korean health screening cohort to derive the retinal age gap (RAG), a measure of the difference between predicted and chronological retinal age as a biological aging biomarker. The bias-corrected multi-task model achieved a mean absolute error of 2.656 years and Pearson correlation of 0.921 in the lifestyle/systemic cohort (n=5,606) and 2.529 years and 0.938 in the ocular disease cohort (n=1,810). Higher RAG was significantly associated with smoking (ex-smokers: beta=+0.46 years; current smokers: beta=+0.50 years) and clinical diabetes (beta=+2.52 years), both surviving false discovery rate and Bonferroni correction, while being married was associated with lower RAG (beta=-0.46 years, FDR-significant). Critically, hypertension and hyperlipidemia were not significantly associated with RAG in this cohort, which challenges the hypothesis that vascular aging markers are universally elevated in hypertension. In the ocular disease cohort, RAG was significantly higher in age-related macular degeneration (beta=+0.60 years) and cataract (beta=+1.86 years) compared to normal controls; however, the cross-sectional design precludes causal inference, and the authors caution that RAG is more appropriate for population-level characterisation than individual risk stratification until longitudinal validation against established aging biomarkers is completed.

Implications Medical Affairs : The finding that hypertension was not independently associated with the retinal age gap in this large Korean screening cohort is an important negative result that tempers enthusiasm for RAG as a universal vascular aging biomarker in hypertensive populations, and should be factored into the design of future hypertension trials seeking non-invasive imaging biomarkers of subclinical organ damage. Medical Affairs teams should nonetheless monitor the retinal age gap literature closely as an emerging digital biomarker space that could eventually serve as a non-invasive surrogate endpoint in cardiovascular outcomes trials, particularly as the field matures towards longitudinal validation and individual-level predictive utility. Strategic engagement with AI-ophthalmology researchers, digital health companies developing retinal imaging diagnostics, and KOLs in preventive cardiology and hypertension research could yield early partnerships for incorporating retinal biomarkers into hypertension clinical trial designs and patient stratification frameworks.

Digital therapeutics and mHealth applications in cardiovascular prevention: strongest evidence in hypertension and emerging perspectives in dyslipidemia.

Revue : Frontiers in Digital Health | Tier : NA | IF estime : NA

Date de publication : 2026-09-02

Resultats principaux : This narrative review synthesises current evidence on digital therapeutics (DTx), mobile health (mHealth) applications, telemonitoring systems, and integrated digital health platforms across hypertension, dyslipidemia, and broader cardiometabolic prevention, identifying key differences in clinical maturity between these cardiovascular risk domains. The strongest and most clinically mature evidence exists for digitally enabled blood pressure management, particularly when home blood pressure monitoring, telemonitoring, behavioral support, medication adherence tools, and clinician-guided treatment adjustment are co-integrated into routine care workflows rather than deployed as standalone consumer applications. In contrast, digital interventions for dyslipidemia remain less clinically established, primarily supporting education, lifestyle modification, adherence, and shared decision-making rather than validated therapeutic effect modification, and should therefore be considered supportive mHealth rather than established DTx. Successful implementation requires addressing multidimensional barriers including clinical workflow integration, professional accountability for telemonitoring data review and alerts, regulatory and reimbursement pathway alignment, data privacy, interoperability with electronic health records, health equity considerations, and demonstration of both clinical and economic value. The primary limitation is the narrative review format, which lacks a systematic search protocol, formal quality appraisal of included studies, and quantitative meta-analytic synthesis of treatment effect sizes.

Implications Medical Affairs : This review confirms that hypertension management holds the leading evidence base among cardiovascular risk factors for digital therapeutic interventions, validating it as the primary area for pharmaceutical companies to invest in digital companion programs or hybrid digital-pharmacological hypertension management platforms that can differentiate product value through improved medication adherence and blood pressure control outcomes. Medical Affairs teams should develop health economic models quantifying the clinical and economic value of telemonitoring-integrated antihypertensive treatment compared to usual care, as these data will be essential for payer negotiations, HTA submissions, and inclusion of digital add-ons in treatment guidelines. Proactive KOL engagement with hypertension specialists, cardiologists, health technology assessment bodies, and digital health regulatory experts will be critical to define the evidence standards and clinical value frameworks needed to secure reimbursement and guideline recognition for digital therapeutic strategies combined with antihypertensive pharmacotherapy.

Development and validation of a modified Dietary Approaches to Stop Hypertension (mDASH) index in the community-based Framingham Heart Study Second Generation cohort.

Revue : Clinical Nutrition | Tier : NA | IF estime : NA

Date de publication : 2026-08-19

Resultats principaux : This prospective cohort study used food frequency questionnaire data from the Framingham Heart Study Second Generation cohort to develop and validate a modified DASH (mDASH) index incorporating eight dietary components: total fruit, total vegetables (including potatoes), total dairy (full-fat and low-fat combined), fish/seafood, whole grains, legumes/nuts/seeds, red and processed meats, and sugar-sweetened beverages. The mDASH index demonstrated moderate-to-strong test-retest reliability across four examination cycles (Pearson r=0.56–0.70) and strong construct validity through correlations with key DASH target nutrients — calcium, potassium, magnesium, fibre, and protein (r=0.51–0.71). In the Second Generation cohort, the highest versus lowest quintile of mDASH adherence was associated with a 27% lower risk of incident hypertension (p-trend across quintiles=0.0056), a magnitude comparable to the Fung DASH index (p-trend=0.0255) and similar to the Gunther DASH index (35% lower risk, p-trend=0.0004). Cross-validation in the younger Third Generation cohort confirmed a 20% lower hypertension risk in the highest mDASH adherence quintile (95% CI: 0.61–1.04), supporting the generalisability of the index across generations. Primary limitations include the observational Framingham cohort design with potential residual dietary and lifestyle confounding, the predominantly White, well-educated northeastern US population reducing generalisability to ethnically diverse populations, and food frequency questionnaire limitations including recall and social desirability biases.

Implications Medical Affairs : The validation of the mDASH index as a reliable predictor of hypertension risk with a 20–27% risk reduction associated with highest dietary adherence provides robust scientific evidence supporting dietary pattern quality as a clinically meaningful tool for hypertension primary prevention, reinforcing the adjunctive value of lifestyle interventions alongside antihypertensive pharmacotherapy in guideline-endorsed treatment algorithms. For Medical Affairs, the quantifiable risk reduction magnitude associated with DASH-pattern adherence provides a compelling scientific narrative for positioning lifestyle modification programs as complementary partners to antihypertensive product strategies, potentially enabling wellness collaboration initiatives and patient support programs that enhance medication adherence and treatment outcomes. KOL engagement should prioritise preventive cardiologists, registered dietitians, and dietary epidemiologists involved in large-scale dietary intervention research to co-author real-world evidence studies that integrate the mDASH index as a covariate in hypertension clinical trial analyses, strengthening the holistic evidence base around antihypertensive treatment in diet-responsive populations.
