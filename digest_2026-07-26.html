Detection rate and associated factors of thyroid nodules among residents in Qingdao, a coastal city in China.

Revue : Medicine (Baltimore) | Tier : NA | IF estime : NA

Date de publication : 2026-07-24

Resultats principaux : This cross-sectional study analyzed data from 15,283 healthy examinees who underwent routine health checkups in Qingdao, China, between January 2013 and December 2022, with the primary aim of determining the detection rate and risk factors for thyroid nodules in this coastal population. The overall detection rate of thyroid nodules was 28.48%, with females showing a significantly higher detection rate than males (35.51% vs. 21.47%; p=0.002), and the detection rate increased substantially with age across both sexes. In males, high C-reactive protein (CRP), hyperglycemia, hypertension, and smoking were significantly more prevalent in the nodule group compared to the non-nodule group; in females, abdominal obesity, high CRP, hyperglycemia, and hypertension were significantly associated with thyroid nodule detection. Logistic regression identified gender, age, elevated CRP, hyperglycemia, and smoking history as independent factors for thyroid nodule occurrence, with hypertension appearing as a significant co-morbid condition clustering alongside metabolic risk factors. Limitations include the retrospective, single-center design, the absence of iodine intake data (a key thyroid risk factor), and the potential for selection bias in a health checkup population which may not represent the general Qingdao community.

Implications Medical Affairs : This large epidemiological study highlights hypertension as a frequent comorbidity clustering with metabolic dysfunction in a Chinese urban health-checkup cohort, reinforcing the cardiovascular risk factor burden in middle-aged and elderly Asian populations and providing real-world prevalence data to support market access arguments for integrated cardiometabolic management programs. From a Medical Affairs standpoint, the findings underscore an opportunity to position antihypertensive treatments within broader metabolic screening and lifestyle intervention programs targeting Asia-Pacific healthcare settings, where the co-occurrence of hypertension, hyperglycemia, and inflammation is highly prevalent. The data support engagement with KOLs in endocrinology and preventive medicine to drive collaborative research on integrated metabolic risk management, a strategy that could reinforce brand differentiation in hypertension markets increasingly focused on comorbidity management.

HALP score: A powerful and promising index for predicting prognosis among hypertension adults.

Revue : Medicine (Baltimore) | Tier : NA | IF estime : NA

Date de publication : 2026-07-24

Resultats principaux : This linked-mortality cohort study analyzed 20,256 hypertensive adults from the National Health and Nutrition Examination Survey (NHANES) 1999 to 2018 (mean age 53.78 +/- 0.22 years; 53.36% male), examining the relationship between the HALP (Hemoglobin, Albumin, Lymphocyte, Platelet) score and all-cause, cardiovascular disease (CVD), and cancer mortality over 189,593 person-years of follow-up. A total of 4,432 all-cause deaths, 1,227 CVD deaths, and 966 cancer deaths were documented during follow-up. Compared to the lowest HALP quartile (Q1), participants in the highest quartile (Q4) had a 30% reduced risk of all-cause mortality (HR=0.70, 95% CI 0.64-0.77, P-trend less than 0.001), a 39% lower risk of CVD mortality (HR=0.61, 95% CI 0.49-0.76, P-trend less than 0.001), and a 22% lower risk of cancer mortality (HR=0.78, 95% CI 0.63-0.96, P-trend=0.086). Restricted cubic spline analyses revealed an L-shaped dose-response relationship between the HALP score and all mortality outcomes, suggesting a threshold effect at lower HALP values. Key limitations include the observational design with potential residual confounding, cross-sectional measurement of HALP (which may not reflect dynamic changes over time), and the reliance on NHANES data that may not fully capture all clinical variables relevant to hypertensive prognosis.

Implications Medical Affairs : The HALP score represents a novel, low-cost composite biomarker integrating inflammatory and nutritional parameters that could serve as a clinically valuable prognostic tool in hypertensive patient management, offering Medical Affairs teams a compelling narrative around comprehensive cardiovascular risk profiling beyond blood pressure control alone. These findings are particularly relevant for positioning antihypertensive portfolios within holistic cardiovascular risk management programs that incorporate nutritional status and systemic inflammation monitoring, areas increasingly emphasized in updated hypertension guidelines. From a KOL engagement perspective, this work supports the development of scientific platform materials promoting integrated patient profiling in hypertension, and invites collaborative research on prospective HALP-guided risk stratification studies that could generate differentiated evidence for medical congresses.

Fast Food, Health Impacts, and Policy Responses in China and India.

Revue : Annals of Global Health | Tier : NA | IF estime : NA

Date de publication : 2026-07-22

Resultats principaux : This narrative review synthesized 52 peer-reviewed studies published since 2010 examining the health impacts of fast food consumption and policy responses in China and India, two countries where the fast-food industry has rapidly expanded over the past three decades alongside rising rates of overweight, obesity, and diet-related non-communicable diseases (NCDs). China's evidence base includes longitudinal and quasi-causal studies linking fast-food exposure to childhood and adult obesity, cardiometabolic risks including hypertension and diabetes, and acute health outcomes, while India relies largely on large population analyses and school-based studies reporting similar associations. Marginalized groups such as migrant workers and pregnant women were identified as particularly vulnerable populations, reinforcing health inequities associated with fast-food access. National policies in both countries prioritize education over binding regulation, and their effectiveness against fast-food consumption and diet-related NCDs is largely limited and under-researched, especially in India. Key limitations include significant heterogeneity across included studies, potential publication bias, the narrative (non-systematic) review methodology, and the absence of formal quality assessment of included studies.

Implications Medical Affairs : This review documents the growing burden of hypertension and associated NCDs attributable to dietary patterns in Asia's two most populous countries, signaling a major long-term market opportunity for antihypertensive and metabolic risk management therapies as fast-food-driven cardiovascular risk continues to rise in these evolving healthcare landscapes. From a Medical Affairs standpoint, the documented inadequacy of educational-only policies and the explicit call for stricter food environment regulations represent an opportunity to engage policymakers and public health KOLs in broader cardiovascular prevention strategies that could position pharmaceutical companies as partners in national NCD control agendas. The emphasis on vulnerable subpopulations such as migrants and pregnant women can inform targeted disease awareness campaigns and patient support programs that align with corporate social responsibility commitments and support market development activities in China and India.

ECG Criteria for Left Ventricular Hypertrophy in Hypertensive Black Africans: Insights from the coArtHA Trial.

Revue : Global Heart | Tier : NA | IF estime : NA

Date de publication : 2026-07-21

Resultats principaux : This subanalysis of the coArtHA randomized controlled trial (NCT04129840) enrolled 1,125 hypertensive Black African adults from rural Tanzania and Lesotho to evaluate the diagnostic performance of established ECG criteria for left ventricular hypertrophy (LVH) compared to echocardiographic left ventricular mass index (LVMI) as the reference standard, using baseline 12-lead ECG and focused transthoracic echocardiography. Echocardiographic LVH, defined as LVMI greater than 95 g/m2 in women and greater than 115 g/m2 in men, was present in only 56 participants (5%), suggesting a low prevalence in this population with untreated, uncomplicated hypertension at baseline. Among continuous ECG indices, Cornell voltage product adjusted by +0.8 mV in women showed the strongest correlation with LVMI (Spearman rho=0.37; p less than 0.001), with an AUROC of approximately 0.76, sensitivity of 64%, and specificity of 80% using established cut-offs. Voltage-only criteria including MESA- and Sokolow-Lyon-based indices demonstrated limited accuracy in this sub-Saharan African setting, underperforming relative to voltage-duration product criteria. Key limitations include the low LVH prevalence limiting statistical power for subgroup analyses, the cross-sectional nature of the baseline assessment, and the need for external validation of population-specific ECG thresholds in African cohorts.

Implications Medical Affairs : These findings directly challenge the universal applicability of ECG-LVH criteria developed primarily in non-African populations, with important implications for the diagnostic workup of hypertensive organ damage in sub-Saharan Africa — a region with a rapidly growing hypertension burden and limited access to echocardiography — and strengthen the scientific case for population-specific diagnostic algorithms in hypertension guidelines. From a Medical Affairs perspective, this multicenter African trial provides a compelling evidence-based platform for KOL engagement in cardiovascular medicine in Africa and supports the development of educational initiatives around organ damage assessment in resource-limited settings where antihypertensive therapies are increasingly being deployed. The study reinforces the strategic importance of inclusive clinical research in diverse populations and positions the coArtHA trial as a key reference for future guideline updates on hypertension management in Black African populations, relevant for companies seeking to expand market presence in African healthcare systems.

Association of household income-mediated fruit and vegetable intake with albuminuria in primary hypertension.

Revue : The American Journal of Medicine | Tier : NA | IF estime : NA

Date de publication : 2026-07-25

Resultats principaux : This cross-sectional matched study enrolled 153 non-diabetic participants with primary hypertension and high urine albumin excretion (greater than 200 microg/min; HIGH group) and 153 matched participants with low urine albumin excretion (less than 20 microg/min; LOW group), all with normal estimated glomerular filtration rate (at least 90 mL/min/1.73 m2), to examine associations between albuminuria, fruit and vegetable intake, and household income as a socioeconomic surrogate. Least squares mean fruit and vegetable intake was significantly lower in the HIGH group compared to the LOW group (1.66 cups/day [95% CI: 1.57-1.74] vs. 2.24 cups/day [95% CI: 2.15-2.33]). After multivariable adjustments, higher urine albumin excretion was significantly associated with lower fruit and vegetable intake (beta=2.23, SE=0.044, p less than 0.0001), and fruit and vegetable intake showed a strong positive association with household income (coefficient=0.85, p less than 0.0001). Urine albumin excretion had a weak but significant negative association with household income (beta=-0.25, SE=0.02, p less than 0.0001), suggesting that income mediates dietary quality which in turn influences renal albumin handling. Limitations include the cross-sectional design precluding causal inference, the modest sample size (n=306), use of household income as a single proxy for socioeconomic status, and the restriction to non-diabetic participants limiting generalizability.

Implications Medical Affairs : This study provides important evidence linking socioeconomic-mediated dietary disparities — specifically low fruit and vegetable intake — to early markers of kidney damage (albuminuria) in patients with primary hypertension, highlighting a potentially modifiable lifestyle risk factor beyond blood pressure control that is relevant to cardiorenal protection narratives. From a Medical Affairs perspective, these findings underscore the need for integrated disease management approaches in hypertension that address social determinants of health, supporting the positioning of antihypertensive portfolios within a broader cardiorenal protection strategy that emphasizes early intervention in at-risk socioeconomic groups. The identification of low-income hypertensive patients as a high-risk subgroup for early renal damage progression offers actionable insights for patient access and support programs, as well as KOL engagement strategies focused on health equity, renal outcomes, and value-based care frameworks in hypertension.

Machine learning-based identification of cardiovascular risk modifiers in patients with very high LDL cholesterol: A cross-sectional study.

Revue : Medicine (Baltimore) | Tier : NA | IF estime : NA

Date de publication : 2026-07-24

Resultats principaux : This single-center, cross-sectional study enrolled 246 patients aged 40 to 80 years with at least one documented LDL-C of at least 190 mg/dL between 2014 and 2025 to identify clinical and laboratory predictors of major adverse cardiovascular events (MACE) using five machine learning models trained on an 80/20 split. CatBoost achieved the highest discrimination (AUROC 0.894), followed by logistic regression (AUROC 0.886), XGBoost (AUROC 0.860), and LightGBM (AUROC 0.845). Variables consistently identified across all models in association with prevalent MACE included STOP-BANG score (obstructive sleep apnea risk proxy), older age, hypertension, duration of prior statin use, glycated hemoglobin, triglycerides, neutrophil count, BMI, and apolipoprotein B. Lipid-lowering therapy was markedly suboptimal: although 61% reported prior treatment, only 28% were on active therapy at evaluation, statin discontinuation was frequent, and high-intensity regimens were underutilized even among MACE patients. Limitations include the single-center, retrospective, cross-sectional design, the modest sample size (n=246), retrospective MACE ascertainment potentially subject to ascertainment bias, and the lack of prospective validation.

Implications Medical Affairs : The consistent ranking of hypertension among the top machine learning-derived predictors of MACE in patients with severely elevated LDL-C reinforces its central role in cardiovascular risk stratification beyond lipid management alone, supporting the scientific case for comprehensive blood pressure control as an integral component of cardiovascular risk reduction in high-LDL populations managed in internal medicine and cardiology settings. This finding is immediately actionable from a Medical Affairs standpoint, providing scientific rationale for positioning antihypertensive treatments as key components of combination cardiovascular risk reduction strategies in patients with familial hypercholesterolemia or severe dyslipidemia. The documented widespread suboptimality of lipid-lowering therapy combined with hypertension as a dominant MACE driver creates a compelling platform for KOL-led scientific exchanges on integrated cardiovascular risk management, treatment adherence, and intensification strategies that could enhance brand visibility at cardiovascular medical congresses.

Nontraditional lipid and lipid-inflammatory parameters for risk stratification of abnormal glucose metabolism: a cross-sectional study in Chinese adults.

Revue : Frontiers in Endocrinology | Tier : NA | IF estime : NA

Date de publication : 2026-07-10

Resultats principaux : This cross-sectional study analyzed data from 2,301 adults from a population-based survey conducted in Fujian Province, China (2020-2021), evaluating the associations and diagnostic ability of eight non-traditional lipid parameters and lipid-inflammation composite indices (incorporating high-sensitivity C-reactive protein, hs-CRP) with impaired glucose metabolism (prediabetes and type 2 diabetes). All eight non-traditional lipid parameters showed significant positive associations with impaired glucose metabolism, with several demonstrating nonlinear dose-response relationships in restricted cubic spline analyses. In prediabetes comparisons, atherogenic coefficient, Castelli's index-I, remnant cholesterol-CRP index, and RC/HDL-C-CRP had the highest diagnostic ability; for type 2 diabetes, the atherogenic index of plasma and RC/HDL-C-CRP performed best. Mediation analysis indicated that hs-CRP partially mediates the association between non-traditional lipid parameters and prediabetes in a bidirectional manner, though no mediation effect was observed for diabetes risk. Limitations include the cross-sectional design precluding causal inference, single-region sampling potentially limiting generalizability to other Chinese provinces, reliance on a single hs-CRP measurement, and the self-reported nature of some covariates.

Implications Medical Affairs : Although this study focuses primarily on glucose metabolism dysregulation rather than hypertension directly, it identifies inflammation-lipid interactions as key drivers of cardiometabolic disease risk in a large Chinese population — conditions that frequently co-occur with and worsen hypertension prognosis — providing important context for Medical Affairs strategies targeting the broader cardiometabolic space. The superior performance of lipid-inflammation composite indices over traditional lipid metrics for identifying high-risk prediabetic individuals supports the scientific rationale for refined cardiometabolic risk stratification that could inform patient selection for intensive cardiovascular risk management programs including antihypertensive therapy. From a KOL strategy perspective, this work supports advocacy for comprehensive cardiometabolic risk factor panels in Chinese populations, where the co-occurrence of hypertension, dyslipidemia, inflammation, and glucose intolerance is highly prevalent, positioning pharmaceutical companies as partners in holistic prevention and early intervention strategies.
