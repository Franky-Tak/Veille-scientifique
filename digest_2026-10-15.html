Maternal risk factors for hypertension in pregnancy: A quantitative study on the role of demographics, obesity, and healthcare compliance.

Revue : African Journal of Reproductive Health | Tier : NA | IF estime : NA

Date de publication : 2026-10-14

Resultats principaux : This prospective observational cohort study was conducted at the Wonoayu Health Center in Sidoarjo, Indonesia, between November 2024 and January 2025, enrolling 57 pregnant women with a gestational age of at least 28 weeks who tested positive on mean arterial pressure screening via consecutive sampling. The primary endpoint was the occurrence of hypertension in pregnancy, assessed through blood pressure measurement, proteinuria testing, and review of medical records. Multivariable logistic regression identified maternal age as a statistically significant independent risk factor for hypertension in pregnancy (adjusted OR = 11.848; 95% CI: 1.422–98.739; p = 0.022), indicating that older mothers carry a substantially elevated risk. Conversely, adherence to calcium lactate supplementation was significantly protective against hypertension in pregnancy (adjusted OR = 0.371; 95% CI: 0.160–0.858; p = 0.021), suggesting a preventive role for calcium in this setting. No subgroup analyses were reported, and key limitations include the small sample size (n = 57), single-center recruitment, and potential selection bias from consecutive sampling, which restricts generalizability to broader populations.

Implications Medical Affairs : These findings reinforce existing guidelines recommending calcium supplementation for hypertension prevention in pregnancy, particularly in low-resource settings where dietary calcium intake is insufficient, and could support label expansion or new indication discussions for calcium-based formulations. From a product positioning standpoint, these data strengthen the evidence base for calcium supplementation as a preventive intervention in women of advanced maternal age, which is a growing demographic segment globally. For KOL strategy, engaging obstetric and maternal-fetal medicine specialists in Indonesia and sub-Saharan Africa — where this journal has reach — to present these data could open new market access discussions and reinforce the value of adherence-supporting programs.

Pregnancy complications and risk of cardiometabolic-renal conditions in 1.4 million women in England: longitudinal study.

Revue : BMJ Medicine | Tier : NA | IF estime : NA

Date de publication : 2026-10-06

Resultats principaux : This large longitudinal study used the Clinical Practice Research Datalink (CPRD) Aurum primary care database in the UK (2000–2022) to examine 1,432,795 women aged 15–49 years with a history of pregnancy, assessing the association between pregnancy complications and subsequent cardiometabolic-renal outcomes including cardiovascular disease, type 2 diabetes mellitus, hypertension, and chronic kidney disease (stages 3–5). Median follow-up ranged from 4.1 to 4.3 years across the four outcome cohorts. Among 72 associations examined, 51 were statistically significant after correction for multiple testing. Gestational hypertension and pre-eclampsia more than tripled the rate of subsequent hypertension and approximately doubled the rate of chronic kidney disease, while gestational diabetes mellitus was associated with a sevenfold higher rate of type 2 diabetes mellitus and meaningfully increased rates of hypertension and cardiovascular disease. Notably, postnatal depression was associated with increased rates of all four outcomes by 35–56% (hypertension HR 1.36, 95% CI 1.33–1.40; CVD HR 1.56, 95% CI 1.50–1.62), and adverse pregnancy events such as stillbirth, miscarriage, placental abruption, small-for-gestational-age birth, and preterm birth were also linked to elevated risks of one or more cardiometabolic-renal outcomes, suggesting that the full reproductive history is clinically informative beyond the index pregnancy.

Implications Medical Affairs : This landmark population-based study provides compelling epidemiological evidence that adverse pregnancy outcomes should be recognized as formal cardiovascular risk factors in guidelines for post-partum and long-term women's health surveillance, directly relevant to any antihypertensive company interested in the women's cardiovascular health space. From a product positioning perspective, these data identify a large, historically underserved patient population — women with prior hypertensive pregnancy disorders — who represent a prime target for early antihypertensive intervention and cardiovascular prevention, supporting commercial strategies focused on cardiovascular risk management in premenopausal women. For KOL engagement, this study provides high-impact evidence to mobilize cardiologists, obstetricians, and primary care physicians around a coordinated "Pregnancy as a Cardiovascular Stress Test" strategy, which could serve as a compelling scientific narrative for advisory boards, congresses, and co-authoring opportunities.

Population burden of diabetes, hypertension, and cardiovascular disease attributable to obesity: a community-based study in Türkiye.

Revue : Frontiers in Public Health | Tier : NA | IF estime : NA

Date de publication : 2026-09-29

Resultats principaux : This community-based cross-sectional study included 22,667 adults (50.6% women; mean age 46.4 ± 16.1 years) recruited at field booths in Amasya, northern Türkiye, between June and July 2025, with standardized anthropometric measurements and self-reported chronic disease data. The prevalence of obesity was 29.9% (35.7% in women vs. 23.9% in men), and age- and sex-adjusted logistic regression demonstrated that obesity was significantly associated with higher odds of hypertension (adjusted OR = 2.50, 95% CI: 2.31–2.70), diabetes mellitus (aOR = 2.88, 95% CI: 2.65–3.14), and cardiovascular disease (aOR = 2.29, 95% CI: 2.05–2.56). The adjusted population attributable fraction of obesity was 25.8% for hypertension, 31.5% for diabetes mellitus, and 26.1% for cardiovascular disease, underscoring that over one quarter of hypertension cases in this population can be attributed to obesity. Age significantly modified the associations between obesity and all three conditions, with stronger effects in middle-aged adults, while no significant sex interaction was found. Study limitations include the cross-sectional design precluding causal inference, reliance on self-reported disease status, and potential underrepresentation of mobile or institutionalized populations.

Implications Medical Affairs : The finding that 25.8% of hypertension prevalence is attributable to obesity in a middle-income country context provides robust quantitative support for positioning weight-management interventions as an integral component of hypertension prevention programs, aligning with the trend toward cardiometabolic combination strategies. For product positioning, this population-level evidence strengthens the scientific rationale for antihypertensives that offer weight-neutral or favorable metabolic profiles (e.g., ARBs with metabolic benefits, or combination therapies with GLP-1 analogs), particularly in markets like Turkey, Eastern Europe, and the MENA region where obesity rates are rapidly rising. Strategically, these data can anchor discussions with health technology assessment bodies about the cost-effectiveness of obesity-integrated hypertension management pathways, supporting market access efforts for combination cardiometabolic therapies.

The associations between dynapenic obesity and cognitive disorder in older hypertension patients: a multicenter cross-sectional study.

Revue : Frontiers in Public Health | Tier : NA | IF estime : NA

Date de publication : 2026-09-29

Resultats principaux : This multicenter cross-sectional study enrolled 9,568 older hypertension patients aged 65 years and older from multiple Chinese centers between July 2023 and March 2024, using structured questionnaires, standardized physical assessments, and cognitive screening tools to investigate the association between dynapenic obesity (the coexistence of low muscle strength and excess adiposity) and cognitive disorder. The prevalence of cognitive disorder in this hypertensive elderly population was 15.67% (95% CI: 14.94–16.40%), substantially higher than in the general elderly population, suggesting hypertension itself compounds cognitive risk. After adjustment for potential confounders including age, sex, education, and comorbidities, dynapenic obesity was significantly associated with cognitive disorder (aOR = 1.418, 95% CI: 1.263–1.591), with path analysis identifying that dynapenic obesity reduced the likelihood of adequate physical activity (aOR = 0.708, 95% CI: 0.640–0.783), which in turn increased depressive disorders (aOR for depression with inadequate PA = inferred) and cognitive impairment (aOR = 0.521, 95% CI: 0.452–0.601). These associations were consistent across different age subgroups and remained robust in sensitivity analyses employing multiple imputation for missing data. Key limitations include the cross-sectional design limiting causal inference, the Chinese monocentric population limiting generalizability, and reliance on cognitive screening tools rather than formal neuropsychological assessment.

Implications Medical Affairs : These findings identify a high-risk phenotype within the hypertensive elderly population — dynapenic obesity — that warrants specific clinical attention, and could support guideline recommendations for routine muscle strength and body composition assessment in older hypertensive patients to identify those at greatest cognitive risk. From a Medical Affairs perspective, this creates an opportunity to position antihypertensive agents with favorable effects on muscle preservation or metabolic profile (e.g., metabolically neutral ARBs or ACE inhibitors) as preferable options for elderly hypertensive patients with or at risk of sarcopenia and cognitive decline. The identified mediating role of physical activity and depression opens a compelling scientific communications narrative around the holistic management of the hypertensive elderly patient, supporting multidisciplinary KOL engagement spanning cardiologists, geriatricians, and psychiatrists.

Metabolomic aging and mortality clocks based on ensemble learning identify systemic aging risk across the human life span in 18,476 individuals.

Revue : Science Translational Medicine | Tier : NA | IF estime : NA

Date de publication : 2026-10-14

Resultats principaux : This multicenter prospective study from six centers across China profiled plasma metabolomes from 18,476 participants aged 1 to 96 years using nano-assisted laser desorption/ionization mass spectrometry (LDI-MS), identifying 56 representative age-associated metabolites across the human lifespan. A stacking ensemble learning model was developed in a healthy cohort to estimate metabolomic age and calculate metabolomic age acceleration (mAA), defined as each individual's deviation from their chronological age based on plasma metabolite profiles. During a follow-up period from 2019 to 2025, individuals with higher mAA showed a significantly increased risk of incident chronic diseases including hypertension, fatty liver disease, and diabetes, as well as all-cause mortality, suggesting that metabolomic aging is a measurable and predictive systemic process. A separate mortality-linked metabolomic metric (age deviation) was derived in a disease cohort and found to correlate with multisystem disease burden and multimorbidity. Key limitations include restriction to Chinese populations limiting ethnic generalizability, the specialized LDI-MS technology not being widely deployed in clinical settings, and the observational design which cannot fully establish causality between metabolomic aging and specific disease onset.

Implications Medical Affairs : This high-impact study in Science Translational Medicine introduces a novel plasma metabolomic aging clock that predicts hypertension onset alongside other cardiometabolic diseases, which could inform the development of precision medicine strategies to identify pre-hypertensive individuals for early therapeutic intervention. From a Medical Affairs perspective, this technology positions metabolomic profiling as a potential companion diagnostic or patient stratification tool for cardiovascular prevention programs, opening partnership opportunities with diagnostics companies and academic centers developing aging biomarker platforms. For KOL strategy, engaging leading cardiovascular metabolomics researchers in China and internationally around these findings could help anticipate the next generation of biomarker-driven hypertension prevention trials, securing early scientific leadership in a rapidly emerging field.

Association between caregiving duration and hypertension among family caregivers: the TMM CommCohort study.

Revue : Hypertension Research | Tier : NA | IF estime : NA

Date de publication : 2026-10-13

Resultats principaux : This cross-sectional study included 13,518 participants from the Tohoku Medical Megabank Community-Based Cohort Study in Japan, using questionnaire-based caregiving duration data and both home blood pressure (HBP) monitoring and research blood pressure (BP) to classify hypertension per the Japanese Society of Hypertension Guidelines 2025 (HBP ≥135/85 mmHg; research BP ≥140/90 mmHg, or medication use). Participants providing care "almost all day" had significantly higher prevalence ratios for morning hypertension (PR = 1.26; 95% CI: 1.07–1.48) and evening hypertension (PR = 1.27; 95% CI: 1.03–1.56) compared with non-caregivers, after multivariable adjustment for demographics, lifestyle, and social-psychological factors. No significant association was found with research BP-defined hypertension, suggesting that the cardiovascular impact of caregiving stress is specifically captured by out-of-office blood pressure monitoring and may be missed in standard clinical settings. Stratified analyses revealed that associations were most prominent among caregivers aged under 65 years (P-interaction for morning hypertension = 0.003), highlighting working-age caregivers as a particularly vulnerable subgroup. Limitations include the cross-sectional design precluding causal inference, and potential recall bias in self-reported caregiving duration.

Implications Medical Affairs : This study provides novel evidence that psychosocial caregiving stress is a measurable and clinically relevant driver of out-of-office hypertension, particularly morning hypertension, which is recognized as the highest-risk BP pattern for cardiovascular events, directly supporting the value proposition for 24-hour antihypertensive coverage and home blood pressure monitoring programs. For product positioning, these findings strengthen the case for long-acting antihypertensives with proven morning BP control as the treatment of choice for working-age, high-stress populations, aligning with cardiometabolic risk stratification strategies that increasingly incorporate non-traditional risk factors. A KOL engagement opportunity exists to support research into caregiver cardiovascular health as an underrecognized public health issue, potentially initiating investigator-sponsored studies or registry programs in collaboration with Japanese Society of Hypertension stakeholders.

High-fat diet and portal hypertension: a triangulation approach combining Mendelian randomization with retrospective clinical analysis.

Revue : Frontiers in Gastroenterology | Tier : NA | IF estime : NA

Date de publication : 2026-09-29

Resultats principaux : This study investigated the causal relationship between high-fat diet and portal hypertension using a triangulation approach combining two-sample Mendelian randomization (MR) with a retrospective cross-sectional clinical analysis. In the MR component, 18 independent SNPs derived from GWAS data on dietary fat intake (n = 268,922) served as genetic instruments (mean F-statistic = 34.2, collectively explaining 2.8% of phenotypic variance), and inverse variance weighted analysis showed a consistent causal association between genetically predicted high-fat diet and portal hypertension risk (IVW OR = 1.28 per SD increase, 95% CI: 1.15–1.42). Sensitivity analyses including MR-Egger intercept test (p = 0.178), Cochran's Q (p = 0.241), and MR-PRESSO global test (p = 0.312) showed no evidence of directional pleiotropy or significant heterogeneity, strengthening causal interpretation. In the clinical cohort of 72 patients undergoing hepatic venous pressure gradient (HVPG) measurement, high-fat diet score was positively correlated with HVPG (r = 0.700, p < 0.001), and this remained significant after multivariate adjustment (beta = 0.85, 95% CI: 0.52–1.18). The main limitations are the relatively small clinical cohort (n = 72) and the inherent assumption that dietary SNPs exclusively proxy fat intake without pleiotropic effects on other confounders.

Implications Medical Affairs : While focused on portal rather than systemic hypertension, these findings carry cross-disease significance by reinforcing the causal role of diet-driven metabolic dysfunction in vascular pressure dysregulation, supporting the scientific framework that dietary fat intake is an actionable and measurable cardiovascular risk modifier. From a Medical Affairs standpoint, these data strengthen the evidence base for lifestyle modification as a first-line or adjunct intervention across hypertensive conditions, potentially supporting health economic arguments for combination pharmacological and dietary management programs in multimorbid patients with hypertension and liver disease. For KOL strategy, this study opens the door to cross-specialty engagement with hepatologists and gastroenterologists as emerging stakeholders in the cardiovascular risk continuum, particularly in the context of growing MASLD (metabolic-associated steatotic liver disease) as a comorbidity in hypertensive patients.

A Nationwide Cross-Sectional Study of Chronic Urticaria in Chinese Adults with Cardiometabolic Diseases: Epidemiology, Risk Factors, and Effect on Disease Management.

Revue : Biomedical and Environmental Sciences | Tier : NA | IF estime : NA

Date de publication : 2026-09-20

Resultats principaux : This nationwide cross-sectional analysis used data from the 2018–2019 China Chronic Disease and Risk Factor Surveillance, including 118,036 adults with cardiometabolic diseases (CMDs), to estimate the weighted prevalence of chronic urticaria (CU) and its impact on CMD awareness, treatment, and control rates. The weighted CU prevalence among CMD patients was 2.90%, peaking at 3.94% in individuals aged 60–69 years, and was higher in females (3.41%) than in males (2.56%), with a rural preponderance. Independent risk factors for CU in this CMD population included current smoking (OR = 1.17, 95% CI: 1.06–1.29) and abnormal sleep duration (less than 5 hours: OR = 1.79, 95% CI: 1.61–1.99; more than 10 hours: OR = 1.39, 95% CI: 1.19–1.61), linking sleep quality and lifestyle factors to inflammatory comorbidity burden. Patients with CU demonstrated significantly higher hypertension awareness (50.71% vs. 40.27%) and treatment rates (41.37% vs. 34.22%) compared to non-CU patients, suggesting that dermatological comorbidity drives increased healthcare utilization and disease detection. Critically, CU was not associated with improved hypertension control rates, highlighting a gap between diagnosis and effective management in this multimorbid population.

Implications Medical Affairs : This study reveals an important awareness-to-control gap in hypertensive patients with chronic urticaria, suggesting that improved healthcare contact driven by dermatological symptoms does not translate into adequate blood pressure control, identifying a high-value subpopulation for targeted therapeutic intervention and adherence support programs. From a product positioning perspective, antihypertensives with favorable dermatological side-effect profiles (avoiding agents associated with urticaria exacerbation, such as ACE inhibitors in susceptible patients) could be differentially positioned for this multimorbid population, supported by data highlighting the importance of treatment tolerability in complex patients. For KOL engagement, this study supports a cross-specialty Medical Affairs strategy bridging dermatology and cardiology, promoting integrated cardiometabolic-dermatology care pathways that could improve control rates in the large Chinese CMD population with concomitant inflammatory skin disease.
