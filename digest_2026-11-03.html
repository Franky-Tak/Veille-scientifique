Global burden of chronic kidney disease attributable to hypertension due to high body mass index: projections to 2050.

Revue : Renal Failure | Tier : NA | IF estime : NA

Date de publication : 2026-11-02

Resultats principaux : This retrospective epidemiological study leveraged Global Burden of Disease (GBD) 2021 data spanning 1990-2021 to quantify the global burden of hypertension-related chronic kidney disease (CKD) attributable to high body mass index (BMI), and projected trends to 2050 using a Bayesian age-period-cohort model. The primary outcomes included attributable deaths, disability-adjusted life years (DALYs), age-standardized mortality rate (ASMR), and age-standardized DALY rate (ASDR). Attributable deaths increased nearly fivefold from 36,476 in 1990 to 179,788 in 2021, while DALYs rose from 1.01 million to 4.26 million; ASMR nearly doubled from 1.09 to 2.19 per 100,000 (EAPC 4.22%), and ASDR rose from 26.04 to 50.11 per 100,000 (EAPC 3.63%). Regionally, Central Sub-Saharan Africa carried the highest ASMR at 7.66 per 100,000, whereas High-income North America showed the steepest increase (EAPC 5.63%); the burden was higher in men, with death counts peaking at ages 85-89 and DALYs peaking at 60-64 in men and 65-69 in women. Bayesian projections forecast ASMR to reach 4.44 per 100,000 (95% CI: 3.70-5.20) and ASDR to reach 77.15 per 100,000 by 2050, with a notable shift in relative burden toward lower sociodemographic index (SDI) populations; concentration indices changed from -0.10 to -0.17 for deaths. Key limitations include reliance on GBD modeled estimates with variable data quality across regions, and the inherent uncertainty of Bayesian projections over a 30-year horizon.

Implications Medical Affairs : The projected doubling of ASMR by 2050 and disproportionate burden in lower-SDI populations underscores an urgent need for guideline updates integrating BMI control as a first-line recommendation alongside antihypertensive therapy for hypertensive patients with obesity. From a product positioning perspective, this data supports framing antihypertensives with pleiotropic renal-protective effects (e.g., RAS inhibitors, SGLT2i) as essential in patients with obesity-hypertension comorbidity, particularly in fast-growing markets in Sub-Saharan Africa and North America. Medical Affairs teams should prioritize real-world evidence studies and KOL engagements in high-EAPC regions to build advocacy for integrated cardiorenal-metabolic prevention programs.

Drug Utilization Patterns and WHO Prescribing Indicators Among Hypertensive Outpatients at Karnali Academy of Health Sciences, Nepal: A Cross-Sectional Study.

Revue : Pharmacology Research & Perspectives | Tier : NA | IF estime : NA

Date de publication : 2026-12

Resultats principaux : This descriptive cross-sectional study enrolled 197 hypertensive outpatients at a resource-limited tertiary center in Jumla, Nepal, using a structured WHO-aligned questionnaire to evaluate antihypertensive prescribing patterns and adherence to WHO rational medicine use indicators. Calcium channel blockers (CCBs, 32.8%) and angiotensin receptor blockers (ARBs, 24.6%) were the most frequently prescribed antihypertensive classes, and dual-drug therapy predominated (43.7%); mean age was 51.6 ± 13.2 years with slight male predominance (51.3%). WHO prescribing indicators revealed significant deviations: average of 4.05 drugs per prescription (WHO target <2.0), generic prescribing rate of only 34.4% (target 100%), adherence to Essential Medicines List at 79.9% (target 100%), antibiotic prescribing at 14.2%, and injection prescribing at 7.6%. Drug selection appeared generally appropriate for hypertension management, but polypharmacy and low generic prescribing represent major concerns for patient affordability and safety in this low-resource setting. Limitations include the cross-sectional single-center design, small sample size, and inability to assess clinical outcomes or long-term adherence.

Implications Medical Affairs : The 34.4% generic prescribing rate and excessive polypharmacy in a resource-limited Nepalese setting highlight significant market access barriers that pharmaceutical companies with affordable branded-generic portfolios can address through health system partnership programs. The dominance of CCBs and ARBs in this prescribing landscape identifies these as key drug classes for medical education initiatives with healthcare providers in South Asian emerging markets. Medical Affairs should engage local health authorities to advocate for rational prescribing guidelines and explore fixed-dose combination formulations that reduce pill burden while maintaining guideline-concordant therapy.

Prevalence, associations and sex-related disparities of hypertension in South Asians: Evidence from a national survey of Sri Lanka.

Revue : PLoS ONE | Tier : NA | IF estime : NA

Date de publication : 2026-11-02

Resultats principaux : This secondary analysis of the Sri Lanka STEPS Survey 2021, a nationally representative community-based cross-sectional study, analyzed 4,634 adults (mean age 43.5 years; 54.8% female) to assess hypertension prevalence, sex-related disparities, and blood pressure control rates. Overall hypertension prevalence was 37.2% (95% CI: 35.5-38.9%), of whom 60.0% were previously undiagnosed; among diagnosed patients, 73.6% were on treatment but only 38.0% achieved BP targets (<140/90 mmHg). Undiagnosed hypertension was significantly higher in males (69.4%) versus females (53.6%, p<0.001); diabetes was associated with hypertension only in females, while wealth quintile and 24-hour urinary salt excretion were associated only in males. Overweight/obesity was significantly associated with undiagnosed hypertension only in males in stratified analyses, suggesting differential mechanisms of hypertension development across sexes in South Asian populations. Limitations include the cross-sectional design limiting causal inference, potential misclassification of BP status from single-visit measurements, and reliance on self-reported antihypertensive use.

Implications Medical Affairs : A 60% undiagnosis rate and only 38% BP control among treated patients in Sri Lanka signals a massive treatment gap that represents both a public health priority and a substantial unmet market for antihypertensive agents in South Asia. Sex-specific risk factor profiles (diabetes in women, salt intake in men) should inform targeted screening campaigns and positioning of specific drug classes, with ARBs and CCBs potentially differentiated by sex-specific risk profiles. Medical Affairs teams should leverage these epidemiological data to engage Sri Lankan and broader South Asian health authorities in developing sex-stratified national hypertension management guidelines.

Revue : The Journal of International Medical Research | Tier : NA | IF estime : NA

Date de publication : 2026-11-02

Resultats principaux : This combined prospective cross-sectional and retrospective study evaluated analytical performance of Biossays homocysteine and lipid assays in a hospital-based population in Pingshan, Shenzhen, China, using a cohort of 400 hypertensive patients and 55,575 individuals from a laboratory database for lipid analysis. The primary outcomes were 10-year cardiovascular disease risk using the China-PAR model, and prevalence of hyperhomocysteinemia-type hypertension (H-type HTN) and dyslipidemia; the Biossays assays demonstrated satisfactory precision, trueness, sensitivity, and linearity per CLSI standards. Implications Medical Affairs : The extremely high prevalence of hyperhomocysteinemia (85.5%) in hypertensive Chinese patients reinforces the importance of homocysteine as a routine screening biomarker, and supports the positioning of folic acid-containing combination antihypertensives (H-type HTN drugs) in the Chinese market. Treatment Adherence and its Association with Demographic Characteristics and Clinical Factors Among Patients with Hypertension: A Cross-Sectional, Descriptive-Correlational Study.

Revue : Vascular Health and Risk Management | Tier : NA | IF estime : NA

Date de publication : 2026-10-28

Resultats principaux : This descriptive-correlational cross-sectional study enrolled 400 hypertensive patients from a health center in Semnan, Iran, selected by multistage sampling, to assess treatment adherence using a standardized adherence questionnaire and its association with demographic and clinical variables. Mean age was 48.2 ± 18.2 years (54.3% female); the mean adherence score was 83.8 ± 12.4 out of 115, and 77.5% of patients were classified as adherent (score ≥86; 95% CI: 73.4-81.6%). Notably, treatment adherence was not significantly associated with any demographic variable including age, gender, BMI, marital status, educational level, employment status, income level, blood pressure levels, medication-related variables, duration of hypertension, or comorbidities (all p>0.05). The lack of demographic predictors of adherence suggests that individual and contextual motivational factors, rather than sociodemographic characteristics, drive adherence behavior in this population. Implications Medical Affairs : The 77.5% adherence rate in a controlled health center setting likely overestimates real-world adherence, highlighting the need for supplementary adherence support programs in clinical practice — an opportunity for pharmaceutical companies to offer patient support programs alongside their antihypertensive portfolios. The absence of demographic predictors of adherence challenges traditional assumptions and suggests that universal rather than targeted adherence interventions may be more appropriate, with implications for how Medical Affairs teams design patient education and health literacy programs. KOL engagement should focus on behavioral adherence models and the integration of digital health solutions (reminder apps, telemonitoring) to close the adherence gap, which remains a key unmet need in hypertension management across the Middle East and Central Asia.

Age-related and temporal patterns of blood pressure among male industrial workers in Shanghai: evidence from occupational health examinations, 2013-2023.

Revue : BMC Public Health | Tier : NA | IF estime : NA

Date de publication : 2026-08-05

Resultats principaux : This large retrospective cohort study analyzed 1,180,955 occupational health examination records from male industrial workers in Shanghai over 10 years (2013-2023) to characterize age-related and temporal blood pressure patterns using generalized additive models and age-standardized analyses. Both systolic BP (SBP) and diastolic BP (DBP) increased with age, with SBP showing a more pronounced rise; prehypertension was common across adulthood while hypertension prevalence increased markedly with age. Among workers with at least 8 annual BP records, 76.9% who developed hypertension had at least one prior prehypertension record, and 41.5% had at least four prior prehypertension records, underscoring the clinical value of prehypertension surveillance. Applying the 2017 ACC/AHA lower thresholds reclassified 75.8% of prehypertension cases to Stage 1 hypertension, suggesting substantially larger treatment-eligible populations under more aggressive guideline definitions. Key limitations include restriction to male workers in a single city, possible healthy worker effect, and the observational nature precluding causal inference regarding temporal trends.

Implications Medical Affairs : The demonstration that nearly 77% of incident hypertension cases are preceded by a prehypertension phase provides strong epidemiological justification for early pharmacological intervention trials targeting prehypertensive individuals, a potentially large commercial market for antihypertensives. The substantially larger treatment-eligible population under 2017 ACC/AHA versus 2024 Chinese guidelines (75.8% of prehypertensives reclassified) has direct implications for market size estimates and should inform commercial strategy for antihypertensives in China. Medical Affairs should use these occupational health data to engage corporate health decision-makers and workplace medicine specialists in early BP management protocols, creating a new channel for antihypertensive brand awareness.

Predictive value of C-reactive protein for progression from prehypertension to hypertension: a prospective cohort study based on the CHARLS database.

Revue : BMC Cardiovascular Disorders | Tier : NA | IF estime : NA

Date de publication : 2026-08-05

Resultats principaux : This prospective cohort study from the China Health and Retirement Longitudinal Study (CHARLS) enrolled 1,236 middle-aged and elderly individuals with prehypertension at baseline (2011) and followed them through 2015 to assess whether C-reactive protein (CRP) predicts progression to hypertension. The primary outcome was incident hypertension, which developed in 269 participants (21.8%) during the 4-year follow-up; higher log-transformed CRP was significantly associated with hypertension risk (HR=1.34; 95% CI: 1.07-1.68) in adjusted interval-censored Cox models. Restricted cubic spline analysis revealed a nonlinear CRP-hypertension relationship (P for nonlinearity=0.040); participants with CRP >3 mg/L had a 61% higher hypertension risk versus those with CRP <1 mg/L (HR=1.61; 95% CI: 1.15-2.26). Adding CRP to conventional risk models improved the C-index modestly from 0.626 to 0.634; subgroup analyses showed no significant interactions across age, sex, BMI, systolic BP, or remnant cholesterol groups. Limitations include the observational design, potential residual confounding, single CRP measurement at baseline without dynamic tracking, and CHARLS database restriction to those aged 45 and older.

Implications Medical Affairs : The CRP-driven inflammatory pathway to hypertension progression provides a scientific rationale for positioning anti-inflammatory agents and statins (which lower CRP) as adjunctive prevention strategies in prehypertensive patients, opening new labeling discussions with regulatory agencies. A C-index improvement from 0.626 to 0.634 with CRP addition suggests modest but potentially actionable incremental predictive value, supporting the development of CRP-stratified risk tools that could be incorporated into companion diagnostic claims for antihypertensives. Medical Affairs should commission prospective studies evaluating whether CRP-guided antihypertensive initiation in prehypertensive adults with elevated inflammation reduces incident hypertension, which would directly support guideline modifications.

Association between the uric acid to high-density lipoprotein cholesterol ratio and H-type hypertension: a cross-sectional study in Chinese adults.

Revue : BMC Cardiovascular Disorders | Tier : NA | IF estime : NA

Date de publication : 2026-08-05

Resultats principaux : This cross-sectional study enrolled 1,835 adults aged over 30 years from six randomly selected regions in Hunan Province, China, to examine the association between the uric acid to HDL cholesterol ratio (UHR) — a novel inflammatory and metabolic composite biomarker — and H-type hypertension (HTH: hypertension with hyperhomocysteinemia). Of the 1,835 participants, 1,026 (55.9%) had H-type hypertension; multivariate logistic regression showed each one-standard-deviation increase in UHR was associated with a 40% higher odds of HTH (OR=1.41; 95% CI: 1.24-1.60, P<0.001). Restricted cubic spline (RCS) analysis identified a nonlinear dose-response relationship with a threshold inflection point at UHR=0.09, above and below which HTH prevalence showed distinct increasing trends; ROC curve demonstrated moderate discriminatory ability (AUC=0.618). Subgroup analyses revealed the UHR-HTH association was more pronounced in females and in the Han Chinese population; the effect size remained stable across most other subgroups. Study limitations include the cross-sectional design precluding causal inference, regional restriction to Hunan Province limiting generalizability, reliance on single-point biomarker measurements, and the composite nature of HTH combining two distinct pathological processes.

Implications Medical Affairs : The UHR-HTH association validates a composite metabolic-inflammatory biomarker approach for identifying the highest-risk hypertensive subpopulations, which supports the development of H-type HTN-specific diagnostic panels as companion tools to folate-containing antihypertensives marketed in China. The higher UHR-HTH association in females suggests sex-specific metabolic pathways in hypertension, an underexplored area that could differentiate antihypertensive products through sex-stratified real-world evidence studies. Medical Affairs should leverage the UHR concept in educational programs for Chinese endocrinologists and cardiologists to promote integrated metabolic risk assessment in hypertensive patients, supporting cross-specialty prescribing of combination therapies.

Barriers and Facilitators of Hypertension and Diabetes Screening in Primary Healthcare Facilities in Addis Ababa, Ethiopia: A Mixed-Methods Study.

Revue : Journal of Primary Care & Community Health | Tier : NA | IF estime : NA

Date de publication : 2026-11-02

Resultats principaux : This qualitatively dominant convergent mixed-methods study conducted May-December 2025 assessed health system and provider-level barriers and facilitators for hypertension and diabetes screening in 10 public primary healthcare facilities in Addis Ababa, Ethiopia, using facility checklists and semi-structured interviews with 21 clinical staff analyzed through the COM-B model and Theoretical Domains Framework. Overall facility readiness for NCD screening was 82.3%; however, only 4.6% of clinical staff had received NCD-related training, and just 4.2% of the workforce was dedicated to NCD care. Key barriers included stock-outs of glucose test strips and HbA1c reagents, limited HbA1c availability, inadequate equipment maintenance, duplicate documentation systems, selective age/symptom-based diabetes screening, high workload, and provider burnout. Facilitators included peer learning networks, supportive supervision, NGO assistance, community-based health insurance, teamwork culture, and professional commitment; the study framed these within the COM-B model as capability, opportunity, and motivation domains. Limitations include the qualitative sampling approach limiting statistical generalizability, possible social desirability bias in staff interviews, single-city focus in Addis Ababa, and cross-sectional design preventing assessment of temporal change.

Implications Medical Affairs : The dramatic mismatch between 82.3% facility readiness and only 4.6% trained NCD staff in Ethiopia highlights a critical implementation gap that creates an opportunity for pharmaceutical companies to partner with governments on capacity-building programs tied to affordable antihypertensive supply chains in Sub-Saharan Africa. Commodity stock-outs and diagnostic limitations as primary screening barriers underscore the value of simplified, point-of-care BP monitoring approaches, supporting Medical Affairs advocacy for task-shifting hypertension protocols in low-resource settings. KOL partnerships in Ethiopia and similar LMICs should focus on health system strengthening research, with Medical Affairs supporting the development of integrated NCD screening guidelines that could catalyze policy-level changes improving access to antihypertensive medicines.

Recent evidence for reducing cardiometabolic risk in chronic kidney disease.

Revue : Postgraduate Medicine | Tier : NA | IF estime : NA

Date de publication : 2026-11-02

Resultats principaux : This narrative review article summarized recent clinical trial evidence for cardiovascular risk reduction in patients with chronic kidney disease (CKD), focusing on three pharmacological classes: sodium-glucose cotransporter-2 inhibitors (SGLT2i), mineralocorticoid receptor antagonists (MRAs), and glucagon-like peptide-1 receptor agonists (GLP-1 RAs), with relevance to contemporary management guidelines. KDIGO 2024 and ADA 2025 guidelines both recommend SGLT2i for patients with eGFR >20 mL/min/1.73 m² to reduce CKD progression and cardiovascular events; finerenone (a nonsteroidal MRA) is specifically recommended for patients with CKD and T2D at high cardiovascular risk. GLP-1 RAs with demonstrated cardiorenal benefits are recommended by ADA for patients with CKD and T2D; the review identifies a potential synergistic combination of SGLT2i + GLP-1 RA + nonsteroidal MRA for comprehensive cardiorenal-metabolic protection. Notably, traditional antihypertensives including diuretics and beta-blockers were not highlighted as primary agents in this CKD cardiovascular protection paradigm, reflecting a guideline shift toward newer cardiorenal protective agents. Limitations of the review include narrative rather than systematic methodology, potential for selection bias in study inclusion, and limited discussion of combination therapy safety data.

Implications Medical Affairs : The convergence of KDIGO 2024 and ADA 2025 guidelines endorsing SGLT2i and nonsteroidal MRAs as standard of care in CKD + T2D patients signals a paradigm shift that will reshape antihypertensive market dynamics, with significant implications for the competitive positioning of traditional diuretics and beta-blockers in cardiorenal patients. The emerging triple therapy paradigm (SGLT2i + GLP-1 RA + nonsteroidal MRA) represents a premium treatment segment with limited real-world utilization data, creating a strong Medical Affairs opportunity for evidence generation programs and KOL engagement on combination therapy safety and sequencing. Medical Affairs teams should develop comprehensive scientific communication platforms for nephrology and cardiology KOLs emphasizing the cardiorenal-metabolic syndrome framework and positioning complementary antihypertensive agents within this new treatment algorithm.

Dapagliflozin attenuates progression from prefrailty to frailty in patients with hypertension and chronic kidney disease.

Revue : The Journal of Clinical Investigation | Tier : NA | IF estime : NA

Date de publication : 2026-11-02

Resultats principaux : The DAPA-FORTRESS trial (DAPAgliflozin and Frailty prOgression in hypeRTension and chRonic kidnEy diSeaSe) enrolled 135 prefrail hypertensive patients with CKD aged >65 years, followed for 1 year; after coarsened exact matching, the balanced cohort comprised 105 patients including 52 receiving dapagliflozin. Primary outcomes included the association between renal injury markers and physical performance, and the effect of dapagliflozin on frailty progression; a significant inverse correlation was found between gait speed and albuminuria (r²=0.48, P<0.0001) and a positive correlation between gait speed and eGFR (r²=0.18, P<0.0001). Patients with eGFR <60 mL/min/1.73 m² had significantly worse physical performance than those with higher eGFR (P=0.0003); in adjusted multivariable logistic regression, dapagliflozin use was independently associated with markedly lower odds of frailty progression at 1 year (OR=0.130; 95% CI: 0.045-0.381; P<0.001). No secondary endpoints were specified a priori for the frailty analysis; exploratory analyses suggest dapagliflozin may exert musculoskeletal or vascular protective effects beyond its established cardiorenal profile, possibly through reductions in inflammation and albuminuria. Important limitations include the observational matching design (not a randomized controlled trial), single-center Italian setting, relatively small matched cohort, and inability to attribute frailty protection to a specific pharmacological mechanism.

Implications Medical Affairs : This is the first study to demonstrate that dapagliflozin may reduce frailty progression in older hypertensive patients with CKD — a finding with major implications for label expansion discussions with regulators, given that frailty prevention is an unmet clinical endpoint with enormous patient and payer interest. The study reinforces the positioning of SGLT2 inhibitors beyond glycemic and cardiorenal benefits into the geriatric/functional medicine space, creating a highly differentiated product narrative that Medical Affairs teams should incorporate into KOL engagement strategies in nephrology, geriatrics, and internal medicine. Medical Affairs should urgently support the design of a larger randomized controlled trial to confirm these findings, as positive RCT data would create a transformative opportunity for label expansion and prescribing guideline updates across cardiology, nephrology, and geriatrics.

Revue : BMC Anesthesiology | Tier : NA | IF estime : NA

Date de publication : 2026-07-28

Resultats principaux : This prospective randomized double-blind controlled trial enrolled 160 women aged 18-60 years (ASA I-II) scheduled for elective hysteroscopy, randomized to four groups: esketamine 0.15 mg/kg (K1), 0.3 mg/kg (K2), 0.5 mg/kg (K3), or sufentanil 0.1 µg/kg (S), all combined with propofol 1.5 mg/kg; 157 patients were analyzed after excluding three protocol deviations. Primary outcomes were postoperative Numeric Rating Scale (NRS) pain scores and total intraoperative propofol consumption; secondary outcomes included perioperative hemodynamics, recovery time, and adverse events; NRS scores at 10 and 30 min post-recovery were significantly lower in K2, K3, and S versus K1 (p<0.001). Limitations include single-center design in China, restriction to healthy women (ASA I-II), limited follow-up for chronic pain outcomes, and retrospective registration of the trial.

Implications Medical Affairs : The significantly higher intraoperative hypertension rate with low-dose esketamine (22.5% with 0.15 mg/kg) has direct implications for anesthesia protocols in hypertensive patients undergoing gynecological procedures, reinforcing the need for dose optimization in perioperative BP management guidelines. The favorable hemodynamic profile of esketamine 0.3 mg/kg versus opioids positions this dose as the preferred opioid-sparing agent in surgical patients with hypertension risk, an area where Medical Affairs teams can develop perioperative analgesia positioning programs for hospital anesthesiologists. Medical Affairs should leverage these data to engage anesthesiology KOLs and develop perioperative hypertension management protocols, particularly emphasizing the cardiovascular safety advantages of esketamine-based versus opioid-based anesthesia in hypertension-prone surgical populations.

Revue : Wiener Klinische Wochenschrift | Tier : NA | IF estime : NA

Date de publication : 2026-11-02

Resultats principaux : The PreCareFem Cohort is a prospective single-center observational study at University Hospital St. Pölten, Austria, enrolling 91 women with hypertensive disorders of pregnancy (HDP: 66 preeclampsia [PE], 25 gestational hypertension [GH]) between June 2023 and March 2025, assessed at peripartum, 3-6 months, and 12 months postpartum. Implications Medical Affairs : The 31% hypertension rate at 12 months postpartum in HDP women — far above general population rates — defines a high-risk population requiring structured long-term cardiovascular monitoring, representing a clear Medical Affairs opportunity to position antihypertensives in women's postpartum cardiovascular health guidelines. The differential cardiorenal risk between preeclampsia and gestational hypertension (21% vs. 8% albuminuria) supports disease staging and targeted therapy protocols, a scientific platform that Medical Affairs can leverage to engage maternal-fetal medicine, cardiology, and nephrology KOLs in developing postpartum cardiovascular management pathways. Sleep and Cardiometabolic Disease: A Wearable-Electronic Health Record Study in the US All of Us Research Program.

Revue : Circulation: Population Health and Outcomes | Tier : Tier 1 | IF estime : > 20

Date de publication : 2026-11-02

Resultats principaux : This cohort study linked Fitbit wearable data with electronic health records for 10,362 US adults in the All of Us Research Program (2009-2023), deriving 11 sleep metrics over 180-day baselines (≥90 valid nights) and following participants for incident cardiometabolic disease over a median 3.7 years. Night-to-night variability in sleep duration (each 1-SD = 29 min) was significantly associated with higher risk of the CVD composite (HR=1.24; 95% CI: 1.09-1.41), heart failure (HR=1.32; 95% CI: 1.13-1.55), hypertension (HR=1.16; 95% CI: 1.08-1.25), and type 2 diabetes (HR=1.20; 95% CI: 1.07-1.35). Critically, mean sleep duration was not associated with the CVD composite (HR=0.96; 95% CI: 0.85-1.08), suggesting sleep regularity — not duration alone — drives cardiometabolic risk, challenging the current Life's Essential 8 scoring which relies solely on duration. An exploratory finding in 7,199 participants showed more wearable-estimated sleep cycles were associated with lower atrial arrhythmia risk (HR=0.72; 95% CI: 0.58-0.89), independent of other sleep metrics. Limitations include the predominantly female cohort (70%), potential misclassification from consumer-grade wearables, observational design with residual confounding, limited racial and socioeconomic diversity, and 180-day washout potentially insufficient to eliminate reverse causation.

Implications Medical Affairs : The finding that sleep irregularity — not duration — drives hypertension risk challenges current Life's Essential 8 guidelines and supports an updated clinical message incorporating sleep regularity as a modifiable hypertension risk factor, creating a new platform for Medical Affairs engagement with preventive cardiology KOLs. Wearable-derived sleep data as predictors of hypertension onset opens opportunities for digital health partnerships in which pharmaceutical companies integrate BP monitoring apps with sleep regularity analytics to identify and engage high-risk pre-hypertensive patients. Medical Affairs teams should commission real-world studies examining whether sleep regularity interventions (chronotherapy, behavioral sleep medicine) reduce hypertension incidence, potentially creating a non-pharmacological adjunct to antihypertensive therapy that strengthens brand educational programs.

Date de publication : 2026-11-02

Resultats principaux : This 6-year prospective study followed 228 law enforcement officers (LEOs; 28% women) in Quebec, Canada, using annual online questionnaires assessing cardiovascular disease (CVD) risk factors, physical activity (active vs. inactive), and fruit and vegetable intake (≥5 vs. <5 servings/day), with incidence analyses of CVD risk factors stratified by baseline lifestyle behaviors. Limitations include a predominantly male sample in Quebec, self-reported dietary and physical activity data subject to recall bias, the online questionnaire format potentially introducing attrition bias, and an inability to control for all occupational stress factors specific to law enforcement.

Implications Medical Affairs : The strong dietary association between inadequate fruit/vegetable intake and incident hypertension (7% vs. 0% over 6 years) in a high-stress occupational cohort quantifies the modifiable lifestyle burden of hypertension, supporting Medical Affairs-led patient education programs targeting dietary patterns in high-stress professions. Law enforcement officers represent a high-risk cardiovascular population with poor lifestyle adherence that is systematically underserved by traditional hypertension prevention programs — a niche segment where pharmaceutical companies could position workplace health partnerships to increase early detection and treatment rates. Medical Affairs should use these occupational data to collaborate with occupational health physicians and corporate wellness program designers, creating a B2B channel for antihypertensive products that reaches pre-hypertensive individuals before they become treatment candidates.

Targeted Metabolites Associated with the Dietary Approaches to Stop Hypertension (DASH) Diet and Blood Pressure Changes in the DASH and DASH-Sodium Feeding Trials.

Revue : The Journal of Applied Laboratory Medicine | Tier : NA | IF estime : NA

Date de publication : 2026-11-02

Resultats principaux : This secondary analysis of the DASH (n=216) and DASH-Sodium (n=345) randomized controlled feeding trials measured serum concentrations of 15 pre-selected DASH-associated metabolites using targeted assays (absolute quantification), then assessed their correlations with untargeted metabolomic data and their associations with changes in systolic and diastolic blood pressure. Relative and absolute concentrations of 7 serum metabolites were strongly correlated between targeted and untargeted measurements (r=0.91 to 0.99), validating the targeted approach; the DASH diet most increased absolute serum concentrations of N-methylproline and significantly decreased theobromine. Absolute concentrations of 12 metabolites in the DASH trial and 10 in the DASH-Sodium trial were significantly different between DASH and control diet interventions; eight significant metabolite-diet interactions were observed for BP changes in both trials (all P for interaction <0.05). These findings suggest that specific serum metabolites mediate the BP-lowering effects of the DASH diet, providing mechanistic insight into dietary antihypertensive pathways and potential biomarkers for dietary adherence or response. Limitations include secondary analysis design with restricted causal inference, controlled feeding trial setting limiting generalizability to free-living populations, relatively short dietary intervention periods, and the focus on 15 pre-specified metabolites potentially missing novel mediators.

Implications Medical Affairs : The identification of specific metabolite-BP interaction signatures from the DASH diet provides a scientific foundation for developing metabolomics-based precision nutrition biomarkers that could complement pharmacological antihypertensive strategies, creating an opportunity for Medical Affairs to support precision medicine positioning of antihypertensives guided by dietary biomarkers. N-methylproline and other DASH-associated metabolites represent candidate surrogate endpoints for dietary adherence monitoring in antihypertensive clinical trials, potentially supporting combination endpoints in trial design discussions with regulatory agencies. Medical Affairs teams should engage nutrition science KOLs to develop guidelines integrating DASH dietary adherence biomarkers into hypertension management protocols alongside pharmacotherapy, reinforcing a comprehensive cardiovascular prevention narrative.

Prevalence, patterns, and correlates of non-communicable disease multimorbidity among healthcare workers: a multicenter study from Bangladesh.

Revue : BMC Public Health | Tier : NA | IF estime : NA

Date de publication : 2026-08-05

Resultats principaux : This cross-sectional multicenter study (July 2021-March 2024) enrolled 2,881 healthcare workers (HCWs) from 20 healthcare facilities across 4 administrative divisions in Bangladesh (63% female; median age 36 years) to assess NCD prevalence and multimorbidity using structured questionnaires, physical examinations, and anthropometric measurements. Overall NCD prevalence was 40% (n=1,162) with multimorbidity (≥2 NCDs) in 25% (n=708); hypertension was the most prevalent NCD at 21%, followed by diabetes (14%), chronic lung disease (11%), dyslipidemia (6%), and heart disease (3%). Multimorbidity was strongly associated with age ≥45 years (AOR=35.2; 95% CI: 22.3-55.7 vs. no NCD), female sex (AOR=1.8; 95% CI: 1.3-2.4), central obesity (AOR=2.8; 95% CI: 1.9-4.0), higher education (AOR=1.9; 95% CI: 1.1-3.2), and employment at tertiary healthcare facilities (AOR=1.9; 95% CI: 1.4-2.7). Notably, 1-in-4 HCWs had multimorbidity, with hypertension as the leading single NCD, raising concerns about undiagnosed and undertreated hypertension among healthcare professionals in LMICs. Limitations include cross-sectional design preventing causal conclusions, purposive rather than random facility selection potentially introducing sampling bias, mixed objective and self-reported NCD data, and restriction to Bangladesh limiting geographic generalizability.

Implications Medical Affairs : The 21% hypertension prevalence among Bangladeshi HCWs — a supposedly health-aware population — highlights the paradox of healthcare provider hypertension burden in LMICs, creating a compelling advocacy story for Medical Affairs engagement with hospital administration and NCD program directors in Bangladesh and similar markets. The high multimorbidity burden (25%) with hypertension as the leading anchor NCD supports the positioning of fixed-dose combination antihypertensives covering multiple risk factors in LMIC markets, where simplifying polypharmacy regimens is a critical unmet need. Medical Affairs should sponsor workplace health screening programs targeting HCWs in LMICs as a high-visibility CSR initiative, simultaneously improving hypertension detection rates and establishing brand presence among healthcare professional prescribers in emerging markets.
