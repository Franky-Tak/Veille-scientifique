Prevalence and determinants of poor outcome in patients with chronic kidney disease in low-income countries in sub-Saharan Africa: a systematic review.

Revue : The Pan African Medical Journal | Tier : NA | IF estime : NA

Date de publication : 2026-04-20

Resultats principaux : This systematic review evaluated the prevalence and outcomes of chronic kidney disease (CKD) in low-income countries in sub-Saharan Africa, covering studies published between 2018 and 2023 sourced from Google Scholar, PubMed, and Cochrane Review. The final analysis included 10 cross-sectional studies comprising a total of 6,963 patients, with quality assessment performed using the Ottawa-Newcastle scoring system, yielding a good overall quality rating. The reported prevalence of CKD ranged widely from 9.8% to 29.9%, with hypertension, diabetes mellitus, and advanced age identified as the principal associated risk factors alongside HIV and cardiovascular disease. The study found no reporting of mortality outcomes in any of the included studies, representing a critical gap in understanding the long-term prognosis of CKD patients in this region. This limitation substantially reduces the ability to draw conclusions about the downstream impact of CKD in sub-Saharan Africa and calls for prospective cohort studies specifically designed to capture mortality endpoints.

Implications Medical Affairs : The documented CKD prevalence of up to 29.9% in sub-Saharan Africa, largely driven by hypertension and diabetes, creates a compelling case for antihypertensive agents with established nephroprotective profiles — such as ACE inhibitors and ARBs — and supports differentiated product positioning in African emerging markets. The absence of mortality outcome data in the existing literature represents a strategic opportunity for Medical Affairs to sponsor or support investigator-initiated real-world evidence studies that could generate the endpoints needed to strengthen guideline recommendations and health technology assessment submissions in these markets. Proactive KOL engagement with African nephrology and hypertension societies could accelerate advocacy for systematic CKD outcome monitoring and increase awareness of the hypertension-CKD continuum among regional prescribers.

Global, Regional, and National Burden of Stroke Attributable to High Systolic Blood Pressure in Adults Aged 45 Years and Above From 1990 to 2021, with Projections to 2045: A Systematic and Comprehensive Analysis of the Global Burden of Disease Study 2021.

Revue : Archives of Iranian Medicine | Tier : NA | IF estime : NA

Date de publication : 2026-02-01

Resultats principaux : This systematic analysis used Global Burden of Disease Study 2021 data to quantify stroke mortality and disability-adjusted life years (DALYs) attributable to high systolic blood pressure (HSBP) in adults aged 45 years and older across sex, age, and socio-demographic index (SDI) strata from 1990 to 2021, with ARIMA model-based projections to 2045. In 2021, HSBP-attributable stroke deaths reached 4.12 million globally and associated DALYs totaled 86.18 million in this age group. Despite a 51.5% increase in absolute stroke deaths and a 47.9% increase in DALYs from 1990 to 2021, age-standardized mortality rates (ASMR) and age-standardized DALYs rates (ASDR) declined substantially (EAPC = -1.72 and -1.61, respectively), indicating improved population-level management but a growing absolute burden driven by aging demographics. Burden was highest in low-SDI regions, with an earlier mortality peak at 70-79 years and highest ASMR and ASDR, while high-SDI regions showed the lowest burden and the largest declines; subgroup analysis revealed that the rate of reduction was faster in females through 2045 projections. A key limitation is the reliance on modeled GBD estimates, which may introduce uncertainty particularly in low-SDI regions where vital registration and health data systems are underdeveloped.

Implications Medical Affairs : The growing absolute burden of HSBP-attributable stroke — 4.12 million deaths and 86.18 million DALYs in 2021 — despite declining age-standardized rates, confirms that hypertension-driven cardiovascular disease remains an urgent and expanding unmet medical need, particularly justifying continued investment in effective antihypertensive therapies targeting blood pressure control for stroke prevention. This GBD 2021 dataset provides robust epidemiological evidence to anchor global value dossiers and health technology assessment submissions, supporting the cardiovascular stroke-prevention value proposition of antihypertensive product portfolios, especially in markets with high low-SDI penetration. Medical Affairs should leverage this publication in KOL engagement focused on stroke neurologists, hypertension specialists, and public health authorities, particularly emphasizing the disproportionate burden in aging populations and low-income regions to drive systolic BP control as a public health priority.
