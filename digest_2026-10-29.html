Environmental and psychosocial factors in hypertension: an emerging landscape of cardiovascular risk.

Revue : Blood pressure | Tier : 3 | IF estime : 3-10

Date de publication : 2026-10-28

Resultats principaux : This narrative review adopts the exposome framework to evaluate the contributions of environmental and psychosocial factors to hypertension development, progression, and control across diverse populations. The review synthesizes evidence on multiple converging pathways, including neuroendocrine activation, oxidative stress, and chronic inflammation, all leading to endothelial dysfunction and blood pressure elevation. A key quantitative finding is that each 10 µg/m³ increase in fine particulate matter (PM2.5) is associated with a 4% rise in hypertension prevalence, establishing air pollution as a major modifiable cardiovascular risk factor. Transportation noise is identified as increasing hypertension risk through stress-related neurohormonal activation, oxidative stress, and sleep disruption, while cold temperatures exert a greater cardiovascular impact than heat through vasoconstriction mechanisms. Post-traumatic stress disorder (PTSD) emerges as an underrecognized determinant of hypertension, particularly among refugees, migrants, and war veterans, through chronic sympathetic activation and inflammatory pathways. Spaceflight provides a unique extreme-environment model showing cardiovascular adaptations including transient BP dysregulation and vascular remodelling resembling accelerated ageing. Notable limitations include the narrative review design which may introduce selection bias, and the emerging nature of data on micro- and nanoplastics which precludes definitive conclusions.

Implications Medical Affairs : This review highlights that hypertension guidelines should urgently incorporate environmental and psychosocial risk factor assessment into routine clinical practice, potentially expanding the scope of guideline-driven management beyond traditional pharmacological targets. The robust association between PM2.5 exposure and hypertension prevalence provides Medical Affairs teams with evidence to position cardiovascular-protective therapies and advocate for policy measures addressing environmental determinants in urban populations. The underrecognized link between PTSD and hypertension opens a significant KOL engagement opportunity with trauma medicine, psychiatry, and cardiovascular specialists to develop integrated screening protocols and multidisciplinary care pathways.

Remnant cholesterol predicts progression from prehypertension to incident hypertension: Evidence from a prospective cohort study and Mendelian randomization analysis.

Revue : Science progress | Tier : NA | IF estime : NA

Date de publication : 2026-10-28

Resultats principaux : This prospective cohort study enrolled 2,858 adults with prehypertension from the China Health and Retirement Longitudinal Study, followed from 2011 to 2015 to assess whether remnant cholesterol (RC) predicts progression to incident hypertension. The primary endpoint was development of incident hypertension, which occurred in 891 participants (31.2%) over the 4-year follow-up period. Each 1 mg/dL increase in RC was independently associated with a 2% higher odds of incident hypertension in fully adjusted multivariable models (p < 0.05), and participants in the highest RC quartile had 67% higher odds (corresponding to an absolute increase of 11.86% in cumulative incidence) versus the lowest quartile. Restricted cubic spline analysis demonstrated a strictly linear dose-response relationship between RC and hypertension risk, with no evidence of nonlinearity. Subgroup analyses revealed stronger associations among older adults, non-smokers, individuals with normal body weight, and participants without depressive symptoms. Two-sample Mendelian randomization analyses, using inverse-variance weighted and Bayesian weighted methods, provided genetic evidence supporting a potential causal role of elevated RC in hypertension-related traits. Key limitations include the single-country observational design and reliance on self-reported data, which may introduce measurement error.

Implications Medical Affairs : The identification of remnant cholesterol as a novel, routinely calculable biomarker for hypertension progression is highly impactful for guideline updates around cardiometabolic risk stratification in prehypertensive patients. From a product positioning perspective, lipid-lowering therapies that specifically reduce triglyceride-rich lipoprotein remnants could be repositioned to address upstream hypertension risk, particularly in Asian populations where remnant cholesterol burden may be disproportionate. Medical Affairs teams should engage with endocrinologists, cardiologists, and general practitioners to disseminate these findings and explore RC monitoring as a complementary screening tool within primary prevention programs for hypertension.

Machine learning-based risk stratification for moderate-to-severe anxiety-depression in patients with hypertension and/or coronary heart disease: a multicenter cross-sectional study.

Revue : BMC medical informatics and decision making | Tier : NA | IF estime : NA

Date de publication : 2026-07-31

Resultats principaux : This multicenter cross-sectional study retrospectively included 4,006 patients with hypertension and/or coronary heart disease from the 2023 Psychology and Behavior Investigation of Chinese Residents (PBICR) database, divided into training (70%, n=2,805) and test (30%, n=1,201) sets. The study aimed to develop machine learning (ML) models for risk stratification of moderate-to-severe anxiety-depression, defined as PHQ-9 ≥ 10 and/or GAD-7 ≥ 10, using LASSO regression to select 15 predictors from 43 initial candidate features. Among 8 ML models evaluated, Random Forest demonstrated the best overall performance with AUROC of 0.837 (95% CI: 0.812-0.862) in training and 0.838 (95% CI: 0.814-0.862; Brier score = 0.134) in the test set. SHAP analysis identified family health status, risk-taking behavior, and quality of life as the three strongest predictors of moderate-to-severe anxiety-depression in this cardio-metabolic population. Decision curve analysis indicated net benefit of the Random Forest model across risk thresholds of 10-40%, suggesting clinical utility in decision-making. Notable limitations include the cross-sectional design preventing causal inference, reliance on self-reported psychological measures, absence of external validation cohorts, and lack of prospective clinical evaluation. The proposed two-stage screening workflow remains hypothetical and has not been prospectively validated.

Implications Medical Affairs : The high prevalence of anxiety-depression comorbidity in hypertensive patients documented by this study highlights a critical gap in integrated cardiopsychiatric care that directly impacts antihypertensive treatment adherence and long-term cardiovascular outcomes. Medical Affairs teams should position this evidence to support integrated mental health screening within hypertension management programs, particularly in Asian markets where stigma may limit psychiatric diagnosis rates. Engaging KOLs at the intersection of cardiology and psychiatry to validate ML-based screening tools in clinical settings represents a strategic opportunity to strengthen evidence generation in this underserved comorbidity space.

Pathways linking to behavioural and biological risk factors on diabetes and overweight/obese among adults in urban slums from Western India: a structural equation modelling analysis.

Revue : Journal of health, population, and nutrition | Tier : NA | IF estime : NA

Date de publication : 2026-07-28

Resultats principaux : This cross-sectional community-based study enrolled 456 adults from urban slums in western India using three-stage sampling and the WHO STEPS methodology to characterize the NCD burden and associated risk factors. The study found that hypertension prevalence was 14.9%, diabetes 13.2%, and overweight/obesity 28.1%, reflecting the substantial NCD transition in low-income urban settings. Behavioral risk factors were highly prevalent: inadequate vegetable consumption (98.9%), insufficient physical activity (78.7%), smoking (23.2%), and alcohol consumption (13.8%). In multivariable logistic regression, smoking (AOR 3.22, 95% CI: 1.62-6.38), alcohol consumption (AOR 2.24, 95% CI: 1.03-4.89), and hyperhomocysteinemia (AOR 2.35, 95% CI: 1.15-4.82) were independently associated with hypertension, while fruit consumption was protective (AOR 0.37, 95% CI: 0.20-0.69). Structural equation modeling identified age (β=0.168), smoking (β=0.143), alcohol consumption (β=0.108), hypercholesterolemia (β=0.097), and socioeconomic status (β=0.092) as having significant direct associations with diabetes. Physical inactivity showed a significant direct association with overweight/obesity (β=0.105, p<0.05). Limitations include the small sample size (n=456), cross-sectional design, restriction to a single urban area, and the inability to establish temporal causality.

Implications Medical Affairs : The documented hypertension prevalence of nearly 15% in a low-resource urban population underscores the urgent need to expand antihypertensive treatment access in underserved urban Indian communities, representing a growing market opportunity for affordable pharmacological therapies. From a Medical Affairs perspective, the identification of smoking, alcohol, and hyperhomocysteinemia as independent hypertension risk factors provides a rationale for multimodal prevention messaging and collaboration with public health bodies in India. These data support the development of KOL-led educational initiatives targeting primary care physicians in tier-2 and tier-3 Indian cities, to improve hypertension diagnosis and treatment rates in high-risk slum populations.

Mind Your Heart-A Web-Based Dietary Approaches to Stop Hypertension Diet and Mindful Eating Intervention for Working Adults at Elevated Cardiovascular Risk: Pilot Randomized Controlled Trial.

Revue : JMIR formative research | Tier : NA | IF estime : NA

Date de publication : 2026-10-28

Resultats principaux : This pilot randomized controlled trial enrolled 37 working adults at elevated cardiovascular risk (mean BMI 31.93, 91.9% female, 83.8% White) at a university workplace, randomized to a web-based DASH diet education program alone or the combined DASH plus mindful eating intervention (Mind Your Heart, MYH). The 6-week fully asynchronous digital intervention was delivered via the Desire2Learn Brightspace platform with no personalized coaching, achieving a mean curriculum completion rate of 85.9% (SD 21.4), with 86.1% completing ≥60% of content. No significant between-group differences were observed for primary outcomes including DASH adherence, anthropometric measures, blood pressure, or cardiometabolic biomarkers, consistent with the feasibility design and insufficient statistical power for efficacy testing. However, the MYH group showed significantly greater improvements in cognitive dietary restraint (TFEQ mean difference 0.39, 95% CI: 0.06-0.72; Hedges' g=0.81; p=.02) and controlled eating regulation (REBS mean difference 3.59, 95% CI: 0.01-7.16; Hedges' g=0.68; p=.05) compared to diet-only. No adverse events were reported, confirming safety and acceptability of the digital intervention format. Ecological momentary assessments captured real-time eating behaviors throughout the intervention period, with 88.9% of participants completing at least half the scheduled EMA prompts. Critical limitations include the very small sample size, near-homogeneous female and White population, absence of blinding, and pilot feasibility design, which preclude any efficacy conclusions.

Implications Medical Affairs : This pilot RCT demonstrates feasibility and high engagement of a digital DASH plus mindful eating intervention, providing a scalable eHealth model for cardiovascular risk reduction that could complement pharmacological hypertension management in workplace settings. From a Medical Affairs perspective, digital lifestyle intervention platforms could be positioned as adjunct therapies to enhance antihypertensive medication adherence and dietary compliance, presenting opportunities for pharmaceutical-digital health partnerships. The promising cognitive restraint improvements in the MYH arm warrant investment in a fully powered RCT, and Medical Affairs teams should engage with digital health KOLs and preventive cardiology experts to support the next phase of evidence generation.

Global Burden of Anorexia Nervosa and Bulimia Nervosa, 1990 to 2023, With Forecasts to 2050: A Global Burden of Disease Study 2023 Analysis.

Revue : JAMA psychiatry | Tier : NA | IF estime : NA

Date de publication : 2026-10-28

Resultats principaux : This Global Burden of Disease 2023 analysis synthesized data from systematic literature reviews, population surveys, and vital registration systems across 204 countries and territories to estimate prevalence, disability burden, and mortality attributable to anorexia nervosa and bulimia nervosa from 1990 to 2023 with forecasts to 2050. In 2023, an estimated 18.1 million people (95% UI: 13.9-24.0 million) were living with anorexia nervosa or bulimia nervosa globally, accounting for 3.83 million DALYs (95% UI: 2.23-5.69 million). The global number of prevalent cases increased by 71.0% since 1990, while age-standardized prevalence and DALY rates rose by approximately 21%, reflecting both population growth and true incidence increases. Females accounted for 69.4% of cases (12.6 million), with 41.2% of all cases aged 10-24 years; the highest age-standardized prevalence rates were observed in high-income regions (Australasia, Western Europe). The largest relative increases in age-standardized prevalence occurred in East Asia, South Asia, and Southeast Asia, suggesting an emerging epidemic in low-to-middle income countries. Forecasts indicate continued increases in prevalent cases through 2050, with potentially significant cardiovascular comorbidity implications due to electrolyte disturbances and autonomic dysregulation observed in these disorders. Limitations include the reliance on limited primary data from low-income settings and the indirect relevance to hypertension management, as the study does not specifically address blood pressure outcomes.

Implications Medical Affairs : Although primarily addressing eating disorders, this GBD 2023 study is relevant for Medical Affairs because eating disorders carry cardiovascular comorbidities, including autonomic dysfunction and electrolyte disturbances, which can significantly complicate hypertension assessment and management. The rapid rise of eating disorders in Asian markets, which are also high-burden hypertension markets, signals an emerging comorbidity challenge that pharmaceutical companies should factor into their regional medical education and market access strategies. Medical Affairs teams with cardiovascular portfolios should collaborate with psychiatry KOLs to develop guidance on antihypertensive drug use in patients with concurrent eating disorders, particularly regarding QTc prolongation and electrolyte monitoring requirements.

Under-recognition of metabolic syndrome in North West London (2000-2023): a 23-year retrospective analysis.

Revue : BMJ public health | Tier : NA | IF estime : NA

Date de publication : 2026-10-26

Resultats principaux : This 23-year retrospective analysis examined the Discover-NOW London Secure Data Environment dataset from January 2000 to July 2023, encompassing 2,096,480 adults registered in North West London. The primary finding was a 600-fold discrepancy between clinician-coded metabolic syndrome diagnoses (975 individuals, 0.04%) and algorithm-defined prevalence using AHA 2005 criteria (594,325 individuals, 21.2%), with extended AHA criteria identifying even more cases (875,993 individuals, 31.3%). Hypertension is a central diagnostic criterion of metabolic syndrome, making this profound under-recognition directly relevant to blood pressure management at the population level. Subgroup analysis revealed that women, individuals of Black or Black British ethnicity, and those from more deprived areas were systematically under-represented in the coded cohort relative to algorithm-defined cohorts, indicating significant health equity gaps. The sustained nature of this under-recognition across 23 years suggests a systemic failure in clinical coding practices rather than a temporary data quality issue. The Extended AHA definition incorporating BMI and HbA1c identified an additional 281,668 individuals (10.1% of the population) beyond the standard AHA criteria. Notable limitations include the retrospective design, restriction to one geographic area, and potential unmeasured confounders affecting coding practices.

Implications Medical Affairs : The dramatic under-recognition of metabolic syndrome, with hypertension as a central component, represents a massive missed opportunity for early cardiovascular risk intervention and highlights the urgent need for algorithm-driven electronic health record flagging systems in routine clinical practice. From a Medical Affairs perspective, this evidence provides a strong case for advocating metabolic syndrome screening programs in primary care, which would substantially expand the eligible patient population for antihypertensive, lipid-lowering, and glucose-lowering therapies. Engaging with NHS health systems, primary care networks, and public health KOLs in the UK to promote structured metabolic syndrome coding and screening algorithms represents a high-impact Medical Affairs activity that aligns with both patient outcomes and market access objectives.
