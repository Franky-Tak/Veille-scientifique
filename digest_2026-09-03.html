Predictive value of homocysteine and blood lipid levels in late pregnancy for neonatal outcomes in women with hypertensive disorders of pregnancy.

Revue : The Journal of Maternal-Fetal & Neonatal Medicine | Tier : NA | IF estime : NA

Date de publication : 2026-09-02

Resultats principaux : This retrospective study analyzed 156 patients with hypertensive disorders of pregnancy (HDP) — 61 with gestational hypertension (GH) and 95 with preeclampsia (PE) — investigating whether late-pregnancy homocysteine (Hcy) and lipid profiles correlate with disease severity and predict adverse neonatal outcomes. Primary endpoints were associations between biomarker levels and PE status, and the predictive performance of individual and combined biomarkers for adverse neonatal outcomes assessed by ROC curve analysis. Significant differences were found between GH and PE groups in systolic blood pressure, Hcy, HDL-C, LDL-C, total cholesterol (TC), triglycerides (TG), and albumin; logistic regression confirmed that elevated Hcy, TC, and TG were independently associated with PE status. Higher Hcy and TG levels and lower HDL-C were each independently associated with increased risk of adverse neonatal outcomes. The combined detection of Hcy, TG, and HDL-C demonstrated superior predictive performance (AUC 0.874, 95% CI: 0.812–0.922) over any individual marker; key limitations include the retrospective single-center design and small sample size (n=156) which may limit external validity.

Implications Medical Affairs : This study provides evidence supporting the integration of homocysteine and lipid panel testing into routine antenatal monitoring protocols for hypertensive disorders of pregnancy, with potential to inform guideline updates on biomarker-based risk stratification in high-risk obstetric populations. From a Medical Affairs perspective, the validated combined biomarker approach reinforces the therapeutic relevance of addressing metabolic dysregulation in pregnancy-related hypertension and could support positioning strategies for agents targeting lipid and metabolic pathways in this indication. The Hcy/TG/HDL-C combination represents an actionable insight for KOL engagement around companion diagnostics and precision medicine in obstetric hypertension management.

Dietary patterns and associated cardiometabolic risk factors among teachers in Makhado Municipality, South Africa.

Revue : African Journal of Primary Health Care & Family Medicine | Tier : NA | IF estime : NA

Date de publication : 2026-08-14

Resultats principaux : This cross-sectional study examined dietary patterns and cardiometabolic risk factor associations among 201 teachers in Makhado Municipality, Limpopo Province, South Africa, recruited via stratified random sampling from rural public schools. Primary endpoints included identification of dietary patterns via exploratory factor analysis and their associations with blood pressure, fasting glucose, lipid profiles, and anthropometric measures. Six distinct dietary patterns emerged: mixed, Western, meat-free, Mediterranean fish-free, animal starch/low-carbohydrate, and Mediterranean; the mixed dietary pattern was significantly associated with systolic blood pressure (p=0.03), and the Western pattern was associated with total cholesterol (p=0.04) and LDL-C (p=0.02). Meat-free and Mediterranean fish-free patterns were associated with HDL-C (p=0.03), while the animal protein/fruit pattern was associated with fasting glucose (p=0.02); no subgroup analyses for sex or age were reported. Limitations include the cross-sectional design precluding causal inference, use of a food frequency questionnaire with inherent recall bias, and single-municipality sampling limiting representativeness to other South African or sub-Saharan African populations.

Implications Medical Affairs : These findings underscore diet as a modifiable contributor to hypertension and cardiometabolic risk in African working populations, supporting the inclusion of culturally appropriate nutritional interventions in hypertension prevention guidelines for sub-Saharan Africa. From a Medical Affairs standpoint, the data identify an underserved segment — employed urban-rural adults — where dietary-driven hypertension prevalence may be high, presenting an opportunity for disease awareness campaigns and patient education programs aligned with antihypertensive product positioning. KOL engagement in sub-Saharan Africa could leverage this real-world epidemiological evidence to advocate for integrated hypertension prevention and treatment programs within workplace wellness frameworks.

Study of weight loss intervention to find an effective strategy for obesity management and the TARGET of weight loss amount for reducing health disorders (SLIM-TARGET): a randomized controlled trial.

Revue : Endocrine Journal | Tier : NA | IF estime : NA

Date de publication : 2026-09-02

Resultats principaux : This multicenter Japanese RCT (UMIN000031862) enrolled 294 participants with at least six obesity-related conditions, randomized to standard treatment (12-month target: ≥3% weight loss, following Japanese Guidelines 2016) or intensive treatment (≥7% weight loss target with enhanced nutritional guidance, diet/exercise therapy, and pharmacotherapy). The primary composite outcome — weight loss combined with improvement in at least two obesity-related disorders at 12 months — was not significantly different between groups (weight loss: -1.7% standard vs. -2.5% intensive; p=NS), largely due to both groups failing to achieve their respective weight loss targets. However, intensive treatment achieved significantly higher obesity-related disorder remission (16.9% vs. 6.2%; adjusted OR 3.079, 95% CI: 1.075–8.823; p=0.036) and greater visceral fat reduction (-20.46 cm² vs. -8.9 cm²; p=0.039). Intensive treatment also demonstrated improved early blood pressure control and significantly higher treatment motivation scores at 24 months (+10.9%, 95% CI: 1.2–20.6; p=0.004). Limitations include failure to achieve predefined weight loss targets in both arms, a specialized clinical setting potentially limiting generalizability, and the use of composite endpoints that may obscure individual disorder-specific effects.

Implications Medical Affairs : This RCT demonstrates that intensive multifaceted lifestyle and pharmacotherapy-augmented weight management yields significantly better blood pressure control and disorder remission even absent target weight loss, supporting the integration of structured weight management programs into hypertension treatment guidelines for patients with comorbid obesity. Medical Affairs teams can use these data to position pharmacotherapy-supplemented obesity programs as adjunctive strategies in hypertensive-obese patients, particularly where combination metabolic-cardiovascular approaches are under regulatory or reimbursement consideration. The sustained improvement in treatment motivation scores at 24 months identifies patient engagement and behavioral support as modifiable drivers of long-term adherence, an actionable insight for designing patient support programs and for KOL discussions around comprehensive cardiometabolic care pathways.

Revue : Journal of the American Society of Nephrology | Tier : NA | IF estime : NA

Date de publication : 2026-09-02

Resultats principaux : This executive summary from the American Society of Nephrology (ASN) presents kidney-specific guidance aligned with the 2024 AHA/ACC/ADA/ASN Cardiovascular-Kidney-Metabolic (CKM) Syndrome guideline, targeting the broad clinical population of patients with CKD, diabetes, obesity, and cardiovascular disease across all healthcare disciplines. The primary recommendations center on routine cardiovascular risk assessment using the PREVENT equation in CKD patients, and establishment of renin-angiotensin system (RAS) inhibitors and SGLT2 inhibitors as foundational therapies for preventing cardiovascular and kidney endpoints. GLP-1-based therapies and nonsteroidal mineralocorticoid receptor antagonists (nsMRAs) are recommended as add-on treatments based on individual cardiovascular and metabolic risk profiles, with the guidance explicitly discouraging therapeutic inertia and encouraging simultaneous or rapid-sequence therapy initiation. Subgroup considerations highlight limited evidence for transplant recipients, dialysis patients, pediatric populations, older adults, and frail patients, though screening and individualized therapy with shared decision-making are still recommended. Limitations include reliance on evidence from predominantly Western populations, heterogeneity of evidence across subgroups, and implementation challenges in resource-limited or fragmented healthcare systems.

Implications Medical Affairs : This ASN guidance document consolidates and strengthens the evidence base for multi-drug CKM management in hypertensive CKD patients, directly reinforcing guideline-supported use of RAS inhibitors as first-line therapy and positioning SGLT2 inhibitors and nsMRAs as essential add-on agents in this high-risk population. For Medical Affairs, this document is a major reference for KOL communications and scientific messaging supporting the combination therapy approach in the growing CKM syndrome patient segment, providing an authoritative basis for educational initiatives targeting nephrologists and cardiologists. The explicit call to avoid therapeutic inertia and initiate combination therapies early represents a strategic opportunity for medical education programs advocating early and comprehensive antihypertensive intervention in CKD.

Displaced people and kidney health.

Revue : Current Opinion in Nephrology and Hypertension | Tier : NA | IF estime : NA

Date de publication : 2026-09-02

Resultats principaux : This narrative review synthesizes current evidence on the burden, risk factors, barriers, and management of kidney disease in globally displaced populations, drawing on reports from crisis settings including Syria, Sudan, Ethiopia, Ukraine, Gaza, and others. The review identifies multiple risk pathways: acute kidney injury (AKI) driven by trauma, dehydration, infections, malnutrition, and environmental exposures, while hypertension and diabetes management interruptions are highlighted as primary drivers of chronic kidney disease (CKD) progression in displaced populations. Major barriers documented include disrupted health systems, dialysis and medication shortages, limited diagnostic capacity, financial constraints, and language/cultural challenges, with case reports and observational data demonstrating substantial treatment interruptions and adverse outcomes for dialysis-dependent and transplant patients. Subgroup evidence is drawn from diverse geographic and conflict settings, revealing consistent patterns of disproportionate kidney disease burden and care interruption across different displacement contexts. Key limitations include the absence of robust epidemiological data from displaced settings, heterogeneity of available study designs, reliance on NGO reports rather than controlled studies, and challenges in collecting reliable outcome data from crisis zones.

Implications Medical Affairs : This review identifies displaced populations as a major underserved segment in which hypertension management interruptions directly contribute to CKD progression and cardiovascular mortality, underscoring the urgency of treatment continuity strategies and essential antihypertensive medication access in humanitarian contexts. From a Medical Affairs perspective, these findings provide a compelling basis for global health access programs and corporate social responsibility initiatives focused on maintaining antihypertensive treatment continuity in crisis settings, aligned with broader disease burden messaging. KOL engagement opportunities include collaboration with humanitarian medicine specialists and global health policy advocates, while the identified evidence gap in displaced population epidemiology could motivate support for targeted research programs.

Revue : The EPMA Journal | Tier : NA | IF estime : NA

Date de publication : 2026-08-10

Resultats principaux : This review proposes a spatiotemporal framework for the gut microbiota-bile acid axis in cardiovascular disease (CVD) within a predictive, preventive, and personalized medicine (PPPM/3PM) paradigm, positioning this axis across the health-suboptimal health-disease continuum. Mechanistic evidence is synthesized for microbial bile salt hydrolase activity and downstream bile acid transformations linking diet and environmental exposures to cholesterol catabolism, inflammatory and barrier responses, vascular homeostasis, platelet activation, and myocardial stress. The review notes that mechanistic evidence is most developed for atherosclerosis and atherothrombosis, while evidence for pulmonary hypertension, atrial fibrillation, heart failure, and cerebrovascular outcomes is more heterogeneous and less mature. Subgroup-level analysis highlights that compartment-specific bile acid pool alterations exert distinct cardiovascular effects depending on hepatobiliary status, metabolic-inflammatory context, and medication exposure, necessitating multi-domain functional phenotyping for clinical interpretation. Key limitations include the predominantly preclinical mechanistic evidence base, absence of standardized multi-compartment bile acid profiling, limited longitudinal human multi-omics studies, and the need for causal validation in clinically relevant models.

Implications Medical Affairs : This review identifies the gut microbiota-bile acid axis as an emerging therapeutic frontier in cardiometabolic disease including hypertension-related conditions, supporting future research programs targeting microbiota-directed interventions as complements to established antihypertensive therapies. For Medical Affairs, these findings provide a biological rationale for scientific engagement around novel cardiometabolic therapies with gut or metabolic mechanisms, particularly for products where renal or inflammatory pathways intersect with the bile acid axis. The proposed PPPM framework creates opportunities for KOL engagement around precision medicine and biomarker-driven patient stratification in cardiovascular prevention, especially with societies at the intersection of cardiology, gastroenterology, and metabolic medicine.

Sex differences in insulin resistance and associated metabolic and inflammatory markers among adults with and without HIV in Zambia.

Revue : Physiological Reports | Tier : NA | IF estime : NA

Date de publication : 2026-09

Resultats principaux : This cross-sectional study of 233 adults in Zambia examined sex-specific associations between insulin resistance (IR, measured by HOMA-IR) and clinical, metabolic, and inflammatory markers in individuals with and without HIV, using sex-stratified multivariable linear regression models. Females had significantly higher BMI (26.9 vs. 23.5 kg/m², p<0.001), waist circumference (88.3 vs. 83.2 cm, p=0.008), fasting insulin, and HOMA-IR compared to males. In the adjusted overall model, female sex was independently associated with higher HOMA-IR (beta=0.95, 95% CI: 0.06–1.84; p=0.037), while HIV-positive status was inversely associated (beta=-1.54, 95% CI: -3.06 to -0.02; p=0.046). Critically, subgroup analyses demonstrated that hypertension was independently associated with higher HOMA-IR specifically in males (beta=0.61; p=0.019), while among females, HIV-positive status showed an inverse association with HOMA-IR (beta=-2.31; p=0.049). Limitations include the cross-sectional design precluding causal inference, small sample size (n=233), single-region Zambian recruitment limiting generalizability, and absence of hormonal or antiretroviral therapy regimen data that may confound IR relationships.

Implications Medical Affairs : The sex-specific finding that hypertension is independently associated with higher insulin resistance in males in sub-Saharan Africa provides epidemiological evidence for cardiometabolic risk clustering in HIV-prevalent populations, supporting integration of insulin resistance screening into hypertension management protocols in these regions. From a Medical Affairs perspective, these sex-disaggregated data strengthen the scientific rationale for targeted prevention and treatment strategies in the cardiometabolic-HIV overlap population — a growing, high-cardiovascular-risk segment that may represent a niche indication for antihypertensive agents with favorable metabolic profiles. This evidence is actionable for KOL engagement with infectious disease and cardiovascular specialists in sub-Saharan Africa, advocating for combined hypertension-metabolic syndrome management programs and supporting studies evaluating antihypertensive effects in HIV-positive populations.

Identification of Mental Health Disorder Symptoms, Predisposing Factors and their Impact on Quality of Life in Young Hypertensive Patients: A Multicenter Longitudinal Study.

Revue : Clinical Practice and Epidemiology in Mental Health | Tier : NA | IF estime : NA

Date de publication : 2026-07-29

Resultats principaux : This multicenter longitudinal study from Indonesia recruited 791 young adults (mean age 33 ± 10.14 years; range 18–45) with established hypertension from outpatient clinics across multiple cities, assessing mental health via the DASS-21 and quality of life via the WHOQOL-BREF, with a subset undergoing qualitative semi-structured interviews on psychosocial stressors. Primary quantitative outcomes showed that longer hypertension duration correlated significantly with worse overall quality of life (r=-0.45) and lower psychological well-being (r=-0.40), as well as with higher levels of anxiety (r=-0.42), depression (r=-0.38), and stress (r=-0.30). The qualitative strand identified socioeconomic and psychosocial stressors — including financial insecurity, work stress, and social isolation — as major predisposing factors to both hypertension onset and psychological burden in this cohort. Although the longitudinal design strengthens temporal associations compared to cross-sectional work, women slightly outnumbered men and sex-specific analyses were not formally reported. Limitations include reliance on self-reported psychometric instruments, potential unmeasured socioeconomic confounders, the Indonesian cultural context limiting direct international generalizability, and absence of a normotensive control group for direct comparison.

Implications Medical Affairs : This large multicenter longitudinal study provides robust epidemiological evidence for the bidirectional relationship between hypertension and mental health burden in young adults, directly supporting guideline-level recommendations for routine psychological screening and psychosocial interventions as integral components of hypertension management in younger populations. From a Medical Affairs perspective, these data strengthen the positioning of antihypertensive therapies demonstrating quality-of-life and patient-reported outcome benefits as meaningful differentiators over standard-of-care agents in young hypertensive patients. This evidence is actionable for KOL engagement with psychiatry, primary care, and hypertension specialists in Southeast Asia and other regions with high rates of early-onset hypertension, advocating for integrated cardiopsychiatric care pathways and mental health parity in cardiovascular disease management.

Optimisation of maternal cardiometabolic outcomes after hypertensive disorders of pregnancy.

Revue : Women's Health (London) | Tier : NA | IF estime : NA

Date de publication : 2026-09-02

Resultats principaux : This narrative review from Imperial College London examines the evidence base for optimizing cardiometabolic surveillance and risk stratification in women after pregnancies complicated by hypertensive disorders of pregnancy (HDP), including preeclampsia (PE), gestational hypertension, and eclampsia. The review synthesizes evidence on optimal timing and duration of postpartum cardiovascular screening, risk stratification approaches for identifying women at greatest risk of future cardiovascular morbidity, and specific strategies for monitoring myocardial, vascular, and metabolic health in this high-risk cohort. Key evidence supports multidisciplinary postpartum cardiovascular clinics with telemonitoring and patient-led medication titration for blood pressure management, as well as lifestyle interventions tailored to PE phenotypes and individual cardiovascular risk profiles, particularly during the "fourth trimester" transition from hospital to community care. Subgroup considerations highlight substantial heterogeneity in international guideline recommendations regarding timing, duration, and modality of cardiovascular follow-up after HDP, with no validated risk stratification tool currently available for postpartum cardiovascular prediction. Limitations include the narrative (non-systematic) design of the review, absence of randomized controlled trial data supporting specific surveillance protocols, heterogeneity of available evidence across study designs and populations, and lack of validated risk stratification tools for guiding postpartum cardiovascular screening.

Implications Medical Affairs : This review highlights the critical postpartum window of elevated cardiovascular risk in HDP-affected women, reinforcing the need for structured guideline development around long-term cardiometabolic surveillance in this growing, high-risk population with potential to influence obstetric and cardiovascular society guidance updates. From a Medical Affairs perspective, these findings support patient-centered positioning strategies for antihypertensive and metabolic therapies in women of reproductive age with prior HDP, particularly for agents with demonstrated long-term cardiovascular protection that could be initiated during or continued after the peripartum period. This evidence provides a compelling basis for KOL engagement with maternal-fetal medicine specialists, women's cardiovascular health experts, and healthcare policy advocates for establishing specialized postpartum cardiovascular clinics and funding validated precision risk stratification research.
