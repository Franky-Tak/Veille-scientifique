Association between Chinese visceral adiposity index and prevalent hypertension among older adults in rural China: A cross-sectional study.

Revue : Clinical and Experimental Hypertension | Tier : 2 | IF estimé : 10–20

Date de publication : 2026-10-09

Résultats principaux : This cross-sectional study enrolled 3,280 adults aged 65 years or older from Baima Town, Taizhou, China, examining the relationship between the Chinese Visceral Adiposity Index (CVAI) and prevalent hypertension. The study found a hypertension prevalence of 82.1% (2,692/3,280), a strikingly high rate reflective of the older rural demographic. After full multivariable adjustment, each 10-unit increase in CVAI was associated with 14.3% higher odds of hypertension (OR = 1.143, 95% CI: 1.114–1.173, p < 0.001), and each 1-SD increase with 70.9% higher odds (OR = 1.709, 95% CI: 1.528–1.915). Restricted cubic spline analysis confirmed a positive, approximately linear dose-response relationship between CVAI and hypertension (p for nonlinearity = 0.153). CVAI outperformed conventional obesity indicators BMI and waist circumference in discriminatory ability on ROC analysis, though the study's cross-sectional design precludes causal inference. Subgroup and sensitivity analyses supported the robustness of the CVAI-hypertension association across different strata.

Implications Medical Affairs : CVAI's superior discriminatory performance over BMI and waist circumference supports its incorporation into hypertension screening algorithms for elderly Asian populations, potentially informing updates to regional guidelines. From a Medical Affairs perspective, CVAI constitutes a simple, low-cost tool that could be positioned alongside antihypertensive therapy initiation criteria, offering added value for products targeting cardiometabolic risk reduction in elderly patients. Pharmaceutical companies developing visceral-fat-targeted therapies or fixed-dose combinations for cardiometabolic risk should consider CVAI as an enrichment biomarker in clinical trial designs for Asian elderly populations.

Prediction of cardiovascular event risk within 90 days in patients with hypertension: A study based on random forest and nomogram models.

Revue : Medicine (Baltimore) | Tier : NA | IF estimé : NA

Date de publication : 2026-10-09

Résultats principaux : This retrospective study included 1,256 hypertensive patients treated between January 2024 and December 2025, of whom 120 (9.55%) experienced an adverse cardiovascular event within 90 days. Multivariate logistic regression identified five independent risk factors: systolic blood pressure ≥140 mmHg (OR 1.70, 95% CI 1.23–3.91), poor medication adherence (OR 1.51, 95% CI 1.10–1.96), comorbid diabetes (OR 1.80, 95% CI 1.23–2.46), lack of regular exercise (OR 1.47, 95% CI 1.07–1.78), and age ≥65 years (OR 1.64, 95% CI 1.15–3.44). A random forest (RF) model and a nomogram model were both developed and internally validated; the RF model achieved an AUC of 0.842 and the nomogram 0.812. Both models showed acceptable calibration on calibration curves and net clinical benefit on decision curve analysis, with internal validation via 10-fold cross-validation and bootstrap resampling. Key limitations include the retrospective, single-center design, absence of external validation cohort, and lack of formal statistical comparison between the two models.

Implications Medical Affairs : This predictive tool identifies poor medication adherence as a modifiable, high-impact risk factor for near-term cardiovascular events, reinforcing the Medical Affairs argument for patient-support programs and adherence-improving formulations. The nomogram's clinical utility makes it a strong candidate for integration into hypertension management pathways alongside pharmaceutical therapy, providing an opportunity for Medical Affairs teams to co-position adherence tools with antihypertensive product launches. An external validation in diverse populations and its integration into real-world clinical decision support systems should be prioritized as a KOL engagement topic at forthcoming cardiovascular congresses.

Self-care practices of people living with HIV and hypertension or diabetes comorbidities in low- and middle-income countries: A systematic review.

Revue : PLoS One | Tier : NA | IF estimé : NA

Date de publication : 2026-10-09

Résultats principaux : This systematic review synthesized 13 qualitative studies from six African countries, representing 458 participants living with HIV and comorbid hypertension or diabetes, published between January 2013 and March 2026. Five major self-care themes were identified using thematic synthesis: sense-making of illnesses and medications; divergent treatment approaches between HIV versus hypertension/diabetes; adoption of health-promoting behaviors; coping with psychosocial distress; and strategies to navigate healthcare insecurities. Evidence confidence ranged from moderate to high across themes per the GRADE-CERQual approach, grounding the review's conclusions in robust qualitative evidence. Treatment burden was a cross-cutting finding, driven by prescribed healthcare tasks, disparities between HIV and NCD care platforms, and patient-initiated compensatory strategies for gaps in hypertension and diabetes management. Limitations include restriction to African LMICs with entirely qualitative data, limiting generalizability to other regions and precluding quantitative burden estimates.

Implications Medical Affairs : The identified gaps in NCD self-care competency among PLHIV highlight a significant unmet need for integrated cardiovascular disease management programs, particularly in sub-Saharan Africa, where hypertension burden is rising sharply. Medical Affairs teams with products targeting HIV-NCD comorbidities can leverage these findings to advocate for integrated care models and support the development of patient education materials tailored to multimorbidity contexts. KOL strategy should prioritize infectious disease and hypertension specialists in African settings for advisory boards focused on co-managing HIV and cardiovascular risk factors, which aligns with growing global health investment in NCD integration.

Pregnancy complications and risk of cardiometabolic-renal conditions in 1.4 million women in England: longitudinal study.

Revue : BMJ Medicine | Tier : 1 | IF estimé : >20

Date de publication : 2026-10-06

Résultats principaux : This large longitudinal cohort study drew on the CPRD Aurum primary care database in England (2000–2022) and included up to 1,432,795 women aged 15–49 years with at least one pregnancy record, examining subsequent cardiometabolic-renal outcomes. Gestational hypertension and pre-eclampsia were associated with more than tripling of subsequent hypertension rates and approximately doubling of chronic kidney disease incidence, while cardiovascular disease and type 2 diabetes also increased significantly. Gestational diabetes mellitus conferred a sevenfold higher rate of type 2 diabetes and elevated rates of hypertension and cardiovascular disease in the years following delivery. Notably, postnatal depression increased the rate of all four outcomes by 35–56%, with hypertension HR 1.36 (95% CI 1.33–1.40), cardiovascular disease HR 1.56 (95% CI 1.50–1.62), type 2 diabetes HR 1.41 (95% CI 1.36–1.46), and chronic kidney disease HR 1.35 (95% CI 1.25–1.45). Less-explored adverse outcomes such as miscarriage, stillbirth, placental abruption, preterm birth, and abnormal fetal growth were also independently associated with elevated cardiometabolic-renal risks, supporting comprehensive reproductive history assessment. Median follow-up across cohorts was approximately 4.1–4.3 years; the observational design limits causal interpretation, and residual confounding is likely.

Implications Medical Affairs : These findings in 1.4 million women establish a compelling evidence base for positioning the postpartum period as an opportune window for cardiovascular and hypertension risk stratification, strongly supporting guideline updates that include detailed reproductive history as a standard element of cardiovascular risk assessment. From a Medical Affairs perspective, antihypertensive agents and cardiometabolic therapies could be repositioned with an evidence-based narrative targeting women of reproductive and post-reproductive age who have had adverse pregnancy outcomes. KOL engagement should target obstetricians, maternal-fetal medicine specialists, and cardiologists to co-develop post-partum cardiovascular follow-up protocols that incorporate early pharmacological intervention thresholds.

A Bias-Adjusted Approach for Logistic Modeling With Predicted Exposures.

Revue : Statistics in Medicine | Tier : NA | IF estimé : NA

Date de publication : 2026-10

Résultats principaux : This methodological paper developed bias-adjusted estimators for logistic regression parameters under a mixture measurement error model, addressing the systematic bias introduced when error-prone predicted exposures (from prediction models) are used in observational studies. The proposed approach uses bootstrap estimators of mixture error model parameters, a user-specified prediction model, and a linear regression calibration function, applicable to cohort designs with unknown measurement error model parameters. The method was validated in simulation studies and in an observational study relating hypertension incidence to glycohemoglobin (HbA1c), where the correction yielded accurate point estimates with near-nominal coverage probabilities. Sensitivity analyses showed that using an oversimplified prediction model causes severe biases, and even flexible machine learning models may yield lower accuracy and precision than correctly specified parametric models. Key limitations include restriction to cohort designs and the assumption that the mixture error model structure is correctly specified, with diagnostics provided for model assumption assessment.

Implications Medical Affairs : This statistical advance has direct relevance for hypertension epidemiology studies utilizing electronic health records or biobank data, where true exposure values (e.g., lifetime blood pressure burden) are estimated rather than directly measured. Medical Affairs teams overseeing real-world evidence generation should consider mandating measurement error correction in post-marketing studies to ensure unbiased estimates of treatment effects and risk associations. Incorporating such methods in pharmacoepidemiological studies strengthens regulatory submissions for label updates and supports higher-quality evidence generation for KOL publications in journals focused on cardiovascular outcomes.

Cardiovascular Death Risk and Cardiovascular Risk Awareness in Individuals with Hypertension.

Revue : Journal of the College of Physicians and Surgeons — Pakistan | Tier : NA | IF estimé : NA

Date de publication : 2026-10

Résultats principaux : This descriptive study enrolled 231 hypertensive patients at the Department of Family Medicine, Hacettepe University, Ankara, Turkey (October 2022–March 2023), of whom 64.5% were women with a mean age of 54.18 years. Cardiovascular death risk was estimated using both the SCORE and SCORE 2 risk models, while CVD risk awareness was assessed using the validated Cardiovascular Disease Risk Awareness Assessment Scale (CDRAAS). The mean CDRAAS score was 39.1 ± 5.57 (range 15–52), indicating suboptimal cardiovascular risk awareness across the hypertensive patient population. The SCORE 2 model demonstrated greater sensitivity than the original SCORE model for risk stratification, with significant differences in diastolic blood pressure between high-risk and very high-risk groups. The study's limitations include a relatively small, single-center sample from a tertiary-care setting, which may not be representative of the broader hypertensive population in Turkey.

Implications Medical Affairs : The finding of suboptimal CVD risk awareness in hypertensive patients, even those seen in tertiary family medicine settings, reinforces the need for structured patient education programs as part of hypertension management, which Medical Affairs teams can incorporate into product support materials. The demonstrated superiority of SCORE 2 over SCORE for risk stratification argues for updating pharmacological treatment initiation thresholds in regional hypertension guidelines, creating opportunities for Medical Affairs engagement with guideline committees in Europe and Turkey. Pharmaceutical companies can utilize these awareness gaps to drive KOL-led educational initiatives on comprehensive cardiovascular risk assessment, positioning their antihypertensive brands within a holistic risk reduction narrative.

Date de publication : 2026-10

Résultats principaux : This implementation science analysis reviewed protocols and follow-up data from six NHLBI-funded trials (2020–2023) across Botswana, Mozambique, Nigeria, South Africa, Uganda, and Zambia, aimed at integrating cardiovascular disease (CVD) care into HIV primary care. Ten cross-cutting implementation strategies were identified using the ERIC taxonomy, spanning individual, facility, and system levels; three strategies — healthcare worker training, audit and feedback, and practice facilitation/coaching — were common to all six trials. Association between platelet-to-high-density lipoprotein cholesterol ratio and incident stroke among adults with hypertension: A longitudinal CHARLS cohort study.

Revue : The Journal of International Medical Research | Tier : NA | IF estimé : NA

Date de publication : 2026-10-08

Résultats principaux : This retrospective secondary analysis of the China Health and Retirement Longitudinal Study (CHARLS) included 3,963 adults aged ≥45 years with hypertension at baseline and no stroke, followed over 34,637 person-years from 2011 to 2020. During follow-up, 551 participants reported incident stroke; each 1-SD increase in the platelet-to-HDL cholesterol (PHR) ratio was independently associated with a 15% higher stroke hazard (HR 1.15, 95% CI 1.07–1.24, p < 0.001) after multivariable adjustment. Quartile 4 versus quartile 1 comparison showed HR 1.33 (95% CI 1.05–1.69, p for trend = 0.007), supporting a dose-response relationship. Platelet count was independently and positively associated with incident stroke (HR per 1-SD increase 1.16, 95% CI 1.07–1.25), whereas HDL cholesterol's inverse association attenuated after mutual adjustment. Restricted cubic spline analysis indicated an overall significant but linear association (p for nonlinearity = 0.812), while key limitations include questionnaire-reported stroke outcomes without adjudication, potential selection bias, and observational design precluding causal claims.

Implications Medical Affairs : The PHR ratio represents a readily available, inexpensive composite biomarker that could improve stroke risk stratification beyond traditional risk factors in hypertensive patients, supporting its evaluation in guideline-endorsed risk scoring tools. Medical Affairs teams for antiplatelet or lipid-modifying agents targeting hypertensive patients with elevated cardiovascular risk may use these findings to support dual-therapy value propositions combining blood pressure control with platelet and lipid management. Validation of PHR in prospective trials with adjudicated stroke endpoints should be a priority research advocacy topic for KOL engagement, particularly in Asian cardiovascular disease registries.

SARS-CoV-2 infection and two-year longitudinal clinical trajectory in pulmonary hypertension: a single-centre retrospective exploratory cohort study.

Revue : Frontiers in Public Health | Tier : NA | IF estimé : NA

Date de publication : 2026-09-24

Résultats principaux : This single-centre retrospective cohort study analysed 40 pulmonary hypertension (PH) patients from the POTENT registry with documented SARS-CoV-2 infection between June 2020 and November 2022, followed across five time windows up to >2 years post-infection. Median age was 37 years with 75% female; PH aetiologies included CTEPH (28%), CHD-PAH (25%), IPAH (20%), CTD-PAH (18%), and HPAH (10%); at baseline, 52.5% were low risk and 27.5% intermediate-high risk. WHO functional class risk profile shifted toward higher-risk categories at T2 (3–12 months post-infection), with partial recovery observed by T4 (>2 years), suggesting a temporal worsening signal followed by partial stabilization. Composite event-free survival was 87.2% at 1 year (95% CI 71.9–94.5%) and 75.1% at 2 years (95% CI 48.2–89.4%); no baseline covariate was significantly associated with the composite endpoint in Cox analyses. Major limitations include the uncontrolled, single-centre, exploratory design with only 40 patients, absence of a contemporaneous non-infected PH comparator cohort, and insufficient statistical power to detect predictors of outcome.

Implications Medical Affairs : The observed 3–12 month post-COVID worsening signal in PH patients underlines the need for enhanced cardiovascular monitoring protocols following SARS-CoV-2 infection in this high-risk population, supporting Medical Affairs advocacy for guideline-level post-COVID surveillance recommendations. Pharmaceutical companies with pulmonary arterial hypertension products should consider Medical Affairs strategies that position close follow-up and early therapy optimization as key differentiators in the post-COVID context, particularly for CTEPH and PAH subtypes. Larger, controlled prospective studies are essential to confirm causality and inform evidence-based management of COVID-19-related PH deterioration, representing a high-priority research gap for KOL advisory discussions.

Multidimensional characteristics of body composition in older adults and their relationship to common chronic diseases.

Revue : Frontiers in Public Health | Tier : NA | IF estimé : NA

Date de publication : 2026-09-24

Résultats principaux : This cross-sectional study enrolled 324 community-dwelling adults aged ≥65 years from health screenings in Beijing, measuring body composition via InBody 770 bioelectrical impedance analysis, including skeletal muscle mass (SMM), percent body fat (PBF), visceral fat level (VFL), and percent trunk fat (PTF). The prevalence of hypertension, dyslipidemia, and diabetes among participants was 62.3%, 53.7%, and 19.8%, respectively, reflecting the high chronic disease burden in the elderly community. Implications Medical Affairs : The multidimensional body composition analysis confirms that visceral fat accumulation is a key modifiable determinant of hypertension in the elderly, strengthening the rationale for including body composition assessment in cardiovascular risk stratification protocols for older patients. Medical Affairs teams for antihypertensive or weight-management drugs targeting the elderly can use these findings to argue for body composition-guided patient selection in clinical trials, particularly in populations where BMI may underestimate cardiometabolic risk. Comparison of heart rate variability in normotensive and newly detected hypertensive adults in a tertiary care hospital, Mysuru, Karnataka.

Revue : The Indian Journal of Medical Research | Tier : NA | IF estimé : NA

Date de publication : 2026-08

Résultats principaux : This hospital-based case-control study compared heart rate variability (HRV) between 26 newly diagnosed hypertensive and 26 normotensive adults at JSS Medical College, Mysuru, Karnataka, India. Hypertensive participants showed significantly reduced time-domain HRV parameters: lower SDNN (28.16 [21.75–39.50] ms², p = 0.020) and lower RMSSD (16.88 [11.79–25.51] ms², p = 0.003) compared to normotensives. In the frequency domain, hypertensives had significantly lower high-frequency (HF) power (102.25 [53.73–209.75], p < 0.001) and a significantly elevated LF/HF ratio (1.94 [1.60–3.19], p < 0.001), indicating reduced parasympathetic activity and sympathetic dominance. The LF/HF ratio correlated positively with systolic (rs = 0.49) and diastolic blood pressure (rs = 0.50, both p < 0.001), and multinomial logistic regression confirmed that higher systolic blood pressure was associated with increased odds of sympathetic dominance (OR 1.09, 95% CI 1.04–1.15, p < 0.001). Study limitations include the small sample size (52 participants), single-center tertiary-care setting, and absence of longitudinal follow-up precluding evaluation of HRV changes with antihypertensive treatment.

Implications Medical Affairs : These results reinforce the clinical utility of HRV as a non-invasive marker of autonomic dysfunction in newly detected hypertension, supporting its incorporation into routine screening and early treatment response monitoring in primary and secondary care settings. Antihypertensive agents with additional autonomic modulating properties (e.g., beta-blockers, certain ACE inhibitors) may be positioned by Medical Affairs teams using HRV restoration as a secondary benefit, particularly relevant in markets where patient-centric endpoints beyond blood pressure control are gaining guideline traction. Future large-scale trials incorporating HRV as both a stratification tool and outcome measure in hypertension management studies would strengthen the evidence base and represent a high-value research investment for companies developing novel antihypertensive compounds.

Immune Checkpoint Inhibitor-Induced Cardiovascular Disease: The Role of Accelerated Senescence in Vascular Dysfunction.

Revue : Antioxidants & Redox Signaling | Tier : NA | IF estimé : NA

Date de publication : 2026-01-11

Résultats principaux : This comprehensive mechanistic review from MD Anderson Cancer Center and international collaborators explores how immune checkpoint inhibitors (ICIs) may increase long-term cardiovascular disease risk, including hypertension, atherosclerosis, and thrombosis, through immune activation-mediated vascular injury. The review synthesizes evidence that ICI-induced immune activation disrupts vascular homeostasis through persistent reactive oxygen species (ROS) production, mitochondrial dysfunction, and accumulation of cell-free DNA (cfDNA), promoting DNA damage and cellular senescence in endothelial and immune cells. ICIs may also promote endothelial-to-mesenchymal transition (EndoMT) via chronic oxidative and inflammatory signaling, contributing to fibrosis, vascular stiffness, and chronic inflammation mediated by TGF-beta, IL-17, and regulator of calcineurin 1 pathways. The relative contributions of ROS, mitochondrial dysfunction, cfDNA-driven inflammation, and EndoMT to ICI-associated vascular senescence remain unclear, and direct ICI-specific mechanisms require stronger prospective clinical evidence. Future research directions include biomarker evaluation (cfDNA, thrombospondin-1) and senescence-targeting or redox-modulating interventions to mitigate cardiovascular toxicity without diminishing antitumor efficacy.

Implications Medical Affairs : As ICI use expands across oncology indications, the emergence of hypertension and cardiovascular disease as significant long-term toxicities creates an important Medical Affairs opportunity to position cardiovascular co-management programs alongside oncological treatments. Antihypertensive agents with antioxidant or anti-inflammatory mechanisms (e.g., ARBs, ACE inhibitors) may have particular relevance in ICI-treated cancer patients; Medical Affairs teams should engage cardio-oncology KOLs to develop evidence-based co-management guidelines. The proposed biomarkers (cfDNA, thrombospondin-1) represent actionable research targets for pharmaceutical companies developing cardiovascular monitoring tools or companion diagnostics in the growing cardio-oncology space.

Revue : Health Science Reports | Tier : NA | IF estimé : NA

Date de publication : 2026-10-07

Résultats principaux : This cross-sectional study used NHANES data (2011–2018) to examine the association between DASH diet adherence and metabolic syndrome (MetS) in 3,332 U.S. adults aged 18–29 years, representing a weighted population of approximately 37.98 million young adults. The MetS prevalence was 35.6%, with high fasting glucose being the most prevalent component (66.4%), while only 12.5% of participants met DASH nutrient intake targets — a striking gap in dietary adherence. Résultats principaux : This study analyzed plasma proteomics data (SomaScan 7k platform, 7,000 protein aptamers) from 206 Indiana Alzheimer's Disease Research Center (IADRC) participants, using network analysis to identify protein modules associated with MIND diet scores and their relationship to Alzheimer's disease (AD) phenotypes. Implications Medical Affairs : The identification of vascular-related biological pathways as potential mediators between MIND diet adherence and Alzheimer's disease risk reinforces the hypertension-dementia continuum and supports the Medical Affairs narrative that blood pressure control and dietary modification may reduce long-term neurodegenerative risk.
