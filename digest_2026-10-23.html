Postpartum and Lifelong Cardiometabolic Risk after Gestational Diabetes and Hypertensive Disorders of Pregnancy

Revue : Endocrinology and Metabolism Clinics of North America | Tier : NA | IF estime : NA

Date de publication : 2026-09-11

Resultats principaux : This review examined postpartum and lifelong cardiometabolic risk in women affected by gestational diabetes mellitus (GDM) and hypertensive disorders of pregnancy (HDP), conditions affecting approximately 1 in 5 pregnancies. The authors synthesized evidence on shared risk factors including obesity, advanced maternal age, and insulin resistance, which drive the co-occurrence and rising incidence of both conditions. Primary analysis demonstrated that GDM and HDP are independently and jointly associated with elevated long-term risks of type 2 diabetes, chronic renal disease, cardiovascular disease, dyslipidemia, and cerebrovascular accidents. The review highlighted professional organizations including the American Heart Association and the American College of Obstetricians and Gynecologists now classify both conditions as major risk factors for cardiometabolic and cerebrovascular morbidity and mortality. An important additional finding was that these conditions alter the intrauterine environment, increasing offspring risk for cardiometabolic disorders, thereby extending the public health implications across generations.

Implications Medical Affairs : This review reinforces the growing evidence that GDM and HDP serve as sentinel events for subsequent cardiovascular disease, strengthening the rationale for including obstetric history in standard cardiovascular risk stratification tools and clinical guidelines. For Medical Affairs teams, this represents a clear opportunity to position antihypertensive therapies within a women's health continuum-of-care narrative, particularly targeting the postpartum window as a critical intervention point. A targeted KOL engagement strategy should include maternal-fetal medicine specialists, cardiologists, and endocrinologists, along with investment in research initiatives focusing on postpartum blood pressure monitoring programs and risk reduction pathways.

Latent Dimension of Comorbidities and Depression in Older Adults: A Cross-Sectional Study Using National Health Survey Data, Brazil, 2019

Revue : Epidemiologia e Servicos de Saude | Tier : NA | IF estime : NA

Date de publication : 2026-10-12

Resultats principaux : This cross-sectional study analyzed data from Brazil's 2019 National Health Survey (Pesquisa Nacional de Saude) to characterize the latent structure of comorbidities and depression in older adults. The study employed latent dimension analysis to identify co-occurring disease clusters, revealing that hypertension and diabetes co-clustered with depression and stroke across distinct latent dimensions in the older Brazilian population. Sex and age emerged as significant modifiers of comorbidity patterns, with differential profiles observed between men and women and across age subgroups. Income level also shaped the latent dimensions, with lower-income older adults exhibiting higher comorbidity burdens. The study was limited by its cross-sectional design which precluded causal inference, and the self-reported nature of diagnoses may introduce misclassification bias.

Implications Medical Affairs : The identification of hypertension as a core component of multimorbidity clusters in Brazilian older adults underscores the need for integrated, multi-condition management programs rather than disease-siloed approaches, which has direct relevance for formulary positioning of combination antihypertensive therapies. Medical Affairs should leverage these findings to advocate for comprehensive cardiometabolic guidelines that address the hypertension-diabetes-depression triad, particularly in middle-income countries with large aging populations. This study provides epidemiological grounding for partnerships with local health authorities in Brazil to develop adherence-focused programs targeting older adults with cardiovascular-metabolic multimorbidity.

Study of Changing Trends, Risk Factors, and Prediction of Disease Burden of Three Acquired Heart Diseases in China, 1990-2023

Revue : Zhonghua Liu Xing Bing Xue Za Zhi | Tier : NA | IF estime : NA

Date de publication : 2026-10-10

Resultats principaux : This ecological study used Global Burden of Disease (GBD) data to analyze disability-adjusted life year (DALY) trends for rheumatic heart disease, hypertensive heart disease (HHD), and ischemic heart disease (IHD) in China from 1990 to 2023. Using Joinpoint regression, the age-standardized DALY rates (ASDRs) for all three conditions declined significantly, with average annual percentage changes (AAPCs) of -6.6%, -3.7%, and -0.8% respectively (all P<0.001). Bayesian age-period-cohort modeling projected that from 2024 to 2050, ASDRs for HHD will diverge by sex, declining in men but rising in women. The three leading modifiable risk factors for HHD in China in 2023 were high systolic blood pressure, high body mass index, and high sodium intake, while IHD was primarily driven by high SBP, lead exposure, and elevated LDL-C. Limitations include reliance on GBD modeling estimates, which may incorporate uncertainty in underlying data sources.

Implications Medical Affairs : The projection of rising hypertensive heart disease burden in Chinese women through 2050 identifies a major unmet need and creates a compelling product differentiation opportunity for antihypertensive therapies with demonstrated sex-specific cardiovascular benefit. Medical Affairs should prioritize sex-stratified evidence generation in clinical trials and real-world studies to support a targeted positioning in the Chinese women's cardiovascular market. The prominence of high SBP and high BMI as leading modifiable risk factors supports cross-indication strategies linking antihypertensive use to broader cardiometabolic risk reduction in a population where dietary sodium intake remains persistently elevated.

Prevalence and Factors Associated with Hypertension Among Individuals Undergoing Premarital Examination in Guangxi, China: A Large Cross-Sectional Study, 2022-2024

Revue : Frontiers in Public Health | Tier : NA | IF estime : NA

Date de publication : 2026-10-06

Resultats principaux : This large cross-sectional study enrolled 11,283 consecutive premarital examination participants aged 20-50 years (5,641 males, 5,642 females) at a maternal and child health hospital in Guangxi, China between 2022 and 2024. The overall hypertension prevalence was 7.68%, with a significantly higher rate in men (10.28%) compared to women (5.09%), using the standard SBP ≥ 140 or DBP ≥ 90 mmHg or self-reported diagnosis definition. Multivariate logistic regression identified overweight (OR=1.88, 95% CI: 1.27-2.77), obesity (OR=3.90, 95% CI: 2.29-6.64), lower education (OR=1.64, 95% CI: 1.13-2.38), abnormal ALT (OR=1.69, 95% CI: 1.12-2.56), and each annual age increment (OR=1.04, 95% CI: 1.01-1.08) as independent risk factors. In sex-stratified analysis, abnormal fasting blood glucose was independently associated with higher hypertension odds in women only (OR=1.96, 95% CI: 1.21-3.16). Restricted cubic spline analysis confirmed nonlinear dose-response relationships between BMI, ALT, age and hypertension risk.

Implications Medical Affairs : The identification of hypertension in nearly 1 in 10 young adult males attending premarital examinations in China suggests a large population of early-onset hypertensives who may benefit from earlier pharmacological and lifestyle interventions, representing a significant commercial opportunity for first-line antihypertensive products in this demographic. The independent association between hepatic dysfunction (elevated ALT) and hypertension risk warrants further investigation into combination cardiometabolic risk profiles in young adults, potentially supporting inclusion of liver health biomarkers in cardiovascular screening algorithms. Medical Affairs should engage with primary care and public health KOLs in China to advocate for integration of comprehensive cardiovascular screening within existing premarital examination frameworks as a cost-effective early detection strategy.

Integrative and Complementary Practices, Hypertension and Social Indicators: An Ecological Study, Brazil, 2023

Revue : Epidemiologia e Servicos de Saude | Tier : NA | IF estime : NA

Date de publication : 2026-10-05

Resultats principaux : This ecological study conducted in Brazil in 2023 examined the relationship between the provision of integrative and complementary health practices (ICPs) within the public health system (SUS) and hypertension prevalence, analyzed alongside key social indicators. The study used health information system data to correlate municipal-level ICP availability with hypertension rates, finding differential associations based on local GDP and socioeconomic development indicators. Municipalities with higher socioeconomic indices showed greater ICP availability but also higher hypertension prevalence, suggesting confounding by access to healthcare and diagnosis rates. The ecological design precludes individual-level inference and the analysis may be subject to ecological fallacy; unmeasured confounders at the municipal level (e.g., urbanization, dietary patterns) were not fully controlled. The study adds to evidence that non-pharmacological public health interventions are distributed inequitably and may not adequately address cardiovascular risk in lower-income communities.

Implications Medical Affairs : This study highlights persistent socioeconomic inequities in access to both diagnosis and complementary healthcare for hypertension management in Brazil, reinforcing the need for Medical Affairs teams to consider access and affordability frameworks when planning market penetration strategies for antihypertensive portfolios in emerging markets. The findings suggest that pharmacological treatment remains the dominant modality in lower-income Brazilian municipalities, where complementary practices are less accessible, creating a relatively medication-dependent treatment landscape favorable for evidence-based pharmaceutical interventions. Engagement with Brazilian health policy KOLs and the Ministry of Health on integrated disease management programs could position pharmaceutical companies as partners in equitable hypertension care rather than solely commercial actors.

Clinical, Radiological, and Pathological Features of Pulmonary Hypertension Associated with Combined Pulmonary Fibrosis and Emphysema

Revue : Respiratory Research | Tier : NA | IF estime : NA

Date de publication : 2026-07-27

Resultats principaux : This multicenter cohort study characterized 113 patients with combined pulmonary fibrosis and emphysema-associated pulmonary hypertension (CPFE-PH) and compared them with 277 COPD-PH and 78 IIP-PH patients, all diagnosed by right heart catheterization. CPFE-PH patients were predominantly older male smokers with preserved ventilatory function but severely reduced diffusing capacity (DLCO 31.6% predicted, IQR 24.8-37.8), representing the most severe hemodynamic compromise with mean pulmonary arterial pressure of 41 mmHg and pulmonary vascular resistance of 7.4 Wood units. Histopathological analysis revealed universal capillary hemangiomatosis-like remodeling in CPFE-PH, a feature absent in COPD-PH and rare in IIP-PH, suggesting a distinct pathobiological mechanism. PAH-targeted pharmacotherapy failed to produce functional or biomarker improvements in CPFE-PH, and transplant-free survival was shortest at a median of 32 months compared to COPD-PH and IIP-PH (P<0.05). Notable limitations include the retrospective design and potential selection bias inherent in a specialty pulmonary center cohort.

Implications Medical Affairs : The characterization of CPFE-PH as a phenotypically and pathologically distinct form of Group 3 pulmonary hypertension with universal capillary hemangiomatosis-like remodeling challenges current treatment paradigms and identifies a critical unmet need where existing PAH-targeted therapies are ineffective. For Medical Affairs, this data supports investment in mechanistic research targeting the capillary remodeling pathway as a novel therapeutic node, and underscores the importance of precise phenotyping for any future clinical trial design in chronic lung disease-associated PH. KOL education initiatives should emphasize the poor prognosis of CPFE-PH and the inadequacy of current treatment options to create urgency around novel therapeutic development and early referral for transplant evaluation.

Lactylation Modification: A New Bridge Connecting Macrophage Metabolism and Pulmonary Hypertension

Revue : Applied Microbiology and Biotechnology | Tier : NA | IF estime : NA

Date de publication : 2026-07-27

Resultats principaux : This review synthesized emerging evidence on lactylation, a post-translational modification linking lactate to lysine residues, and its role in macrophage-mediated pulmonary vascular remodeling in pulmonary hypertension (PH). The authors described how macrophage polarization imbalance, predominantly toward the pro-fibrotic M2 phenotype, drives vascular remodeling in PH via metabolic reprogramming. Lactylation of histones (particularly H3K18la), non-histone proteins, and mitochondrial proteins was identified as the epigenetic mechanism translating elevated glycolytic activity (and elevated lactate) into sustained pro-fibrotic gene expression. The review presented evidence that lactylation regulates fibrosis-related pathways, arginine metabolism signaling, and pulmonary vascular cell dysfunction. Limitations of this review include the nascent state of the field with most mechanistic evidence derived from preclinical models, and limited human translational data.

Implications Medical Affairs : The identification of lactylation as a metabolic-epigenetic bridge in pulmonary hypertension pathogenesis opens a novel therapeutic axis distinct from currently approved vasodilatory therapies, and may catalyze a new class of epigenetic interventions targeting macrophage reprogramming in PH. Medical Affairs teams tracking pulmonary hypertension pipelines should monitor emerging lactylation-targeted compounds and consider supporting translational biomarker studies measuring H3K18la in PH patients to establish clinical relevance and patient stratification criteria. This mechanism may represent a convergent point between metabolic syndrome, insulin resistance, and pulmonary vascular disease, offering cross-therapeutic area synergies for companies with cardiometabolic portfolios.

Population-Specific Association and Risk Discrimination Utility of the Cardiometabolic Index for Prevalent Hypertension-Diabetes Comorbidity: Insights from CHARLS and NHANES

Revue : Clinical and Experimental Medicine | Tier : NA | IF estime : NA

Date de publication : 2026-07-26

Resultats principaux : This cross-national study utilized CHARLS (12,261 Chinese participants) and NHANES (1,329 American participants) datasets to evaluate the association between the cardiometabolic index (CMI, integrating triglyceride-to-HDL ratio and waist circumference) and prevalent hypertension-diabetes comorbidity. Multivariable logistic regression demonstrated significant independent associations between elevated CMI and comorbidity in both cohorts: OR=2.16 (95% CI: 2.00-2.34) in Chinese and OR=1.99 (95% CI: 1.48-2.69) in Americans. Restricted cubic spline analysis revealed a nonlinear dose-response relationship with a threshold effect in the Chinese cohort (P-nonlinear<0.001) compared to a more linear relationship in Americans (P-nonlinear=0.764). Machine learning models (LightGBM, GBM, CatBoost) showed strong risk discrimination, with LightGBM AUCs of 0.841 (CHARLS) and 0.948 (NHANES). Mediation analysis indicated BMI significantly partially mediated the CMI-comorbidity association, accounting for 32.9% in Chinese and 43.3% in American cohorts.

Implications Medical Affairs : The CMI emerges as a clinically accessible composite biomarker for identifying individuals at high risk for hypertension-diabetes comorbidity across diverse populations, providing a practical tool for cardiovascular risk screening that could be readily incorporated into clinical decision-support systems. Medical Affairs teams should note the population-specific heterogeneity in risk curves (nonlinear threshold in Chinese vs. linear in Americans), which highlights the importance of population-appropriate risk stratification tools and cautions against direct extrapolation of Western-derived algorithms to Asian populations. The strong performance of machine learning-enhanced CMI models supports investment in digital health partnerships and real-world data initiatives to develop AI-powered cardiometabolic risk prediction platforms that integrate with antihypertensive treatment pathways.

Socioeconomic Factors and Out-of-Pocket Healthcare Spending Among Older Adults with Hypertension and Type 2 Diabetes in the US: Findings from the 2018-2022 MEPS Data

Revue : BMC Health Services Research | Tier : NA | IF estime : NA

Date de publication : 2026-07-25

Resultats principaux : This secondary data analysis used pooled 2018-2022 Medical Expenditure Panel Survey (MEPS) data to examine socioeconomic determinants of combined out-of-pocket (OOP) healthcare expenditures in 7,341 US adults aged 50+ with co-occurring hypertension and type 2 diabetes. Mean annual OOP costs were $474.95 combined, with diabetes-specific expenditures ($413.16) substantially exceeding hypertension-specific spending ($84.37). Survey-weighted linear regression on log-transformed OOP costs identified Northeast residence, age 65+, non-Hispanic White ethnicity, married status, higher income, high school or higher education, poor perceived health, and uninsured or private insurance as independent predictors of significantly higher OOP spending. The analysis adjusted for the complex MEPS sampling design with stratification, clustering, and weighting, with expenditures inflation-adjusted to December 2022 US dollars. Limitations include the self-reported nature of disease diagnosis and the cross-sectional pooled design preventing longitudinal trajectory analysis.

Implications Medical Affairs : The markedly lower hypertension-specific OOP costs ($84.37) relative to diabetes-related expenditures in comorbid patients suggests that hypertension medication adherence may be disproportionately supported by existing insurance coverage, yet the wide socioeconomic gradients in total OOP spending signal significant access inequities that pharmaceutical companies must consider in patient support program design. Medical Affairs teams should use these health economics data to substantiate value propositions for combination antihypertensive products that reduce overall treatment complexity and associated total healthcare expenditures, particularly for the high-burden comorbid hypertension-diabetes phenotype. Understanding the sociodemographic drivers of OOP costs enables more targeted development of patient assistance programs and real-world evidence strategies aligned with the needs of underinsured and lower-income patients with cardiometabolic multimorbidity.

Vascular Risk Factors After Stroke in Older People: A Population-Based Study

Revue : BMC Geriatrics | Tier : NA | IF estime : NA

Date de publication : 2026-07-25

Resultats principaux : This observational retrospective longitudinal population-based study analyzed 35,918 patients diagnosed with ischemic stroke or TIA between 2014-2019 in Catalonia, Spain, using health system administrative data, with median age 77 years (IQR 68-88) and 23% aged over 87 years. Blood pressure data were available for 63% of patients within the first year post-stroke; of these, only 49% of adults aged 18-67 and 42% of the very oldest (>87 years) achieved the ESO-recommended target of <130 mmHg. LDL-cholesterol was specifically measured in only 5% of patients, with 31% (younger) and 35% (very old) reaching the <70 mg/dL target, suggesting severely underused secondary prevention for dyslipidemia. Among diabetic patients, HbA1c monitoring was more complete (84%), with adequate control rates of 81% (younger) and 83% (very old). Anticoagulant use among atrial fibrillation patients was suboptimal, particularly in the very old (63% vs. 72-73%), despite increasing AF prevalence with age.

Implications Medical Affairs : The substantial gap in blood pressure target achievement post-stroke (only 42% in very elderly patients reaching <130 mmHg) represents a directly addressable clinical unmet need, supporting the positioning of well-tolerated antihypertensive agents with demonstrated cerebrovascular protection in the geriatric secondary prevention setting. Medical Affairs should engage neurology and geriatrics KOLs to develop structured post-stroke follow-up pathway tools that systematically capture and optimize blood pressure, lipid, and anticoagulation metrics, particularly for the rapidly growing very elderly stroke survivor population. The extreme underutilization of LDL-cholesterol monitoring (measured in only 5% of patients) reveals a significant adherence gap in clinical practice that can be addressed through medical education programs emphasizing integrated cardiovascular risk factor management after cerebrovascular events.

A Consensus Statement on the Role of Bisoprolol across the CV Continuum

Revue : The Journal of the Association of Physicians of India | Tier : NA | IF estime : NA

Date de publication : 2026-09-01

Resultats principaux : This consensus statement from Indian cardiologists synthesized evidence on bisoprolol's role across the cardiovascular continuum in the Indian context, where CVD burden has risen sharply due to increasing rates of coronary heart disease and cerebrovascular events. The consensus reviewed evidence establishing elevated resting heart rate as an independent predictor of adverse cardiovascular outcomes in patients with hypertension, myocardial infarction, and heart failure, with bisoprolol's selective beta-1 blockade directly addressing this modifiable risk factor. Key evidence reviewed supported bisoprolol's efficacy in managing angina, atrial fibrillation, and heart failure with reduced ejection fraction (HFrEF), with the agent demonstrating both symptom relief and disease-modifying effects in these conditions. The experts highlighted bisoprolol's favorable tolerability profile relative to non-selective beta-blockers, a particularly relevant consideration in Indian patients with high rates of metabolic comorbidities. Limitations acknowledged by the consensus include the scarcity of large-scale head-to-head comparative trials of bisoprolol vs. other beta-blockers specifically in Indian populations, and the reliance on data predominantly generated in Western cohorts.

Implications Medical Affairs : This Indian expert consensus provides a high-level endorsement of bisoprolol's multi-indication positioning across the cardiovascular continuum, creating a valuable tool for medical education, formulary advocacy, and KOL alignment in the rapidly growing Indian cardiovascular market. The consensus underscores the importance of heart rate management in hypertensive patients as an independent therapeutic target beyond blood pressure reduction, supporting an expanded messaging strategy for beta-blockers in the hypertension treatment algorithm. Medical Affairs teams should leverage this consensus to develop India-specific clinical practice materials and support investigator-initiated studies that generate real-world evidence on bisoprolol outcomes in Indian patients, thereby building a locally relevant evidence base alongside the existing international data.

High Prevalence and Limited Perinatal Prediction of Kidney Dysfunction in Adolescents Born Extremely Preterm

Revue : Kidney International Reports | Tier : NA | IF estime : NA

Date de publication : 2026-09-17

Resultats principaux : This prospective cohort analysis from the ELGAN-ECHO study (14 US hospitals) assessed kidney health in 675 adolescents with mean age 18 years who had been born at extremely low gestational age (<28 weeks). Abnormal kidney health, defined as any one of: eGFR <90 ml/min per 1.73m2, blood pressure ≥120/80 mmHg, or microalbuminuria >30 mg/g, was found in 66% of complete cases (125/188) and in 82% of all determinate cases (291/354). Individual measurement rates were alarming: 30.5% had reduced eGFR, 42% had elevated blood pressure, and 9% had microalbuminuria, with many adolescents presenting multiple concurrent abnormalities. Despite testing five feature selection machine learning methods using perinatal covariates, predictive performance for abnormal kidney health was poor (test-set AUC 0.48-0.55), indicating that currently available perinatal predictors do not adequately stratify risk at the individual level. Key limitations include high rates of missing outcome data (only 52% had classifiable kidney health status) and the restriction to perinatal predictors, leaving postnatal exposures unstudied.

Implications Medical Affairs : The finding that 42% of adolescents born extremely preterm already have elevated blood pressure establishes this population as a high-priority target for early antihypertensive screening and treatment programs, given the trajectory toward progressive kidney and cardiovascular disease in early adulthood. Medical Affairs teams should monitor this emerging high-risk population as a potential indication expansion opportunity for nephroprotective antihypertensive agents, particularly those with demonstrated efficacy in early CKD such as RAAS inhibitors. The failure of perinatal predictors to identify high-risk individuals supports advocating for universal, protocolized longitudinal kidney health surveillance programs in all extremely preterm survivors, a health system engagement opportunity for pharmaceutical companies with renal-cardiovascular protection portfolios.

Gut Dubosiella newyorkensis Alleviates Noise-Related Atherosclerosis by Reducing N1-methyl-4-pyridone-3-carboxamide

Revue : Pharmacological Research | Tier : NA | IF estime : NA

Date de publication : 2026-10-21

Resultats principaux : This translational study combined UK Biobank analysis with preclinical rodent experiments to investigate the cardiovascular effects of environmental noise exposure and the underlying molecular mechanisms. UK Biobank analyses confirmed that self-reported noise exposure was associated with a significantly increased risk of coronary heart disease. In both human participants and rodents, noise exposure elevated circulating levels of N1-methyl-4-pyridone-3-carboxamide (4PY), a nicotinamide metabolite, which suppressed endothelial NR4A1 expression, activated NF-kB signaling, facilitated monocyte adhesion, and accelerated atherosclerotic plaque progression. Mechanistically, noise exposure reduced gut microbiome abundance of Dubosiella newyorkensis, and supplementation with this bacterial species upregulated hepatic ACMSD enzyme expression, lowering circulating 4PY levels and attenuating atherosclerosis in mice. Study limitations include the observational nature of UK Biobank noise exposure data, potential unmeasured confounding, and the mouse-to-human translational gap in microbiome intervention studies.

Implications Medical Affairs : This study establishes a novel gut microbiota-metabolite (4PY) axis as a mechanistic link between environmental noise exposure and cardiovascular disease, identifying a new modifiable environmental risk factor relevant to urban hypertensive populations who experience chronic noise exposure. From a Medical Affairs perspective, this work opens a potential positioning opportunity for gut microbiome-cardiovascular health intersections, particularly as the field explores novel preventive strategies targeting inflammation-driven atherogenesis in patients with environmentally-modulated cardiovascular risk. Engagement with environmental cardiology KOLs and investment in clinical studies measuring 4PY and gut microbiome composition in hypertensive patients from high-noise-exposure environments could help establish patient stratification criteria for future preventive or therapeutic interventions.
