Young-onset stroke in the UAE: A single-centre retrospective analysis of risk factors and outcomes.

Revue : PloS One | Tier : NA | IF estime : NA

Date de publication : 2026-08-06

Resultats principaux : This retrospective chart review analyzed 419 patients under 50 years presenting with stroke at Al Qassimi Hospital in Sharjah, UAE over a 6-year period (2016-2022). The cohort was predominantly male (78.28%), with South Asian patients representing 63.96% of the population. Ischemic stroke was the most frequent subtype (50.12%), followed by intracerebral hemorrhage (27.21%) and non-traumatic subarachnoid hemorrhage (22.67%). Hypertension was the leading comorbidity at 45.45%, followed by diabetes mellitus at 24.42%, underscoring their central role in young-onset cerebrovascular disease in this region. The overall in-hospital mortality rate was 12.68%, with coronary artery disease independently associated with death (p<0.001), and South Asian patients demonstrated a 2.19-fold higher odds of large-artery atherosclerosis compared to non-South Asians (95% CI: 1.11-4.30; p=0.02). Acute reperfusion therapy was strikingly underutilized, with only 9.41% receiving intravenous thrombolysis and 0.24% mechanical thrombectomy. The retrospective single-center design and reliance on chart data limit generalizability, and no long-term functional outcomes were captured.

Implications Medical Affairs : The high prevalence of hypertension (45.45%) as the dominant modifiable risk factor in young-onset stroke in the UAE strongly supports aggressive antihypertensive intervention strategies in younger Middle Eastern and South Asian populations. From a Medical Affairs perspective, this data reinforces the need to position antihypertensive therapies for stroke prevention in South Asian diaspora communities—a large and growing segment in the Gulf region—where stroke onset occurs at younger ages. This study identifies a clear opportunity for KOL engagement with stroke neurologists and preventive cardiologists in the UAE to promote earlier hypertension screening, treatment initiation, and adherence protocols.

Association of physical activity and sedentary behavior with metabolic syndrome and its components among older adults: a cross-sectional study.

Revue : Anais da Academia Brasileira de Ciencias | Tier : NA | IF estime : NA

Date de publication : 2026-08-03

Resultats principaux : This cross-sectional study enrolled 473 older adults aged 60 years or above residing in the urban municipality of Alcobaca, Brazil, all enrolled in the local Family Health Strategy program. The prevalence of metabolic syndrome (MS) in this population was high at 52.8%, with prolonged sedentary behavior (SB) independently associated with central obesity (OR 2.85; 95% CI: 1.52-5.25) and hypertension (OR 1.86; 95% CI: 1.23-2.85). In combined analyses, insufficiently active older adults with longer sedentary time had substantially higher odds of accumulating two (OR 3.90; 95% CI: 1.46-10.42) to four MS components (OR 4.18; 95% CI: 1.12-15.62). Notably, even physically active older adults who engaged in prolonged sedentary behavior faced increased odds of central obesity (OR 3.44; 95% CI: 1.51-7.87), hypertension (OR 2.30; 95% CI: 1.30-4.05), and low HDL-c levels (OR 1.84; 95% CI: 1.03-3.28), indicating that physical activity alone does not fully mitigate the adverse cardiometabolic effects of prolonged sitting. Cross-sectional design precludes causal inference, and self-reported behavioral data may be subject to recall bias.

Implications Medical Affairs : The finding that prolonged sedentary behavior independently elevates hypertension risk even among physically active older adults has significant implications for complementary strategies to antihypertensive pharmacotherapy, reinforcing that behavioral counseling must target total sedentary time—not just physical activity levels. For Medical Affairs, this supports positioning antihypertensive treatment as part of a comprehensive cardiometabolic risk reduction strategy in elderly patients who may be physically active but sedentary for extended periods throughout the day. KOL partnerships with geriatric medicine and public health specialists in Brazil could facilitate development of sedentary behavior interruption protocols that complement pharmacological hypertension management in primary care settings.

Prevalence, associated factors, and population attributable fractions of Diabetes Mellitus in adults from Brazilian capitals.

Revue : Ciencia & Saude Coletiva | Tier : NA | IF estime : NA

Date de publication : 2026-08-03

Resultats principaux : This nationally representative cross-sectional study utilized 2019 Brazilian National Health Survey data from 32,111 adults across state capitals, applying Poisson regression to identify factors associated with diabetes mellitus (DM) prevalence and calculate population attributable fractions (PAFs). DM prevalence was 7.7% overall (7.2% in men vs. 8.0% in women), with Poisson modeling revealing significant associations between DM and self-reported hypertension, age above 60 years, low education, physical inactivity, hypercholesterolemia, overweight, and obesity. In women, mixed-race or Black race/skin color and leisure-time physical inactivity were additional risk factors. The PAFs with greatest contribution to prevalent DM cases were age over 60 years, self-reported hypertension, hypercholesterolemia, and obesity, establishing these as priority targets for prevention policy. The cross-sectional design and reliance on self-reported diagnoses—including hypertension and DM—represent important methodological limitations that may introduce differential misclassification bias.

Implications Medical Affairs : The recognition of hypertension as one of the highest population-attributable fraction contributors to prevalent diabetes in Brazil underscores the value of integrated cardiometabolic risk management programs that address both conditions simultaneously. Medical Affairs teams can leverage these PAF data to support product positioning of antihypertensive agents within cardiometabolic risk reduction programs targeting the Brazilian market, particularly in primary care and endocrinology settings. Engaging KOLs in endocrinology and preventive medicine in Brazil with these population-level data could strengthen advocacy for hypertension control as a diabetes prevention pillar in national public health guidelines.

Spatial analysis of the association between convenience store density and the prevalence of obesity and hypertension in South Korea.

Revue : Frontiers in Public Health | Tier : NA | IF estime : NA

Date de publication : 2026-07-22

Resultats principaux : This ecological study used district-level data from 250 South Korean administrative units to examine the relationship between convenience store density and chronic disease prevalence, drawing from the 2022 Korea Community Health Survey and 2022 Census on Establishments. Spatial autocorrelation was assessed using Moran's I and LISA statistics, while both ordinary least squares (OLS) and spatial error model (SEM) regressions were applied to account for geographic clustering. While the OLS model identified associations between convenience store density and both obesity and hypertension, only the association with hypertension remained statistically significant after adjusting for spatial autocorrelation in the SEM. Specifically, each additional convenience store per 1,000 population was associated with a 0.968% increase in district-level hypertension prevalence, suggesting that access to energy-dense, high-sodium processed foods in the food environment contributes meaningfully to population-level blood pressure burden. The ecological design is an important limitation, as individual-level confounders including dietary patterns, sodium intake, and socioeconomic status cannot be adequately controlled, and generalizability beyond the South Korean urban context is uncertain.

Implications Medical Affairs : This spatial epidemiology study adds ecological evidence to the food environment-hypertension nexus in a rapidly urbanizing Asian country, establishing that convenience store density—a proxy for high-sodium processed food accessibility—may independently contribute to hypertension burden at the population level. Medical Affairs stakeholders can use this evidence to engage public health authorities in Asia-Pacific markets on food environment policy as a structural driver of hypertension prevalence and an important context for pharmacological intervention. KOL engagement with preventive cardiologists and nutrition epidemiologists in South Korea and neighboring countries may open pathways for collaborative population studies on combined dietary intervention and pharmacological blood pressure management strategies.

Prevalence of disease in 549 older cats undergoing health screening at two United Kingdom-based veterinary clinics.

Revue : Journal of Veterinary Internal Medicine | Tier : NA | IF estime : NA

Date de publication : 2026-07-01

Resultats principaux : This retrospective analysis evaluated 549 cats aged 8 years or older screened at two UK primary care veterinary clinics during a 52-month period, providing a comprehensive picture of age-related disease prevalence in the geriatric feline population. Health screening included history, physical examination, plasma biochemistry, blood pressure measurement by Doppler method (performed in 97.1% of cases), and urinalysis. Hyperthyroidism was diagnosed in 19.2% (95% CI: 15.9-23.0%) of tested cats, azotemic chronic kidney disease in 11.4% (95% CI: 9.0-14.4%), and hypertension in 7.1% (95% CI: 5.2-9.6%), demonstrating the co-occurrence of cardiorenal and endocrine conditions in aging cats. Secondary findings included severe dental disease (6.1%), bacteriuria (9.7%), suspected neoplasia and diabetes mellitus (8 cats each). The study is limited by its retrospective single-institution design restricted to two UK clinics, reducing geographic generalizability, and hypertension thresholds in cats differ substantially from those applied in human medicine. This is a veterinary study with limited direct translational relevance to human hypertension management.

Implications Medical Affairs : This veterinary epidemiology study has limited direct applicability to human antihypertensive medicine, but highlights parallels in disease prevalence clustering—hypertension, CKD, and endocrine disorders—occurring together in geriatric subjects, a pattern also well recognized in aging human patients with cardiorenal syndrome. Medical Affairs teams may note comparative medicine data as background context for the systemic cardiorenal burden associated with aging, though this study should not be actioned for product positioning or human clinical practice communications. Given its veterinary focus, this publication warrants monitoring only for researchers interested in translational or one-health cardiovascular research frameworks.

A polymorphism in the DOCK7 gene (rs1167998) is associated with salt restriction behavior in hypertensive patients.

Revue : Nagoya Journal of Medical Science | Tier : NA | IF estime : NA

Date de publication : 2026-05

Resultats principaux : This genetic epidemiology study enrolled 817 Japanese patients with hypertension and analyzed the associations between 21 pre-selected single-nucleotide polymorphisms (SNPs) related to blood pressure regulation, salt metabolism, or neurotransmission and self-reported salt restriction behavior. Of the cohort, 69.5% (n=568) reported adhering to dietary salt restriction. Two SNPs were independently associated with salt restriction behavior: rs1167998 in the DOCK7 gene (OR per allele 1.48; 95% CI: 1.11-1.95) and rs2970847, with rs1167998 demonstrating the strongest association. The DOCK7 gene encodes a guanine nucleotide exchange factor involved in axon formation and neuronal polarization, and this SNP has been previously associated with blood lipid levels and cardiovascular disease risk. As a secondary finding, approximately 30% of patients did not restrict salt despite medical recommendation, a clinically important non-adherence rate with dietary implications. The study is limited by its self-report measure for salt restriction, cross-sectional design, and enrollment of a homogeneous Japanese cohort, requiring replication in ethnically diverse populations before broader conclusions can be drawn.

Implications Medical Affairs : The identification of a DOCK7 genetic variant associated with dietary salt adherence behavior may have important implications for precision medicine approaches to hypertension management, particularly in identifying patients who are genetically predisposed to poor dietary compliance and therefore require more intensive pharmacological intervention. For Medical Affairs, these findings can support the therapeutic narrative that pharmacological antihypertensive therapy remains essential even in patients reporting lifestyle modifications, reinforcing the rationale for combination antihypertensive regimens in genetically non-adherent individuals. KOL engagement with hypertension geneticists and pharmacogenomics researchers in Japan could help evaluate whether DOCK7 variant testing has clinical utility as a biomarker to guide individualized antihypertensive treatment intensification.

Pharmacologic Options for Treatment and Prevention of Hypertensive Disorders of Pregnancy.

Revue : Clinics in Perinatology | Tier : NA | IF estime : NA

Date de publication : 2026-06-26

Resultats principaux : This narrative review from Clinics in Perinatology comprehensively examines the pharmacological management of hypertensive disorders of pregnancy (HDP), which affect approximately 15% of US pregnancies and constitute a major contributor to maternal and neonatal morbidity and mortality. The review describes the profound hemodynamic adaptations of normal pregnancy—including decreased systemic vascular resistance and increased cardiac output—and explains how failure of these adaptations leads to preeclampsia and gestational hypertension, diagnosed at blood pressure thresholds of 140/90 mm Hg or above. Labetalol and nifedipine are firmly established as first-line antihypertensive treatments based on their safety profiles and established clinical efficacy in pregnancy, while angiotensin-converting enzyme inhibitors and angiotensin II receptor blockers are strictly contraindicated due to documented fetotoxicity, including renal dysgenesis and oligohydramnios. Low-dose aspirin is recommended as prophylaxis in high-risk patients to reduce preeclampsia incidence. The review emphasizes that early recognition and evidence-based pharmacologic intervention are critical for improving obstetric outcomes across the spectrum of hypertensive disorders of pregnancy. As a narrative review without systematic search methods or meta-analysis, the study is limited by potential literature selection bias and does not provide quantitative comparative efficacy estimates between agents.

Implications Medical Affairs : This review reinforces the categorical contraindication of ACE inhibitors and ARBs in pregnancy as a critical safety message with direct implications for prescribers of antihypertensive agents in women of childbearing age, requiring active communication through product labeling, educational programs, and HCP communications. Medical Affairs teams should prioritize ensuring that educational materials and prescriber communications clearly delineate pregnancy-related contraindications for RAAS blockers and highlight the preferred alternatives—labetalol and nifedipine—particularly in markets where gestational hypertension is a growing therapeutic area. Engaging KOLs in maternal-fetal medicine and obstetric pharmacology to support evidence-based treatment algorithms for HDP represents a strategic opportunity to position safe antihypertensive options in the peripartum setting and to promote appropriate therapy selection across different stages of the reproductive lifespan.

Relationship Between Urinary Tract Stones and Incident Hypertension Among Postmenopausal Women: Results from The Women's Health Initiative.

Revue : Annals of Epidemiology | Tier : NA | IF estime : NA

Date de publication : 2026-08-06

Resultats principaux : This prospective analysis of the Women's Health Initiative (WHI) studied 100,562 postmenopausal women aged 50-79 years without baseline hypertension to evaluate the association between self-reported urinary tract stones (UTS) and incident hypertension using Cox proportional hazards models with UTS modeled as a time-varying exposure. In unadjusted models, UTS were associated with a 29% increased risk of incident hypertension (HR=1.29; 95% CI: 1.11-1.45; p=0.001), which attenuated to a 16% increase after full adjustment for metabolic, lifestyle, dietary, and socioeconomic confounders (HR=1.16; 95% CI: 1.02-1.35; p=0.05). Physical activity significantly modified the UTS-hypertension association (interaction p=0.03), while dietary inflammatory index (DII/E-DII) did not (interaction p>0.20). Recurrent UTS conferred a modest but statistically significant increase in hypertension risk compared to no stone history (HR=1.14; 95% CI: 1.02-1.28; p=0.03), but not when compared to a single episode of UTS. Key limitations include reliance on self-reported UTS diagnosis without radiological confirmation, potential residual confounding in the observational design, and restriction of the cohort to postmenopausal women limiting generalizability to other sex and age groups.

Implications Medical Affairs : The documented association between urinary tract stones and incident hypertension in one of the largest prospective cohorts of postmenopausal women adds to an emerging body of evidence linking urolithiasis to cardiometabolic risk, likely mediated through shared mechanisms including hyperuricemia, insulin resistance, and renal inflammation. From a Medical Affairs perspective, this finding highlights the clinical rationale for proactive blood pressure monitoring and earlier antihypertensive intervention in women with a history of nephrolithiasis, identifying a potentially underrecognized high-risk subgroup for targeted cardiovascular prevention. KOL engagement with nephrologists and women's health cardiologists could facilitate integration of urolithiasis history into hypertension risk stratification tools and support development of targeted hypertension screening protocols in nephrology outpatient settings.

Early Mycotoxins Exposure: A Hidden Driver Of Cardiometabolic Risk.

Revue : Toxicon | Tier : NA | IF estime : NA

Date de publication : 2026-08-06

Resultats principaux : This comprehensive narrative review synthesizes experimental and epidemiological evidence on the role of developmental mycotoxin exposure—specifically aflatoxins, ochratoxins, fumonisins, zearalenone, and deoxynivalenol—as a programming driver of long-term cardiometabolic risk including hypertension, obesity, insulin resistance, and cardiovascular disease. Exposure occurs prenatally via placental transfer and postnatally through breast milk, infant formula, and contaminated complementary foods, with fetuses and young children particularly vulnerable due to immature detoxification pathways and rapidly developing physiological systems. The proposed mechanisms linking early mycotoxin exposure to cardiometabolic disease encompass impaired endocrine signaling, oxidative stress, chronic systemic inflammation, altered lipid and glucose metabolism, gut microbiome dysbiosis, vascular injury, and epigenetic modifications—an interconnected web contributing to metabolic dysregulation and long-term susceptibility to hypertension. These mechanisms are supported by experimental animal studies and a growing body of epidemiological data from high-exposure regions including sub-Saharan Africa and Southeast Asia. As a narrative review, the evidence base is limited by potential publication bias and the majority of mechanistic data derives from experimental animal models, requiring more robust prospective human epidemiological data to confirm causal relationships with adult hypertension outcomes.

Implications Medical Affairs : Mycotoxins represent an underappreciated environmental risk factor for hypertension and cardiovascular disease that is particularly relevant in emerging market contexts characterized by high dietary mycotoxin burden, such as sub-Saharan Africa, South Asia, and Southeast Asia—regions where hypertension prevalence is rising rapidly. Medical Affairs teams targeting emerging markets can leverage this evidence to advocate for early-life environmental interventions as upstream contributors to adult hypertension burden, contextualizing the increasing demand for antihypertensive pharmacotherapy in these regions. KOL engagement with environmental toxicologists, nutritional epidemiologists, and pediatric cardiologists could support the development of integrated exposure-reduction and cardiovascular prevention frameworks that position antihypertensive treatment within a broader One Health approach to cardiometabolic disease.

Diet and Atherosclerosis Prevention: Rethinking the Significance of "The Japan Diet".

Revue : Journal of Atherosclerosis and Thrombosis | Tier : NA | IF estime : NA

Date de publication : 2026-08-06

Resultats principaux : This narrative review examines the evidence base for "The Japan Diet" as a comprehensive strategy for atherosclerotic cardiovascular disease prevention, covering multiple cardiometabolic risk factors including visceral obesity, dyslipidemia, metabolic dysfunction-associated steatotic liver disease, hypertension, diabetes, and chronic kidney disease. The Japan Atherosclerosis Society (JAS) recommends the Japan Diet pattern—characterized by high intake of fish, soy products, vegetables, citrus fruits, low-fat dairy, seaweed, mushrooms, and unrefined grains, combined with strict salt restriction and reduced consumption of animal fat, red meat, confectioneries, and alcohol. The review highlights that Japanese individuals carry higher frequencies of candidate gene polymorphisms associated with salt-sensitive hypertension compared to Caucasians, making dietary sodium reduction a particularly high-priority intervention in this population. Preliminary nutrition education interventions based on the Japan Diet improved serum lipid and metabolic parameters in patients with dyslipidemia in observational settings. Limitations include the predominance of narrative rather than meta-analytic evidence synthesis, limited randomized clinical data specifically on the Japan Diet's effect on hard cardiovascular endpoints, and dietary behavior trends showing younger Japanese generations increasingly adopting Western-style diets with higher sodium and saturated fat content.

Implications Medical Affairs : The emphasis on strict salt reduction within the Japan Diet framework, particularly pertinent given Japan's documented genetic susceptibility to salt-sensitive hypertension, reinforces the complementary role of structured dietary guidance alongside antihypertensive pharmacotherapy and supports combination lifestyle-pharmacological approaches for blood pressure control in Japanese and Asian populations. Medical Affairs teams in Japan and the Asia-Pacific region can leverage these dietary framework data to support lifestyle modification components within hypertension treatment guidelines and position antihypertensive agents as part of comprehensive CVD prevention packages endorsed by national cardiology societies. Collaboration with the Japan Atherosclerosis Society and KOLs in preventive cardiology could facilitate educational initiatives combining the Japan Diet recommendations with evidence-based antihypertensive prescribing practices to maximize cardiovascular risk reduction.

Liquiritigenin attenuates high-salt diet-induced neuroinflammation, mitochondrial dysfunction, and cognitive impairment via NF-kappaB pathway inhibition.

Revue : Toxicology and Applied Pharmacology | Tier : NA | IF estime : NA

Date de publication : 2026-08-05

Resultats principaux : This experimental pharmacology study investigated the neuroprotective potential of liquiritigenin (LG), a flavonoid derived from licorice root, in a mouse model of high-salt diet (HSD)-induced neurological injury and in NaCl-treated HT22 hippocampal neuronal cells. Multiple LG dosing experiments demonstrated that LG significantly attenuated hippocampal neuronal atrophy and death, resulting in preserved brain tissue morphology on histological assessment. Behavioral testing revealed that LG improved motor performance and exploratory behavior, mitigating HSD-induced cognitive deficits across multiple validated behavioral assays. At the molecular level, LG reduced pro-inflammatory mediators and upregulated anti-inflammatory cytokines, effectively suppressing neuroinflammation; it also decreased cytoplasmic expression of mitochondrial DNA-related genes (d-loop, cox1, non-numt), increased ATP production, and improved overall mitochondrial function. Mechanistic studies confirmed that LG inhibited NF-kappaB p65 phosphorylation, and overexpression of NF-kappaB p65 completely abrogated LG's protective effects, establishing NF-kappaB signaling as the key mechanistic pathway. Major limitations include the purely preclinical experimental design with no human translational data, the use of an artificial HSD model rather than naturally occurring hypertensive neurological injury, and the experimental nature of LG as an agent not yet validated in clinical settings.

Implications Medical Affairs : While this preclinical study provides mechanistic insights into the neurological sequelae of high dietary salt intake and identifies a potential phytochemical intervention, its direct relevance to current clinical antihypertensive management remains limited given the early-stage preclinical design without pharmacokinetic or safety data in humans. Medical Affairs teams monitoring the research landscape for combination cardiometabolic and neuroprotective strategies may note the emerging interest in NF-kappaB pathway modulation and dietary bioactive compounds as adjuncts to blood pressure management in high-salt exposure populations, particularly in regions with high-sodium dietary patterns. Future development of LG-based compounds or NF-kappaB-targeting agents for HSD-related cardiometabolic and neurological comorbidities in hypertensive patients warrants continued monitoring as a potential early-stage pipeline opportunity in the hypertension-CNS comorbidity space.

Individual and Cumulative Stress and Cardiovascular Health Among Contemporary U.S. Service Members: The Millennium Cohort Study.

Revue : Military Medicine | Tier : NA | IF estime : NA

Date de publication : 2026-08-01

Resultats principaux : This cross-sectional study analyzed data from 40,525 contemporary US military service members enrolled in the Millennium Cohort Study (2020-2021) to evaluate relationships between psychosocial stress exposures and cardiovascular health (CVH), adapted from the American Heart Association's Life's Essential 8 composite metric which includes hypertension status as one of eight components. The study population was predominantly male (71%), non-Hispanic White (58%), and young (94% under 35 years of age). Stressors—including combat severity, bullying, hazing, discrimination, sexual harassment, adverse childhood experiences (ACEs), and stressful life events (SLEs) such as divorce and financial difficulties—were individually z-score transformed and combined into a cumulative stress score. Linear regression analyses adjusted for sociodemographic, military, and behavioral factors showed that higher cumulative stress scores were significantly associated with lower overall CVH scores. Among individual stressors, SLEs demonstrated the strongest inverse association with CVH, followed by ACEs, bullying, and combat severity. Cross-sectional design precludes causal inference, and the military population may not be representative of broader civilian populations due to selection effects and occupational health standards.

Implications Medical Affairs : The identification of psychosocial stress—particularly non-combat life stressors such as financial difficulties, divorce, and residential moves—as a significant determinant of cardiovascular health including hypertension in a large military cohort underscores the importance of holistic CVD risk assessment that systematically incorporates psychological and social domain screening. Medical Affairs teams can leverage this evidence to support positioning antihypertensive therapies within broader cardiovascular prevention programs targeting high-stress occupational groups including military personnel, emergency service workers, and healthcare professionals. KOL engagement with occupational cardiologists and military medicine specialists could facilitate research into combined pharmacological and psychosocial intervention strategies, and this data set provides a compelling rationale for advocating stress-integrated CVD risk screening tools in occupational medicine guidelines.

Ethnic Differences in Cardiometabolic Risk Factors for Premature Coronary Artery Disease in Iranian Women: Results From the Iran Premature Coronary Artery Disease (IPAD) Study.

Revue : Cureus | Tier : NA | IF estime : NA

Date de publication : 2026-07-06

Resultats principaux : The IPAD multicenter case-control study analyzed 1,609 Iranian women aged 70 years or younger—789 premature CAD (PCAD) cases confirmed by coronary angiography and 820 controls with normal coronary arteries—across eight ethnic groups from 15 cities, providing a uniquely diverse examination of cardiometabolic risk stratification in Middle Eastern women. PCAD cases were consistently older than controls across all ethnicities, with common characteristics including low educational attainment, predominantly low socioeconomic status, very low smoking prevalence, and higher adiposity measures. Across ethnic groups, hypertension and diabetes were the most prevalent risk factors among PCAD cases, while low HDL-C was highly prevalent in both cases and controls regardless of ethnicity. Ethnicity-stratified analyses revealed distinct profiles: hypertension, diabetes, low HDL-C, and family history dominated among Fars women; hypertension and diabetes were primary risk factors in Arab women; hypertriglyceridemia was the leading lipid risk factor in Gilak women; and family history of CVD was most prominent among Kurd and Bakhtiari women. These findings demonstrate that applying a uniform cardiovascular risk factor paradigm to heterogeneous Iranian female populations would miss critical ethnicity-specific drivers. Limitations include the case-control design with potential selection bias, and extending the PCAD age cutoff to 70 years (rather than the conventional 65) may have diluted the premature CAD signal and introduced some heterogeneity in the case definition.

Implications Medical Affairs : The prominent role of hypertension as a leading risk factor for premature CAD across the majority of Iranian ethnic groups in women—a traditionally underdiagnosed and undertreated population—reinforces the critical importance of aggressive antihypertensive management as a primary cardiovascular prevention strategy in the Middle Eastern and Central Asian female population. Medical Affairs teams targeting Iran, the broader MENA region, and diaspora populations should incorporate this ethnicity-stratified risk landscape into market access strategies, supporting tailored hypertension treatment guideline advocacy and patient awareness campaigns developed in partnership with Iranian and regional cardiology societies. The multicentric, angiography-confirmed design provides robust clinical evidence to support KOL engagement with MENA-based cardiologists and preventive medicine specialists to co-develop ethnicity-informed CVD prevention frameworks that systematically address hypertension as the single most impactful modifiable risk factor across diverse Iranian ethnic groups.
