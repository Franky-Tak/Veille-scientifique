Assessment and decomposition of excessive red meat consumption among hypertensive adults: A Wagstaff decomposition analysis.

Revue : PLoS One | Tier : NA | IF estime : NA

Date de publication : 2026-10-05

Resultats principaux : This cross-sectional study utilized data from the 2021 Iranian STEPS survey, a nationally representative sample of 9,024 hypertensive adults with a mean age of 56.1 years, to quantify socioeconomic inequality in excessive red meat consumption (defined as four or more servings per week). The primary endpoint was the Concentration Index (CI) measuring wealth-related inequality, which was 0.179 (p < 0.001), indicating that excessive red meat consumption was significantly concentrated among wealthier individuals. Wagstaff decomposition analysis identified higher educational attainment (greater than or equal to 12 years of schooling) as the largest contributor to observed inequality at 20.6%, followed by unpaid work status (6.8%), unemployment (4.4%), and membership in the 40-59 age group (4.2%), with all measured determinants collectively explaining 29.4% of the inequality. No significant subgroup analyses by sex or urban-rural strata were reported in the abstract. Key limitations include the cross-sectional study design preventing causal inference, reliance on self-reported dietary data prone to recall bias, and the unexplained 70.6% of inequality suggesting important unmeasured socioeconomic and cultural determinants.

Implications Medical Affairs : These findings demonstrate that hypertension dietary management programs must be differentiated by socioeconomic status to effectively reach all patient segments, as wealthier and more educated patients disproportionately consume excess red meat, a major modifiable cardiovascular risk factor. For Medical Affairs teams with antihypertensive portfolios in middle-income countries, these data highlight that dietary inequality may confound treatment outcome comparisons across socioeconomic groups, necessitating stratified real-world evidence designs. KOL engagement should be broadened to include nutritional epidemiologists and public health experts in Iran and comparable MENA-region countries to co-develop evidence-based hypertension management guidelines incorporating dietary equity considerations.

Childhood Housing Assistance Associated With Lower Rates Of Adult Diabetes And Hypertension, 1970-2021.

Revue : Health Affairs (Project Hope) | Tier : NA | IF estime : NA

Date de publication : 2026-10

Resultats principaux : This retrospective matched cohort study leveraged 52 years of longitudinal data from the Panel Study of Income Dynamics (1970-2021) to evaluate the long-term association between receipt of housing assistance during childhood and adult incidence of obesity, diabetes, and hypertension in the United States. Children in families receiving housing assistance were propensity-score matched to comparable non-recipient families and followed from preconception to middle adulthood, with the primary endpoints being adult rates of diabetes and hypertension. Compared to non-recipients, receiving five or more years of childhood housing assistance was associated with a 69% lower risk for adult diabetes and a 39% lower risk for adult hypertension, representing clinically and public health-relevant risk reductions. The association was strongest when housing assistance was first received during the preconception-to-infancy window, which conferred a 67% lower risk for diabetes and a 41% lower risk for hypertension in adulthood, suggesting a critical developmental window for social determinant effects. Limitations include the observational matched design with potential residual confounding, long follow-up with inevitable attrition and changes in housing program characteristics over five decades, and the possibility that housing assistance acts as a marker for a broader package of social supports rather than an isolated intervention.

Implications Medical Affairs : This landmark 52-year study provides robust evidence that housing stability in early childhood acts as a powerful upstream social determinant reducing adult hypertension risk by nearly 40%, underscoring that antihypertensive pharmaceutical interventions address only part of the disease burden and must be positioned within a broader social determinants framework. For Medical Affairs, these data support engagement with health policy stakeholders, payers, and integrated care organizations to advocate for multi-pronged prevention strategies, positioning antihypertensive therapies as a crucial component of late-stage disease management when upstream interventions have not been applied. KOL strategy should be expanded to include social determinants of health researchers, pediatric health policy advocates, and housing policy economists who can champion linkages between childhood social policy and long-term cardiovascular outcomes in national guidelines and payer discussions.

Theme 5 : Diuretics and Beta-blockers (1 article)

Esaxerenone attenuates renal injury by inhibiting NR3C2-mediated inflammatory and fibrotic pathways.

Revue : Renal Failure | Tier : NA | IF estime : NA

Date de publication : 2026-10-04

Resultats principaux : This preclinical study employed a rat model of high-salt diet combined with continuous aldosterone infusion to evaluate the nephroprotective effects of esaxerenone, a non-steroidal mineralocorticoid receptor antagonist (MRA) indicated for hypertension. The primary endpoints were renal histopathology and molecular markers of inflammation and fibrosis, assessed via immunohistochemistry and Western blotting. Rats receiving high-salt diet plus aldosterone showed significant upregulation of KIM-1, TLR4, p-NF-kappaB, F4/80, IL-6, TNF-alpha, and interferon-gamma (all p < 0.05), confirming robust renal inflammatory activation, as well as elevation of TGF-beta, p-Smad2, p-Smad3, alpha-SMA, vimentin, and collagen I (all p < 0.05), confirming progressive renal fibrosis. Esaxerenone administration significantly attenuated all inflammatory and fibrotic markers and reduced renal pathological injury scores compared to untreated model animals (p < 0.05). No subgroup analyses were reported; the mechanistic analysis points specifically to TLR4/NF-kappaB (inflammatory) and TGF-beta/Smad (fibrotic) pathway inhibition as mediators of esaxerenone's reno-protective effect. Limitations include the exclusively preclinical design using male Sprague-Dawley rats, absence of dose-response data, lack of direct comparison with other MR antagonists such as spironolactone or finerenone, and uncertain clinical translatability.

Implications Medical Affairs : This preclinical mechanistic evidence significantly strengthens the scientific narrative for esaxerenone as a renoprotective agent beyond its current antihypertensive indication, providing a molecular basis for potential label expansion in hypertensive patients with concurrent chronic kidney disease or aldosterone-driven renal inflammation and fibrosis. From a Medical Affairs standpoint, these data can support investigator-initiated clinical study (IIS) grants targeting renal outcomes in esaxerenone-treated hypertensive patients, particularly in populations with high-salt diets and hyperaldosteronism, to generate clinical proof-of-concept for renoprotection. KOL engagement should prioritize nephrology and hypertension specialists to build awareness of the dual antihypertensive-nephroprotective mechanistic profile and to position esaxerenone as part of a cardiorenal protective strategy distinct from older steroidal MR antagonists.

Theme 6 : Clinical Trials and Guidelines (1 article)

Acute effects of volume-matched isometric exercise intensity on vascular function and autonomic regulation in men with elevated blood pressure.

Revue : Clinical Physiology and Functional Imaging | Tier : NA | IF estime : NA

Date de publication : 2026-11

Resultats principaux : This randomized crossover trial (three conditions, n=17 men with prehypertension or stage I hypertension) compared the acute cardiovascular effects of volume-matched high-intensity isometric exercise (HIIE: 60% maximum voluntary contraction) versus low-intensity isometric exercise (LIIE: 30% MVC) and a non-exercise control, measuring blood pressure, heart rate variability (HRV), brachial-ankle pulse wave velocity (baPWV), and flow-mediated dilation (FMD). Neither HIIE nor LIIE produced a statistically significant reduction in blood pressure during recovery, representing the primary null result. HIIE induced significantly greater acute autonomic disturbance compared to LIIE, characterized by elevated heart rate and reduced HRV indices during recovery (p < 0.05), reflecting transient sympathetic predominance that may limit acute applicability in high-risk patients. Right baPWV was significantly reduced following HIIE (p < 0.05) indicating improved acute arterial stiffness, and FMD increased after both exercise conditions (p < 0.001) regardless of intensity, suggesting endothelial benefits at both intensity levels. Limitations include the small sample of 17 males only, single-session acute design that does not reflect chronic training adaptations, absence of subgroup analysis by hypertension stage, and lack of female representation limiting generalizability.

Implications Medical Affairs : The absence of acute blood pressure reduction following single-session isometric exercise, even at high intensity, reinforces that non-pharmacological exercise interventions cannot substitute for antihypertensive drug therapy in stage I hypertension, supporting continued pharmacological management as the cornerstone of treatment. The observed improvements in vascular endothelial function (FMD) and arterial stiffness (baPWV) from isometric exercise provide evidence-based support for recommending structured exercise programs as adjunct therapy alongside antihypertensive medications, which can be incorporated into patient education materials and digital companion programs developed by Medical Affairs. Collaboration with sports medicine and exercise physiology KOLs offers an opportunity to develop a differentiated hypertension management narrative that positions pharmacotherapy within a comprehensive lifestyle-plus-medication framework, enhancing adherence and long-term cardiovascular risk reduction.

Psychosocial, Mental, and Suboptimal Health Measures Are Not Affected by Provision of Abdominal Aortic Calcification Imaging Results: A 12-Week RCT.

Revue : AJPM Focus | Tier : NA | IF estime : NA

Date de publication : 2026-05-09 (indexe 2026-10-05)

Resultats principaux : This secondary analysis of a 12-week single-blind RCT enrolled 240 community-dwelling older Australian adults (mean age 67.8 years, 57.5% female) randomized to receive abdominal aortic calcification (AAC) imaging results plus cardiovascular disease risk-reducing education (n=121) or education alone (n=119). Approximately 57% of participants had baseline AAC evidence (score greater than or equal to 1 on a 24-point scale), and the primary outcome of interest was change in psychosocial distress, mental health score, and suboptimal health status assessed by validated questionnaires. After 12 weeks, the provision of AAC imaging results produced no statistically significant differences in any psychosocial or mental health measure compared to education alone (all p > 0.05), with mean net differences for depression, anxiety, and stress of -0.50 (-1.42, 0.42), -0.20 (-0.81, 0.41), and -0.21 (-1.18, 0.76), respectively. The mean net difference for the mental health component score was 1.69 (-1.29, 4.67) and for total suboptimal health status was -0.84 (-2.08, 0.39), both non-significant. The high study completion rate of 95% (227/240 participants) supports internal validity, though the single-blind design, older predominantly White Australian cohort, and 12-week follow-up limit generalizability and long-term inference.

Implications Medical Affairs : The finding that AAC imaging result disclosure does not negatively impact psychosocial or mental health in older adults is clinically important for Medical Affairs teams supporting cardiovascular screening programs, as it directly addresses the physician and patient concern that early disease disclosure causes anxiety or psychological harm. For companies developing vascular imaging technologies or cardiovascular risk stratification tools used in hypertension management pathways, these data can be incorporated into medico-scientific materials to demonstrate a favorable risk-benefit profile of early AAC screening and facilitate guideline adoption. Future research should prospectively evaluate whether AAC-guided risk communication improves long-term antihypertensive medication adherence, which would generate actionable evidence for aligning screening program promotion with drug therapy positioning.

Annual average exposure to ambient air pollution, polygenic risk, and incidence of major chronic diseases: a large-scale prospective cohort study.

Revue : Human Genetics | Tier : NA | IF estime : NA

Date de publication : 2026-10-05

Resultats principaux : This large-scale prospective cohort study included 313,534 UK Biobank participants and used land-use regression models to estimate annual average residential exposure to four air pollutants (PM2.5, PM10, NO2, NOx), assessing their independent and joint associations with five major chronic disease categories including hypertension, cardiovascular disease, respiratory disease, neurodegenerative disease, and diabetes, using Cox proportional hazard models. Annual average PM2.5 exposure showed the strongest independent association with chronic disease risk at HR = 1.22 per 5 micrograms per cubic meter increase (95% CI: 1.19-1.26), with all four pollutants significantly associated with increased risk. Weighted polygenic risk scores (PRS) were independently associated with disease incidence, with diabetes PRS showing HR = 1.77 per standard deviation increase as an illustrative example. Significant additive gene-environment interactions were observed for neurodegenerative disease (RERI = 0.14) and diabetes (RERI = 0.21) between high genetic risk and high PM2.5 exposure, indicating that genetically predisposed individuals face disproportionately greater pollution-attributable risk. Limitations include the predominantly White UK Biobank population limiting ethnic generalizability, potential exposure misclassification using residential rather than personal pollution measurements, and residual confounding from unmeasured lifestyle variables not captured in the biobank.

Implications Medical Affairs : The additive interaction between high polygenic risk and air pollution exposure establishes that genetically predisposed hypertension patients residing in high-pollution environments represent a priority subgroup for earlier pharmacological intervention, providing epidemiological grounding for precision medicine patient stratification in antihypertensive drug targeting and market segmentation. From a Medical Affairs perspective, these data strengthen the scientific case for pharmacogenomics-informed prescribing approaches in hypertension and can support engagement with cardiovascular prevention societies and regulatory bodies around environmental air quality as an underappreciated co-determinant of hypertension treatment need. Companies should consider funding or co-sponsoring research into the intersection of air pollution, genetic susceptibility, and antihypertensive treatment response, which would build a unique and differentiated scientific platform while enabling engagement with environmental health and genomics KOLs.

Health checkup profile before age 65 and long-term care needs: a retrospective analysis of the Fukuoka-City Information Platform for Community-based Integrated Care.

Revue : Environmental Health and Preventive Medicine | Tier : NA | IF estime : NA

Date de publication : 2026

Resultats principaux : This retrospective cohort study used administrative data from the Fukuoka-City Information Platform in Japan, enrolling 13,379 residents aged 57-64 years without long-term care needs who underwent Japan's Specific Health Checkup in fiscal year 2012 and were followed for a median of 8 years until March 2021. The primary endpoint was new certification under Japan's long-term care insurance system (level 1 or higher), which was reached by 406 participants (approximately 3% of the cohort). Hypertension was confirmed as a statistically significant independent risk factor for future long-term care needs in age- and sex-adjusted Cox proportional hazard analyses, alongside older age, female sex, underweight status, diabetes, proteinuria, history of cerebrovascular disease, heart disease or renal failure, current smoking, physical inactivity, self-reported slow walking speed, skipping breakfast, and eating before bedtime. The study is strengthened by its large-scale real-world administrative database design covering a geographically defined population, minimizing selection bias inherent to voluntary clinical cohorts. Limitations include the retrospective observational design preventing causal inference, reliance on claims-based outcome ascertainment which may miss mild or unreported care needs, inability to fully adjust for confounders in the primary analysis presented, and restriction to Fukuoka City limiting geographic generalizability.

Implications Medical Affairs : The confirmation that hypertension is a significant midlife predictor of long-term care dependency in a real-world Japanese cohort reinforces the public health and economic imperative for optimal blood pressure control starting before age 65, providing epidemiological evidence supporting proactive antihypertensive treatment in the pre-elderly population. From a Medical Affairs perspective, these data can directly support health-economic value proposition models demonstrating that effective blood pressure management reduces not only cardiovascular events but also delays or prevents costly long-term care certification, strengthening reimbursement dossiers and payer negotiations for antihypertensive therapies in aging societies. KOL engagement should be expanded to include geriatric medicine specialists, public health policymakers, and health economists in Japan and other rapidly aging Asian countries to develop integrated cardiovascular-geriatric care pathway proposals that incorporate evidence-based antihypertensive prescribing as a long-term care prevention strategy.
