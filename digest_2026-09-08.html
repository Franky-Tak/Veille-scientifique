Associations of antihypertensive medication class with liver fibrosis and mortality in US adults with MASLD.

Revue : Clinical and Experimental Hypertension | Tier : Tier 3 | IF estime : 3–10

Date de publication : 2026-09-07

Resultats principaux : This prevalent-user cross-sectional and prospective cohort analysis leveraged NHANES 1999-2018 data linked to national mortality records through 2019, enrolling 458 US adults with metabolic dysfunction-associated steatotic liver disease (MASLD) on antihypertensive monotherapy, of whom 86 died during follow-up. The study examined all-cause mortality and liver fibrosis (assessed by FIB-4 and APRI model-based indices) as primary outcomes, with antihypertensive medication class as the exposure; inverse probability of treatment weighting with survey weights was applied to address confounding. ACE inhibitors (ACEIs) were associated with a 70% reduction in all-cause mortality compared to calcium channel blockers (adjusted HR 0.30; 95% CI 0.11-0.82), while angiotensin receptor blockers (ARBs) showed a 75% reduction (aHR 0.25; 95% CI 0.07-0.94), with notably wide confidence intervals reflecting the limited monotherapy sample size. Antihypertensive use overall was associated with substantially lower odds of advanced fibrosis by both FIB-4 (aOR 0.51; 95% CI 0.29-0.91) and APRI (aOR 0.49; 95% CI 0.28-0.89) in the MASLD population. Sensitivity analyses using alternative model specifications and expanded covariate adjustment yielded broadly consistent findings; key limitations include the cross-sectional design of fibrosis analyses, prevalent-user healthy-user bias, reliance on self-reported medication use, and restricted statistical power in the monotherapy subgroup.

Implications Medical Affairs : These hypothesis-generating findings suggest that RAAS inhibitors may confer hepatoprotective benefits beyond blood pressure control in MASLD patients, a comorbidity of rapidly growing prevalence in hypertensive populations, with potential implications for antihypertensive class selection in cardiovascular-metabolic guidelines. From a Medical Affairs positioning perspective, the differential mortality benefit of ACEIs and ARBs over CCBs in MASLD reinforces the cardiovascular-metabolic narrative of RAAS inhibitors and could support preferential class recommendations in treatment guidelines for hypertensive patients with concurrent hepatic steatosis. Medical Affairs teams should prioritize engaging hepatology and cardiovascular KOLs to design prospective real-world evidence studies that could confirm these associations and support evidence-based formulary positioning of RAAS inhibitors in the expanding MASLD patient segment.

Comparative Analysis of Hypertension Stages Prevalence and Associated Factors in Bangladesh: Evidence From BDHS 2017-18 and 2022.

Revue : Journal of Clinical Hypertension | Tier : Tier 3 | IF estime : 3–10

Date de publication : 2026-09

Resultats principaux : This comparative epidemiological study analyzed two nationally representative cross-sectional datasets from the Bangladesh Demographic and Health Survey (BDHS 2017-18: n=12,189; BDHS 2022: n=13,843) to examine temporal changes in hypertension stage distribution and determinants among adults aged 18 years and older, using multinomial logistic regression and SHAP-based machine learning approaches. BMI emerged as the most powerful and consistent predictor of hypertension severity across both survey years, with obesity conferring a 5.3-fold higher odds of severe hypertension in 2022 (AOR 5.3; 95% CI 3.2-8.7) and a significant association with controlled hypertension (AOR 1.4; 95% CI 1.1-1.8) at secondary education level. Wealth index and diabetes status were newly identified as significant determinants in 2022, reflecting increasing cardiometabolic risk concentration in higher socioeconomic groups, while geographic divisional variation persisted across both survey years with consistent spatial heterogeneity. Marital status showed a temporally changing association, with divorced respondents demonstrating significantly higher risk of severe hypertension in 2022, a novel finding warranting further psychosocial investigation. Limitations include the cross-sectional design precluding causal inference, potential measurement bias in self-reported variables, possible methodological differences between survey rounds, and single-point blood pressure measurement not meeting current diagnostic gold standards.

Implications Medical Affairs : The rising obesity-associated severe hypertension burden in Bangladesh underscores the urgent need for region-specific antihypertensive guidelines that integrate metabolic screening, particularly in South Asian populations where cardiometabolic risk clustering differs from Western cohorts. For Medical Affairs, this growing evidence base supports targeted health economic arguments for antihypertensive therapies in South Asian markets, and can anchor disease awareness campaigns promoting weight management and glycemic control as essential adjuncts to antihypertensive pharmacotherapy in high-prevalence low-and-middle-income country settings. KOL engagement strategy should prioritize collaboration with Bangladeshi and South Asian hypertension epidemiologists to co-develop real-world evidence studies examining the impact of antihypertensive drug class on cardiometabolic outcomes in this demographic, strengthening market access and formulary positioning narratives.

Standardized chronic disease management reduces comorbidity risk in China.

Revue : Nature Communications | Tier : NA | IF estime : NA

Date de publication : 2026-08-07

Resultats principaux : This large-scale population-based cohort study leveraged linked health profiles and electronic medical records from 26 million residents in Shenzhen, China, to evaluate China's nationwide Chronic Disease Patient Management (CDPM) program, enrolling 701,759 newly diagnosed hypertensive patients and 320,179 with newly diagnosed type 2 diabetes between 2017 and 2023. Instrumental variable analyses were employed to address selection bias and unmeasured confounding inherent in observational program evaluations, yielding causal effect estimates of CDPM enrollment on subsequent comorbidity development across a broad disease spectrum. Program enrollment was causally associated with a 9% reduction in overall comorbidity risk among hypertensive patients and a 14% reduction among type 2 diabetes patients, with protective effects documented across more than 20 distinct chronic disease categories. Intervention-specific analyses revealed heterogeneous effects: alcohol-use counseling, medication-adherence support, and psychological support interventions demonstrated broad efficacy across both patient groups, while smoking cessation showed more complex, non-uniform risk patterns specifically in the hypertensive cohort. Key limitations include the observational design with instrumental variable assumptions requiring external validation, potential unmeasured confounders in program participation, geographic restriction to Shenzhen urban settings limiting rural and national generalizability, and potential variation in program implementation quality across health centers.

Implications Medical Affairs : This real-world evidence study from a 26-million-person cohort provides compelling large-scale support for standardized integrated chronic disease management programs in substantially reducing multimorbidity burden associated with hypertension, offering a model directly applicable to health system policy advocacy in rapidly urbanizing emerging markets. For Medical Affairs, the documented impact of medication-adherence interventions on comorbidity risk reduction provides a powerful real-world argument for embedding patient adherence support tools within antihypertensive treatment protocols, directly strengthening the value proposition of once-daily formulations and fixed-dose combination therapies that simplify regimens. KOL strategy should prioritize collaboration with China's primary care networks and chronic disease management specialists to generate adherence-focused evidence supporting guideline updates and payer negotiations, while exploring adaptation of the CDPM model to other high-burden markets as a differentiated medical affairs engagement platform.

[Tafluprost/timolol fixed-dose combination versus tafluprost monotherapy for open-angle glaucoma and ocular hypertension: a multicenter, randomized, double-blind, parallel-group trial].

Revue : [Zhonghua Yan Ke Za Zhi] Chinese Journal of Ophthalmology | Tier : NA | IF estime : NA

Date de publication : 2026-09-11

Resultats principaux : This multicenter, randomized, double-blind, parallel-group clinical trial conducted across 25 centers in China enrolled 219 patients with open-angle glaucoma (OAG) or ocular hypertension (OHT) who required enhanced intraocular pressure (IOP) reduction after a 4-week washout period, randomized 1:1 to preservative-free tafluprost/timolol fixed-dose combination or tafluprost monotherapy for 3 months. The primary endpoint was change from baseline in mean diurnal IOP (average of 8:00, 10:00, and 16:00 measurements) at Month 3, analyzed by ANCOVA using Markov Chain Monte Carlo methods, with superiority established if the upper 95% confidence interval was below 0 mmHg. The combination arm achieved a mean diurnal IOP reduction of 6.56 +/- 3.44 mmHg versus 5.36 +/- 2.94 mmHg for monotherapy; the adjusted between-group difference was -1.312 mmHg (95% CI: -2.010 to -0.696), confirming superior IOP-lowering efficacy of the combination. Responder rates for IOP reductions of 15%, 25%, and 30% or greater were all significantly higher in the combination group (p=0.016, 0.028, and 0.032, respectively), with ocular adverse events occurring in 20.0% vs. 26.6% and conjunctival hyperemia in 2.7% vs. 8.3% for combination versus monotherapy. Limitations include the 3-month duration restricting long-term assessment, the exclusively Chinese study population, and the trial design focused on ocular hypertension rather than systemic arterial hypertension, with timolol's systemic beta-blockade effects on arterial blood pressure constituting a relevant cardiological cross-over consideration.

Implications Medical Affairs : Note: This trial addresses ocular hypertension in glaucoma management rather than systemic arterial hypertension; however, timolol as a non-selective beta-blocker represents a pharmacologically relevant overlap with the cardiovascular antihypertensive portfolio, warranting monitoring by Medical Affairs teams tracking cross-specialty applications of beta-blocker class agents. The demonstrated superiority of the fixed-dose combination with preserved safety supports the broader pharmaceutical principle that fixed-dose combinations improve both therapeutic outcomes and adherence, an argument directly applicable to systemic antihypertensive combination therapy positioning across multiple drug classes. Medical Affairs teams should monitor emerging data at the ophthalmology-cardiology interface for systemic hemodynamic effects of topical timolol, which may have regulatory and pharmacovigilance relevance for antihypertensive product labeling and patient counseling in glaucoma-hypertension comorbid populations.

Global, regional, and national prevalence of second-hand smoke and attributable disease burden in 204 countries and territories, 1990-2023: a systematic analysis for the Global Burden of Disease Study 2023.

Revue : The Lancet. Public Health | Tier : Tier 1 | IF estime : >20

Date de publication : 2026-09-07

Resultats principaux : This systematic analysis from the Global Burden of Diseases, Injuries, and Risk Factors Study (GBD) 2023 estimated second-hand smoke (SHS) exposure prevalence and its attributable disease burden across 204 countries and territories from 1990 to 2023, using spatiotemporal Gaussian process regression to synthesize population-based surveys and household composition data stratified by age and sex. In 2023, an estimated 2.71 billion people (95% UI 2.44-3.02 billion) were exposed to SHS globally, including 767 million children aged 0-14 years, with age-standardized prevalence highest in Southeast Asia, East Asia, and Oceania (48.4%; 95% UI 44.0-53.4%). Despite a 22.2% (95% UI 10.6-32.4%) decline in global age-standardized SHS prevalence since 1990, absolute exposure numbers remained stable globally and rose sharply in sub-Saharan Africa (+98.5%; 95% UI 62.0-144.4%) and North Africa and the Middle East (+72.1%; 95% UI 47.8-101.8%). SHS was attributable to 1.66 million deaths (95% UI 1.33-2.07 million) and 44.8 million DALYs (35.8-54.3 million) in 2023, with ischaemic heart disease as the leading contributor to SHS-attributable DALYs (12.0 million; 9.31-15.2 million), directly linking SHS to the cardiovascular hypertension disease pathway. Methodological limitations include uncertainty in SHS quantification in data-sparse regions, relative risk estimates from observational studies susceptible to residual confounding, and the newly included asthma outcome carrying higher uncertainty through the Burden of Proof methodology.

Implications Medical Affairs : The persistence of SHS as a driver of 12 million cardiovascular DALYs annually, particularly through ischaemic heart disease strongly linked to hypertension, provides powerful population-level data to anchor Medical Affairs messaging about residual cardiovascular risk factors unaddressed by pharmacotherapy alone, supporting advocacy for comprehensive cardiovascular risk management programs. For companies with antihypertensive portfolios, this GBD 2023 data can strengthen health economic models demonstrating the unmet burden of cardiovascular disease prevention, supporting investment cases for antihypertensive therapies targeting high-SHS-burden regions in sub-Saharan Africa and the Middle East where exposure is rapidly increasing. KOL engagement should target GBD collaborators, global cardiovascular epidemiologists, and tobacco control specialists to develop joint scientific content linking SHS exposure to hypertension incidence, supporting disease awareness campaigns and regulatory advocacy for comprehensive tobacco control as a hypertension prevention strategy.

Cardiometabolic Health and Risk of Breast Cancer Subtypes Among US Black Women.

Revue : International Journal of Cancer | Tier : NA | IF estime : NA

Date de publication : 2026-09-07

Resultats principaux : This prospective cohort analysis used data from 57,453 participants in the Black Women's Health Study (BWHS) to evaluate associations between cardiometabolic conditions (BMI, type 2 diabetes, hypertension, and hypercholesterolemia) and breast cancer subtype incidence using competing risk Cox proportional hazards models with adjustment for age, reproductive, and lifestyle factors. Among postmenopausal women, BMI 30 kg/m2 or above was significantly associated with increased ER-positive (ER+) breast cancer risk (HR 1.35; 95% CI 1.15-1.59) and the luminal A-like subtype specifically (HR 1.54; 95% CI 1.19-1.98), while no individual cardiometabolic factor including hypertension showed an independent association with breast cancer subtypes after adjustment. Postmenopausal women with 3 or more concurrent cardiometabolic conditions had a 43% higher ER+ cancer risk (95% CI 1.04-1.97) and a 38% lower ER- cancer risk (95% CI 0.37-1.02), with statistically significant heterogeneity between ER+ and ER- associations (p-heterogeneity=0.006), suggesting metabolic-endocrine crosstalk as an etiologic mechanism. Analyses of all possible two- and three-factor cardiometabolic combinations consistently yielded HRs above 1.0 for ER+ breast cancer risk, reinforcing the cumulative cardiometabolic burden hypothesis without evidence of statistically significant multiplicative interaction between individual factors. The study is limited by its observational design with potential residual confounding despite extensive adjustment, self-reported medication and lifestyle data, the possibility of breast cancer subtype misclassification, and restriction to Black women which, while a study strength for health equity, limits generalizability to other racial groups.

Implications Medical Affairs : While hypertension alone was not an independent predictor of breast cancer subtype, its clustering in cardiometabolic multimorbidity significantly elevated ER+ breast cancer risk, highlighting that comprehensive cardiometabolic risk management in hypertensive women may carry protective oncologic benefits extending beyond traditional cardiovascular endpoints. For Medical Affairs, this evidence supports cross-specialty messaging linking antihypertensive therapy and broader cardiometabolic risk factor management to potential oncologic risk mitigation, particularly relevant in Black women facing disproportionate rates of both hypertension and aggressive breast cancer subtypes, strengthening the holistic patient value story. KOL strategy should bridge cardio-oncology and women's health specialists to explore joint research initiatives examining whether specific antihypertensive drug classes with favorable metabolic or anti-proliferative profiles differentially impact breast cancer risk in cardiometabolically complex populations, informing future clinical study design.

Triglyceride-glucose related indices, cardiometabolic multimorbidity, and risk of incident osteoarthritis: A prospective cohort study in UK Biobank.

Revue : Therapeutic Advances in Musculoskeletal Disease | Tier : NA | IF estime : NA

Date de publication : 2026-09-03

Resultats principaux : This prospective cohort study using UK Biobank data included 255,232 participants without baseline osteoarthritis (OA), examining associations of triglyceride-glucose (TyG) index and adiposity-integrated TyG variants (TyG-BMI, TyG-WC, TyG-WHtR) with hospital-recorded incident OA, stratified by cardiometabolic disease (CMD) burden and adjusted for demographic, socioeconomic, and lifestyle factors via Cox proportional hazards models. Among 43,554 participants developing OA during follow-up, the highest versus lowest quartile of TyG-BMI was associated with an HR of 2.18 (95% CI 2.12-2.24), TyG-WC with HR 2.14 (2.08-2.21), and TyG-WHtR with HR 2.00 (1.94-2.06), while TyG alone showed only a marginal association (HR 1.03; 95% CI 1.00-1.06), demonstrating that adiposity integration substantially amplifies the metabolic OA risk signal. CMD burden was independently associated with OA risk: one CMD yielded HR 1.37 (95% CI 1.29-1.46) and cardiometabolic multimorbidity (CMM) yielded HR 1.44 (95% CI 1.31-1.58), with the highest observed risk in participants with both high adiposity-integrated TyG indices and CMM (HRs of 2.05, 1.99, and 1.97 for TyG-BMI, TyG-WC, and TyG-WHtR quartile 4 with CMM, respectively). Restricted cubic spline analyses confirmed dose-response relationships between adiposity-integrated TyG indices and OA risk, and no statistically significant multiplicative interactions were detected between TyG indices and CMD burden strata. Limitations include reliance on ICD-10-based hospital inpatient records that may undercount mild OA cases and cannot differentiate OA by joint or severity, potential residual confounding, the predominantly White UK Biobank population limiting ethnic generalizability, and TyG indices as surrogate markers of insulin resistance requiring clinical validation.

Implications Medical Affairs : The strong association between adiposity-integrated metabolic markers and OA risk, compounded by hypertension and other cardiometabolic conditions, demonstrates that cardiovascular risk factors extend their systemic effects to musculoskeletal disease, with implications for holistic patient management protocols in multi-morbid hypertensive populations. For Medical Affairs, this evidence strengthens the narrative that comprehensive cardiometabolic risk control including antihypertensive therapy may carry musculoskeletal protective benefits beyond traditional cardiovascular endpoints, potentially supporting expanded value dossiers for antihypertensive agents in health technology assessments that evaluate broader multi-system outcomes. KOL engagement strategy should explore co-development of scientific content with rheumatology and metabolic medicine experts to investigate whether antihypertensive agents with anti-inflammatory or insulin-sensitizing properties, particularly RAAS inhibitors, provide OA risk reduction benefits, informing the design of prospective observational studies or data linkage analyses.

Single-Visit Screening-Positive Blood Pressure Among School-Going Adolescents in Coimbatore, South India: A Cross-Sectional Study.

Revue : Cureus | Tier : NA | IF estime : NA

Date de publication : 2026-08-07

Resultats principaux : This school-based cross-sectional study conducted from July 2024 to November 2025 in Coimbatore, Tamil Nadu, India, measured blood pressure using triplicate bilateral-arm readings in 3,160 adolescents aged 12-18 years (55.98% male) across private, urban government, and aided schools, classified using the 2017 American Academy of Pediatrics (AAP) criteria. A total of 38.39% (95% CI 36.68-40.11%) of the cohort met combined Stage 1 plus Stage 2 screening-positive criteria for hypertensive-range blood pressure, with an additional 13.86% having elevated blood pressure; the vast majority were asymptomatic, underscoring the silent burden of early-onset hypertension. Obesity was the strongest independent modifiable risk factor (aOR 3.16), followed by overweight (aOR 1.43), male sex (aOR 1.59), and increasing age (aOR 1.09 per year), while underweight was independently protective (aOR 0.59); prevalence ranged from 27.76% in underweight to 69.27% in obese adolescents. Screening-positive prevalence peaked at 41.72% at age 17 years and showed consistent escalation with BMI category, revealing a clear dose-response relationship between adiposity and early-onset blood pressure elevation in this South Indian adolescent cohort. The study is substantially limited by its single-visit design which does not allow formal clinical diagnosis (repeated measurements required per guidelines), the overrepresentation of private school students (81.61%), the absence of biochemical data for secondary hypertension workup, and the cross-sectional design precluding causal inference about modifiable risk trajectories.

Implications Medical Affairs : The high prevalence of screening-positive hypertensive-range blood pressure (38.39%) in South Indian adolescents driven primarily by obesity signals an early-onset cardiometabolic risk burden that, without systematic early intervention, will translate into a substantial future prevalence of adult hypertension requiring pharmacological treatment across South Asian markets. For Medical Affairs, this data provides epidemiological evidence to support advocacy for routine school-based blood pressure screening programs in India, positioning pharmaceutical companies as credible public health partners while establishing scientific credibility in the pediatric-to-adult hypertension continuum and informing lifecycle management strategies for antihypertensive products. KOL strategy should target pediatric cardiologists and public health specialists in South India to design collaborative repeated-measurement epidemiological studies that would validate confirmed hypertension prevalence estimates and generate stronger guideline-grade evidence for school screening programs, supporting long-term market development in the high-prevalence South Asian region.
