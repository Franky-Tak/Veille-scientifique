Cancer and Hypertension: Shared Mechanisms, Treatment Interactions, and Clinical Management.

Revue : Cancer Medicine | Tier : NA | IF estime : NA

Date de publication : 2026-10

Resultats principaux : This narrative review systematically examines the clinical intersection between cancer and hypertension (HTN), synthesizing epidemiological evidence from multiple studies and evaluating how shared risk factors — including age, obesity, metabolic syndrome, dietary patterns, and psychosocial stressors — contribute to their frequent co-occurrence in clinical practice. The review identifies overlapping biological mechanisms encompassing chronic inflammation, renin-angiotensin-aldosterone system (RAAS) activation, calcium signaling abnormalities, sympathetic nervous system overactivation, metabolic dysregulation, gut microbiota dysbiosis, and genetic and epigenetic alterations that may underpin both conditions simultaneously. Particular attention is devoted to bidirectional treatment interactions, specifically how cancer therapies — including VEGF inhibitors and immune checkpoint inhibitors — can induce or exacerbate HTN, and how antihypertensive medications may influence cancer outcomes, with CTCAE grading and BP monitoring recommendations drawn from ESH and AHA guidelines. Emerging evidence on immune checkpoint inhibitor-associated cardiovascular toxicity, the prognostic value of cardiac biomarkers (troponin, natriuretic peptides), and the heterogeneous associations between specific antihypertensive drug classes and cancer prognosis are critically assessed, with acknowledgment that no uniform causal relationship has been established. The primary limitation of this work is its narrative design relying on heterogeneous observational evidence, underscoring the need for future prospective studies that integrate tumor biology, cardiovascular risk stratification, treatment exposure, and longitudinal blood pressure assessment to clarify causal pathways and guide individualized cardio-oncology care.

Implications Medical Affairs : This review consolidates the cardio-oncology interface as a clinically distinct and rapidly growing domain, and may accelerate the development of integrated BP management protocols within future oncology and hypertension guidelines, particularly for patients receiving anti-angiogenic or immunotherapy regimens. From a product positioning perspective, antihypertensive agents prescribed in oncology settings represent a high-growth market segment, and demonstrating cancer-outcome neutrality or favorable interaction profiles could differentiate specific drug classes — notably ARBs and ACE inhibitors acting on the RAAS — against competitors. KOL strategy should prioritize engagement of cardio-oncologists and key opinion leaders at the interface of cardiovascular and cancer medicine to drive educational programs, advisory boards, and collaborative prospective registry initiatives.

The cardiometabolic significance of post-dexamethasone cortisol levels below the MACS threshold in adrenal incidentaloma.

Revue : Endocrine | Tier : NA | IF estime : NA

Date de publication : 2026-09-28

Resultats principaux : This retrospective cross-sectional study enrolled 156 patients with adrenal incidentaloma stratified into three groups by post-dexamethasone suppression test (DST) cortisol: Group 1 (≤0.9 µg/dL, n=48), Group 2 (0.91–1.79 µg/dL, n=60), and Group 3 (≥1.8 µg/dL, n=48), allowing evaluation of cardiometabolic risk below the conventional mild autonomous cortisol secretion (MACS) threshold. Hypertension prevalence and both systolic and diastolic blood pressure values increased significantly in a graded fashion across all three cortisol categories; critically, Group 2 already differed significantly from Group 1 in hypertension prevalence and BP values, challenging the assumption that sub-threshold cortisol is metabolically benign. Additional significant differences in Group 2 versus Group 1 included higher diabetes mellitus prevalence, elevated fasting glucose, higher HbA1c, elevated LDL cholesterol, total cholesterol, and non-HDL cholesterol. Atherogenic risk indices including the triglyceride-glucose (TyG) index and Castelli Risk Indices I and II differed significantly across all three groups, with Group 2 consistently exhibiting an intermediate cardiometabolic phenotype between Groups 1 and 3. Post-DST cortisol levels correlated positively with blood pressure, glucose metabolism parameters, and atherogenic lipid parameters via Spearman correlation analysis, though the retrospective single-center cross-sectional design limits causal inference and prospective multicenter validation is required.

Implications Medical Affairs : These findings challenge current clinical thresholds by demonstrating that cardiometabolic risk — including hypertension — begins accumulating at post-DST cortisol levels well below the MACS cutoff, with implications for guideline bodies to consider lower intervention thresholds and broader cardiometabolic screening in adrenal incidentaloma patients. From a Medical Affairs standpoint, this identifies adrenal incidentaloma patients as an underappreciated hypertension subgroup where antihypertensive therapy initiation and metabolic monitoring may need to occur earlier than current practice suggests, creating a segmentation opportunity for targeted therapeutic positioning. KOL engagement should focus on endocrinologists and metabolic disease specialists managing adrenal incidentaloma to explore refined diagnostic algorithms and develop collaborative research protocols addressing the unresolved continuum of cortisol-associated cardiometabolic risk.

Drug-induced sleep endoscopy-defined upper-airway collapse phenotypes and the presence of cardiometabolic comorbidity in adults with obstructive sleep apnea: A single-center retrospective cross-sectional study.

Revue : The Journal of international medical research | Tier : NA | IF estime : NA

Date de publication : 2026-09-28

Resultats principaux : This single-center retrospective cross-sectional study analyzed 360 drug-induced sleep endoscopy (DISE) records from 355 adults with polysomnography-confirmed obstructive sleep apnea (OSA), focusing on whether upper-airway collapse phenotypes (VOTE classification) were associated with documented cardiometabolic comorbidity defined as at least one of hypertension, diabetes mellitus, coronary artery disease, or cerebrovascular disease. Cardiometabolic comorbidity was present in 156 of 360 records (43.3%), reflecting the high burden of hypertension and related conditions in the OSA population. In the primary logistic regression model adjusted for age, sex, BMI, apnea-hypopnea index, and all VOTE sites, epiglottic collapse showed a nominal inverse association with cardiometabolic comorbidity (aOR=0.55; 95% CI 0.32–0.95; p=0.031), but this association did not survive Holm multiplicity correction (adjusted p=0.153). Exploratory sensitivity analyses suggested that complete, but not partial, epiglottic collapse was associated with lower estimated odds (aOR=0.42; 95% CI 0.22–0.80; p=0.008), though the addition of DISE variables improved clinical model AUC only minimally (from 0.675 to 0.694) and the likelihood-ratio test was non-significant (p=0.203). Major limitations include the retrospective single-center design, inability to establish causal or temporal relationships, and the composite comorbidity outcome that prevents distinguishing the hypertension-specific contribution from other cardiometabolic conditions.

Implications Medical Affairs : The 43.3% prevalence of cardiometabolic comorbidity including hypertension in this OSA cohort reinforces the critical importance of systematic BP screening and evidence-based antihypertensive management protocols in sleep medicine practices, a setting where hypertension may be underdiagnosed. From a Medical Affairs perspective, DISE phenotyping as a potential cardiometabolic risk stratification tool in OSA remains exploratory and hypothesis-generating, and does not yet support clinical implementation, but creates an opportunity for educational programs positioning the need for integrated cardiovascular risk assessment in sleep clinic settings. KOL engagement should bridge sleep medicine specialists and cardiologists/hypertension experts to drive multidisciplinary protocols and position antihypertensive products for the high-prevalence, high-risk OSA patient population.

Theme 6 : Clinical Trials and Guidelines (1 article)

High-Frequency Oscillatory Ventilation Combined With Inhaled Nitric Oxide for Persistent Pulmonary Hypertension of the Newborn: A Single-Center Randomized Clinical Trial.

Revue : Canadian respiratory journal | Tier : NA | IF estime : NA

Date de publication : 2026

Resultats principaux : This prospective, single-center, 3-arm open-label randomized clinical trial enrolled 138 neonates born at ≥34 weeks' gestation with echocardiographically confirmed persistent pulmonary hypertension of the newborn (PPHN) and oxygenation index ≥25, randomized 1:1:1 to high-frequency oscillatory ventilation combined with inhaled nitric oxide (HFOV+iNO), conventional mechanical ventilation with iNO (CMV+iNO), or HFOV alone. The complete-case per-protocol analysis included 126 infants (42 per arm) with ascertainable 120-day primary outcomes, with the primary endpoint defined as death or requirement for extracorporeal membrane oxygenation (ECMO) within 120 days. Death or ECMO occurred in 3/42 (7.1%) with HFOV+iNO versus 11/42 (26.2%) with CMV+iNO and 14/42 (33.3%) with HFOV alone (overall p=0.012); the HFOV+iNO versus CMV+iNO risk ratio was 0.27 (95% CI 0.08–0.91; risk difference -19.0 percentage points, Holm-adjusted p=0.038). The mean oxygenation index at 72 hours was 7.1, 11.0, and 15.9 for HFOV+iNO, CMV+iNO, and HFOV alone respectively (group-by-time interaction p<0.001), with right-ventricular echocardiographic parameters also favoring HFOV+iNO. Critical limitations include the single-center open-label design, 12 randomized infants excluded from per-protocol analysis, and the primary comparison was not robust to the differential worst-case missing-outcome sensitivity analysis, classifying these findings as exploratory and hypothesis-generating pending multicenter confirmation.

Implications Medical Affairs : Although this trial addresses pulmonary hypertension in neonates rather than adult systemic arterial hypertension, PPHN represents a high-unmet-need specialty segment where this exploratory RCT provides the strongest available evidence supporting HFOV+iNO combination as a potentially practice-changing approach, positioning inhaled nitric oxide as a component of an optimized ventilatory strategy rather than a standalone rescue therapy. From a Medical Affairs perspective, this study justifies investing in a prospectively registered multicenter confirmatory RCT to establish the combination regimen's efficacy, which could lead to label expansion or strengthened prescribing guidance for iNO in neonatal ICU settings. KOL engagement should target leading neonatal intensivists and pediatric pulmonologists with the data to co-design a multicenter confirmatory trial and generate consensus statements positioning HFOV+iNO as the preferred approach for moderate-to-severe PPHN pending regulatory confirmation.

Theme 7 : Environment and Risk Factors (1 article)

Postpartum anthropometric and cardiovascular profile following hypertensive disorders of pregnancy: 12-month follow-up.

Revue : Ultrasound in obstetrics & gynecology | Tier : NA | IF estime : NA

Date de publication : 2026-09-28

Resultats principaux : This prospective longitudinal study at Prince of Wales Hospital, Hong Kong, enrolled 201 women with first-trimester pre-eclampsia (PE) risk screening, comprising 41 high-risk women who subsequently developed hypertensive disorders of pregnancy (HDP), 130 high-risk women who did not develop HDP, and 30 low-risk normotensive controls, with assessments at 6–12 weeks and longitudinal follow-up at 6 and 12 months postpartum. At 6–12 weeks postpartum, women with HDP exhibited significantly higher mean arterial pressure, left ventricular (LV) mass index, systemic vascular resistance, and myocardial performance index, alongside more impaired global longitudinal strain and lower mitral annular plane systolic excursion, compared to both comparator groups (all P<0.05), and these cardiovascular abnormalities largely persisted through 12 months even after multivariable adjustment. The high-risk-without-HDP group showed a statistically significant declining trend in weight, waist circumference, and waist-to-hip ratio from 6–12 weeks to 12 months, with concurrent improvements in LV mass index and cardiac functional indices; in contrast, the HDP group demonstrated no significant anthropometric improvement and only delayed cardiac improvements becoming apparent at 12 months. Subgroup analysis confirmed that elevated cardiovascular risk markers in HDP women persisted independently of residual anthropometric risk factors, pointing to a potentially autonomous cardiac remodeling process. Primary limitations include the single-center design with predominantly Chinese ethnicity limiting generalizability, potential selection bias from screening-based enrollment, and the practical challenges of widespread echocardiographic surveillance that reduce feasibility for large-scale implementation.

Implications Medical Affairs : This 12-month longitudinal evidence that HDP women maintain persistently elevated blood pressure and unfavorable cardiac remodeling well beyond delivery strengthens the scientific case for guideline revision to extend postpartum cardiovascular surveillance and antihypertensive follow-up beyond the standard 6-week visit, identifying a clear evidence gap that Medical Affairs can help address through publication planning and guideline engagement. From a product positioning standpoint, postpartum women with prior HDP represent a high-risk, underserved patient population for sustained antihypertensive therapy and cardiovascular risk reduction, representing an emerging commercial opportunity for long-acting antihypertensive agents with favorable safety profiles in the postpartum and breastfeeding context. KOL strategy should engage maternal-fetal medicine specialists, obstetric cardiologists, and cardiovascular guideline committee members to champion evidence-based postpartum BP monitoring protocols and cross-specialty care pathways, using this longitudinal dataset to drive advocacy for prolonged cardiovascular follow-up in HDP patients.
