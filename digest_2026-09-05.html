Indigenous Health Disparities in Hypertensive Disorders of Pregnancy Within the United States.

Revue : Current Hypertension Reports | Tier : NA | IF estime : NA

Date de publication : 2026-09-04

Resultats principaux : This narrative review examines multi-level determinants of hypertensive disorders of pregnancy (HDP) in American Indian and Alaska Native (AI/AN) populations across structural, institutional, interpersonal, and individual domains, with the aim of identifying pathways toward equitable research and clinical practice. AI/AN women have the highest HDP prevalence among all racial and ethnic groups in the United States at 9.1%, and experience severe maternal morbidity at 1.6 times the rate of non-Hispanic white women, underscoring a profound and persistent disparity. Pre-existing cardiometabolic disease is highly prevalent in this population, with obesity affecting 40% and metabolic syndrome affecting up to 55% of AI/AN adults, substantially amplifying pregnancy-related cardiovascular risk. Structural determinants—including the legacy of colonization, racial misclassification in medical records, violations of tribal data sovereignty, and disruption of traditional birthing practices—have perpetuated systemic barriers to care, compounded by qualitative evidence of pervasive discrimination and provider bias during perinatal encounters. Despite a 2-fold increased long-term cardiovascular disease risk following HDP, AI/AN women face persistent barriers to postpartum follow-up and cardiovascular screening, and evidence-based preventive strategies such as low-dose aspirin prophylaxis, blood pressure management, and telehealth-based postpartum monitoring remain critically understudied in this population. A key limitation is the heavy reliance on secondary data subject to racial misclassification, which likely underestimates the true epidemiological burden in AI/AN communities.

Implications Medical Affairs : This review highlights a profound and underaddressed unmet medical need in a high-risk obstetric population, with implications for guideline bodies considering equity-focused recommendations for HDP prevention—including earlier initiation of antihypertensive prophylaxis and telehealth-based postpartum monitoring in AI/AN women. From a Medical Affairs perspective, these findings support the positioning of antihypertensive and cardiovascular preventive therapies in maternal health equity frameworks, offering narrative material for payer engagement and advocacy around healthcare access disparities. Actionable KOL strategy opportunities include partnering with maternal-fetal medicine and health equity specialists to co-design community-based participatory research respecting tribal data sovereignty, which could generate the real-world evidence needed to substantiate therapeutic claims in this underrepresented population.

Plasma Proteomic Profiling of Comorbid and Noncomorbid COVID-19 Patients in ICU.

Revue : Journal of Proteome Research | Tier : NA | IF estime : NA

Date de publication : 2026-09-04

Resultats principaux : This prospective study analyzed plasma protein signatures in critically ill COVID-19 patients stratified by the presence or absence of type 2 diabetes and hypertension as comorbidities (COVID comorbid group [CTHG] vs. COVID-only group [COG]), using mass spectrometry-based proteomics on samples collected on the first and last days of ICU admission. A systemic shift characterized by upregulated immune responses and downregulated metabolic processes was observed at admission across all patients regardless of comorbidity status, suggesting shared early critical illness biology. Survival across both groups was defined by restoration of homeostasis, with liver-derived proteins—including LPA, TTR, and AHSG—initially suppressed but rebounding significantly in survivors, while this recovery was significantly attenuated in CTHG survivors. Among COG non-survivors, persistent suppression of haptoglobin indicated liver failure and severe hemolysis, whereas in CTHG non-survivors, mortality was driven by lipid metabolism dysregulation with drops in CD5L and APOA2 accompanied by paradoxical elevation of APOA4, consistent with impaired renal clearance. Subgroup findings confirm that pre-existing hypertension and type 2 diabetes create distinct mortality-associated proteomic trajectories that hinder physiological metabolic resolution, and a key limitation is the single-center, relatively small-sample exploratory design limiting generalizability.

Implications Medical Affairs : These findings demonstrate that pre-existing hypertension substantially alters the proteomic trajectory of COVID-19 critical illness, providing molecular evidence for the systemic burden of undertreated comorbid hypertension beyond traditional cardiovascular endpoints—a narrative directly applicable to Medical Affairs communications on the systemic value of blood pressure control. From a Medical Affairs standpoint, the identification of comorbidity-specific proteomic mortality signatures supports future development of biomarker-based risk stratification tools in patients with hypertension admitted to the ICU, reinforcing the value proposition of antihypertensive therapy as a modifier of systemic disease severity. Engagement of KOLs at the interface of cardiology, critical care, and proteomics around these findings could catalyze research collaborations aimed at evaluating whether optimized antihypertensive regimens modify proteomic risk trajectories in severe infectious disease, a novel and differentiated research axis.

Associations of menstrual cycle length with GDM and gestational hypertension in frozen embryo transfer.

Revue : Journal of Ovarian Research | Tier : NA | IF estime : NA

Date de publication : 2026-09-03

Resultats principaux : This retrospective cohort study investigated the association between menstrual cycle length and the risk of gestational diabetes mellitus (GDM) and gestational hypertension in women without polycystic ovary syndrome (PCOS) undergoing frozen embryo transfer (FET) at a single center in China, restricting the analysis to women who delivered singleton live births with complete pre-pregnancy menstrual cycle data. Women were grouped into four menstrual cycle length categories (≤27, 28-31, 32-35, and ≥36 days), and multivariable logistic regression models were adjusted for age, BMI, parity, infertility factors, IVF-FET parameters, and embryo characteristics. Women with prolonged cycles (≥36 days) had a significantly higher risk of GDM (aOR 1.54; 95% CI 1.14-2.09; P=0.005) and gestational hypertension (aOR 1.60; 95% CI 1.05-2.42; P=0.027) compared to those with 28-31-day cycles. Cycles of 32-35 days were also independently associated with an increased risk of gestational hypertension (aOR 1.59; 95% CI 1.02-2.48; P=0.041), while short cycles (≤27 days) were linked to a significantly reduced risk of GDM (aOR 0.48; 95% CI 0.30-0.76; P=0.002). Key limitations include the retrospective single-center design with restriction to IVF/FET patients without PCOS, limiting generalizability, and the absence of hormonal profiling data that could elucidate the biological mechanisms underlying these associations.

Implications Medical Affairs : The identification of prolonged menstrual cycle length as an independent risk factor for gestational hypertension highlights a novel, readily accessible clinical marker for early cardiometabolic risk stratification in women undergoing assisted reproduction, with potential to inform pre-conception counseling and monitoring guidelines. For Medical Affairs, these findings support development of educational materials for reproductive endocrinologists and obstetricians emphasizing integrated cardiometabolic risk assessment in IVF patients, potentially creating a rationale for early preventive antihypertensive strategies in high-risk reproductive medicine populations. Collaboration with KOLs in reproductive medicine and maternal-fetal medicine to design prospective studies evaluating whether early lifestyle and pharmacological interventions in high-risk IVF patients with prolonged cycles reduce gestational hypertension incidence represents a concrete and differentiated research opportunity.

Temporal Trends and Demographic Disparities in Mortality Due to Coronary Artery Disease and Hypertension in Adults Aged 25 and Above in the United States (2000 to 2024).

Revue : Clinical Cardiology | Tier : NA | IF estime : NA

Date de publication : 2026-09

Resultats principaux : This retrospective multicenter analysis used the CDC WONDER database to examine temporal trends and demographic disparities in mortality due to coronary artery disease (CAD) and hypertension among US adults aged ≥25 years from 2000 to 2024, identifying 3,776,396 deaths over the study period. The overall age-adjusted mortality rate (AAMR) rose significantly from 57.22 to 76.95 per 100,000 population, with Joinpoint regression revealing a steep increase between 2018 and 2021 (APC: +10.13%), likely COVID-19-driven, followed by a recent partial decline (2021-2024, APC: -3.50%). By 2024, male AAMR (106.43) doubled female AAMR (52.68), and non-Hispanic African American adults experienced the highest AAMR at 96.90 per 100,000, reflecting profound racial disparities in hypertension-related mortality outcomes. Non-metropolitan areas showed greater sustained mortality increases (AAPC: 2.79%) compared to metropolitan areas (AAPC: 1.23%), with the South recording the highest regional AAMR (70.25; AAPC: 1.53%); hypertensive heart disease (AAPC: 4.35%) and hypertensive renal disease (AAPC: 8.83%) surged as the fastest-growing hypertension subtypes. A key limitation is reliance on death certificate administrative data, which may underestimate hypertension-related mortality due to inter-jurisdictional coding variability and misattribution of contributing causes of death.

Implications Medical Affairs : The sustained rise in hypertension-related mortality—particularly hypertensive renal and heart disease—combined with widening demographic disparities, underscores an urgent need for more aggressive guideline-directed blood pressure management and nephroprotective antihypertensive therapy, directly supporting portfolio positioning strategies for organ-protective agents. From a Medical Affairs perspective, the pronounced racial and geographic disparities provide a compelling equity-focused evidence base for access programs, real-world evidence initiatives, and scientific exchange materials targeting underserved and rural communities where antihypertensive therapy penetration is insufficient. These data offer high-value input for KOL advisory boards on cardiovascular health equity, enabling targeted scientific exchange and advocacy activities around differential access to guideline-recommended antihypertensive care in non-metropolitan and minority populations.

Revue : Revista da Escola de Enfermagem da U S P | Tier : NA | IF estime : NA

Date de publication : 2026-08-21

Resultats principaux : This cross-sectional and analytical study enrolled 350 patients with post-COVID condition (PCC) treated in primary health care services in Brazil, comparing the prevalence of PCC symptoms between patients with and without chronic comorbidities—specifically diabetes, hypertension, and obesity—using Poisson regression to estimate prevalence ratios. The study identified a bidirectional relationship between PCC and pre-existing chronic conditions, with each comorbidity demonstrating a distinct symptom signature that modifies the clinical presentation of long COVID. Hypertension was specifically associated with a twofold increase in the prevalence of muscle weakness and/or loss of strength compared to individuals with normal blood pressure, a finding with direct implications for rehabilitation burden in this population. Key limitations include the cross-sectional design precluding causal inference, potential recall bias in symptom reporting, restriction to primary care patients that may exclude more severe cases, and absence of adjustment for PCC severity or COVID-19 variant.

Implications Medical Affairs : The finding that hypertension independently doubles the risk of muscle weakness in post-COVID condition broadens the clinical narrative around the systemic consequences of undertreated blood pressure, providing evidence that optimal antihypertensive management may reduce long-term morbidity burden in the growing post-COVID population. From a Medical Affairs standpoint, these data support advocacy for integrated cardiometabolic-post-COVID rehabilitation pathways, positioning antihypertensive products within a broader chronic disease management framework relevant to post-infectious medicine and primary care. Actionable KOL engagement opportunities span primary care, infectious disease, and rehabilitation medicine, where Scientific exchange around the long-term systemic consequences of hypertension—beyond traditional cardiovascular endpoints—can reinforce differentiated product value propositions and future real-world evidence priorities.

Remodeling of the major arteries of the brain and serum cystatin C levels in patients with non-communicable diseases.

Revue : Zhurnal nevrologii i psikhiatrii imeni S.S. Korsakova | Tier : NA | IF estime : NA

Date de publication : 2026

Resultats principaux : This cross-sectional study enrolled 460 patients with non-communicable diseases (NCDs) from Central Asia, including 229 males (49.8%) and 231 females (50.2%) with a mean age of 53.4±14.2 years, categorized into three groups based on serum cystatin C levels ranging from 1 to 3 mg/L. As serum cystatin C increased from 1 to 3 mg/L, systolic blood pressure rose significantly from 128±17 mmHg to 145±24 mmHg, and diastolic blood pressure from 84±10 to 87±12 mmHg, directly linking renal biomarker elevation to hypertension severity. Lipid abnormalities progressed in parallel: total cholesterol rose from 4.68 to 5.24 mmol/L, LDL from 3.15 to 3.31 mmol/L, and triglycerides from 1.13 [0.89;1.61] to 1.59 [1.10;2.45] mmol/L, confirming the cardiometabolic clustering associated with cystatin C elevation. Arterial remodeling was quantified by progressive increases in ascending aorta diameter (2.96 to 3.22 cm), CCA intima-media complex (IMC) thickness (0.87 to 1.04 mm), and aortic atherosclerosis prevalence (50% to 81.3%); serum cystatin C correlated significantly with ascending aorta diameter (r=0.307), vertebral artery diameter (r=0.224), and CCA IMC thickness (r=0.324; all P<0.05). Limitations include the cross-sectional design preventing causal inference, the regional origin of the cohort limiting external validity, and the absence of antihypertensive treatment data that could confound the relationship between cystatin C levels and arterial remodeling outcomes.

Implications Medical Affairs : The demonstration that serum cystatin C reliably correlates with both blood pressure severity and quantitative arterial remodeling in NCD patients supports its potential role as an additional monitoring biomarker in hypertension management protocols, with implications for guideline discussions around biomarker-guided cardiovascular and cerebrovascular risk stratification. For Medical Affairs, these findings reinforce the concept of hypertension as a systemic disease with measurable vascular consequences beyond blood pressure metrics, supporting product positioning narratives around organ protection and vascular risk reduction for antihypertensive therapies with demonstrated renal and vascular endpoint benefits. KOL engagement in nephrology, neurology, and internal medicine around integrating cystatin C into hypertension clinic monitoring protocols could support future investigator-initiated research examining whether optimized antihypertensive therapy attenuates the cystatin C-arterial remodeling relationship—a longitudinal evidence gap with strategic value.

Large-scale multiethnic electronic health record resource for diabetes complications research: the North West London Diabetes Cohort (NWLDC) - cohort profile.

Revue : BMJ open | Tier : NA | IF estime : NA

Date de publication : 2026-09-04

Resultats principaux : This cohort profile describes the North West London Diabetes Cohort (NWLDC), a large multiethnic electronic health record-based resource derived from primary care data in North West London, encompassing 337,271 patients with diabetes with longitudinal tracking of 14 major complications, with the earliest diagnosis dating to January 1932 and data updated to May 2025. The cohort includes 279,067 patients with type 2 diabetes, 17,638 with type 1 diabetes, 33,590 with gestational diabetes, and 6,916 with unspecified diabetes, with detailed clinical measures (HbA1c, BMI, blood pressure, lipids, eGFR) and sociodemographic data including ethnicity and deprivation index. Among type 2 diabetes patients, hypertension was the second most frequent complication after diabetic retinopathy, with an incidence of 51.0 per 1,000 person-years, confirming the central importance of blood pressure management in diabetes care. Cumulative incidence analyses using the Aalen-Johansen estimator (accounting for mortality as a competing risk) demonstrated significant ethnic disparities, with black, Asian, mixed, and other ethnic groups showing elevated complication risk compared to white patients; time-varying Cox models confirmed strong cardiometabolic-renal complication clustering consistent with a cardiorenal syndrome. A notable limitation is that medication data were not yet integrated at the time of publication, preventing treatment-outcome analyses, and the retrospective electronic record design may underestimate conditions with lower coding rates across ethnicities.

Implications Medical Affairs : The high incidence of hypertension as a diabetes complication (51.0 per 1,000 person-years) in this diverse real-world cohort establishes an urgent platform for evaluating integrated antihypertensive and antidiabetic treatment strategies, directly supporting Medical Affairs positioning of antihypertensives within cardiorenal protection frameworks for type 2 diabetes. The documented ethnic disparities in complication risk and cardiometabolic-renal clustering provide a compelling evidence base for equity-focused Medical Affairs advocacy around guideline adherence and differentiated access to nephroprotective antihypertensive therapy across ethnic groups. This cohort represents a strategically important real-world evidence platform for Medical Affairs-supported studies examining antihypertensive treatment outcomes by ethnicity and cardiorenal risk profile, enabling differentiated product positioning and supporting KOL partnerships in diabetology, nephrology, and health equity research.

Child-To-Adult Body Size Change and Risk of Depression and Anxiety: A Large Prospective Cohort Study.

Revue : Depression and anxiety | Tier : NA | IF estime : NA

Date de publication : 2026-09-03

Resultats principaux : This large prospective cohort study from the UK Biobank included 447,821 participants and examined whether child-to-adult body size transitions—categorized by self-reported relative body size at age 10 and measured adult BMI—constitute a distinct life-course marker of vulnerability to depression and anxiety, with exploratory mediation analyses for lifestyle factors and chronic diseases including hypertension. In fully adjusted Cox proportional hazards models, individuals transitioning from a lean childhood phenotype to an overweight/obese adult phenotype had the highest risk of major depression (HR: 1.90; 95% CI: 1.79-2.01) and anxiety (HR: 1.58; 95% CI: 1.48-1.68) compared to those remaining leanest throughout. Exploratory mediation analyses demonstrated that unhealthy lifestyle factors and chronic diseases together—including hypertension, type 2 diabetes, and cardiovascular disease—statistically accounted for 21.9% of the body size transition-depression association and 22.2% of the anxiety association in the highest-risk group. Subgroup analyses confirmed that the direction and magnitude of risk varied substantially across body size transition patterns, highlighting that childhood-to-adulthood weight gain trajectories are more informative than single time-point adult BMI alone. Key limitations include the retrospectively self-reported childhood body size introducing potential recall bias, the exploratory nature of mediation analyses susceptible to unmeasured confounding, and the predominantly white UK Biobank sample limiting ethnic generalizability.

Implications Medical Affairs : The identification of hypertension as a partial mediator in the body size-mental health pathway broadens the clinical narrative around undertreated hypertension's systemic consequences—beyond cardiovascular outcomes—supporting educational messaging to primary care about the mental health dimensions of cardiometabolic disease management. From a Medical Affairs standpoint, this study reinforces the rationale for positioning antihypertensive therapy within a life-course prevention framework, with opportunities to engage psychiatry and primary care KOLs around the interconnected cardiometabolic-mental health disease burden. These findings support future research investment into whether early antihypertensive intervention in young adults with obesity-related hypertension can reduce downstream depression and anxiety risk—a novel outcome with potential for differentiated Phase IV research and health economic modeling.

Effects of combined exercise training with or without nutritional counseling on cardiovascular risk factors in older adults with hypertension: a pragmatic randomized trial.

Revue : Journal of human hypertension | Tier : 3 | IF estime : 3–10

Date de publication : 2026-09-03

Resultats principaux : This 12-week pragmatic randomized controlled trial enrolled 32 participants aged 50-80 years with hypertension in community settings in Brazil, comparing combined aerobic and calisthenics exercise training alone (EX; n=16, 60 min/session, twice weekly) versus exercise training plus group nutritional counseling based on Brazilian Dietary Guidelines (EX+N; n=16; six biweekly sessions emphasizing food quality and reduction of ultra-processed foods without energy restriction). The primary outcome was fasting blood glucose, with secondary endpoints including blood pressure, body composition, and cardiorespiratory fitness; data were analyzed using generalized estimating equations with intention-to-treat principles, Bonferroni correction for multiple comparisons, and effect sizes reported as Hedges' g. Fasting blood glucose was significantly lower in EX+N than EX at 12 weeks (-10 mg/dL; P=0.004; Hedges' g=1.07), representing a clinically meaningful effect size supporting the additive glucose-lowering benefit of nutritional counseling. Systolic blood pressure decreased within EX+N (-10 mmHg; P=0.034; Hedges' g=0.89) but not within EX, though the between-group difference did not reach statistical significance (P=0.220; g=0.43), suggesting a trend-level benefit insufficient to confirm superiority in this underpowered sample. No between-group differences were found for diastolic blood pressure, body composition, or cardiorespiratory fitness; key limitations include the small sample size (n=32), short 12-week follow-up, absence of dietary adherence assessment, and pragmatic community design precluding blinding.

Implications Medical Affairs : This trial provides preliminary evidence that structured nutritional counseling added to community-based exercise programs produces clinically meaningful cardiometabolic improvements—including a 10 mmHg reduction in systolic blood pressure—in hypertensive older adults, reinforcing non-pharmacological guideline recommendations and supporting antihypertensive pharmacotherapy as part of an integrated management approach. From a Medical Affairs perspective, these results support educational content for primary care physicians and cardiologists on the additive cardiometabolic value of nutrition counseling alongside pharmacological treatment, positioning lifestyle modification as a complementary strategy that may optimize blood pressure outcomes and medication adherence. The pragmatic community-based design of this study offers KOL engagement opportunities with public health and primary care stakeholders in Latin America around real-world integrated hypertension management programs, where a combination of lifestyle intervention plus pharmacotherapy may be most cost-effective and culturally appropriate.

Impact of the new 2024 ESC blood pressure classification in a low-risk cohort: the 70 mmhg diastolic threshold may reduce risk discrimination in primary prevention.

Revue : Journal of hypertension | Tier : 2 | IF estime : 10–20

Date de publication : 2026-09-02

Resultats principaux : This cross-sectional study assessed the clinical impact of the 2024 ESC hypertension guidelines—which introduced an 'elevated blood pressure' category (120-139/70-89 mmHg) and lowered the diastolic threshold from 85 to 70 mmHg compared to 2018 ESC/ESH definitions—in a cohort of 1,394 adults undergoing standardized preventive health assessment in France using validated oscillometric devices. Under the 2024 ESC classification, only 10.0% of participants were categorized as nonelevated, 64.2% as elevated, and 25.8% as hypertensive, representing a dramatic reclassification driven predominantly by the lowered diastolic threshold—with 70% of previously 'optimal' individuals reclassified as 'elevated.' The diastolic-only reclassified group (systolic BP <120 mmHg, diastolic ≥70 mmHg) differed from nonelevated peers only in age (45.5 vs. 42.7 years; P=0.007), with no significant differences in sex, BMI, lifestyle, cardiovascular history, or biomarkers, indicating this reclassification does not identify a clinically distinct higher-risk phenotype. SCORE2 cardiovascular risk estimates were marginally higher in diastolic-reclassified individuals, but this was attributable to systolic BP and age, not the diastolic threshold itself; by contrast, individuals reclassified from normal to elevated had a less favorable cardiometabolic profile, suggesting diastolic-based reclassification lacks discriminatory value. Critical limitations include the cross-sectional design without long-term outcome data, restriction to a low-risk preventive medicine population which limits generalizability to higher-risk clinical settings, and the relatively small sample size for subgroup analyses.

Implications Medical Affairs : This study raises substantive clinical questions about the validity of the 70 mmHg diastolic threshold in the 2024 ESC guidelines, with the risk of large-scale misclassification of low-risk individuals as 'elevated' potentially leading to overdiagnosis and unnecessary treatment initiation—a concern with significant implications for antihypertensive therapy positioning and prescribing patterns at the primary prevention boundary. From a Medical Affairs perspective, the reclassification of approximately 70% of previously 'optimal' patients to 'elevated' without meaningful risk differentiation warrants proactive monitoring of its impact on therapy uptake, formulary decisions, and real-world prescribing, and represents an opportunity for scientific exchange with guideline bodies around the need for prospective outcome-based validation of this diastolic threshold. This study provides compelling data for engaging European Society of Hypertension and ESC KOLs and guideline committee members in scientific discussions about evidence gaps in the 2024 classification, potentially influencing future guideline revisions and identifying a strategically important clinical evidence gap for future trial design.

Revue : Obstetrical & gynecological survey | Tier : NA | IF estime : NA

Date de publication : 2026-09-04

Resultats principaux : This narrative review synthesized 73 selected publications from PubMed and Embase databases to characterize the etiology, pathophysiology, risk factors, diagnosis, management, and maternal and perinatal outcomes of nonalcoholic steatohepatitis (NASH) and nonalcoholic fatty liver disease (NAFLD) complicating pregnancy, without restriction on publication year. NAFLD is defined as the hepatic manifestation of metabolic syndrome and has an overall prevalence of approximately 14% among pregnant women in a large prospective study, driven by increasing rates of pre-pregnancy obesity, metabolic syndrome, type 2 diabetes, and polycystic ovarian syndrome. Maternal complications directly associated with NAFLD in pregnancy include gestational diabetes, hypertension, cesarean delivery, postpartum hemorrhage, and congestive heart failure, while fetal complications encompass spontaneous abortion, fetal growth restriction, large-for-gestational-age neonates, and preterm birth. Diagnosis requires liver biopsy demonstrating steatosis, ballooning, and inflammation—a limitation in clinical practice—while current management is restricted to weight reduction and lifestyle modifications, as no pharmacological treatments are approved for NAFLD in pregnancy, representing a critical unmet medical need. Methodological limitations include the narrative rather than systematic review design introducing potential selection bias, heterogeneity across included studies, and limited high-quality evidence limiting the strength of evidence-based recommendations.

Implications Medical Affairs : The association of NAFLD/NASH in pregnancy with hypertensive disorders—including gestational hypertension—highlights an overlapping metabolic-hypertensive syndrome with significant implications for antihypertensive therapy selection, safety profiling, and monitoring protocols in this high-risk obstetric population. From a Medical Affairs standpoint, the escalating prevalence of NAFLD in pregnancy—paralleling global obesity trends—creates a substantial unmet need and positions future antihypertensive therapies with established pregnancy safety profiles as high-priority development opportunities, with opportunities for scientific advocacy around early integrated cardiometabolic management in pregnant women with NAFLD. This review supports engagement of KOLs in maternal-fetal medicine and hepatology around the need for prospective registry studies and ultimately randomized trials to establish safe and effective antihypertensive management strategies for this growing, high-risk patient population—a concrete and strategically valuable research agenda.
