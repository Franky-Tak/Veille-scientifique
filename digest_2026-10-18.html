Sacubitril/Valsartan Improves Cardiovascular Outcomes in Elderly Patients with Coronary Artery Disease and Hypertension: A Retrospective Cohort Study.

Revue : Journal of Visualized Experiments (JoVE) | Tier : NA | IF estime : NA

Date de publication : 2026-10-16

Resultats principaux : This retrospective cohort study enrolled 150 elderly patients with concurrent coronary artery disease (CAD) and hypertension, comparing sacubitril/valsartan versus amlodipine (both combined with conventional therapy) over 12 months. The primary endpoint was major adverse cardiovascular events (MACE), assessed under two definitions: a predefined composite including aggravated heart failure and arrhythmia, and a conventional MACE definition comprising myocardial infarction, stroke, cardiovascular death, and revascularization. Patients receiving sacubitril/valsartan experienced a significantly lower MACE incidence versus amlodipine (10.26% vs. 27.14%, P = 0.008). Cox proportional hazards regression confirmed sacubitril/valsartan as an independent protective factor against cardiovascular adverse events (HR = 0.38, 95% CI 0.18-0.80, P = 0.011), representing a 62% relative risk reduction. Secondary endpoints also favored sacubitril/valsartan with superior blood pressure control and improved left ventricular ejection fraction, and both regimens were well-tolerated with no severe adverse events. Key limitations include the retrospective single-center design, modest sample size of 150 patients, 12-month follow-up duration, and absence of randomization which may introduce selection bias.

Implications Medical Affairs : These findings reinforce the cardiovascular benefit of sacubitril/valsartan beyond its established heart failure indication, suggesting potential for label expansion to elderly hypertensive patients with concurrent CAD, which could reshape prescribing patterns for this high-risk multimorbid population. The significant MACE reduction (HR 0.38) provides clinically compelling differentiation data versus conventional calcium channel blocker therapy, strengthening the product's value proposition in the elderly cardiovascular disease segment. Medical Affairs teams should leverage these results to engage cardiovascular and geriatric KOLs in designing adequately powered prospective randomized trials to confirm these findings and build the evidence base for future guideline integration.

Causal effects of antihypertensive drugs on thyroid diseases: A trans-ancestry drug-target Mendelian randomization study.

Revue : Medicine (Baltimore) | Tier : NA | IF estime : NA

Date de publication : 2026-10-16

Resultats principaux : This study applied a two-sample, trans-ancestry drug-target Mendelian randomization (MR) framework to evaluate the causal associations between seven antihypertensive drug-target classes and thyroid diseases in European and East Asian populations. Target-proximal SNPs associated with systolic blood pressure were used as genetic proxies, with estimates scaled per 1-mmHg genetically predicted increase in SBP, such that protective drug effects were interpreted in the opposite direction. In the European population, beta-blocker proxies were associated with increased thyroid carcinoma susceptibility (OR 0.93, 95% CI 0.87-0.99), while calcium channel blocker proxies linked to elevated hyperthyroidism incidence (OR 0.97, 95% CI 0.96-0.99) and reduced thyroid cancer risk (OR 1.07, 95% CI 1.03-1.10). CYP17A1 showed the broadest cross-ancestry pattern, exhibiting protective effects against hyperthyroidism (OR 1.04) and Graves disease (OR 1.10), but increased hypothyroidism and thyroid cancer risk in both European and East Asian cohorts. Sensitivity analyses including weighted-median, MR-Egger, and MRPRESSO methods were applied to assess pleiotropy. Limitations include the hypothesis-generating nature of MR estimates reflecting lifelong genetic effects rather than clinical drug effects, and the need for pharmacoepidemiological replication in prospective studies.

Implications Medical Affairs : These Mendelian randomization findings raise important safety signals for antihypertensive drug classes — particularly beta-blockers and calcium channel blockers — regarding thyroid cancer risk, warranting pharmacovigilance attention and potential inclusion in risk management communications directed at physicians treating hypertensive patients with thyroid comorbidities. From a product positioning standpoint, the differential thyroid risk profiles across antihypertensive classes provide an opportunity to differentiate RAS-inhibitor-based therapies if these findings are further validated in pharmacoepidemiological studies. Medical Affairs should prioritize engagement with KOLs across endocrinology and hypertension to design prospective studies examining thyroid outcomes in patients on long-term antihypertensive therapy, supporting both post-marketing safety strategies and targeted prescribing guidance.

Outcome-specific value of longitudinal clinical information and model complexity for dynamic prediction of type 2 diabetes-related outcomes: a pooled landmarking study.

Revue : Frontiers in Endocrinology | Tier : NA | IF estime : NA

Date de publication : 2026-10-02

Resultats principaux : This pooled landmarking study included 646 type 2 diabetes patients with complete annual records from baseline through year 3, examining annual dynamic prediction of five diabetes-related outcomes including hypertension, coronary heart disease, diabetic kidney disease, diabetic retinopathy, and metabolic dysfunction-associated steatotic liver disease (MASLD). Landmark times at years 1 and 2 were used to predict outcomes over the subsequent year using four progressively expanded feature sets, comparing elastic-net logistic regression and LightGBM models. Pooled out-of-fold AUC ranged from 0.722 to 0.952 across representative models, with hypertension prediction showing the greatest benefit from longitudinal clinical history (elastic-net ΔAUC 0.097, 95% CI 0.057-0.136; LightGBM ΔAUC 0.118, 95% CI 0.081-0.158). The incremental value of longitudinal data was outcome-specific: MASLD also benefited modestly (ΔAUC ~0.02-0.025), whereas coronary heart disease, diabetic retinopathy, and diabetic kidney disease showed no comparable improvement from longitudinal enrichment. Algorithm differences between elastic-net regression and LightGBM were modest and outcome-dependent across all five outcomes. Limitations include single-center origin restricting generalizability, a modest cohort of 646 patients, and the explicit requirement for independent external validation before clinical translation.

Implications Medical Affairs : The demonstrated superiority of longitudinal data for hypertension prediction in T2D patients validates the strategic value of continuous patient monitoring platforms and real-world data collection, which pharmaceutical companies can integrate into disease management and patient support programs to identify high-risk individuals earlier. From a Medical Affairs perspective, these ML-based risk stratification findings strengthen the case for precision medicine approaches to antihypertensive therapy initiation in T2D populations, providing evidence for value-based reimbursement arguments to payers and health technology assessors. Investing in prospective collaborations with data science-focused KOLs to externally validate and expand these prediction models would deliver actionable evidence supporting both clinical decision tools and branded disease state awareness campaigns.

The Impact of Anaesthesia Method on the Use of Anaesthesia Department Resources in Total Knee Arthroplasty.

Revue : Acta Anaesthesiologica Scandinavica | Tier : NA | IF estime : NA

Date de publication : 2026-11 (Epub 2026-10)

Resultats principaux : This randomized controlled trial enrolled 395 patients undergoing total knee arthroplasty, comparing spinal anaesthesia versus total intravenous anaesthesia (TIVA/general anaesthesia) for operating room resource utilization, costs, and perioperative outcomes. No significant difference was found in total operating room time between methods (median 152 min vs. 150 min, p = 0.49). However, patients receiving spinal anaesthesia required significantly longer post-anaesthesia care unit (PACU) stays than general anaesthesia patients (median 104 vs. 73 min, p less than 0.001), representing a 31-minute increase. Regarding hemodynamic management, medication for hypertension was more frequently required in the spinal anaesthesia group, whereas medication for hypotension was more common after general anaesthesia, illustrating differential perioperative blood pressure profiles. General anaesthesia generated considerably more disposable material (1252 vs. 611 g) with higher total costs (EUR 83 vs. EUR 36), supporting spinal anaesthesia as more economical and environmentally sustainable. Limitations include the single-center design and specific anaesthetic protocols used, which may limit generalizability to broader institutional settings.

Implications Medical Affairs : The finding that spinal anaesthesia is associated with greater need for antihypertensive medication in the perioperative period highlights an underrecognized clinical scenario where acute blood pressure management is critical, representing a niche but actionable positioning opportunity for short-acting antihypertensive agents in surgical care pathways. From a Medical Affairs perspective, perioperative hypertension in spinal anaesthesia patients opens dialogue with anesthesiology and orthopedic surgery KOLs on acute hemodynamic management protocols, potentially supporting expanded indication studies for antihypertensive products in elective surgery settings. Engagement with surgical society guideline committees to incorporate evidence-based antihypertensive management recommendations for spinal anaesthesia procedures would strengthen Medical Affairs presence at the interface of cardiovascular and surgical medicine.

From high-salt diet to cancer immunity: gut microbiota and immune system interactions and their mechanisms.

Revue : Frontiers in Immunology | Tier : NA | IF estime : NA

Date de publication : 2026-10-02

Resultats principaux : This narrative review comprehensively examines the relationship between high-salt diet (HSD), gut microbiota composition, immune system function, and cancer biology, with hypertension noted as one of the classical cardiovascular consequences of chronic high sodium intake. The review details that HSD directly modulates inflammatory activation in key immune cell populations including T cells, macrophages, and NK cells, while simultaneously reshaping gut microbial composition and altering production of microbial metabolites such as short-chain fatty acids (SCFAs), bile acids, and tryptophan metabolites. The HSD-microbiota-immune axis is described as having context-dependent effects on cancer biology: in some experimental settings, HSD may transiently enhance antitumor immune responses, whereas chronic exposure promotes inflammation, immune exhaustion, microbial dysbiosis, and tumor progression. Mechanistic pathways are described linking dietary sodium to tumor microenvironment remodeling, potentially explaining epidemiological associations between HSD and certain cancer types. Limitations acknowledged include heterogeneity of experimental models, the largely preclinical nature of the evidence base, and the absence of prospective clinical studies validating these associations in human populations, underscoring that findings remain hypothesis-generating.

Implications Medical Affairs : The growing mechanistic evidence linking high-salt diet to immune dysregulation and cancer progression reinforces the public health urgency of dietary sodium reduction — already a cornerstone of hypertension prevention guidelines — suggesting that antihypertensive lifestyle interventions carry potential benefits extending beyond cardiovascular protection into oncology prevention. From a product positioning standpoint, situating antihypertensive lifestyle counseling within a broader multi-disease risk reduction framework could strengthen engagement with guideline committees and payers who prioritize integrated multi-morbidity prevention strategies. Medical Affairs teams should monitor the rapidly evolving HSD-microbiota-immune literature to identify cross-specialty KOL engagement opportunities, particularly with oncologists and microbiome scientists whose research increasingly intersects with cardiovascular risk factor management.
