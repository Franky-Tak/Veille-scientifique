Large-scale plasma proteomic profiling enhances prediction of heart failure among hypertensive individuals: prospective insights from the UK Biobank.

Revue : Journal of Global Health | Tier : NA | IF estime : NA

Date de publication : 2026-10-19

Resultats principaux : This prospective analysis included 16,128 hypertensive UK Biobank Pharma Proteomics Project participants without baseline heart failure (HF), followed over a median of 15.1 years, during which 1,074 participants developed HF. Using the Olink Explore platform measuring 2,911 plasma proteins, 534 proteins were independently associated with incident HF after adjustment for demographic and clinical risk factors, enriched in cytokine signalling, extracellular matrix organisation, and immune-cell migration pathways. A 101-protein risk score (PRS) constructed via LASSO regression improved the C-index from 0.670 to 0.786 beyond age and sex, and from 0.727 to 0.787 beyond the conventional Pooled Cohort Equations to Prevent Heart Failure (PCP-HF) model, with significant Net Reclassification Improvement and Integrated Discrimination Improvement. Notably, PRS levels were elevated years before clinical HF diagnosis, indicating an early detectable biological signature. A parsimonious six-protein panel (NT-proBNP, GDF15, BCAN, WFDC2, CTHRC1, CNTN5) achieved an AUC of 0.775, outperforming NT-proBNP alone; key limitations include the predominantly European UK Biobank population, potential unmeasured confounders, and the cost and complexity of large-scale proteomics for routine clinical implementation.

Implications Medical Affairs : This study demonstrates that plasma proteomic profiling substantially improves HF risk stratification beyond standard clinical models in hypertensive patients, supporting potential updates to prevention guidelines that incorporate precision biomarker-based screening. From a Medical Affairs perspective, these findings open strategic opportunities for pharmaceutical companies to explore companion diagnostic partnerships alongside antihypertensive therapies, particularly for agents targeting HF prevention in high-risk hypertensive populations. The identification of a measurable biological signal years before clinical HF onset provides a compelling scientific foundation for KOL engagement focused on early intervention paradigms and for future research collaborations with proteomic diagnostic developers.

Effects of muscle strength training with blood flow restriction on glomerular function in people with stage 3 chronic kidney disease.

Revue : Jornal Brasileiro de Nefrologia | Tier : NA | IF estime : NA

Date de publication : 2026

Resultats principaux : This randomized longitudinal clinical trial enrolled 40 participants with stage 3 chronic kidney disease (CKD), predominantly male (52.5%), with high rates of hypertension (87.5%) and some diabetes (37.5%), assigned to one of four groups: control (G1), low-load muscle strength training (MST; G2), high-load MST (G3), and low-load MST combined with blood flow restriction (BFR; G4). Over 12 weeks of 3 sessions per week, the MST+BFR group (G4) showed a statistically significant increase in albuminuria levels (p=0.048) and significant differences from the control group for creatinine (p=0.032) and from the low-load MST group for creatinine (p=0.036) and eGFR-Cr (p=0.032). No severe adverse effects on serum creatinine, cystatin C, eGFR estimates, or urinary creatinine were detected overall, but the BFR combination appears to exert a distinct renal stress on glomerular filtration markers. The BFR-induced increase in albuminuria is particularly relevant given the very high prevalence of hypertension in the study cohort. Limitations include the small sample size of 40 participants, relatively short follow-up of 12 weeks, single-center design, and challenges generalizing results beyond CKD-3 patients with high comorbidity burden.

Implications Medical Affairs : The observation that BFR-augmented resistance training significantly increases albuminuria in hypertensive CKD-3 patients raises important renal safety signals for exercise prescription recommendations in this population. Medical Affairs teams should monitor evolving evidence to ensure that patient education materials and clinical guidance on adjunctive non-pharmacological interventions in hypertensive nephropathy patients appropriately reflect these renal safety considerations. These findings represent an opportunity for KOL engagement at the intersection of nephrology and cardiology to refine individualized cardiorenal risk management protocols and to highlight the critical role of pharmacological blood pressure control in protecting renal endpoints when exercise interventions are applied.

Gut Regulated Associations of Neuronal Signalling in High Blood Pressure (GRAINS-BP): a double-blinded randomised crossover placebo-controlled trial.

Revue : Trials | Tier : NA | IF estime : NA

Date de publication : 2026-07-22

Resultats principaux : The GRAINS-BP trial is a registered double-blind, crossover, placebo-controlled randomized clinical trial designed to enroll 29 adults with untreated hypertension (>=140/90 mmHg) at the Alfred Hospital in Melbourne, Australia, to investigate whether short-chain fatty acids (SCFAs) derived from high amylose maize starch enriched with acetate and butyrate (HAMSA/B) reduce blood pressure by modulating autonomic nervous system activity. The trial compares HAMSA/B shakes delivered twice daily versus placebo over 14-day intervention periods with a 20-day washout, with the primary endpoint being change in muscle sympathetic nerve activity (MSNA). Secondary endpoints include 24-hour blood pressure, gut microbiome composition, SCFA plasma levels, immune cell profiles, gastrointestinal transit time, quality of life, cognitive function, gut barrier integrity, and circulating micro- and nanoplastics — a notably broad and innovative endpoint set. The study builds on a prior clinical trial demonstrating that HAMSA/B significantly reduced blood pressure in hypertensive patients, and proposes the gut-brain axis as the mechanistic pathway, with MSNA as the key mediator. Important limitations at this stage include that this is a protocol publication without clinical results, the very small sample size of 29 participants, single-center design in Melbourne, and the complexity of interpreting the autonomous nervous system endpoints in the context of the crossover design.

Implications Medical Affairs : The GRAINS-BP trial is among the first rigorous mechanistic investigations linking dietary fiber-derived SCFAs to sympathetic nervous system modulation in hypertension, potentially redefining the scientific understanding of non-pharmacological BP reduction mechanisms via the gut-brain axis. Medical Affairs teams should monitor this trial's outcomes closely, as positive MSNA results could catalyze new guideline recommendations on prebiotic dietary interventions as adjunctive hypertension management, creating opportunities to position antihypertensive pharmacotherapies within an integrated, microbiome-aware treatment framework. Engaging KOLs at the intersection of cardiovascular pharmacology, nutrition science, and gut microbiome research could be a differentiating strategic investment in anticipation of this emerging scientific field influencing future treatment algorithms.

Independent and Joint Associations of Long-Term Ambient Meteorological Exposures and Socioeconomic Deprivation with Major Adverse Cardiovascular Events: A Prospective Cohort Study.

Revue : Environmental Pollution | Tier : NA | IF estime : NA

Date de publication : 2026-10-19

Resultats principaux : This prospective cohort study analyzed 469,347 UK Biobank participants over a median follow-up of 13.7 years, identifying 31,626 MACE events, 12,565 strokes, 17,062 myocardial infarctions, and 7,161 cardiovascular deaths. In Cox proportional hazard models, higher long-term ambient temperature was inversely associated with MACE risk (HR 0.87; 95% CI 0.84-0.91), while higher relative humidity (HR 1.10; 95% CI 1.05-1.14) and greater socioeconomic deprivation per the Townsend Deprivation Index (HR 1.39; 95% CI 1.34-1.44) were independently associated with elevated MACE risk. Counterfactual analysis estimated that 11.75% (95% CI 9.68-13.77%) of incident MACE and 17.15% (95% CI 12.68-21.39%) of cardiovascular deaths over 10 years were attributable to deprivation. Multiplicative interactions between meteorological exposures and deprivation were significant, with the combination of low temperature and high deprivation showing HR 1.55 (95% CI 1.40-1.71) for MACE, exceeding the independent effects of either factor. Limitations include reliance on annual average meteorological exposures, potential unmeasured confounding by behaviours and comorbidities, and generalizability concerns given the predominantly UK-based and predominantly White UK Biobank cohort.

Implications Medical Affairs : The dominant and independent contribution of socioeconomic deprivation to cardiovascular risk — quantified as attributing over 11% of MACE and 17% of cardiovascular deaths — provides compelling epidemiological support for health equity-focused antihypertensive treatment strategies targeting disadvantaged populations. Medical Affairs teams can leverage these findings in value dossiers and reimbursement frameworks, demonstrating that antihypertensive therapies provide disproportionately high public health benefit when deployed in socioeconomically deprived populations. The demonstrated synergistic interaction between climate conditions and deprivation creates a strong scientific basis for KOL engagement with public health stakeholders, health ministries, and payers advocating for integrated climate-social welfare policies as complementary to pharmacological hypertension management.

Midlife physical activity trajectories are associated with reduced physical pre-frailty/frailty in older age: insights from the prospective ATTICA sub-study 2002-2024.

Revue : The Journal of Frailty and Aging | Tier : NA | IF estime : NA

Date de publication : 2026-10-19

Resultats principaux : This population-based prospective cohort study from the ATTICA cohort (Athens, Greece, 2002-2024) followed adults across 22 years, examining the association between midlife physical activity trajectories and pre-frailty/frailty in older age after adjusting for cardiometabolic risk factors including hypertension. Four ordered physical activity trajectories were defined based on binary self-reported activity at baseline, 5-year, and 10-year follow-up: persistently inactive, declining, inclining, and persistently active. Compared to persistent inactivity, persistently active and inclining physical activity trajectories were associated with 80% (OR 0.20) and 79% (OR 0.21) decreased odds of pre-frailty/frailty at 22-year follow-up, respectively, after full multivariable adjustment including hypertension and diabetes. The population-attributable fraction of pre-frailty/frailty due to unhealthy physical activity trajectories was 49.5% (95% CI 36.3-59.9%), rising to 64.8% (95% CI 49.6-75.4%) when adding hypertension, diabetes, hypercholesterolemia, and obesity as joint contributors. Limitations include potential recall bias in self-reported physical activity data, the observational design precluding causal inference, and restriction to an urban Greek population limiting generalizability to other ethnicities and settings.

Implications Medical Affairs : This study identifies sustained or improving physical activity in midlife as a major protective factor against frailty in older age, with hypertension confirmed as a key co-modifier of the population-attributable burden, strengthening the case for hypertension control as an integral component of healthy aging programs. Medical Affairs teams should consider frailty prevention as an additional clinically meaningful outcome to communicate when positioning antihypertensive therapies, particularly in aging populations where functional independence and quality of life are primary patient priorities. These long-term cohort data provide a compelling scientific narrative for KOL engagement across cardiology, geriatrics, and preventive medicine specialties, supporting the development of lifestyle-inclusive clinical trial designs that measure both cardiovascular and functional aging endpoints.

The higher adherence to the DASH diet the lower incidence and mortality from cardiovascular disease and all causes: ELSA-Brasil study.

Revue : International Journal of Food Sciences and Nutrition | Tier : NA | IF estime : NA

Date de publication : 2026-10-18

Resultats principaux : This analysis of 14,621 participants from the ELSA-Brasil prospective cohort (mean age 52 years, SD 9) examined the association between adherence to the DASH diet and major adverse cardiovascular events (MACE), cardiovascular disease (CVD) mortality, and all-cause mortality over follow-up. DASH adherence was measured at baseline using a continuous score (mean 23.9, SD 4.4), and Fine-Gray competing risk models and Cox regression were applied with adjustment for sociodemographic factors, behaviors, comorbidities, weight change, and total energy intake. Each one-point increase in the DASH score was independently associated with lower risk of MACE (sHR 0.96; 95% CI 0.92-0.99), CVD mortality (sHR 0.95; 95% CI 0.92-1.00), and all-cause mortality (HR 0.97; 95% CI 0.95-0.98), confirming an inverse dose-response relationship between DASH adherence and cardiovascular and total mortality outcomes. Subgroup analyses were not reported, and the continuous scoring approach may obscure threshold effects; key limitations include the observational design limiting causal inference, baseline dietary assessment only (no longitudinal dietary tracking), and potential residual confounding from factors not captured at enrollment.

Implications Medical Affairs : This large prospective Brazilian study provides robust, population-level evidence that DASH diet adherence independently reduces MACE and mortality across a diverse Latin American cohort, reinforcing the dietary-pharmacological complementarity in antihypertensive cardiovascular risk management. Medical Affairs teams can incorporate these data into educational frameworks that position antihypertensive therapies as part of comprehensive cardiovascular risk reduction strategies requiring lifestyle co-intervention, thereby strengthening product differentiation through a holistic treatment narrative. Collaborating with KOLs in cardiology, nutrition, and preventive medicine to co-develop integrated hypertension management guidelines that include DASH diet compliance metrics could represent a high-impact, scientifically credible Medical Affairs initiative in Latin America and globally.

The association of seven dietary patterns and the risk of dementia: a systematic review and meta-analysis of prospective cohort studies.

Revue : Psychiatry Research | Tier : NA | IF estime : NA

Date de publication : 2026-10-14

Resultats principaux : This systematic review and meta-analysis synthesized 21 prospective cohort studies comprising 590,029 subjects and 19,401 dementia cases to evaluate the associations between seven distinct dietary patterns and dementia risk. The MIND (Mediterranean-DASH Intervention for Neurodegenerative Delay) diet was associated with a significant and clinically meaningful reduction in dementia risk (HR 0.79; 95% CI 0.73-0.85), while the healthy plant-based diet index (hPDI) showed a significant inverse association (HR 0.85; 95% CI 0.75-0.97). Neither the standard DASH diet (HR not significant), Mediterranean diet (HR 0.90; 95% CI 0.78-1.05), Healthy Eating Index, unhealthy plant-based diet index, nor Western dietary pattern demonstrated statistically significant associations with dementia risk in pooled analysis. The superiority of the MIND diet over standard DASH or Mediterranean diets alone suggests that the neurodegenerative-targeted optimization embedded in the MIND diet framework provides additional neuroprotective benefit beyond cardiovascular-focused dietary patterns. Key limitations include substantial heterogeneity across the 21 included studies, heavy reliance on self-reported dietary assessments, observational study designs precluding causal inference, and the pooling of varied population cohorts with differing dementia ascertainment methods.

Implications Medical Affairs : The specific protective effect of the MIND diet against dementia risk is highly relevant for Medical Affairs given that hypertension is a well-established major modifiable risk factor for both cardiovascular disease and dementia, placing antihypertensive therapy at the center of an integrated cardiometabolic-cognitive health strategy. Medical Affairs teams can leverage these data to develop cross-specialty educational programs positioning antihypertensive treatment within a broader healthy aging narrative that explicitly addresses cognitive outcomes, a dimension increasingly valued by patients, caregivers, and health systems. Strategic KOL engagement spanning cardiology, neurology, and nutrition sciences to promote the scientific concept of the MIND-cardiovascular continuum could create new competitive differentiation opportunities and support novel clinical trial designs examining cognitive endpoints in hypertension treatment studies.
