Prehypertension and prediabetes in Rwandan adults aged 18-69 years: insights from the 2022 STEPwise survey.

Revue : International Journal of Epidemiology | Tier : NA | IF estime : NA

Date de publication : 2026-10-14

Resultats principaux : This cross-sectional study used data from the 2022 Rwanda STEPwise survey to describe the epidemiology of prehypertension (PreHTN) and prediabetes (PreDM) in 5,516 adults aged 18-69 years (61.4% female, median age 39 years). The primary endpoints were prevalence and determinants of PreHTN (defined per Rwanda 2024 NCD guidelines) and PreDM (per WHO definitions). The overall prevalence of PreHTN was 22.1% (95% CI: 20.7-23.4), compared to overt hypertension at 16.9% (95% CI: 15.7-18.1), meaning PreHTN was roughly 1.3 times more prevalent than established hypertension. PreDM prevalence was 4.5% (95% CI: 3.7-5.3), approximately 1.5 times the overt diabetes rate of 3.0%. Residence (p=0.036), wealth status (p=0.023), alcohol consumption (p<0.001), and fruit and vegetable intake (p=0.039) were significantly associated with PreHTN in multivariate analysis, while education, central obesity, and cholesterol were associated with PreDM. Key limitations include the cross-sectional design precluding causal inference, potential misclassification using single BP readings, and reliance on self-reported behavioral data.

Implications Medical Affairs : The high prevalence of PreHTN (22.1%) in Rwanda identifies a critical prevention window that pharmaceutical companies can leverage to advocate for early pharmacological intervention before progression to established hypertension, supporting guideline updates toward earlier treatment thresholds. The association of PreHTN with modifiable lifestyle factors (alcohol, diet) provides a rationale for positioning antihypertensive agents alongside structured lifestyle modification programs, particularly for Medical Affairs outreach in low-income settings. Engagement with Rwandan public health KOLs and leveraging the existing community health worker infrastructure are actionable insights for building local awareness of pre-disease stage interventions.

Prevalence and Predictors of Blood Pressure Control in Pre-dialysis Chronic Kidney Disease Patients in Calabar, South-South Nigeria.

Revue : West African Journal of Medicine | Tier : NA | IF estime : NA

Date de publication : 2026-08-28

Resultats principaux : This hospital-based cross-sectional study enrolled 59 consecutive pre-dialysis CKD patients at the renal clinic of University of Calabar Teaching Hospital, Nigeria, between September 2020 and February 2021, representing adults not yet on maintenance haemodialysis. The primary endpoint was the prevalence of blood pressure control, defined using the average of three most recent clinic readings taken one month apart. A striking 62.7% of patients had poor BP control, reflecting a significant treatment gap in this high-risk population. The majority were male (54.2%) and in CKD stage 5 (44.1%), indicating a late-presenting, high-complexity cohort. Bivariate and multivariate analyses identified the use of multiple antihypertensive medications and the presence of dyslipidemia as factors significantly associated with poor BP control; elevated lipid profile was identified as a statistically significant predictor (p<0.05). Notable limitations include the small sample size (n=59), single-center design limiting generalizability, and the retrospective nature of BP data collection from clinic records.

Implications Medical Affairs : The 62.7% prevalence of poor BP control in CKD patients in Nigeria highlights a substantial unmet therapeutic need in Sub-Saharan Africa where RAAS inhibitors and combination antihypertensive therapies remain underutilized. The association of dyslipidemia with poor BP control underscores the need for integrated cardiometabolic management protocols, which represents a strategic positioning opportunity for pharmaceutical products addressing both lipid and blood pressure targets. Medical Affairs teams should engage nephrology KOLs in Nigeria and West Africa to promote combination therapy approaches and patient adherence programs in CKD-hypertension settings.

Revue : West African Journal of Medicine | Tier : NA | IF estime : NA

Date de publication : 2026-08-28

Resultats principaux : This cross-sectional survey, conducted from April to July 2025, enrolled 85 Nigerian women aged 18-48 years with rheumatologist-confirmed systemic lupus erythematosus (SLE), recruited through both virtual and clinic-based settings. The study described familial clustering and comorbidity patterns in this population. Hypertension was identified as the most prevalent comorbidity at 38.8%, with 28.2% having hypertension as their sole comorbidity, highlighting a substantial cardiovascular risk burden in this autoimmune population. Familial SLE clustering was uncommon, with only 8.2% of participants reporting a family history of SLE. Important limitations include the small sample (n=85), potential selection bias from online recruitment, and reliance on self-reported data without formal registry validation.

Implications Medical Affairs : The high prevalence of hypertension (38.8%) as the leading comorbidity in SLE patients underscores the need for systematic cardiovascular risk screening protocols within rheumatology care pathways, representing an opportunity for Medical Affairs engagement with rheumatologists. This data supports positioning antihypertensive agents as essential components of SLE co-management, particularly in Sub-Saharan African populations where comorbidity burden has been under-studied. KOL strategy should consider establishing joint rheumatology-cardiology guidelines for BP management in autoimmune disease patients in the Nigerian context.

Antihypertensive treatment non-adherence and spontaneous intracerebral hemorrhage: a prospective observational study from Northern India.

Revue : BMC Neurology | Tier : NA | IF estime : NA

Date de publication : 2026-07-23

Resultats principaux : This prospective observational study enrolled 100 consecutive adult patients with non-traumatic spontaneous intracerebral hemorrhage (ICH) at a tertiary-care referral center in Northern India between March 2023 and February 2024, with the primary aim of characterizing epidemiological, clinical, and radiological features and identifying preventable risk factors. The study population had a mean age of 61.2 +/- 12.4 years with 63% males, and hypertension was present in 78% of patients. Critically, among hypertensive patients, 70.5% (95% CI 59.6-79.5) self-reported non-adherence to antihypertensive therapy, making treatment non-adherence the dominant modifiable risk factor. Active smoking was present in 62% of patients. Deep hemorrhages in the basal ganglia (46%) and thalamus (26%) accounted for 72% of all cases, and hydrocephalus occurred in 28%. Higher admission blood pressure correlated with larger hematoma volumes, with no specific quantitative statistics provided for this finding. Key limitations include the single-center design, small sample size, prospective enrollment at a tertiary center introducing selection bias, and reliance on self-reported adherence data.

Implications Medical Affairs : The extremely high rate of antihypertensive non-adherence (70.5%) among hypertensive ICH patients in India provides a compelling evidence base for promoting simplified dosing regimens and once-daily formulations in the Indian market to improve adherence and reduce stroke burden. These data strengthen the case for Medical Affairs investment in adherence monitoring programs and patient education campaigns targeting physicians treating hypertension in low- and middle-income countries. Collaboration with neurologist and neurosurgeon KOLs in India around adherence-focused prevention of hypertensive ICH is a high-priority actionable insight.

Neutrophil to high-density lipoprotein cholesterol ratio is positively associated with congestive heart failure among hypertensive adults: a population-based study.

Revue : BMC Cardiovascular Disorders | Tier : NA | IF estime : NA

Date de publication : 2026-07-23

Resultats principaux : This cross-sectional study utilized pooled NHANES data from 1999 to 2018, including 19,820 hypertensive adults, to evaluate the association between the neutrophil-to-high-density lipoprotein cholesterol ratio (NHR) and congestive heart failure (CHF) prevalence. Primary endpoint was CHF prevalence across NHR quartiles, assessed through weighted multivariate logistic regression. After full multivariable adjustment, NHR was independently associated with CHF (OR=1.15, 95% CI 1.10-1.21 per unit increase). Hypertensive adults in the highest NHR quartile had a 69% higher CHF prevalence (OR=1.69, 95% CI 1.34-2.13) compared to the lowest quartile. Mediation analysis showed serum albumin mediated 4.18% of the NHR-CHF association, suggesting nutritional inflammation as a partial pathway. Among seven machine learning models tested, XGBoost performed best with a validation AUC of 0.781 (95% CI 0.758-0.803). The main limitation is the cross-sectional design preventing causality inferences, along with self-reported CHF as the outcome measure.

Implications Medical Affairs : NHR as an accessible inflammatory-lipid composite biomarker adds clinically relevant prognostic value for CHF risk stratification in hypertensive patients, supporting its potential inclusion in routine cardiovascular risk panels. This finding reinforces the Medical Affairs narrative for anti-inflammatory and lipid-lowering adjunct therapies alongside antihypertensives in high-risk patients, particularly those with elevated NHR. Collaboration with cardiologists to validate NHR in prospective studies and its potential integration into risk scoring tools represents a strategic KOL engagement opportunity.

Theme 4 : Calcium Channel Blockers (1 article)

Clevidipine vs. nicardipine in hypertensive crisis: a systematic review and meta-analysis.

Revue : BMC Cardiovascular Disorders | Tier : NA | IF estime : NA

Date de publication : 2026-07-24

Resultats principaux : This systematic review and meta-analysis compared intravenous clevidipine and nicardipine in adults with hypertensive crises, pooling data from three studies (2 RCTs and 1 observational study) totaling 599 patients. The primary outcome was achievement of target blood pressure reduction, and secondary outcomes included medication volume administered and the rate of blood pressure over-reduction. For the primary outcome, clevidipine and nicardipine demonstrated comparable efficacy (OR 0.96, 95% CI 0.48-1.94; I^2=60%; very low-certainty evidence per GRADE), with significant heterogeneity between studies. Clevidipine was associated with statistically significantly lower odds of blood pressure over-reduction (OR 0.44, 95% CI 0.21-0.93; I^2=0%), suggesting superior titratability. Clevidipine was also associated with substantially lower medication volume (mean difference -258.43 mL, 95% CI -313.38 to -203.48; I^2=0%). The main limitations include only three included studies, very low certainty of evidence for the primary outcome, and significant heterogeneity, with the authors acknowledging the findings should be considered hypothesis-generating.

Implications Medical Affairs : The comparable efficacy but superior BP precision of clevidipine (fewer over-reductions, lower medication volume) reinforces its clinical positioning as a preferred intravenous calcium channel blocker for acute hypertensive emergencies requiring tight titration control. The significantly reduced medication volume has practical pharmacoeconomic implications, including reduced nursing workload and IV fluid burden, which are compelling arguments for hospital formulary inclusion during Medical Affairs engagement with critical care committees. Given the very low certainty of evidence, Medical Affairs teams should support the design and conduct of larger, high-quality RCTs comparing these agents to conclusively differentiate their clinical profiles.

Effects of Aerobic Exercise on Autonomic and Endothelial Function and Splanchnic Hemodynamics in Compensated Cirrhosis: RCT Study.

Revue : Liver International | Tier : NA | IF estime : NA

Date de publication : 2026-11

Resultats principaux : This parallel-group randomized controlled trial enrolled 50 sedentary patients with compensated cirrhosis, randomized to 16 weeks of moderate-intensity aerobic exercise (AE) plus nutritional consultation versus nutritional consultation alone. Primary endpoints included heart rate variability (HRV), flow-mediated dilation (FMD), mesenteric Doppler hemodynamics, and functional capacity (6-minute walk test, 6MWT). No statistically significant between-group differences were found for the primary hemodynamic endpoints (HRV or FMD). However, LMM analysis identified a significant worsening of the LF/HF ratio (sympatho-vagal balance) in the control group (beta=-0.87, p=0.04), indicating the AE group maintained autonomic stability. The AE group demonstrated a significant within-group increase in portal vein volume flow (median delta=350.66 mL/min, p=0.003) and significantly improved 6MWT performance (+32 m vs control, p<0.01). The study was limited by its small sample size, modest statistical power for hemodynamic endpoints, and focus on portal rather than systemic arterial hypertension.

Implications Medical Affairs : Although this RCT addresses portal hypertension in cirrhosis rather than systemic arterial hypertension, it contributes to the emerging evidence base supporting structured aerobic exercise as an adjunct to pharmacotherapy in cardiovascular disease management, a principle translatable to hypertension guidelines. The positive functional capacity and hemodynamic stability findings reinforce guidelines recommending exercise as a non-pharmacological strategy that complements antihypertensive treatment, particularly in complex comorbid patients. Medical Affairs teams should monitor exercise-hemodynamic trials to support holistic hypertension management narratives for KOL communications and evidence-based guidelines.

Effectiveness of Weekly Semaglutide Treatment (0.5 or 1 mg) in Chilean Patients with Type 2 Diabetes Mellitus.

Revue : Revista Medica de Chile | Tier : NA | IF estime : NA

Date de publication : 2026-08

Resultats principaux : This Phase 4 multicenter interventional study evaluated 24 weeks of weekly subcutaneous semaglutide (0.5 or 1 mg) added to standard care in 104 Chilean patients with inadequately controlled type 2 diabetes (T2DM), predominantly women (79.8%, mean age 58.3 years), managed in the public health system. Key comorbidities included hypertension in 71.2% and obesity in 71.2%, reflecting the typical high-risk cardiometabolic profile. The primary endpoint of HbA1c reduction showed a significant improvement from 8.6+/-0.7% to 7.0+/-1.0% (delta -1.6%) at 24 weeks, and 56.4% achieved HbA1c <7%. Body weight decreased significantly by 4 kg (89.3 to 85.3 kg). Among patients on basal insulin, 31% reduced their dose by a median of 6 IU. Gastrointestinal adverse events were the most frequent, consistent with semaglutide's known safety profile. Key limitations include the absence of a placebo comparator arm, observational real-world design, and lack of pre-specified blood pressure endpoints despite the high HTN prevalence.

Implications Medical Affairs : The 71.2% prevalence of hypertension among semaglutide-treated T2DM patients emphasizes the cardiometabolic co-morbidity profile where both antihypertensive and antidiabetic therapies interact, creating strategic relevance for combined cardiovascular risk management positioning. The significant weight reduction (-4 kg) observed with semaglutide has downstream implications for blood pressure control, supporting the broader message that GLP-1 receptor agonists may indirectly benefit hypertension management in obese T2DM patients. Medical Affairs teams should advocate for future studies incorporating blood pressure outcomes as a co-primary endpoint in GLP-1 agonist trials within hypertensive T2DM populations to capture the full cardiometabolic benefit.

Incident Type 2 Diabetes in Individuals With CKD: The REGARDS Study.

Revue : Kidney Medicine | Tier : NA | IF estime : NA

Date de publication : 2026-08-13

Resultats principaux : This prospective cohort analysis from the REGARDS (Reasons for Geographic and Racial Differences in Stroke) study included 7,511 participants with 10 years of follow-up, of whom 994 had CKD at baseline (eGFR <60 mL/min/1.73m2 or ACR >30 mg/g). The primary endpoint was incident T2DM over 10 years. CKD participants developed T2DM at a higher rate (15.8%) compared to non-CKD participants (11.0%) when CKD was defined by creatinine-based equations alone, though this association was attenuated when cystatin-C was incorporated. Hypertension was confirmed as an independent risk factor for incident diabetes in both CKD and non-CKD groups, with prediabetes carrying the highest risk (OR=4.94, 95% CI 4.22-5.78, p<0.01). Additional risk factors included race, age, BMI, and current smoking, while alcohol use was associated with reduced risk. The main limitations include potential attrition bias over the 10-year follow-up, limited generalizability to non-US populations, and limited ability to classify CKD progression.

Implications Medical Affairs : Hypertension's role as a significant predictor of incident T2DM in CKD patients highlights the interconnected pathophysiology of cardiometabolic disease, supporting the positioning of antihypertensives with additional metabolic benefits (e.g., RAAS inhibitors) in this high-risk population. Optimal BP control as a strategy to reduce both CKD progression and diabetes development provides a compelling multi-outcome narrative for Medical Affairs communications with nephrologists. This dataset supports KOL engagement at the intersection of nephrology, endocrinology, and cardiology, emphasizing early aggressive hypertension management in CKD populations.

Construction and validation of a predictive model for sleep disorders among rural older adults with mild cognitive impairment.

Revue : Neurological Sciences | Tier : NA | IF estime : NA

Date de publication : 2026-10-20

Resultats principaux : This cross-sectional study used data from the 2024 baseline survey of the Active Aging Cohort in Rural Areas in Shandong Province, China, enrolling 1,049 rural older adults aged 60 years and above with mild cognitive impairment (MCI). The primary endpoint was sleep disturbance, assessed using the Pittsburgh Sleep Quality Index (PSQI). Sleep disturbance prevalence was notably high at 54.2%. Logistic regression identified sex, hypertension, self-rated health, and depression as independent predictors of sleep disturbance in this population. These predictors were incorporated into a nomogram that demonstrated good discrimination (AUC=0.759, 95% CI 0.731-0.778) and calibration, with bootstrap internal validation confirming model robustness. Hypertension emerged as a consistent independent predictor, highlighting its bidirectional relationship with sleep quality in cognitively impaired elderly populations. Limitations include the cross-sectional design, single-region geographic scope limiting generalizability, and no causal directionality between hypertension and sleep disturbance.

Implications Medical Affairs : Hypertension's independent association with sleep disorders in cognitively vulnerable elderly patients underscores the importance of comprehensive sleep assessment as part of hypertension management protocols in geriatric populations. This bidirectional relationship between hypertension and sleep quality supports the case for chronotherapy approaches to antihypertensive prescribing (e.g., evening dosing) that may simultaneously improve BP control and sleep quality. Medical Affairs engagement with geriatric neurologists and sleep medicine specialists around the hypertension-sleep-cognition triad represents a strategic opportunity to differentiate antihypertensive products in the growing geriatric market.

Revue : Frontiers in Public Health | Tier : NA | IF estime : NA

Date de publication : 2026-10-05

Resultats principaux : This community-based cross-sectional study enrolled 380 rural older adults aged 60 years and above residing in the rural field practice area of a tertiary care hospital in Bhubaneswar, Odisha, India. Data were collected using the Mini Nutritional Assessment (MNA), WHO Quality of Life-BREF (WHOQOL-BREF), and Self-Administered Comorbidity Questionnaire (SCQ). Hypertension was the second most prevalent comorbidity after osteoarthritis, affecting 32.6% of participants, while 82.6% had at least one comorbidity. A notable 4.2% were malnourished and 69.7% were at risk of malnutrition. WHOQOL-BREF scores were highest in the environmental domain (mean 27) and lowest in the social relationship domain (mean 8). Malnutrition was significantly associated with lower physical, psychological, and total QoL scores. Limitations include the cross-sectional design, single-center data, and non-validated comorbidity ascertainment via self-reported questionnaire.

Implications Medical Affairs : The 32.6% hypertension prevalence co-existing with high malnutrition risk (69.7%) in rural Indian elderly highlights a challenging clinical context where antihypertensive management must be tailored to nutritionally vulnerable patients with polypharmacy and tolerability concerns. This data supports the positioning of antihypertensive agents with favorable tolerability profiles and minimal nutritional interaction as preferred options in geriatric care settings in India, representing an unmet need for medical affairs engagement. KOL partnerships with geriatricians and public health specialists in Eastern India should be developed to address the integrated management of hypertension and malnutrition in this growing demographic.

Gut microbiota and innate immune barrier dysfunction in hepatic portal venous gas: a hypothesis-generating review.

Revue : Frontiers in Immunology | Tier : NA | IF estime : NA

Date de publication : 2026-10-05

Resultats principaux : This hypothesis-generating review synthesizes evidence on the role of gut dysbiosis and innate immune barrier dysfunction in hepatic portal venous gas (HPVG), a rare radiographic finding often associated with bowel necrosis and shock. The review draws on evidence from proxy conditions including cirrhosis, porto-sinusoidal vascular disease, and intestinal ischemia/reperfusion to construct a mechanistic model. Portal hypertension is considered as a host modifier of barrier geometry and vascular washout in the proposed model, rather than as an independent generator of intraportal gas. No primary clinical data is presented; the article is an interpretive synthesis proposing that gut dysbiosis (through butyrate loss, bile acid shifts, LPS-TLR4 priming, and fermentative pathogen expansion) may amplify immune failure in HPVG. Key fermentative isolates include Clostridium perfringens and Escherichia coli. The article explicitly states that no microbiota-directed intervention has been tested against an HPVG endpoint, and all microbiome interventions remain research questions. Limitations include the hypothesis-generating nature, absence of HPVG-specific microbiome data, and inability to define causal directionality.

Implications Medical Affairs : While this review is primarily focused on portal hypertension and hepatic immunology rather than systemic arterial hypertension, it reflects the growing interest in the gut-microbiome axis as a modulator of vascular and hemodynamic homeostasis that is relevant to hypertension research. Emerging data on gut dysbiosis as a contributor to systemic hypertension through endotoxemia and vascular inflammation suggest that microbiome-modulating strategies may have future relevance as adjunct antihypertensive approaches, warranting surveillance by Medical Affairs teams. Research collaborations with gastroenterology and immunology KOLs on the gut-vascular axis could position pharmaceutical companies at the forefront of next-generation antihypertensive therapeutic discovery.
