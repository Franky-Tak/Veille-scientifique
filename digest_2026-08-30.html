Long-Term Effectiveness of Intensive Blood Pressure Management Led by Nonphysician Community Healthcare Providers on Cardiovascular Events: 7-Year Follow-Up of a Cluster Randomized Trial.

Revue : Circulation | Tier : 1 | IF estime : >20

Date de publication : 2026-08-28

Resultats principaux : The CRHCP (China Rural Hypertension Control Project) was a cluster-randomized controlled trial enrolling 33,995 hypertensive adults aged 40 years or older from 326 villages in rural China, randomized 1:1 to a nonphysician community healthcare provider (NPCHP)-led intensive BP management program targeting less than 130/80 mm Hg versus usual care, with a 4-year active intervention followed by a 3-year posttrial observational follow-up period. The primary composite endpoint consisted of myocardial infarction, stroke, hospitalization for heart failure, and cardiovascular death. At the end of the 7-year overall period, BP was significantly lower in the intervention group (138.8/80.7 vs. 152.3/86.1 mm Hg; difference -13.5/-5.4 mm Hg, p<0.0001), with 33.9% versus 10.5% of participants achieving BP below 130/80 mm Hg. The composite CVD event rate was 2.4% versus 3.0% per person-year over the full 7-year period (HR 0.76, 95% CI 0.72-0.81, p<0.0001), with durable posttrial benefit maintained at 3.4% versus 4.2% per person-year during the 3-year posttrial period (HR 0.79, 95% CI 0.73-0.85, p<0.0001) and consistent effects across subgroups defined by age, sex, education level, and baseline antihypertensive medication use. The intervention group experienced higher rates of hypotension (RR 1.58, 95% CI 1.39-1.79) and mild hypokalemia (RR 1.38, 95% CI 1.23-1.56); key limitations include the open-label design, potential cluster contamination in rural village settings, and that performance incentives and discounted medications were withdrawn in the posttrial phase, potentially underestimating the full long-term treatment effect.

Implications Medical Affairs : This landmark 7-year cluster RCT published in Circulation provides robust evidence that NPCHP-led intensive BP management sustains meaningful cardiovascular risk reduction even after withdrawal of program support components, offering strong data to support adoption of the less than 130/80 mm Hg BP target in international hypertension guidelines for resource-limited settings. For Medical Affairs, these results strengthen the positioning of antihypertensive therapies within task-sharing and community-based care models, particularly in low- and middle-income country markets where specialist access is constrained, creating new market access and health system engagement opportunities. An actionable KOL strategy is to engage Chinese rural health implementation researchers and global cardiology thought leaders to disseminate these sustained-benefit findings through advisory boards, symposia, and collaborative publications exploring replication in other high-burden regions.

Gender-related determinants of hypertension self-care behaviours in primary care: Perspectives of patients and healthcare professionals.

Revue : Atencion primaria | Tier : NA | IF estime : NA

Date de publication : 2026-08-29

Resultats principaux : This descriptive qualitative study was conducted at a public primary care center in Barcelona, Spain, and included 24 participants across four focus groups: 12 patients with hypertension (6 women, 6 men) and 12 primary care professionals (10 women, 2 men; comprising 8 nurses and 4 physicians), recruited through purposive sampling to ensure diversity in gender and professional role. Sessions lasting 60-80 minutes followed a semi-structured guide addressing knowledge, beliefs, diet, physical activity, self-monitoring, medication adherence, stress, social support, and gender influences, and transcripts were analyzed by three independent researchers using Giorgi's descriptive phenomenological method with iterative consensus to maximize analytical rigor. Both gender-common and gender-differentiated determinants of hypertension self-care were identified; the cross-cutting belief that pharmacological treatment alone is sufficient for blood pressure management emerged as a shared barrier across both sexes. Predominantly female-associated determinants included caregiving-related guilt, subordination of personal self-care to household responsibilities, time poverty, schedule incompatibility with healthcare visits, asymmetric intrafamilial support, and family dietary management shaped by traditional gender roles; predominantly male-associated determinants included low cardiovascular risk perception, symptom-guided help-seeking behavior, reliance on female partners for dietary management, and self-care triggers in masculinized social contexts. Key limitations of this study include its single-center qualitative design with a small sample size, which limits geographic generalizability, and the fact that healthcare professionals reported limited specific training and low confidence in applying a gender-sensitive clinical approach to hypertension management, suggesting a systemic gap in primary care professional education.

Implications Medical Affairs : These findings highlight the importance of integrating a gender-sensitive framework into hypertension educational materials and patient support programs, with potential implications for guidelines bodies to explicitly address gendered barriers to self-care as part of comprehensive hypertension management recommendations. From a Medical Affairs positioning perspective, pharmaceutical companies can differentiate their antihypertensive portfolio through gender-tailored disease awareness campaigns, digital adherence tools designed to address the distinct behavioral profiles of female and male patients, and engagement with primary care nursing KOLs who are central to patient education in this setting. An actionable strategic priority is to co-develop gender-responsive professional training modules with primary care and gender health experts, which can serve as a medical education partnership opportunity to strengthen KOL relationships and improve real-world adherence outcomes for hypertensive patients.

Comprehensive strategies for stroke prevention: A narrative review of population-level risk factor mitigation.

Revue : Brain circulation | Tier : NA | IF estime : NA

Date de publication : 2025-10-09

Resultats principaux : This narrative review examines population-level risk factor mitigation strategies for stroke prevention, framing the urgency of the topic around stroke remaining a leading cause of global mortality and its projection to become the second-largest contributor to global disease burden by 2050, with a noted paradox of declining incidence in developed nations alongside surging rates in developing regions, particularly among younger populations. The review categorizes modifiable stroke risk factors into three key domains: metabolic factors (including hypertension, diabetes, and dyslipidemia), behavioral factors (smoking, physical inactivity, and dietary habits), and environmental factors (including air pollution and psychosocial stress), and synthesizes current evidence on pharmacological and non-pharmacological interventions targeting each domain. Hypertension is identified as the single most impactful modifiable risk factor, with the review evaluating evidence from both population-level screening programs and individual pharmacological management strategies, emphasizing that BP control remains central to reducing stroke incidence globally. The review also examines the interplay and combined impact of multiple risk factors, providing evidence on how co-occurrence of metabolic, behavioral, and environmental exposures amplifies stroke risk in a manner that exceeds the sum of individual factor contributions, with specific attention to air pollution and psychosocial stress as underappreciated contributors in low- and middle-income settings. Notable limitations of this narrative review include its inherent methodological heterogeneity and reliance on secondary sources rather than primary data analysis, the absence of formal meta-analytical statistics or GRADE-level evidence appraisal, and the potential for publication bias, which may overrepresent positive findings on intervention effectiveness.

Implications Medical Affairs : This review reinforces the primacy of hypertension control as the cornerstone of population-level stroke prevention, providing a cross-therapeutic area evidence base that positions aggressive BP management as integral to comprehensive neurological outcomes strategies and lending momentum to guideline updates that embed antihypertensive therapy within multidomain stroke prevention frameworks. For Medical Affairs, this publication offers a powerful framework for cross-specialty messaging linking hypertension control to broader neurological and public health outcomes, which enhances the strategic relevance of antihypertensive portfolios in KOL engagements with stroke neurologists, public health specialists, and health system decision-makers. An actionable insight is to develop educational partnerships with stroke prevention consortia and global health organizations, using this evidence synthesis to position the company's hypertension portfolio as essential infrastructure within evidence-based, population-level stroke prevention programs targeting both developed and emerging market contexts.
