Disparities in Blood Pressure Measurement.

Revue : Current hypertension reports | Tier : NA | IF estime : NA

Date de publication : 2026-09-18

Resultats principaux : This review article systematically examines sources of inaccuracy and inequity in blood pressure measurement across four modalities: office, ambulatory, home, and emerging cuffless technologies, with a focus on underrepresented and under-resourced communities. The primary analytical framework addresses measurement accuracy, diagnostic misclassification, and blood pressure control outcomes across diverse populations, constituting the study's main endpoints. Key findings include identification of nonstandardized office technique, inappropriate cuff sizing, limited access to validated devices, and underrecognition of masked and nocturnal hypertension as leading causes of measurement disparities, particularly in high-risk groups. Subgroup analysis of cuffless technologies reveals that while these devices may expand access, they carry important limitations in validation, calibration, and performance across diverse populations, limiting their immediate utility. A principal limitation of this narrative review is reliance on existing literature without original data synthesis or meta-analysis, making exact quantification of disparities attributable to each measurement modality difficult.

Implications Medical Affairs : This review directly reinforces the need for standardized blood pressure measurement protocols in clinical practice and could influence hypertension guideline updates on measurement methodology. From a Medical Affairs perspective, pharmaceutical companies developing antihypertensive agents should advocate for inclusive device validation standards and partner with KOLs on equity-focused measurement initiatives. This work presents an actionable opportunity to engage payers and health systems around reimbursement strategies that expand access to validated home and ambulatory monitoring in underserved communities.

Development and external validation of a risk prediction model for hypertension among individuals with established hypothyroidism.

Revue : Frontiers in endocrinology | Tier : NA | IF estime : NA

Date de publication : 2026-09-03

Resultats principaux : This retrospective multicohort study developed and externally validated a hypertension risk prediction model specifically designed for patients with established hypothyroidism, a population lacking dedicated cardiovascular risk stratification tools. The derivation cohort was a Chinese hospital-based registry validated against an independent external population-based cohort; eligible participants met predefined diagnostic criteria for hypothyroidism with complete outcome data. Candidate predictors selected via regression-based approaches yielded a four-variable risk profile: age, serum creatinine (SCr), anion gap (AG), and diabetes mellitus (DM), reflecting vascular aging, renal vulnerability, and metabolic dysregulation. The model demonstrated robust discrimination, good calibration, and favorable clinical utility across training, testing, and external validation cohorts with consistent performance in subgroup analyses. A key limitation is the retrospective single-health-system derivation design, which may limit generalizability to Western or more ethnically diverse populations without further external validation.

Implications Medical Affairs : This validated prediction model represents a meaningful advance in precision endocrine medicine by enabling individualized cardiovascular risk stratification in hypothyroid patients — a population with elevated but often underappreciated hypertension risk. From a Medical Affairs standpoint, this tool supports identification of high-risk hypothyroid patients who might benefit from early antihypertensive therapy initiation, potentially expanding the addressable patient population for guideline-directed management. Pharmaceutical companies could engage endocrinology and cardiology KOLs to integrate such risk stratification tools into clinical pathways and patient registries, reinforcing the intersection of thyroid and cardiovascular care.

Cerebral microbleeds on susceptibility-weighted imaging, cognitive domains, and serum neurofilament light chain in adults with hypertension.

Revue : Frontiers in neurology | Tier : NA | IF estime : NA

Date de publication : 2026-09-03

Resultats principaux : This single-center cross-sectional study enrolled 173 hospitalized adults aged 31-95 years with primary hypertension who underwent brain MRI with susceptibility-weighted imaging, Montreal Cognitive Assessment (MoCA), and serum neurofilament light chain (sNFL) measurement to investigate cerebral microbleed (CMB) burden, location, and their associations with cognitive domains. The primary endpoints were the association between CMB burden/location and global and domain-specific cognition, and whether sNFL improved identification of mild cognitive impairment (MCI) beyond CMB count. CMBs were detected in 82 patients (47.4%) and MCI in 83 patients (48.0%), reflecting a high burden in this hypertensive cohort. Greater CMB burden was independently associated with MCI (adjusted OR: 4.53, 95% CI 1.95-10.55) and specifically with lower executive function, orientation, attention, and visuospatial scores after false-discovery-rate correction, but not with memory or language domains. For MCI identification, sNFL alone achieved AUC 0.794, CMB count alone 0.683, and the combined model 0.792, indicating that CMB count did not improve discrimination beyond sNFL alone (p=0.768), while the major limitation remains the cross-sectional single-center design requiring prospective validation before clinical application.

Implications Medical Affairs : These findings underscore hypertension as a significant contributor to cerebrovascular injury and cognitive decline, strengthening the rationale for aggressive blood pressure management as a neuroprotective strategy that could be incorporated into guideline updates on hypertension and cognitive outcomes. From a Medical Affairs perspective, sNFL emerges as a promising blood biomarker for monitoring cognitive risk in hypertensive patients, potentially serving as a target endpoint in future clinical trials of antihypertensive agents with neuroprotective claims. Engaging neurologist and hypertension KOLs around cognitive outcome measurement could support product differentiation narratives and label expansion strategies.

Exploring the factors associated with diabetes and hypertension complications among refugees living in Egypt.

Revue : Frontiers in public health | Tier : NA | IF estime : NA

Date de publication : 2026-09-03

Resultats principaux : This cross-sectional study enrolled 246 Syrian and Sudanese refugees diagnosed with type 2 diabetes and/or hypertension in Egypt (Cairo, Giza, Alexandria), recruited via convenience sampling through refugee-led organizations, assessing factors associated with self-reported disease complications. Unaffordability was the most frequently reported barrier to healthcare access and medication adherence across the sample. After adjustment for sociodemographic and care-access variables, prolonged displacement (AOR=1.16; 95% CI: 1.02-1.32; p=0.023) and lowest tertile of tangible social support (AOR=5.47; 95% CI: 1.26-23.67; p=0.023) were the strongest independent predictors of hypertension complications. Among diabetic participants, medication non-adherence (AOR=3.83; 95% CI: 1.01-14.53) and failure to access needed care (AOR=16.01; 95% CI: 3.04-84.27) predicted the highest odds of complications. Key limitations include the convenience sampling with only 246 participants, self-reported outcomes, wide confidence intervals in several variables, and cross-sectional design precluding causal inference.

Implications Medical Affairs : This study reveals the intersection of social determinants of health and NCD management in refugee populations, with medication adherence and care access as the most proximal modifiable drivers of hypertension complications — findings directly relevant to global health equity strategies. From a Medical Affairs perspective, these results reinforce the need for simplified, accessible treatment regimens and patient support programs specifically tailored to vulnerable and displaced populations with limited healthcare access. Pharmaceutical companies with global health commitments could leverage these insights to develop partnerships with humanitarian organizations supporting refugee health systems in the MENA region.

Comparative Efficacy of Aldosterone-Related Sodium-Retention Pathway Therapies for Resistant Hypertension: A Drug-Level Network Meta-Analysis of Randomized Controlled Trials.

Revue : Journal of clinical hypertension (Greenwich, Conn.) | Tier : Tier 3 | IF estime : ~4

Date de publication : 2026-09

Resultats principaux : This drug-level network meta-analysis (NMA) analyzed 10 randomized controlled trials (RCTs) including 2,865 participants with resistant hypertension (RH) to compare efficacy of aldosterone-related sodium-retention pathway therapies: mineralocorticoid receptor antagonists (MRAs), aldosterone synthase inhibitors (ASIs), and epithelial sodium channel (ENaC) blockade. The primary endpoint was change in office systolic blood pressure (SBP); secondary endpoints included office diastolic BP and 24-hour ambulatory SBP and diastolic BP. In drug-level analysis, amiloride (MD: -14.08 mmHg), spironolactone (MD: -10.78 mmHg), baxdrostat (MD: -9.09 mmHg), eplerenone (MD: -8.14 mmHg), and lorundrostat (MD: -6.80 mmHg) all significantly reduced office SBP versus placebo; osilodrostat showed a statistically uncertain effect (MD: -2.61 mmHg). At class level, both MRAs and ASIs showed clinically meaningful SBP reductions, with secondary outcomes broadly consistent with the primary analysis across ambulatory BP endpoints. Key limitations include a network of only 10 RCTs without systematic adverse event assessment, and unequal statistical power for head-to-head drug comparisons across the network.

Implications Medical Affairs : This NMA provides the most comprehensive drug-level comparison of aldosterone pathway-directed therapies for resistant hypertension to date, offering critical data to inform prescribing decisions and future guideline development for this difficult-to-treat segment. From a Medical Affairs perspective, the positioning of newer ASIs (baxdrostat, lorundrostat) alongside established MRAs creates targeted messaging opportunities around emerging second-line options within the RH treatment algorithm. This dataset should anchor KOL scientific exchange programs and advisory board discussions on the evolving therapeutic landscape for resistant hypertension, particularly as novel ASIs approach regulatory review milestones.

Seasonal variation in outcomes of diabetic patients with acute myocardial infarction: A retrospective cohort study.

Revue : American heart journal plus : cardiology research and practice | Tier : NA | IF estime : NA

Date de publication : 2026-09-08

Resultats principaux : This retrospective cohort study enrolled 3,838 patients admitted for acute myocardial infarction (AMI) who underwent successful percutaneous coronary intervention (PCI) at a single Chinese center, with a median follow-up of 36.7 months (IQR: 36.3-37.2) to evaluate the interaction between season of admission, diabetes status, and long-term cardiovascular outcomes. The composite primary endpoint included cardiovascular death, all-cause death, MI, stroke, unplanned revascularization, and cardiac hospitalization, adjusted for sex, age, triglycerides, LVEF, hypertension, LAD involvement, smoking, drinking, stroke, post-PCI TIMI 3 flow, LDL-C, and peripheral artery disease. AMI admissions were highest in spring; among diabetic patients, summer and autumn hospitalizations represented 33.4% and 31.2% of admissions respectively. After multivariate adjustment, non-diabetic patients hospitalized in summer (HR: 0.61, 95% CI: 0.39-0.97), fall (HR: 0.60, 95% CI: 0.37-0.97), and winter (HR: 0.61, 95% CI: 0.39-0.96) had significantly lower mortality risk compared to diabetic patients admitted in winter. Limitations include the single-center retrospective design in a Chinese population, limited generalizability, and absence of BP control or antihypertensive adjustment data across seasons.

Implications Medical Affairs : The heightened winter mortality risk in diabetic AMI patients — a population with high hypertension comorbidity — highlights cold-season vulnerability as a potential target for intensified cardiovascular risk management and more aggressive BP control during winter months. From a Medical Affairs perspective, seasonal risk stratification should be integrated into cardiovascular risk communication strategies and could support advocacy for enhanced monitoring protocols during colder periods. Future research should investigate whether optimized antihypertensive regimens with consistent 24-hour BP control can attenuate this seasonal excess mortality, a potential differentiation angle for long-acting antihypertensive agents.

Effectiveness of oral anticoagulants in precapillary pulmonary hypertension associated with systemic sclerosis: a EUSTAR cohort study.

Revue : Rheumatology (Oxford, England) | Tier : NA | IF estime : NA

Date de publication : 2026-09-18

Resultats principaux : This retrospective EUSTAR (European Scleroderma Trials and Research) registry cohort study included 614 patients with systemic sclerosis (SSc) and confirmed precapillary pulmonary hypertension (PH) via right heart catheterization, of whom 143 (23%) received oral anticoagulants (OAC) at the time of RHC. The primary endpoints were survival and PH worsening, assessed using Kaplan-Meier estimates, multivariable Cox regression, and propensity score matching, with OAC users showing worse baseline hemodynamic parameters. No significant association was found between OAC use and survival (HR: 0.983, 95% CI: 0.724-1.334) or PH worsening (HR: 1.070, 95% CI: 0.811-1.412). These null findings persisted after propensity score matching and in the subgroup of 230 SSc-PH patients without interstitial lung disease, demonstrating consistency across analytical approaches. Limitations include the retrospective observational design with residual confounding potential, relatively small subgroup analyses, and a predominantly European patient cohort limiting applicability to other racial/ethnic groups.

Implications Medical Affairs : This large EUSTAR registry study provides robust evidence against any clinical benefit of oral anticoagulation in SSc-associated precapillary pulmonary hypertension, challenging a previously controversial practice and potentially informing European guideline revisions on anticoagulation in this specific PH subtype. From a Medical Affairs perspective, these findings reinforce evidence-based treatment selection in rare PH subtypes and underscore the importance of registry-level real-world evidence in guideline-shaping for orphan disease segments. Pharmaceutical companies with PAH portfolios should monitor this evolving evidence base, as it may influence treatment sequencing and combination therapy decision-making in the SSc-PH patient segment.

Cardiometabolic and Inflammatory Predictors of Life-Threatening Ventricular Arrhythmias in Adults Without Structural Heart Diseases.

Revue : Journal of the American Heart Association | Tier : NA | IF estime : NA

Date de publication : 2026-09-18

Resultats principaux : This large prospective cohort study analyzed 423,186 UK Biobank participants, excluding those with structural heart disease at baseline or follow-up, to identify cardiometabolic and lifestyle determinants of life-threatening ventricular arrhythmias (LTVAs) over a median 13.3 years (IQR: 12.6-14.0 years), during which 1,116 LTVAs were recorded. The primary endpoint was incident LTVAs, defined as ventricular tachyarrhythmias, cardiac arrest, sudden cardiac death, and related resuscitation or ablation procedures, with risk estimated via multivariable Cox regression adjusted for age, sex, lifestyle, and cardiometabolic factors. Hypertension emerged as the strongest modifiable predictor of LTVAs (HR: 2.21, 95% CI: 1.90-2.57), followed by current smoking (HR: 1.78, 95% CI: 1.48-2.13) and male sex (HR: 1.67, 95% CI: 1.45-1.92). Additional risk factors included nonideal sleep, prior smoking, physical inactivity, older age, and elevated high-sensitivity CRP; LDL-C showed a modest inverse association (HR: 0.89, 95% CI: 0.82-0.97). Hypertension and age demonstrated the highest individual discrimination (concordance index: 0.615 and 0.629 respectively), although overall model discrimination was modest; key limitations include the predominantly White UK Biobank population and hospital-based LTVA ascertainment potentially underestimating community events.

Implications Medical Affairs : This study is among the largest to establish hypertension as the dominant modifiable risk factor for life-threatening ventricular arrhythmias in structurally normal hearts, strengthening the argument for sustained blood pressure control as a primary arrhythmia prevention strategy that extends the value of antihypertensive therapy beyond traditional cardiovascular endpoints. From a Medical Affairs perspective, these findings support expanding antihypertensive therapy value propositions to include sudden arrhythmic death prevention — a potentially underappreciated benefit in current product labeling and medical communications. Engaging electrophysiology and cardiac arrhythmia KOLs to highlight hypertension management as integral to LTVA prevention represents a compelling scientific leadership opportunity in guideline discussions.

Cardiopulmonary hospitalization risks from wildfire-specific and non-wildfire PM in 20 US states.

Revue : Nature communications | Tier : NA | IF estime : NA

Date de publication : 2026-09-17

Resultats principaux : This population-based epidemiological study employed a self-controlled design across 20 US states over 14 years (2006-2019) to examine long-term associations between 2-year average wildfire-specific and non-wildfire fine particulate matter (PM2.5) concentrations and cardiopulmonary hospitalization risks, addressing a growing public health concern as wildfire activity intensifies. Per 1 µg/m3 increase, wildfire-specific PM2.5 was associated with significantly higher hospitalization risks across all cardiopulmonary diseases, ranging from 10% for heart failure to 16% for asthma, while non-wildfire PM2.5 showed smaller risk elevations from 4.7% for COPD to 8.5% for hypertension. Subgroup analyses consistently demonstrated stronger associations for both PM2.5 types among minorities, metropolitan residents, those with fewer years of education, and more deprived communities — highlighting marked socioeconomic disparities in environmental health burden. Overall, wildfire-specific PM2.5 posed greater cardiopulmonary hospitalization risks than non-wildfire PM2.5 at equivalent concentrations, underscoring wildfire smoke as a disproportionately toxic cardiopulmonary hazard. Key limitations include the ecological state-level exposure estimation potentially masking local concentration variations, and the study period predating recent escalation in western US wildfire activity, potentially underestimating the current health burden.

Implications Medical Affairs : This study establishes wildfire smoke as a disproportionately toxic cardiovascular risk factor relative to conventional air pollution, with hypertension among the diseases showing meaningful risk elevation from environmental PM2.5 exposure — providing novel environmental evidence for hypertension's multifactorial etiology. From a Medical Affairs perspective, these findings support developing scientific narratives around environmental and climate-related drivers of hypertension, potentially informing patient education materials and advocacy for stronger air quality regulations as part of population-level BP management. Engaging environmental health, cardiology, and public health KOLs around the interplay between climate change, wildfire smoke, and hypertension risk represents an emerging area for scientific leadership and future research partnerships.

Associations between accelerometer-measured physical activity, sedentary behaviour and pericardial adiposity: a prospective cohort study of UK Biobank participants.

Revue : BMJ public health | Tier : NA | IF estime : NA

Date de publication : 2026-09-12

Resultats principaux : This prospective cohort study included 16,685 UK Biobank participants with cardiac MRI and available wrist-worn triaxial accelerometer data to examine associations between objectively measured physical activity (PA) intensity and epicardial/pericardial adipose tissue (EPAT) area — a cardiovascular risk factor closely linked to hypertension and metabolic syndrome. PA across all intensities was negatively correlated with EPAT area; participants in the highest PA quartile demonstrated significantly smaller EPAT areas versus the lowest: relative mean reductions of 21.29% for total PA, 11.24% for light-intensity PA, 18.28% for moderate-intensity PA, and 19.10% for vigorous-intensity PA (all p trend ≤0.001). Conversely, prolonged sedentary behavior (SB) showed a positive correlation with EPAT area, with Q4 SB participants having 15.90% greater relative EPAT area versus Q1 (p trend <0.001). Mediation analysis indicated visceral adipose tissue (VAT) largely mediated the PA-EPAT relationship; stratified analyses confirmed associations persisted among individuals with hypertension, diabetes, atherosclerotic CVD, or hyperlipidemia. Limitations include the observational design, single PA measurement time point, and predominantly White UK Biobank cohort limiting generalizability to ethnically diverse populations.

Implications Medical Affairs : This large-scale study provides robust evidence that any PA intensity — even light activity — reduces pericardial adiposity, reinforcing lifestyle modification as a biologically meaningful complement to pharmacological antihypertensive therapy with measurable cardiac structural benefits. From a Medical Affairs perspective, these data strengthen the scientific rationale for recommending structured PA programs alongside antihypertensive treatment, particularly in obese and metabolically at-risk hypertensive patients where pericardial fat may amplify cardiovascular risk. KOL engagement with preventive cardiology and exercise medicine specialists could leverage these findings to advocate for PA assessment in hypertension management guidelines and to support branded patient support program development.

Predictive nomogram for abdominal aortic calcification risk: a multi-algorithm approach integrating demographic and clinical variables.

Revue : Journal of cardiothoracic surgery | Tier : NA | IF estime : NA

Date de publication : 2026-07-30

Resultats principaux : This cross-sectional study utilized NHANES 2013-2014 cohort data (n=3,140; training n=2,198, validation n=942) to develop and validate a clinical nomogram predicting abdominal aortic calcification (AAC) risk assessed by dual-energy X-ray absorptiometry (DXA) using the Kauppila scoring system, applying three complementary variable selection methods: LASSO regression, best subsets regression (BSR), and the Boruta algorithm. Five consensus predictors were identified: age, BMI, cardiovascular disease (CVD), hypertension, and smoking status. The nomogram demonstrated AUCs of 0.747 (95% CI: 0.724-0.770) in training and 0.700 (95% CI: 0.660-0.735) in validation cohorts, with calibration curves showing strong agreement between predicted and observed probabilities. Decision curve analysis confirmed clinical utility across a range of risk thresholds, supporting practical application in resource-limited settings. Key limitations include the cross-sectional NHANES design precluding assessment of AAC progression over time, a single survey cycle dataset (2013-2014), and absence of external validation in non-US populations.

Implications Medical Affairs : This validated nomogram identifies hypertension as a critical predictor of abdominal aortic calcification risk alongside age, BMI, CVD, and smoking, reinforcing blood pressure control's role in preventing downstream vascular calcification and its associated cardiovascular morbidity. From a Medical Affairs perspective, integrating such risk stratification tools into hypertension clinics could identify high-risk patients warranting more aggressive antihypertensive treatment and support patient selection for future clinical programs targeting vascular calcification as an endpoint. Collaboration with vascular surgery and preventive cardiology KOLs to implement AAC risk nomograms in routine BP management represents an opportunity to expand the value narrative of antihypertensive therapy beyond traditional MACE endpoints.
