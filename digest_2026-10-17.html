A baseline cross-sectional survey of women's and household-level health indicators in Tsakare Community, Mount Darwin District, Zimbabwe: findings to inform targeted public health interventions.

Revue : Global Health Action | Tier : NA | IF estime : NA

Date de publication : 2026-10-16

Resultats principaux : This baseline cross-sectional survey was conducted at Tsakare Community, Mount Darwin District, Zimbabwe, enrolling 401 women randomly selected through a structured questionnaire under the University of Zimbabwe's Education 5.0 initiative to generate foundational evidence for targeted public health interventions. The study described sociodemographic characteristics, maternal and child health, sexual and reproductive health, HIV awareness, chronic disease literacy, and household management of childhood illnesses. Key findings showed that only 41.7% of women recognized severe headaches as a symptom of hypertension, while 17.1% had elevated blood pressure on measurement, revealing a substantial detection gap. For diabetes awareness, 53.4% recognized fatigue as a symptom but only 11.8% identified genetic predisposition as a risk factor, with moderate but incomplete HIV knowledge also observed. Notable limitations include the descriptive cross-sectional design precluding causal inference, geographic restriction to a single rural Zimbabwean community, exclusion of male household members, and reliance on self-reported data without clinical confirmation of disease prevalence.

Implications Medical Affairs : This study reveals critical health literacy deficits in rural sub-Saharan Africa where hypertension affects 17.1% of surveyed women yet symptom recognition remains below 50%, highlighting substantial unmet needs for community awareness campaigns in low-income settings relevant to pharmaceutical access strategies. From a Medical Affairs standpoint, these epidemiological gaps in rural Zimbabwe represent an opportunity for companies to partner with academic institutions and health ministries in NCD screening and education programs, building a presence in emerging African markets while supporting patient identification pipelines. Strategic KOL engagement with the University of Zimbabwe Faculty of Medicine and national health authorities could position the company as a committed partner in expanding hypertension prevention and treatment access across sub-Saharan Africa.

Age-stratified prevalence and associated risk factors of hypertension, diabetes and hypercholesterolemia in Nepal: Insights from the STEPS survey 2019.

Revue : PloS one | Tier : NA | IF estime : NA

Date de publication : 2026-10-16

Resultats principaux : This analysis used nationally representative cross-sectional data from Nepal's 2019 STEPS Survey including 3,219 participants aged 15 to 69 years (mean age 35.3 +/- 12.8 years; 56% female) to characterize age-stratified prevalence of hypertension, diabetes, and hypercholesterolemia alongside associated risk factors. Multivariable logistic regression revealed that men had significantly higher odds of hypertension compared to women (OR 2.04; 95% CI 1.62-2.55), while participants aged 60 years or older had nearly fivefold higher odds (OR 4.92; 95% CI 3.35-7.22). Diabetes peaked in the 45-59 age group (OR 6.34; 95% CI 3.58-11.23 compared to the youngest group), and hypercholesterolemia also followed an age-related gradient (OR 2.66; 95% CI 1.77-3.98). Physical inactivity was identified in 18.9% of the population overall, and stress affected 80.1% of adults aged 30-44 years, the highest rate of any age group. Limitations include the cross-sectional design preventing causal inference, restriction to participants aged 15-69 years excluding those above that range, potential underestimation of physical inactivity due to measurement constraints, and single-country data limiting global generalizability.

Implications Medical Affairs : The near-fivefold increase in hypertension odds for Nepalese adults aged 60 and above, alongside substantial burdens in younger adults, documents a rapidly expanding treatment-eligible population in a low- and middle-income South Asian country that represents a priority market development opportunity for antihypertensive therapies. Medical Affairs teams can leverage these national STEPS survey data to engage with the Nepalese Ministry of Health and provincial health authorities, framing hypertension as a high-priority NCD requiring accelerated screening and treatment guidelines. The age-sex interaction data provide a compelling scientific narrative for KOL educational programs targeting primary care physicians and cardiologists in Nepal and neighboring South Asian countries facing similar epidemiological transitions.

Geographic Access to Primary Care and Racial and Ethnic Disparities in the Prevalence of Maternal Chronic Conditions 2020-2024.

Revue : Preventing chronic disease | Tier : NA | IF estime : NA

Date de publication : 2026-10-15

Resultats principaux : This ecological study linked 2020-2024 US natality data with Health Resources and Services Administration Area Health Resource Files, applying spatial Bayesian conditional autoregressive models to estimate county-level associations between geographic primary care access and prevalence of maternal chronic conditions—obesity, hypertension, and diabetes—across 1,374 counties in the contiguous United States. Residing in a county designated as a primary care Health Professional Shortage Area (HPSA) was associated with modestly increased prevalence of obesity and hypertension, but not diabetes. The disparity in obesity prevalence between non-Hispanic Black and non-Hispanic White women was 11.3 per 100 births (95% CI 10.5-12.0) in HPSA counties versus 9.9 per 100 births (95% CI 9.5-10.4) in non-HPSA counties, with the adjusted beta for the difference in rate differences being non-significant at 0.005 (95% CI -0.005 to 0.014). Geographic access to primary care did not significantly attenuate racial and ethnic disparities in hypertension or other chronic condition prevalence among women having a live birth across any model specification. Key limitations include the ecological design precluding individual-level causal inference, restriction to women with live births potentially introducing selection bias, and the inability to account for quality of care within accessed primary care settings.

Implications Medical Affairs : This study demonstrates that improving geographic access to primary care alone is insufficient to close persistent racial disparities in maternal hypertension prevalence, pointing to systemic social determinants of health as operative factors that pharmacological access programs must complement. From a Medical Affairs perspective, these findings reinforce that equity-focused patient support programs addressing structural barriers—such as affordability, cultural competency, and care navigation—are necessary alongside geographic access initiatives to achieve meaningful hypertension outcome improvements in diverse populations. Engagement with obstetric and cardiovascular KOLs in the United States, combined with patient advocacy groups representing minority populations, could position the company as a leader in addressing health equity dimensions of maternal hypertension management.

Combined SGLT-2 Inhibitor and Mineralocorticoid Receptor Antagonist Therapy: Effects on the Renin-Angiotensin-Aldosterone System in Heart Failure Treatment.

Revue : International journal of molecular sciences | Tier : NA | IF estime : NA

Date de publication : 2026-09-24

Resultats principaux : This single-center retrospective observational study enrolled 114 patients with heart failure admitted to the Emergency Medicine Unit of Policlinico Umberto I, Rome, between March and December 2025, stratified into three groups: initiation of combined SGLT2i and mineralocorticoid receptor antagonist (MRA) therapy (Group A), initiation of SGLT2i alone (Group B), or ongoing SGLT2i therapy for more than 12 months (Group C). Hypertension was the most prevalent comorbidity across all groups at 74%, followed by atrial fibrillation (49%), dyslipidemia (47%), and coronary artery disease (39%). Hemodynamic parameters, serum and 24-hour urinary electrolytes, and hormonal parameters were assessed at admission (T0), 7 days (T1), and 30 days (T2), with baseline 24-hour urinary glucose excretion significantly higher in Group C compared to Groups A and B. Groups A and B showed activation of the renin-angiotensin-aldosterone system (RAAS), while Group A exhibited increased 24-hour urinary sodium and chloride excretion alongside lower urinary albumin excretion compared to the other groups. Limitations include the retrospective single-center design, absence of randomization limiting causal inference, relatively small group sizes, lack of blinding, and the short 30-day follow-up interval potentially insufficient to capture longer-term cardiorenal outcomes.

Implications Medical Affairs : The finding that 74% of this heart failure cohort had hypertension as a comorbidity reinforces the central role of blood pressure management within cardiorenal treatment pathways, and the favorable natriuretic and antiproteinuric effects of combined SGLT2i plus MRA provide mechanistic rationale for considering dual therapy as part of integrated hypertension-heart failure management. Medical Affairs teams should position these cardiorenal interaction data in scientific communication strategies emphasizing that mineralocorticoid receptor antagonists—when combined with SGLT2 inhibitors—may offer additive benefits beyond diuresis in hypertensive heart failure patients, particularly through RAAS modulation and renoprotection. Future randomized, prospective studies comparing SGLT2i plus MRA versus SGLT2i monotherapy in hypertensive heart failure patients are warranted and represent an actionable investigator-initiated research opportunity for KOL partnership.

Chios Mastic in Well-Controlled Hypertension: Findings From the HYPER-MASTIC Randomized Controlled Trial.

Revue : Pharmacology research & perspectives | Tier : NA | IF estime : NA

Date de publication : 2026-12 (online ahead of print)

Resultats principaux : The HYPER-MASTIC trial was a double-blind, randomized, placebo-controlled study enrolling 106 adults with stable, well-controlled hypertension and variable cardiovascular risk (assessed by SCORE2) who received low-dose Chios mastic (1500 mg/day), high-dose Chios mastic (2800 mg/day), or placebo for 3 months, with primary endpoints of change in 24-hour ambulatory systolic and diastolic blood pressure. No significant between-group differences were observed in 24-hour systolic or diastolic blood pressure after intervention, while dose-dependent plasma increases in masticadienonic and isomasticadienonic acids confirmed adequate adherence and treatment delivery. Oxidative stress markers and proteasome activity in peripheral blood mononuclear cells and red blood cells remained unchanged across all groups throughout the study period. In a predefined high-risk subgroup, transcriptomic profiling revealed distinct changes in pathways related to endothelial junction organization and vascular integrity, suggesting biologically plausible vascular effects selectively in higher-risk individuals. Limitations include the small sample size of 106 participants, the 3-month intervention duration which may be insufficient to detect slower-acting vascular benefits, enrollment restricted to well-controlled and predominantly low-risk hypertensive patients creating a ceiling effect, absence of a positive active comparator arm, and the exploratory nature of the transcriptomic subgroup analysis.

Implications Medical Affairs : The null primary endpoint in well-controlled hypertensive patients is best interpreted as a ceiling effect rather than a lack of biological activity, an important methodological consideration that Medical Affairs teams should communicate when evaluating nutraceutical and complementary therapy claims in hypertension management. The transcriptomic signals in high-risk subgroups indicate that Chios mastic may warrant further investigation as an adjunct in patients with residual cardiovascular risk despite blood pressure control, providing a rationale for future investigator-initiated trials in higher-risk hypertensive populations. This RCT also illustrates the growing rigorous scientific interest in nutraceuticals as adjuncts to established antihypertensive pharmacotherapy, a trend Medical Affairs should monitor proactively in anticipation of potential guideline commentary on complementary approaches to residual cardiovascular risk reduction.

Association Between Diet Quality and 20-Year Heart Failure Incidence: Findings from the ATTICA Cohort Study (2002-2022).

Revue : Nutrients | Tier : NA | IF estime : NA

Date de publication : 2026-10-06

Resultats principaux : This population-based prospective cohort study from the ATTICA cohort in Greece enrolled 3,042 adults free of cardiovascular disease at baseline (2001-2002), with 20-year heart failure follow-up data available for 2,159 participants in 2022, among whom 241 (11.2%) had developed incident heart failure over the study period. Diet quality was assessed at baseline using the Global Diet Quality Score (GDQS) derived from a validated food-frequency questionnaire, and HF incidence was 20.7%, 5.1%, and 6.6% among participants with low, intermediate, and high diet quality respectively (p less than 0.001). In fully adjusted logistic regression models including hypertension as a covariate (n=1,973), better overall diet quality was independently associated with lower odds of incident HF per 1-point GDQS increment (OR 0.97; 95% CI 0.94-0.99), representing a modest but statistically significant protective association. Greater consumption of healthy food groups was independently inversely associated with HF incidence (OR 0.96; 95% CI 0.93-0.98), while the unhealthy food component showed no independent association (OR 1.04; 95% CI 0.96-1.13). Notable limitations include the observational design precluding causal inference, potential dietary recall bias from self-reported food frequency questionnaires, attrition at 20-year follow-up with only 71% of the original cohort providing outcome data, and restriction to a Greek Mediterranean population limiting generalizability to other dietary cultures.

Implications Medical Affairs : The 20-year prospective design provides compelling evidence that diet quality independently reduces heart failure incidence even after controlling for hypertension, reinforcing that dietary counseling is an essential complementary strategy alongside antihypertensive pharmacotherapy in cardiovascular risk reduction that Medical Affairs teams should incorporate into clinical education programs. These data support framing antihypertensive products within broader cardiometabolic risk reduction narratives where dietary quality acts as a long-term co-modifier of cardiovascular outcomes, strengthening the value proposition of integrated prevention strategies over 20-year time horizons. KOL collaborations with leading cardiologists and nutrition experts in Mediterranean countries represent a targeted engagement opportunity to generate regional guideline endorsements for combined dietary and pharmacological cardiovascular prevention approaches.

Diet, Obesity and Cardiovascular Health: Molecular Mechanisms, Clinical Evidence and Future Perspectives.

Revue : Journal of clinical medicine | Tier : NA | IF estime : NA

Date de publication : 2026-09-24

Resultats principaux : This comprehensive narrative review synthesized evidence from epidemiological studies, landmark randomized controlled trials, prospective cohort studies, meta-analyses, and mechanistic investigations to critically examine the relationships between dietary patterns, obesity, and cardiovascular disease including hypertension across multiple biological pathways. The Mediterranean diet was identified as having the most extensive randomized evidence base for cardiovascular prevention, with trials demonstrating reductions in major adverse cardiovascular events, while DASH, plant-based, and Nordic dietary patterns consistently improve intermediate cardiometabolic outcomes including blood pressure reduction, though with less robust evidence for hard cardiovascular endpoints. Mechanistic pathways linking obesity and unhealthy diet to cardiovascular disease include adipose tissue dysfunction, chronic low-grade inflammation, oxidative stress, endothelial impairment, mitochondrial dysfunction, and gut microbiota dysbiosis acting through interconnected metabolic and molecular networks. Western dietary patterns characterized by high ultra-processed food consumption are associated with visceral adiposity, insulin resistance, vascular inflammation, mitochondrial dysfunction, endothelial injury, and increased cardiovascular risk. Limitations include the narrative design introducing potential selection bias, heterogeneity in dietary definitions and endpoint ascertainment across included primary studies, and variability in the quality and duration of the underlying trials.

Implications Medical Affairs : This synthesis provides a robust and mechanistically grounded scientific foundation supporting the positioning of Mediterranean and DASH dietary patterns as evidence-based lifestyle interventions complementary to antihypertensive pharmacotherapy, which Medical Affairs teams should integrate into physician education and patient engagement materials. The highlighted emerging pathways—particularly gut microbiota dysbiosis, endothelial dysfunction, and mitochondrial impairment—represent scientifically credible targets that pharmaceutical companies should monitor for pipeline relevance, potential biomarker development, and future therapeutic combinations addressing residual cardiovascular risk beyond blood pressure normalization. Physician education programs that combine evidence-based dietary counseling with optimized pharmacotherapy represent a high-value Medical Affairs investment validated by the comprehensive evidence base assembled in this review.

Promoting the Mediterranean Diet in the Workplace: An Observational Cross-Sectional Study.

Revue : Nutrients | Tier : NA | IF estime : NA

Date de publication : 2026-09-23

Resultats principaux : This observational cross-sectional study enrolled 1,475 of 1,608 invited workers (91.7% participation rate) from 22 companies in Latium, Italy, undergoing routine occupational health surveillance, to investigate Mediterranean Diet adherence and its associations with worker health outcomes using the Mediterranean Diet Adherence Screener (MEDAS). The prevalence of high MEDAS score (indicating high adherence) was 53.8% (95% CI 51.3-56.4%), with significantly greater adherence among female workers, older workers, non-smokers, and those engaging in leisure-time physical activity. In multivariable logistic regression models adjusted for age, gender, smoking status, physical activity, night work, and occupational sector, higher MEDAS score was inversely associated with hypertension (adjusted OR 0.922; 95% CI 0.856-0.994), hypertriglyceridemia (aOR 0.919; 95% CI 0.844-0.999), and metabolic syndrome (aOR 0.911; 95% CI 0.831-0.999). The MEDAS score was also positively associated with self-reported happiness (aOR 1.074; 95% CI 1.017-1.134), suggesting psychosocial co-benefits of dietary adherence in working populations. Notable limitations include the cross-sectional design precluding causal inference, geographic restriction to a single Italian region limiting national and international generalizability, reliance on MEDAS self-report for dietary assessment, modest absolute effect sizes acknowledged by the authors, and potential healthy worker bias inherent to occupational cohort studies.

Implications Medical Affairs : The inverse association between Mediterranean Diet adherence and hypertension in a large working-age Italian cohort, even with modest effect sizes, provides real-world evidence supporting the workplace as an underutilized channel for integrated hypertension prevention programs that combine dietary promotion with pharmacological management. Medical Affairs teams can leverage these occupational health data to engage with corporate wellness programs, occupational medicine societies, and employer health benefit administrators, positioning antihypertensive products within integrated cardiometabolic risk management initiatives targeting working-age adults. The simultaneous protective associations with hypertension, metabolic syndrome, and hypertriglyceridemia reinforce holistic cardiovascular risk reduction messaging strategies relevant for primary care and occupational health KOL engagement across European markets.

Sugar-Sweetened Beverages and Childhood Obesity: A Life-Course Perspective.

Revue : Nutrients | Tier : NA | IF estime : NA

Date de publication : 2026-09-22

Resultats principaux : This narrative review examined sugar-sweetened beverage (SSB) consumption and adiposity across the life course with particular focus on the first 1000 days as a sensitive developmental period, using targeted literature searches from PubMed, Scopus, and Web of Science covering January 2021 to August 2026 and prioritizing systematic reviews, meta-analyses, prospective cohort studies, randomized trials, and mechanistically informative investigations. SSB intake was consistently associated with higher body mass index, waist circumference, body fat percentage, and pediatric obesity across multiple study designs and diverse populations, with potential mechanistic pathways including fructose-driven hepatic de novo lipogenesis, incomplete energy compensation for liquid calories, and insulin resistance—though independent fructose contributions remain difficult to disentangle from overall excess energy intake. Downstream metabolic consequences of SSB-related obesity include dyslipidemia, hypertension, and metabolic dysfunction-associated steatotic liver disease (MASLD), establishing SSBs as upstream dietary determinants of hypertension risk originating in early life. Prevention strategies evaluated include family- and school-based behavioral interventions, beverage reformulation, and fiscal policies such as sugar taxes, with evidence favoring structural and environmental approaches over individual-level behavioral interventions. Key limitations include the narrative design with purposive rather than systematic literature selection introducing potential bias, variability in primary study quality and definitions of SSB intake, difficulty isolating SSB effects from overall dietary patterns, and heterogeneous outcome ascertainment methods across included studies.

Implications Medical Affairs : The identification of hypertension as a downstream metabolic consequence of SSB-driven childhood obesity establishes early-life dietary exposures as modifiable upstream determinants of lifelong hypertension risk, providing a scientific basis for Medical Affairs strategies targeting prevention in pediatric populations and their healthcare providers. Evidence supporting structural policy interventions—sugar taxes, beverage reformulation, school-based restrictions—alongside pharmacological management of established hypertension frames an advocacy opportunity for Medical Affairs teams engaged with public health policymakers, payors, and regulatory bodies seeking integrated prevention frameworks. Longitudinal cohort studies tracking SSB-exposed children into adulthood to quantify attributable hypertension burden and treatment onset age would generate evidence directly informing risk stratification models relevant to pharmaceutical market access forecasting and health technology assessment submissions.
