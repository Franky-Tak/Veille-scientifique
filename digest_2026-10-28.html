Evaluating the cost-effectiveness of enhanced cardiovascular-renal-metabolic (CVRM) screening in UK, France, Germany and Australia: a microsimulation-based enhanced CVRM screening analysis.

Revue : Journal of medical economics | Tier : NA | IF estime : NA

Date de publication : 2026-10-27

Resultats principaux : This multinational microsimulation study evaluated the 20-year clinical and economic impact of enhanced cardiovascular-renal-metabolic (CVRM) screening — covering hypertension, type 2 diabetes, chronic kidney disease, dyslipidemia, obesity, and heart failure — compared with standard of care in the UK, France, Germany, and Australia. A patient-level discrete-time model was calibrated to country-specific demographic and epidemiological profiles for adults aged 35 years and older, incorporating multimorbidity interactions across six conditions. Over 20 years, enhanced screening was associated with mortality reductions ranging from 109,863 in Australia to 467,297 in Germany, and corresponding life-years gained from 640,870 to 2,803,783. The strategy was projected to be cost-saving in all four countries, with total savings ranging from $368.9 million (Australia) to $46.5 billion (Germany) from both payer and societal perspectives. Additional benefits included fewer hospital bed-days, lower productivity losses, and reduced CO2 emissions; however, uncertainty was assessed through deterministic rather than probabilistic sensitivity analysis, which is an important methodological limitation.

Implications Medical Affairs : This study provides a strong economic rationale for national CVRM screening programs, representing a compelling value story for pharmaceutical companies with antihypertensive portfolios embedded in broader cardiometabolic disease management. From a Medical Affairs perspective, this evidence can support market access dossiers for antihypertensives positioned within integrated CVRM pathways, particularly in European and Australian markets. Engaging health-economics KOLs and preventive cardiology leaders with these data should be a near-term priority to shape reimbursement discussions and guideline adoption.

Adjusting for Medication Use in GWAS and Its Impact on Mendelian Randomisation Analyses: An Example of Systolic Blood Pressure in UK Biobank.

Revue : Genetic epidemiology | Tier : NA | IF estime : NA

Date de publication : 2026-12

Resultats principaux : This methodological GWAS study in 407,960 White British UK Biobank participants compared seven approaches for adjusting antihypertensive medication use when analyzing systolic blood pressure (SBP). Direct SBP adjustments — particularly adding class-specific constants to measured values — yielded a greater number of genome-wide significant loci and stronger effect estimates compared to unadjusted SBP, while restriction methods (limiting analysis to untreated individuals) reduced statistical power due to smaller sample sizes. Treated individuals showed deflated SBP effect estimates relative to untreated individuals, demonstrating the importance of medication correction. In Mendelian randomization analyses, no substantial differences were observed in inverse-variance weighted estimates for SBP on coronary artery disease across adjustment methods; however, larger variations emerged for the causal effect of BMI on SBP. The class-specific constant adjustment also revealed potentially novel genetic loci, offering additional biological insight into blood pressure regulation.

Implications Medical Affairs : These methodological findings are critical for interpreting large-scale GWAS results used in pharmacogenomics and Mendelian randomization studies relevant to antihypertensive drug development. The demonstration that class-specific medication adjustment unmasks the strongest SBP genetic signals supports more rigorous analytical standards in genetic epidemiology studies that inform drug target validation. Medical Affairs teams should monitor pharmacogenomics publications leveraging UK Biobank to ensure study quality and correct interpretation of antihypertensive class effects in genetic analyses.

Stroke knowledge and intended care-seeking behavior among adults with hypertension and type 2 diabetes: A cross-sectional study.

Revue : PloS one | Tier : NA | IF estime : NA

Date de publication : 2026-10-27

Resultats principaux : This cross-sectional study conducted between October 2023 and April 2024 enrolled 5,402 adults with hypertension and/or type 2 diabetes in Bogota, Colombia, via a structured questionnaire administered by telephone or in person. While most participants recognized that stroke can cause disability (89.6%) and death (91.1%), 32.5% reported no knowledge of specific stroke symptoms, and 26.6% stated they would not activate emergency medical services in response to a suspected stroke. Limited symptom knowledge was independently associated with male sex (OR 1.26, 95% CI 1.12-1.42), low educational attainment (OR 1.59, 95% CI 1.38-1.82), and low socioeconomic status (OR 2.20, 95% CI 1.85-2.60). Approximately 52% of respondents reported they would schedule an outpatient appointment rather than seek emergency care, highlighting critical gaps in intended care-seeking behavior. The non-probabilistic sampling strategy and reliance on self-reported data are notable limitations of this study.

Implications Medical Affairs : This study highlights a critical public health gap in stroke health literacy among hypertensive patients in Latin America, a region of growing strategic importance for cardiovascular pharmaceuticals. Pharmaceutical companies with antihypertensive and stroke prevention portfolios can leverage these findings to support patient education campaigns and awareness initiatives in emerging markets. These data strengthen the case for KOL partnerships with neurologists and public health specialists in Colombia to address stroke literacy deficits in high-risk populations.

Measurement error adjusted prevalence of undiagnosed hypertension in England: A cross-sectional study.

Revue : PloS one | Tier : NA | IF estime : NA

Date de publication : 2026-10-27

Resultats principaux : This cross-sectional study analyzed individual-level data from the 2017-2019 Health Survey for England (HSE) among adults aged 40 years and older without a prior hypertension diagnosis, applying the validated PROOF-BP algorithm to estimate out-of-office blood pressure from clinic measurements. The observed prevalence of undiagnosed hypertension was 27.7% (95% CI 26.4%-29.1%), whereas the predicted out-of-office prevalence was 21.8% (95% CI 20.6%-23.0%), representing a reduction after accounting for white-coat effect and measurement variability. Prevalence was nearly double in males (29.8%) compared with females (14.6%) and varied substantially by region, from 26.3% in the West Midlands to 16.9% in London. Extrapolation to the English population aged 40 and above estimates approximately 4.2 million individuals with undiagnosed hypertension, of whom over 2.9 million could be identified through current diagnostic pathways. The reliance on a statistical algorithm and cross-sectional design rather than 24-hour ambulatory monitoring represents a key methodological limitation.

Implications Medical Affairs : With an estimated 4.2 million undiagnosed hypertensive adults in England alone, this study quantifies a massive unmet diagnostic opportunity that directly supports the value of both case-finding programs and antihypertensive pharmacotherapies. The pronounced male sex gap in undiagnosis is particularly actionable for Medical Affairs strategy, suggesting targeted male-directed awareness campaigns and outreach to primary care physicians. These data provide a strong evidence-based foundation for health authority engagement in England and for positioning antihypertensive portfolios within NHS detection and treatment initiatives.

Comparative Effectiveness of Individual Angiotensin Receptor Blockers on Osteoporosis and Osteoporotic Fracture Risk: A Nationwide Active-Comparator New-User Cohort Study.

Revue : Calcified tissue international | Tier : NA | IF estime : NA

Date de publication : 2026-10-27

Resultats principaux : This nationwide active-comparator new-user cohort study used Taiwan's Health and Welfare Data Science Center Longitudinal Generation Tracking Database to identify hypertensive patients aged 51-80 years who newly initiated telmisartan or another angiotensin receptor blocker (ARB) between 2002 and 2021; after 1:4 propensity score matching, a total of approximately 230,000 patient-years were analyzed across a mean follow-up of 4.35 years. Telmisartan use was associated with a significantly lower composite risk of osteoporosis or osteoporotic fracture compared with other ARBs (adjusted HR 0.65, 95% CI 0.53-0.79). In analyses of individual outcomes, telmisartan was associated with lower osteoporosis risk (aHR 0.60, 95% CI 0.43-0.84) and a markedly lower hip fracture risk (aHR 0.37, 95% CI 0.19-0.70). The secondary fracture-only analysis also favored telmisartan over other ARBs (aHR 0.71, 95% CI 0.57-0.90). The authors hypothesize that PPAR-gamma agonistic activity of telmisartan may mediate additional bone-protective effects, though the retrospective design and residual confounding remain important limitations.

Implications Medical Affairs : These findings demonstrate clinically relevant pharmacological heterogeneity within the ARB class, with telmisartan potentially offering a meaningful skeletal protective benefit beyond blood pressure reduction — a differentiated value proposition for Medical Affairs discussions with prescribers treating elderly hypertensive patients at fracture risk. From a product positioning perspective, this evidence could support expanded use narratives for telmisartan in patients with concurrent hypertension and osteoporosis risk, particularly as populations age. A dedicated KOL engagement initiative in geriatric cardiology and endocrinology, combined with an investigator-initiated study agenda to replicate these findings in Western populations, is warranted.

Projected obstetric risks in an aging maternal population in Taiwan: a Bayesian age-period-cohort analysis.

Revue : International journal of epidemiology | Tier : NA | IF estime : NA

Date de publication : 2026-10-14

Resultats principaux : This population-based Bayesian age-period-cohort study linked National Health Insurance and Birth Certificate Application databases in Taiwan, covering all singleton pregnancies from 2004 to 2021, and projected maternal and neonatal outcomes through 2050. During the study period, total pregnancies declined 25.3% while pregnancies among women aged 35 and older tripled (11.8% to 34.6%), reflecting pronounced demographic aging. Gestational hypertension (GHTN) prevalence rose from 0.4% to 2.1%, and gestational diabetes mellitus (GDM) from 2.0% to 9.3%. Implications Medical Affairs : The alarming projected 26.4% prevalence of gestational hypertension in Taiwan by 2050 signals a growing unmet medical need for antihypertensives safe and effective in pregnancy, with implications for drug development and market expansion strategies. Medical Affairs teams should flag these demographic projections to obstetric cardiology KOLs and payers to make the case for evidence-based treatment guidelines addressing gestational hypertension at scale. Companies with methyldopa, labetalol, or nifedipine franchises, as well as pipeline agents for pregnancy-safe hypertension management, should incorporate such epidemiological projections into their long-range strategic planning.

Morning Hypertension as a Factor Independently Associated with Renal Dysfunction in Hypertensive Patients with Chronic Kidney Disease.

Revue : Medicina (Kaunas, Lithuania) | Tier : NA | IF estime : NA

Date de publication : 2026-10-03

Resultats principaux : This cross-sectional study analyzed 132 hypertensive patients with chronic kidney disease (CKD) stages 1-5 who underwent 24-hour ambulatory blood pressure monitoring (ABPM), transthoracic echocardiography, and comprehensive biochemical evaluation. Morning hypertension, defined according to ESC 2024 guidelines, was present in 42.4% of patients, while nocturnal hypertension was identified in 78%. Patients with morning hypertension exhibited significantly lower eGFR and higher urine albumin-to-creatinine ratio (UACR) levels (both p < 0.001). In multivariable regression, morning hypertension was independently associated with lower eGFR (beta = -0.26, p = 0.002) after adjustment for covariates. Nocturnal hypertension was positively associated with interventricular septal diastolic diameter (IVSd; beta = 0.19, p = 0.024) and UACR, supporting its role in cardiac remodeling. The study's limitations include its single-center cross-sectional design and relatively small sample size.

Implications Medical Affairs : The independent association of morning hypertension with renal dysfunction in CKD patients highlights an opportunity for antihypertensives with demonstrated efficacy in controlling early-morning blood pressure surges, such as long-acting ARBs or CCBs, to be positioned as preferred options in this high-risk subgroup. Medical Affairs teams should leverage these data in KOL discussions on ABPM integration into CKD management and the selection of antihypertensive agents based on 24-hour BP profiles. Longitudinal prospective studies confirming the prognostic value of morning BP phenotyping in CKD would strengthen the evidence base for this patient-centered treatment approach.

Association between migraine and angiogenic biomarkers in women with suspected or confirmed hypertensive disorders of pregnancy.

Revue : Cephalalgia | Tier : NA | IF estime : NA

Date de publication : 2026-10-27

Resultats principaux : This retrospective cohort study evaluated 234 pregnant women with suspected or confirmed hypertensive disorders of pregnancy (HDP), of whom 91 had a history of migraine, examining sFlt-1, PlGF, and the sFlt-1/PlGF ratio measured during index pregnancy. No significant association was found between migraine and angiogenic biomarkers within any HDP diagnostic subgroup — preeclampsia/HELLP, gestational hypertension, or no HDP — in either unadjusted or adjusted linear regression analyses. In the pooled overall cohort analysis, migraine was associated with lower sFlt-1 levels and lower sFlt-1/PlGF ratios in unadjusted analysis; however, after adjustment, migraine was associated with higher log PlGF levels (estimate = 0.3, 95% CI 0.03-0.6) and lower sFlt-1/PlGF ratios (estimate = -0.5, 95% CI -1.0 to -0.08), suggesting migraine does not enhance angiogenic imbalance within HDP subgroups. The retrospective design, moderate sample size, and heterogeneous HDP diagnoses limit the conclusions.

Implications Medical Affairs : These findings suggest that migraine status does not require specific modification of sFlt-1/PlGF biomarker interpretation in the clinical workup of HDP, providing reassurance to clinicians and reinforcing current diagnostic thresholds for preeclampsia. For pharmaceutical companies developing or marketing angiogenic biomarker assays or treatments for preeclampsia, this data supports the use of standard sFlt-1/PlGF cut-offs regardless of migraine comorbidity. The pooled cohort finding of altered angiogenic balance associated with migraine history warrants prospective investigation, representing a potential niche research agenda for academic KOL collaboration.

Association between food insecurity and five cardiometabolic conditions in a national network of federally qualified health centers.

Revue : BMC public health | Tier : NA | IF estime : NA

Date de publication : 2026-07-31

Resultats principaux : This large cross-sectional analysis used electronic health record data from 419 federally qualified health center (FQHC) clinic sites across 20 US states between 2017 and 2021, including 182,499 adults who completed a validated 2-item food insecurity screener. Among participants, 10.1% screened positive for food insecurity; these individuals were older (mean 43.8 vs. 40.3 years), more likely to be Black (45.6% vs. 37.2%), and more likely to have Medicaid coverage. Food insecurity was independently associated with hypertension (aOR 1.11, 95% CI 1.07-1.16), dysglycemia (aOR 1.08, 95% CI 1.04-1.12), and cardiovascular disease (aOR 1.18, 95% CI 1.08-1.28), but not obesity or dyslipidemia. Among female patients, food insecurity was significantly associated with all five cardiometabolic conditions, suggesting a stronger sex-specific social determinant effect. The cross-sectional design precludes causal inference.

Implications Medical Affairs : This study reinforces the role of food insecurity as a significant social determinant of hypertension in underserved US populations, with implications for the design of comprehensive hypertension management programs that extend beyond pharmacotherapy to address social needs. Medical Affairs teams should engage primary care KOLs and health equity advocates to explore bundled interventions combining antihypertensive treatment with social prescribing in FQHC settings. The female-specific effect pattern provides an actionable subgroup rationale for gender-tailored cardiovascular prevention campaigns in low-income communities.

Clinical risk factors associated with coronary slow flow: a meta-analysis.

Revue : BMC cardiovascular disorders | Tier : NA | IF estime : NA

Date de publication : 2026-07-29

Resultats principaux : This systematic review and meta-analysis included 41 case-control studies published between January 2015 and December 2025 to investigate risk factors for coronary slow flow (CSF), a condition involving delayed coronary artery opacification without obstructive disease. Quantitative analysis identified hypertension as a strong risk factor (OR 2.32, 95% CI 1.27-4.26), alongside smoking (OR 1.21), higher BMI (OR 1.20), male sex (OR 1.51), elevated homocysteine (OR 1.55), elevated high-sensitivity CRP (OR 1.62), and higher uric acid (OR 1.90). Higher HDL-C levels were protective (OR 0.45, 95% CI 0.38-0.55). Extreme heterogeneity across included studies (I-squared 93.5-99.3%) and moderate-to-high residual heterogeneity in pooled analyses are key limitations. The meta-analysis was registered with PROSPERO (CRD420261334323).

Implications Medical Affairs : The identification of hypertension as having over a twofold odds of being associated with coronary slow flow underscores the systemic vascular consequences of poorly controlled blood pressure beyond macrovascular atherosclerosis. These findings can be incorporated into Medical Affairs educational materials highlighting the broad cardiovascular benefits of optimal blood pressure control, including protection against microvascular dysfunction. Engaging interventional cardiologists and imaging specialists as KOLs around the hypertension-CSF connection could open new prescriber segments for antihypertensive agents.

Effects of Two Rg3-Enriched Korean Ginseng Preparations on Blood Pressure in Adults with Early-Stage Hypertension: A Randomized, Double-Blind, Placebo-Controlled Trial.

Revue : Nutrients | Tier : NA | IF estime : NA

Date de publication : 2026-10-20

Resultats principaux : This 12-week randomized, double-blind, placebo-controlled trial enrolled 90 adults with early-stage hypertension (stage 1) and randomized them equally to vitamin C-treated white ginseng (VTWG), enzyme-treated red ginseng (ETRG), or placebo (30 per group), with both active arms providing approximately 40 mg/day of ginsenoside Rg3. From baseline to week 12, systolic BP (SBP) decreased by 7.27 ± 6.15 mmHg in the VTWG group and 9.86 ± 6.47 mmHg in the ETRG group, compared with an increase of 5.03 ± 6.52 mmHg in the placebo group (both adjusted p < 0.0001 versus placebo). Diastolic BP decreased by 5.27 and 6.32 mmHg in the VTWG and ETRG groups, respectively, versus 0.90 mmHg in the placebo group. Mean arterial pressure declined significantly in both active groups. Nitric oxide changes differed significantly only between ETRG and placebo (p = 0.017). No clinically meaningful safety concerns were identified. The small sample size (30 per group) and single-center design limit generalizability.

Implications Medical Affairs : Although ginsenoside Rg3 demonstrated statistically significant blood pressure reduction in early-stage hypertension, the small sample size and industry-sponsorship (BTGin Co., Ltd.) require cautious interpretation of clinical relevance, particularly against established first-line antihypertensives. Medical Affairs teams should monitor the integrative medicine landscape, as growing interest in nutraceutical alternatives may influence patient self-treatment behaviors and adherence to guideline-directed pharmacotherapy. Engagement with primary care KOLs to reinforce the evidence hierarchy — and the superior efficacy and safety track record of guideline-approved antihypertensives — remains an important strategic priority in markets with high nutraceutical uptake.

Changes in Nutrition and Physical Activity up to 4 Years Following a Hypertensive Pregnancy: A Blood Pressure Postpartum Longitudinal Sub-Study.

Revue : Nutrients | Tier : NA | IF estime : NA

Date de publication : 2026-10-20

Resultats principaux : This longitudinal sub-study of the Blood Pressure Postpartum (BP3) multicenter RCT followed 180 women with a history of hypertensive disorders of pregnancy (HDP) from 6-12 months to 3-4 years postpartum across Australia. Vegetable and fruit intake increased significantly over time (vegetable servings/day: 2.6 at 6 months to 4.2 at 3-4 years; fruit servings: 1.7 to 2.2; both p < 0.001), accompanied by some unfavorable dietary changes. The use of different dietary assessment instruments at different time points is a significant methodological limitation that affects dietary trend comparability. Findings suggest a complex, mixed lifestyle trajectory in this high-risk population.

Implications Medical Affairs : These longitudinal data highlight the paradox of improving lifestyle metrics in post-HDP women while psychological engagement with healthy behaviors deteriorates, underscoring that pharmacological and non-pharmacological approaches must be combined for long-term cardiovascular risk reduction after hypertensive pregnancy. Medical Affairs programs should support post-HDP cardiovascular screening pathways and patient engagement tools that maintain motivation over multi-year follow-up, representing an unmet need where digital health and patient support programs could complement antihypertensive pharmacotherapy. KOL engagement with maternal-fetal medicine specialists and obstetrician-gynecologists is essential to align post-HDP management standards with cardiovascular disease prevention objectives.

Revue : Medicina (Kaunas, Lithuania) | Tier : NA | IF estime : NA

Date de publication : 2026-10-20

Resultats principaux : This single-centre, prospective, randomized, single-blind exploratory crossover trial enrolled 22 permanently paced patients with sinus node dysfunction (SND) and hypertension (median age 76 years, 73% women) on antihypertensive medication, who completed two 8-week intervention periods at DDD 55 bpm and DDD 75 bpm separated by a 2-week washout. Central systolic BP, measured non-invasively with oscillometric cuff-based SphygmoCor XCEL, was significantly higher at DDD 55 bpm (mean 127.8 ± 13.4 mmHg) compared to DDD 75 bpm (121.3 ± 10.6 mmHg; p = 0.04), with a difference of approximately 6.5 mmHg. Implications Medical Affairs : This exploratory study introduces the clinically relevant hypothesis that pacing rate strategy affects central aortic blood pressure in elderly hypertensive patients with pacemakers, a growing patient population as device implantation increases with demographic aging. For Medical Affairs teams, this finding suggests a potential interaction between device programming and antihypertensive therapy efficacy that warrants rigorous investigation — an opportunity to initiate KOL-led research examining whether antihypertensive agents provide equivalent central BP reduction across different pacing strategies. Collaboration with electrophysiology and device KOLs on larger confirmatory trials would be strategically valuable for optimizing combined pharmacological-device management of hypertension in elderly patients.

The relationship between cardiometabolic disease and vertebral bone strength, mineral density, and mass in US adults.

Revue : Journal of the Endocrine Society | Tier : NA | IF estime : NA

Date de publication : 2026-10-26

Resultats principaux : This cross-sectional analysis of the CARDIA (Coronary Artery Risk Development in Young Adults) cohort examined 2,728 participants who underwent abdominal CT scans at year 25 (2010-2011), applying biomechanical CT analysis to assess vertebral bone strength, volumetric BMD, and bone mass. At least one cardiometabolic disease (CMD) component — abdominal obesity, diabetes, dyslipidemia, or hypertension — was present in 72.3% of men and 70.5% of women. Implications Medical Affairs : These findings support a growing evidence base linking cardiometabolic disease, including hypertension, to skeletal health impairment in men, which may inform integrated bone-cardiovascular risk management approaches. For Medical Affairs teams managing antihypertensive portfolios, this data reinforces the broader protective rationale for optimal blood pressure control as part of cardiometabolic risk reduction that extends to bone health outcomes. Engaging endocrinology and rheumatology KOLs alongside cardiology specialists could open new interdisciplinary conversations about comprehensive cardiometabolic management programs that benefit skeletal as well as vascular health.

A plant-based diet attenuates hypertension-induced renal and hepatic stress: an exploratory study.

Revue : Food & function | Tier : NA | IF estime : NA

Date de publication : 2026-10-27

Resultats principaux : This preclinical exploratory study used spontaneously hypertensive rats (SHRs) fed either a refined control diet or a plant-based diet (PBD) comprising 28% fruits, vegetables, nuts, and legumes from 4 weeks of age for 24 weeks, with normotensive Wistar-Kyoto rats serving as controls. Implications Medical Affairs : This exploratory preclinical study provides biological plausibility for plant-based dietary interventions as adjunctive strategies to reduce hypertension-induced organ damage, supporting the broader dietary modification narrative in hypertension guidelines. For Medical Affairs, these data can be incorporated into patient education materials promoting lifestyle modification alongside antihypertensive pharmacotherapy, reinforcing a holistic hypertension management approach. The translational value is currently limited, and the findings warrant confirmation in well-designed human dietary intervention studies before inclusion in KOL educational programs or guideline submissions.

Resultats principaux : This PRISMA-registered systematic review and meta-analysis (PROSPERO CRD420251032235) synthesized 36 unique datasets from Saudi Arabian studies of adults with type 2 diabetes (T2DM). Pooled prevalences were 24% for retinopathy (95% CI 18-30%), 16% for nephropathy (11-23%), 24% for neuropathy (16-34%), 10% for MI/CHD/CAD (6-15%), 5% for peripheral arterial disease, 10% for diabetic foot, and 3% for cerebrovascular stroke.
