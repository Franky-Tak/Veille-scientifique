[1] Association of Chuanhuang Patent Formula with prognosis in patients with acute kidney injury on chronic kidney disease: a retrospective cohort study.

Revue : Renal Failure | Tier : NA | IF estime : NA

Date de publication : 2026-08-13

Resultats principaux : This retrospective cohort study enrolled 205 adult patients with acute kidney injury superimposed on chronic kidney disease (AKI-on-CKD) admitted to a Traditional Chinese Medicine hospital in Shanghai between January 2016 and October 2025. After 1:1 propensity score matching, 81 patients per group were included, with the intervention group receiving Chuanhuang Patent Formula (CHPF) as adjunctive therapy. The primary composite endpoint—progression to CKD stage 5, initiation of maintenance renal replacement therapy, or all-cause mortality—was significantly lower in the CHPF group (HR = 0.56; 95% CI: 0.32–0.98; p = 0.041). Secondary outcomes showed more favorable renal function parameters in the CHPF group at both 2 and 4 weeks post-initiation. Subgroup analysis stratified by hypertension comorbidity revealed a more pronounced treatment benefit in hypertensive patients (p = 0.045), whereas no significant benefit was observed in non-hypertensive patients (p = 0.26), suggesting hypertension as a potential treatment-effect modifier; however, this exploratory finding should be interpreted with caution and requires prospective validation.

Implications Medical Affairs : The hypertension-stratified subgroup benefit of CHPF in AKI-on-CKD reinforces the importance of comorbid hypertension as a risk modifier in renal outcomes, which may support adjunctive therapy narratives in hypertensive CKD populations for Medical Affairs positioning. From a product perspective, this study highlights an underexplored intersection between blood pressure management and acute-on-chronic renal injury that could differentiate nephroprotective strategies in hypertensive patient subgroups. A well-powered prospective RCT targeting hypertensive patients is needed before this finding can inform KOL strategy or support guideline inclusion.

[2] Long-Term Persistence and Treatment Changes of Antihypertensive Therapy: A 5-Year Cohort Study.

Revue : Basic & Clinical Pharmacology & Toxicology | Tier : NA | IF estime : NA

Date de publication : 2026-09 (Epub)

Resultats principaux : This longitudinal cohort study used the IADB.nl pharmacy database in the Netherlands, including 22,027 adults (mean age 58.1 years; 51.1% women) who initiated antihypertensive therapy (AHT) between 2014 and 2018 following a 2-year washout period. Annual persistence declined from 88.8% in year 1 to 80.7% in year 5, with only 52.7% remaining persistent over the entire 5-year follow-up. Treatment modifications were most frequent in year 1 (53.5% of persistent patients underwent intensification, de-intensification, or switching) and declined thereafter, with the probability of remaining in the same treatment state rising to 0.86 in a Markov model. Female sex, initiation with combination therapy, and initiation on beta-blockers or calcium-channel blockers were independently associated with greater treatment change probability. Limitations include use of dispensing records as adherence proxy, absence of blood pressure outcome data, and potential residual confounding inherent to real-world database studies.

Implications Medical Affairs : The finding that nearly half of antihypertensive initiators are non-persistent at 5 years highlights a critical real-world gap in hypertension management, providing Medical Affairs with compelling evidence to support adherence optimization programs and fixed-dose combination positioning. The differential treatment change rates associated with beta-blockers and calcium-channel blockers offer actionable insight for therapeutic area strategy, informing congresses and prescriber communications around treatment stability and guideline-concordant sequencing. Engaging KOLs with these real-world persistence data can reinforce the importance of individualized regimen selection and support development of patient support programs targeting the critical first year of therapy.

[3] Blood pressure control after solid organ transplantation: opportunities for optimizing care.

Revue : Transplant International | Tier : NA | IF estime : NA

Date de publication : 2026-07-30

Resultats principaux : This cross-sectional analysis assessed blood pressure control at 1 year post-transplantation in 1,112 solid organ transplant recipients (SOTR; 39% female, mean age 57 ± 13 years) from the TransplantLines biobank in the Netherlands, encompassing heart, liver, lung, and kidney recipients. A striking 90% of SOTR had hypertension, and of these, 72% exhibited suboptimal control (SBP >130 mmHg or DBP >80 mmHg), including 20% who received no antihypertensive treatment despite elevated blood pressure. Suboptimal control rates were consistently high across all organ types (71–84%). In multivariable analysis, older age (OR = 1.03; 95% CI: 1.01–1.04), diabetes (OR = 1.99; 95% CI: 1.18–3.36), and higher cholesterol (OR = 1.23; 95% CI: 1.01–1.51) were independently associated with poor control. Even using a more lenient ≤140/90 mmHg threshold, suboptimal control affected 40% of hypertensive SOTR, confirming a substantial and persistent care gap in post-transplant management.

Implications Medical Affairs : The finding that 72% of post-transplant hypertensive patients have suboptimal blood pressure control under contemporary guidelines represents a major unmet medical need, offering Medical Affairs a compelling platform for KOL engagement with transplant nephrologists, cardiologists, and hepatologists around standardized antihypertensive protocols. For product positioning, the high prevalence of hypertension and poor control in immunosuppressed patients underscores the importance of tolerability and drug-drug interaction profiles, which are key differentiators for agents compatible with calcineurin inhibitors and other immunosuppressants. Initiating medical education programs and real-world evidence studies targeting the transplant care gap could generate differentiated data at ESOT, AST, and ERA congresses to support guideline advocacy.

[4] Quality of Life Among Young Adult Patients with Cardiovascular Disease.

Revue : Georgian Medical News | Tier : NA | IF estime : NA

Date de publication : 2026-05

Resultats principaux : This cross-sectional study assessed health-related quality of life (HRQoL) using the SF-36 questionnaire in 294 young adults (aged 25–44; mean age 38.4 ± 5.2 years; 64.3% women) diagnosed with arterial hypertension as the primary cardiovascular condition in Kazakhstan, with a subset having additional comorbidities including ischemic heart disease or chronic heart failure. Men reported significantly higher HRQoL scores than women across most SF-36 domains, particularly physical functioning, bodily pain, vitality, and social functioning (p < 0.05). Employment status was significantly associated with QoL: workers and entrepreneurs had the highest scores, while retirees and unemployed patients had the lowest (p < 0.05). Physical health component scores consistently exceeded mental health component scores across all groups, suggesting a proportionally greater mental health burden. No significant differences were found based on marital or educational status, and the cross-sectional design precludes causal inference.

Implications Medical Affairs : These findings from Kazakhstan underscore the disproportionate QoL burden experienced by young hypertensive women and unemployed patients, reinforcing the value of patient-reported outcome measures in clinical programs targeting working-age populations with hypertension. From a Medical Affairs perspective, QoL data from emerging markets expand the global evidence base that hypertension burden extends well beyond blood pressure numbers, supporting payer discussions and disease awareness campaigns around the societal cost of undertreated hypertension. Regional KOL engagement in Central Asia can leverage these data in medical education initiatives focused on the mental and functional health dimensions of hypertension in young patients.

Revue : Georgian Medical News | Tier : NA | IF estime : NA

Date de publication : 2026-05

Resultats principaux : This case-control study enrolled 200 patients with type 2 diabetes mellitus from Iraq (100 cases with diabetic foot; 100 controls without), with a mean age predominantly ≥60 years and a male-to-female ratio of 3:2. Multivariable logistic regression identified hypertension as a strong independent predictor of diabetic foot (OR = 8.99; p = 0.003), alongside HbA1c ≥7% (OR = 59.47; p < 0.001), diabetes duration ≥15 years (OR = 59.66; p < 0.001), and peripheral neuropathy (OR = 7.82; p = 0.008). Additional independent predictors included foot deformities (OR = 22.32), history of corns and calluses (OR = 51.67), lack of foot care awareness (OR = 4.51), unemployment (OR = 14.33), and not using emollients (OR = 21.36). Limitations include the convenience sampling method, single-site design, and the inherent inability of a case-control design to establish directionality of associations.

Implications Medical Affairs : The strong independent association between hypertension and diabetic foot (OR = 8.99) underscores the critical interplay between blood pressure dysregulation and microvascular and macrovascular complications in type 2 diabetes, reinforcing Medical Affairs narratives linking cardiovascular risk reduction to extremity outcomes. This evidence supports positioning comprehensive antihypertensive management as integral to the holistic care of high-risk diabetic patients, particularly in Middle Eastern and Central Asian regions with high comorbidity burdens. Engaging diabetology and internal medicine KOLs with these data at regional congresses can reinforce guideline-concordant blood pressure targets in diabetic patients as a strategy to prevent serious and costly complications.

[6] The effect of a family-centered empowerment model with traditional Chinese medicine health education on self-management in community-dwelling older adult hypertensive patients with a chance health locus of control.

Revue : Frontiers in Public Health | Tier : NA | IF estime : NA

Date de publication : 2026-07-30

Resultats principaux : This two-armed randomized controlled trial was conducted in Lanzhou, China from May 2023 to January 2024, enrolling 60 community-dwelling older adults with hypertension and a chance health locus of control (CHLC), using multistage sampling, with 58 completing 6-month follow-up. The experimental group received community nurse-led HTN education based on the Family-Centered Empowerment Model (FCEM) integrating Traditional Chinese Medicine principles, while the control group received standard general community HTN education. At 6 months, the FCEM group showed significant reductions in systolic blood pressure (t = -3.325; p = 0.002) and diastolic blood pressure (t = -5.110; p < 0.001), alongside significant improvements in internal health locus of control (t = 8.493; p < 0.001) and total self-management scores (t = 4.406; p < 0.001) compared to control. CHLC scale scores decreased significantly in the intervention group (t = -4.781; p < 0.05), while BMI reduction did not reach significance (t = -1.207; p = 0.232). Key limitations include the small sample size (n = 60), single-center setting, and restricted geographic generalizability to Lanzhou, China.

Implications Medical Affairs : This RCT provides evidence that culturally adapted, family-centered educational interventions can improve blood pressure control and self-management in older hypertensive patients with external health locus of control, a clinically relevant but underserved subgroup, representing an opportunity for Medical Affairs to support patient empowerment program design. From a product positioning perspective, these findings reinforce a holistic disease management narrative in which pharmacotherapy is most effective when paired with structured behavioral and educational support, which can be used to differentiate adherence-oriented companion programs in Asia-Pacific markets. Nurse-led empowerment models integrating family involvement can serve as a scalable framework for KOL-endorsed disease management initiatives at geriatric cardiology and community health congresses.

[7] Developmental origins of hypertension and renal injury: epigenetic memory in renal stem and progenitor cells.

Revue : Hypertension Research | Tier : NA | IF estime : NA

Date de publication : 2026-08-13

Resultats principaux : This mini-review synthesizes experimental and clinical evidence on the developmental origins of health and disease (DOHaD) framework applied to hypertension and renal injury, focusing on epigenetic regulation in renal stem and progenitor cells across the life course. The review proposes that adverse early-life environments—including fetal malnutrition, growth restriction, ischemia, and prematurity—program persistent epigenetic alterations in renal mesenchymal stem cells, label-retaining cells, and endothelial progenitor cells that impair nephron endowment, differentiation capacity, and intrarenal renin-angiotensin system regulation. Beyond reduced nephron number, metabolic programming via mitochondrial dysfunction and key metabolite alterations is proposed as an additional mechanism linking fetal adversity to adult hypertension and kidney disease. Postnatal amplifiers—high-salt diet, obesity, kidney injury, and aging—may interact with this developmental imprinting to accelerate hypertension onset and progression. The review discusses potential reversibility of epigenetic changes and highlights maternal nutrition, early surveillance, and lifestyle intervention as modifiable life-course windows.

Implications Medical Affairs : This review advances the mechanistic understanding of hypertension origins beyond classical risk factors toward fetal programming and epigenetic biology, providing Medical Affairs with a compelling scientific narrative for primary prevention positioning and early intervention research investment. The central role of intrarenal RAAS dysregulation in developmentally programmed hypertension reinforces the deep mechanistic relevance of RAAS-targeting antihypertensive agents even in early-onset disease, supporting scientific platform development and thought leadership at nephrology and cardiology forums. Longitudinal cohort studies examining RAAS modulation in developmentally at-risk populations—such as preterm infants and those with low birth weight—represent a high-impact opportunity for differentiated evidence generation and KOL engagement at perinatal and pediatric cardiovascular congresses.

[8] The association between the indication for antenatal fetal surveillance and an abnormal result: A retrospective cohort study.

Revue : Pregnancy (Hoboken) | Tier : NA | IF estime : NA

Date de publication : 2026-03-23

Resultats principaux : This retrospective cohort study analyzed 19,034 non-stress tests (NSTs) performed in 2,695 singleton high-risk pregnancies across four academic testing sites between January 2022 and May 2023, using Generalized Estimating Equation modeling to account for repeated measures per patient. Overall, only 0.4% of all tests yielded a non-reassuring result (non-reactive NST with BPP < 8/10), while the overall NST reactivity rate was 87.3%. Among 2,327 BPPs performed for non-reactive NSTs, 96.4% returned a passing score ≥8/10. Chronic hypertension (aOR = 1.5; 95% CI: 1.2–1.8) was significantly associated with NST non-reactivity alongside fetal growth restriction, obesity, and hypertensive disorders of pregnancy. However, only fetal growth restriction (aOR = 2.7), vaginal bleeding (aOR = 6.0), and post-term status (aOR = 8.6) were independently associated with BPP < 8. In single-indication analyses, chronic hypertension remained significantly associated with non-reactive NST (aOR = 1.5; 95% CI: 1.1–2.1). Limitations include retrospective design and restriction to an academic healthcare system.

Implications Medical Affairs : The independent association of chronic hypertension with non-reactive fetal NSTs reinforces hypertension's systemic impact on placental function and fetal well-being, providing Medical Affairs with evidence to engage maternal-fetal medicine specialists around the fetal risks of poorly controlled maternal hypertension during pregnancy. These data support the benefit narrative for safe, effective antihypertensive agents in pregnancy, particularly as fetal surveillance burden translates into clinical resource utilization and patient anxiety—outcomes that can be mitigated through optimal blood pressure management. Obstetric cardiologists and maternal-fetal medicine KOLs represent key stakeholders for Medical Affairs engagement at SMFM, ISSHP, and ESC-EORP pregnancy cardiology forums, especially as updated monitoring guidelines for hypertensive pregnancies continue to evolve.

[9] Associations of multiple dimensions of socioeconomic status with adverse pregnancy outcomes.

Revue : Pregnancy (Hoboken) | Tier : NA | IF estime : NA

Date de publication : 2025-09-30

Resultats principaux : This prospective observational cohort study from the Stress, Pregnancy, and Health (SPAN) cohort enrolled 592 participants who completed psychosocial and demographic assessments during the second and third trimesters, examining nine dimensions of socioeconomic status (SES) in relation to preterm birth (PTB) and hypertensive disorders of pregnancy (HDP). Among participants, 11.5% delivered preterm. Multiple SES dimensions were independently associated with HDP: current social position (RR: 1.43; 95% CI: 1.18–1.74), financial resources (RR: 1.28; 95% CI: 1.003–1.63), composite disadvantage (RR: 1.15; 95% CI: 1.04–1.28), subjective financial strain (RR: 1.19; 95% CI: 1.03–1.36), childhood SES (RR: 1.77; 95% CI: 1.29–2.43), and neighborhood SES (RR: 1.43; 95% CI: 1.24–1.67). Social position was the only SES measure associated with both PTB (RR: 1.37; 95% CI: 1.02–1.85) and HDP. Limitations include moderate sample size, potential SES measure misclassification, and residual confounding from unmeasured social variables.

Implications Medical Affairs : The broad and consistent association of multiple SES dimensions—including childhood socioeconomic conditions and neighborhood deprivation—with hypertensive disorders of pregnancy positions HDP firmly within a social determinants of health framework, with direct implications for Medical Affairs equity-focused disease management programs. These findings support the development of SES-sensitive access and adherence programs for antihypertensive therapy in pregnant populations, which could be highlighted in payer and health system discussions around value-based care in obstetrics. Engaging maternal-fetal medicine, public health, and health equity KOLs at SMFM, ACOG, and health equity research forums with these data can position the company as a leader in addressing socioeconomic disparities in pregnancy hypertension outcomes.
