Comorbidity Trajectories Before and After Hip Fracture in Elderly Patients: A Korean Nationwide Database Cohort Study.

Revue : Journal of Korean medical science | Tier : NA | IF estime : NA

Date de publication : 2026-08-31

Resultats principaux : This retrospective matched cohort study analyzed data from the Korean National Health Insurance Service-Senior cohort (2005-2016), including 8,319 elderly patients (age ≥65) with surgically treated hip fractures matched 1:2 with 16,638 controls by age, sex, and year. Hypertension was identified as the most prevalent comorbidity at the time of fracture, underscoring its ubiquity in the geriatric surgical population. Difference-in-differences (DID) analysis using generalized estimating equations revealed that comorbidity burden escalated more steeply in hip fracture patients compared to controls after the fracture event; notable post-fracture increases were observed for osteoporosis (DID ratio 1.19), dementia (1.17), Parkinson disease (1.11), congestive heart failure (1.09), and COPD (1.04), whereas diabetes (0.96) and asthma (0.96) showed relative decreases. No subgroup analyses specifically stratifying by hypertension status were reported. Key limitations include the observational design relying on administrative ICD-10 claims codes, which may underestimate comorbidity burden, and potential residual confounding despite matching.

Implications Medical Affairs : The high baseline prevalence of hypertension in this elderly fracture cohort reinforces the need for antihypertensive management protocols integrated into perioperative and rehabilitation pathways, a space where pharmaceutical companies can engage orthopedic and geriatric KOLs. Post-fracture acceleration of cardiovascular comorbidities such as congestive heart failure—where hypertension is a driver—positions antihypertensive agents as part of comprehensive post-acute care bundles, offering product positioning opportunities. Medical Affairs should leverage this real-world evidence to advocate for early blood pressure monitoring protocols and multidisciplinary comorbidity management in geriatric fracture programs.

Sex differences in ambulatory blood pressure among patients referred for 24-hour monitoring: A retrospective cross-sectional study.

Revue : Medicine | Tier : NA | IF estime : NA

Date de publication : 2026-08-28

Resultats principaux : This retrospective cross-sectional study analyzed 1,204 patients (two-thirds women) who underwent 24-hour ambulatory blood pressure monitoring (ABPM) at referral centers in Istanbul, Turkey. Both systolic and diastolic blood pressure (BP) were significantly lower in women than in men across the general population and in subgroups with and without a prior diagnosis of hypertension (p-values not explicitly stated but reported as significant). Women with a prior diagnosis of hypertension exhibited consistently lower mean 24-hour systolic and diastolic values compared to their male counterparts, irrespective of antihypertensive treatment status. No subgroup analyses by age, antihypertensive drug class, or menopausal status were performed, limiting mechanistic interpretation. The retrospective single-center design and absence of matched analysis limit causal inference, and the predominance of women (66%) may reflect referral bias in the Turkish clinical setting.

Implications Medical Affairs : These findings highlight the need for sex-specific BP thresholds and monitoring protocols in clinical guidelines, a gap that Medical Affairs teams should track as international hypertension societies update their recommendations. The significantly lower BP values observed in women suggest potential over-treatment risks in women currently managed to male-defined targets, which has direct implications for product labeling, risk-benefit communication, and KOL engagement around sex-disaggregated efficacy data. Pharmaceutical companies with antihypertensive portfolios should consider sponsoring adequately powered prospective studies stratified by sex and menopausal status to generate guideline-ready evidence.

Renal Damage In Sickle Cell Disease: The Experience Of A Sickle Cell Reference Center In Sub-Saharan Africa.

Revue : Le Mali medical | Tier : NA | IF estime : NA

Date de publication : 2026-01-19

Resultats principaux : This descriptive and analytical retrospective study from the Centre de Recherche et de Lutte contre la Drepanocytose in Bamako, Mali (2010-2017) included 125 sickle cell disease patients (70 SS, 43 SC, 10 Sbeta+thalassemia, 2 Sbeta0 thalassemia; mean age 33.03 +/- 15.53 years; 67 women, 58 men). Renal damage was documented in all participants: sickle cell nephropathy (84.8%), chronic kidney disease (10.4%), and hematuria (4.8%); five patients (4%) were on chronic dialysis. A history of hypertension was significantly associated with renal damage (p<0.05), alongside diabetes and NSAID use; no renal damage was observed in patients with fetal hemoglobin >20%. No subgroup analysis by antihypertensive treatment type was conducted, and the retrospective design introduces ascertainment bias. The small sample size and single-center setting in sub-Saharan Africa limit generalizability but provide important data on an underserved population.

Implications Medical Affairs : The significant association of hypertension with renal damage in sickle cell disease patients highlights a therapeutic intersection between antihypertensive management and nephroprotection in this population, relevant for products with both BP-lowering and renoprotective indications such as ACE inhibitors and ARBs. The sub-Saharan African setting reveals a critical unmet medical need where hypertension control could modify disease progression, offering a rationale for Medical Affairs to support access programs and KOL development in African nephrology and hematology. Future research should prioritize prospective cohort studies evaluating whether specific antihypertensive classes delay the progression to end-stage renal disease in sickle cell patients.

PepGate: A Dual-Path Diffusion Framework for ACE Inhibitory Peptide De Novo Design.

Revue : Journal of medicinal chemistry | Tier : NA | IF estime : NA

Date de publication : 2026-08-27

Resultats principaux : This study presents PepGate, a gated dual-path discrete diffusion framework for de novo design of ACE inhibitory peptides, developed by fine-tuning a protein language model (PepGPT) into a short-peptide-specific foundation model with integrated generative (PepGen) and discriminative (PepClass and PepIC50) components. The pipeline identified 30 promising peptides with a median IC50 of 4.79 microM against ACE, substantially improving upon traditional empirical mining approaches. In vivo validation of the top candidate YIPVPF in spontaneously hypertensive rats (SHRs) demonstrated an acute systolic blood pressure (SBP) reduction of 48 mmHg and a chronic SBP reduction of 43 mmHg, demonstrating sustained antihypertensive efficacy. Computational target-network analyses suggested the peptide operates through mechanisms beyond direct ACE inhibition, potentially engaging broader blood-pressure-related pathways. Limitations include the absence of human pharmacokinetic data, lack of head-to-head comparison with established antihypertensive peptides, and the limited in vivo validation confined to an animal model.

Implications Medical Affairs : The emergence of AI-driven de novo peptide design for ACE inhibition represents a disruptive innovation in the antihypertensive drug discovery landscape that Medical Affairs should monitor closely, as it could accelerate the pipeline of next-generation bioactive peptide therapies. From a product positioning perspective, pharmaceutical companies should assess competitive differentiation of existing small-molecule ACE inhibitors versus AI-designed peptides, particularly regarding oral bioavailability and stability challenges that peptides still face. Medical Affairs should engage with KOLs in computational pharmacology and cardiovascular therapeutics to track translational milestones and anticipate potential guideline implications of peptide-based ACE inhibition.

Closed reduction and percutaneous fixation versus open reduction and internal fixation for displaced medial malleolar fractures: a randomized controlled trial.

Revue : European journal of trauma and emergency surgery | Tier : NA | IF estime : NA

Date de publication : 2026-08-31

Resultats principaux : This multicenter randomized controlled trial enrolled 50 adults with isolated displaced medial malleolar fractures (Herscovici type B and C) across two hospitals, randomly assigned 1:1 to closed reduction and percutaneous fixation (CRPF, n=25) or open reduction and internal fixation (ORIF, n=25). At 6-month follow-up, AOFAS scores showed no significant difference between groups (p>0.05): Group A had 13 excellent, 7 good, 4 fair, and 1 poor; Group B had 12 excellent, 9 good, 3 fair, and 1 poor. Complication rates were comparable: wound infection (CRPF 4% vs. ORIF 12%), malunion/non-union (8% vs. 4%), hardware discomfort (12% vs. 20%), and deep venous thrombosis (4% vs. 12%). Comorbidities including hypertension and diabetes were significantly associated with increased sequelae risk (p<0.05), underscoring hypertension as a modifier of surgical outcomes in musculoskeletal procedures. Critical limitations include no formal sample size calculation or prespecified equivalence margin, and 6-month follow-up may be insufficient to capture late complications.

Implications Medical Affairs : Although this RCT addresses orthopedic surgery, the finding that hypertension independently predicts increased sequelae risk highlights the systemic burden of uncontrolled hypertension across surgical specialties, reinforcing messages for Medical Affairs around perioperative BP optimization programs. Pharmaceutical companies with antihypertensive portfolios should collaborate with surgical KOLs to develop evidence-based perioperative hypertension management protocols that could reduce complication rates in elective and emergency procedures. This evidence supports cross-specialty engagement strategies targeting not only cardiologists and internists, but also trauma surgeons and orthopedists as secondary prescribers concerned with hypertension-related surgical risk.

Norepinephrine infusion for prophylaxis against spinal-induced hypotension in elderly patients undergoing hip surgery: a randomized controlled comparison of two doses.

Revue : BMC anesthesiology | Tier : NA | IF estime : NA

Date de publication : 2026-08-20

Resultats principaux : This randomized non-inferiority clinical trial (NCT07077265) enrolled 84 elderly patients undergoing hip surgery under spinal anesthesia at Cairo University, randomized to norepinephrine 0.1 mcg/kg/min (n=42) or 0.07 mcg/kg/min (n=42). The primary outcome was average intraoperative mean arterial pressure (MAP): 87.9 +/- 9.5 mmHg in the 0.1 mcg group versus 84.5 +/- 8.5 mmHg in the 0.07 mcg group (mean difference -3.5 mmHg, 95% CI -7.4 to 0.4 mmHg), meeting the prespecified non-inferiority margin of 8 mmHg. Incidence of post-spinal hypotension, reactive hypertension, and bradycardia was comparable between the two groups. The occurrence of reactive hypertension as an adverse event in both groups is clinically relevant, particularly in elderly patients with pre-existing hypertension where BP spikes carry cardiovascular risk. Limitations include relatively small sample size, single-center design, and the study does not report subgroup outcomes by pre-existing hypertension status.

Implications Medical Affairs : The occurrence of reactive hypertension with norepinephrine infusion in elderly surgical patients underscores the need for careful BP management algorithms in the perioperative setting, an area where antihypertensive agents may play a bridging role to prevent rebound hypertensive events. Medical Affairs should monitor emerging evidence on vasopressor-induced hypertensive complications to inform safety communications and differentiate BP-lowering agents in the peri-anesthesia context. Engagement with anesthesiology KOLs and inclusion of perioperative hypertension endpoints in future cardiovascular trials represents a strategic opportunity to broaden product relevance beyond outpatient BP management.

Acupressure and hypnosis in arterial hypertension: a series of N-of-1 trials.

Revue : Frontiers in medicine | Tier : NA | IF estime : NA

Date de publication : 2026-08-17

Resultats principaux : This N-of-1 trial series from Charite Universitatsmedizin Berlin enrolled 17 patients with grade 1 or 2 hypertension (59% female, mean age 54.4 years, SD 7.6) to compare self-administered acupressure plus routine care (RC), hypnosis plus RC, and RC alone in a six-week randomized cross-over intervention phase with washout periods after each treatment block. Primary outcomes were systolic and diastolic blood pressure and pulse rate measured by patients using a standardized protocol; adherence exceeded 90%. Meta-analysis across N-of-1 trials showed no overall group-level effect of acupressure or hypnosis on cardiovascular outcomes (blood pressure, pulse rate), though two patients showed BP reductions with both interventions and one with acupressure alone, suggesting individual variability. Stress levels were higher during the run-in period (mean 43.6, SD 18.9) compared to subsequent blocks but were similar between treatment periods; reported side effects were mild and predominantly during acupressure. The study is limited by a small sample (n=17), absence of an objective blinding mechanism, self-measured outcomes susceptible to regression to the mean, and lack of follow-up beyond the intervention period.

Implications Medical Affairs : The absence of a clinically meaningful group-level blood pressure reduction with acupressure and hypnosis supports the continued primacy of pharmacological treatment in guideline-based hypertension management, providing reassurance to Medical Affairs that complementary approaches do not threaten market share for antihypertensive medications but may serve as add-on strategies for patient engagement. Individual-level response variability observed in this N-of-1 design highlights the importance of personalized medicine approaches, a narrative Medical Affairs teams can leverage to emphasize tailored pharmacotherapy over one-size-fits-all complementary interventions. Collaborating with integrative medicine KOLs to develop combination protocols that pair evidence-based antihypertensives with stress-reduction interventions could expand product positioning into a holistic hypertension management paradigm.

Prevalence of unruptured intracranial aneurysms according to comorbidities, risk factors, country, and time period: a systematic review and meta-analysis.

Revue : The Lancet. Neurology | Tier : NA | IF estime : NA

Date de publication : 2026-09-01

Resultats principaux : This systematic review and meta-analysis (PROSPERO CRD420261296728) screened 4,708 studies and included 162 articles reporting on 316,131 participants and 11,822 individuals with unruptured intracranial aneurysms (UIAs). In a reference population (mean age 50, 50% women, no comorbidities), the estimated UIA prevalence was 3.9% (95% CI 3.0-5.1%); hypertension was identified as a significant risk factor with a risk ratio of 1.6 (95% CI 1.5-1.7) based on 4,043 UIA cases among 83,053 participants. Other significant risk factors included female sex (RR 1.9, 95% CI 1.8-2.0), current smoking (RR 1.4, 95% CI 1.2-1.6), autosomal dominant polycystic kidney disease (adjusted PR 4.4), connective-tissue disorders (adjusted PR 3.9), and positive family history (adjusted PR 2.4). A temporal trend analysis demonstrated that UIA prevalence in healthy individuals increased from 2002-2015 to 2016-2022 (adjusted PR 1.8, 95% CI 1.1-2.8), with UIAs ≥5 mm rising from 0.7% to 1.4%, suggesting environmental or detection factors beyond improved imaging alone. Limitations include significant heterogeneity between studies (high tau values), GRADE certainty ranging from very low to moderate, and the inability to fully separate improved detection from true prevalence increase.

Implications Medical Affairs : The RR of 1.6 for hypertension in unruptured intracranial aneurysms represents compelling epidemiological evidence for the neurovascular consequences of uncontrolled blood pressure, strengthening the Medical Affairs narrative around hypertension as a risk factor for cerebrovascular disease beyond stroke and lending urgency to blood pressure control. This high-impact Lancet Neurology publication offers a strategic opportunity for Medical Affairs to engage neurology and neurosurgery KOLs who screen for UIAs, reinforcing hypertension treatment as a primary prevention tool for aneurysmal subarachnoid hemorrhage. Pharmaceutical companies should consider supporting collaborative real-world studies tracking UIA incidence and progression in hypertensive patients on different treatment regimens to generate differentiated evidence for guidelines.

Type 1 diabetes in the era of obesity: Pathophysiology and therapeutic implications.

Revue : Journal of diabetes investigation | Tier : NA | IF estime : NA

Date de publication : 2026-09-01

Resultats principaux : This narrative review from the Icahn School of Medicine at Mount Sinai summarizes the epidemiology, pathophysiology, and therapeutic implications of insulin-resistant ('double') type 1 diabetes in the context of rising obesity. Overweight and obesity are now common in type 1 diabetes and are associated with variable combinations of high insulin requirement, hypertension, dyslipidemia, metabolic syndrome, metabolic dysfunction-associated steatotic liver disease (MASLD), and increased cardiovascular and renal risk. The review describes mechanisms of insulin resistance in type 1 diabetes including disproportionate peripheral versus hepatic insulin action from intensive insulin therapy, inherited and environmental factors, glucagon dysregulation, ectopic fat, and inflammation, all of which contribute to hypertension risk. Established and emerging therapeutic approaches reviewed include amylin replacement (the established precedent), incretin receptor agonists (GLP-1 RAs, dual GIP/GLP-1 RAs), and next-generation amylin receptor agonists; the cardiovascular benefits of GLP-1 RAs include blood pressure reduction. Limitations include the narrative rather than systematic review format and the absence of quantitative pooled effect estimates on BP outcomes in this specific population.

Implications Medical Affairs : The integration of hypertension as a central cardiometabolic feature of insulin-resistant type 1 diabetes expands the conceptual framework for antihypertensive product positioning into the diabetology space, where treatment decisions increasingly account for metabolic and cardiovascular co-morbidities simultaneously. The review's endorsement of incretin-based therapies with demonstrated BP-lowering effects (GLP-1 RAs) alongside antihypertensives signals an evolving combination treatment paradigm, representing a positioning opportunity for ACE inhibitors or ARBs with established renoprotective evidence in diabetic populations. Medical Affairs should engage endocrinology KOLs around hypertension burden in type 1 diabetes, an underserved research area compared to type 2 diabetes, and support investigator-initiated studies examining BP control in this growing population.

Adverse pregnancy outcomes and long-term cardiovascular risk: lifelong connections.

Revue : European heart journal | Tier : Tier 2 | IF estime : ~39

Date de publication : 2026-09-01

Resultats principaux : This comprehensive review article in the European Heart Journal synthesizes extensive epidemiological evidence demonstrating that adverse pregnancy outcomes (APOs)—including hypertensive disorders of pregnancy (HDP), gestational diabetes, preterm birth, small-for-gestational-age birth, and pregnancy loss—are early clinical markers of underlying cardiometabolic dysfunction. Shared mechanisms linking APOs to long-term cardiovascular disease include endothelial dysfunction, systemic inflammation, and insulin resistance, establishing a pathophysiological continuum between obstetric complications and later coronary artery disease, heart failure, stroke, chronic kidney disease, and premature cardiovascular mortality in affected women. Early post-partum studies reveal persistent hypertension, adverse cardiac remodelling, and metabolic abnormalities as mediators of much of this excess long-term cardiovascular risk, underscoring the 'fourth trimester' as a critical preventive window. Integrated cardio-obstetric care models, digital follow-up tools, and systematic transition to primary care are proposed as implementation strategies for sustained cardiovascular prevention; the review highlights disparities in follow-up quality across socially disadvantaged populations. As a narrative-synthesis review, quantitative pooled risk estimates are not provided, and the evidence base cited varies in quality from observational to registry-level data.

Implications Medical Affairs : The formal recognition of hypertensive disorders of pregnancy as early markers of long-term cardiovascular risk in the European Heart Journal significantly strengthens Medical Affairs messaging around hypertension as a lifelong women's cardiovascular risk factor, creating opportunities to engage obstetric, cardiological, and primary care KOLs simultaneously. This publication reinforces the clinical and commercial case for early postpartum blood pressure monitoring and sustained antihypertensive therapy initiation in women with HDP history, a gap that antihypertensive portfolios can address through dedicated evidence generation and patient support programs for the postpartum period. Medical Affairs should leverage this evidence to develop cardio-obstetric scientific platforms that support guideline development, patient advocacy, and cross-specialty KOL engagement around women's cardiovascular health.

Prevalence of metabolic syndrome among primary and secondary school teachers: a systematic review and meta-analysis.

Revue : Frontiers in public health | Tier : NA | IF estime : NA

Date de publication : 2026-08-17

Resultats principaux : This PRISMA 2020-compliant systematic review and meta-analysis (PROSPERO CRD420251034493) included 14 cross-sectional studies involving 8,107 school teachers from 10 countries, examining metabolic syndrome (MetS) prevalence in primary and secondary school teachers as a high-risk occupational group. The pooled MetS prevalence was 30% (95% CI 22-38%), with very high heterogeneity (I2=98.34%); central obesity, dyslipidemia, and hypertension were the most frequently reported MetS components. Subgroup analysis revealed the highest prevalence in Asia (35%, 95% CI 25-45%) and the lowest in a single European study (9%, 95% CI 8-10%), with psychosocial demands, sedentary occupational routines, and restricted physical activity proposed as contributing occupational risk factors for hypertension and MetS. The high heterogeneity (I2=98.34%) and reliance on cross-sectional studies limit causal conclusions; the absence of standardized MetS diagnostic criteria across studies and the dominance of Asian populations introduce additional uncertainty. Publication bias was assessed using Egger's test and trim-and-fill, results of which are not fully detailed in the available abstract.

Implications Medical Affairs : A pooled MetS prevalence of 30% among school teachers—with hypertension as a key component driven by sedentary occupational environments and psychosocial stress—highlights occupational settings as an underappreciated venue for hypertension screening and prevention programs, where Medical Affairs can support employer-based health initiatives. This evidence supports Medical Affairs engagement with occupational medicine KOLs and public health policymakers to advocate for workplace cardiovascular risk assessment programs, positioning antihypertensive therapy within a broader primary prevention framework that addresses lifestyle and environmental determinants of hypertension. Future longitudinal studies using standardized MetS criteria and objective occupational exposure measures should be prioritized to generate guideline-quality evidence that could justify dedicated pharmacological intervention strategies in high-risk occupational groups.
