Cognitive impairment in uncontrolled vs controlled hypertension - systematic review and meta-analysis

Revue : European journal of internal medicine | Tier : NA | IF estime : NA

Date de publication : 2026-10-31

Resultats principaux : This systematic review and meta-analysis searched three databases (MEDLINE, Embase, PsycINFO) between June and July 2024, ultimately including 18 predominantly cross-sectional studies comparing cognitive performance and brain structural abnormalities in hypertensive adults with controlled versus uncontrolled blood pressure. The primary pooled analysis of cognitive impairment (CImp) prevalence across five studies demonstrated that well-controlled hypertension was associated with significantly lower odds of CImp (OR 0.32, 95% CI: 0.13–0.80), though this result was statistically significant only among individuals aged 65 years or older. White matter hyperintensities (WMH) assessed by MRI in four studies tended to be less advanced in the controlled hypertension group, with a standardized mean difference of 0.58 (95% CI: 0.08–1.08), suggesting structural cerebral benefit from effective antihypertensive treatment. Individual neuropsychological tests — verbal fluency, delayed word recall, digit symbol substitution, and digit span — did not reveal significant differences between controlled and uncontrolled groups, indicating that global cognitive screening instruments may be more sensitive than single-domain tests for detecting hypertension-related cognitive change. The predominance of cross-sectional studies limiting causal inference, the small number of pooled studies, considerable between-study heterogeneity, and variability in definitions of 'controlled' hypertension across studies are the main limitations of this work.

Implications Medical Affairs : These findings reinforce current guideline recommendations for tight blood pressure control to prevent cerebrovascular complications, especially in elderly hypertensive patients, and provide compelling evidence for positioning antihypertensive therapies around cognitive protection as a differentiated value proposition beyond classical cardiovascular risk reduction. Medical Affairs teams should prioritize collaborations with neurologists and geriatricians as key opinion leaders to develop educational programs linking hypertension control to dementia prevention, particularly given the growing regulatory and payer interest in cognitive endpoints in cardiovascular trials. Prospective randomized trials examining the differential effects of specific antihypertensive drug classes on validated cognitive outcomes in elderly patients represent a critical evidence gap that this meta-analysis supports prioritizing.

Prevalence and Associated Factors of Hypertension Among School-Going Children in Hyderabad City, India: A Cross-Sectional Study

Revue : Cureus | Tier : NA | IF estime : NA

Date de publication : 2026-09-30

Resultats principaux : This school-based cross-sectional study enrolled 1,212 children aged 7–15 years from four schools in Hyderabad, India (August 2014–May 2015), obtaining three blood pressure measurements per visit at 0, 30, and 60 minutes and applying Fourth Report NHBPEP Working Group age-, sex-, and height-specific percentile criteria. The overall prevalence of hypertension was 6.68% (95% CI: 5.41–8.23%) and prehypertension 8.09% (95% CI: 6.68–9.76%), with both systolic and diastolic blood pressures correlating significantly with age and more strongly with BMI (r = 0.50–0.54 and 0.31–0.35, respectively; p < 0.001). Multivariable logistic regression identified obesity (aOR: 8.60, 95% CI: 3.84–19.26), overweight (aOR: 3.01, 95% CI: 1.33–6.79), parental history of hypertension (aOR: 2.14, 95% CI: 1.12–4.09), mixed dietary pattern (aOR: 1.97), and male sex (aOR: 1.93) as independent predictors, with good model discrimination (AUC: 0.886). Among obese children, the hypertension prevalence was dramatically higher at 39.1% compared with 2.1% in normal-weight children (Pearson chi-square = 437, p < 0.001), demonstrating a strong, graded association between adiposity and elevated blood pressure in this age group. Key limitations include the cross-sectional design preventing causal inference, single-city recruitment limiting national generalizability, and data collected over a decade ago, which may not capture current secular trends in childhood obesity and hypertension.

Implications Medical Affairs : The high prevalence of childhood hypertension in urban India — particularly its strong association with obesity — signals an emerging epidemic that will substantially increase demand for antihypertensive therapies in South Asian markets over the next two decades and supports early market-shaping investment in this region. Medical Affairs can leverage these data to engage pediatric cardiologists, endocrinologists, and public health authorities as key opinion leaders, advocating for national school-based hypertension screening programs that could identify future patient populations for early lifestyle and pharmacological interventions. Future longitudinal studies tracking hypertensive children into adulthood are needed to establish the full cardiovascular risk trajectory and assess the long-term benefit of early intervention, representing a high-priority research area to support global pediatric hypertension guidelines.

Associations Between Self-Reported Experiences of Racial Discrimination and Hypertensive Disorders of Pregnancy among Black and White Women in the Coronary Artery Risk Development in Young Adults Study

Revue : Journal of racial and ethnic health disparities | Tier : NA | IF estime : NA

Date de publication : 2026-10-31

Resultats principaux : The Coronary Artery Risk Development in Young Adults (CARDIA) prospective cohort followed 1,184 parous Black and White women for over 30 years from baseline ages 18–30 years (1985–1986), assessing self-reported experiences of racial discrimination (EORD) and incident hypertensive disorders of pregnancy (HDP) in pregnancies lasting at least 23 weeks. EORD was significantly associated with increased odds of HDP with an adjusted odds ratio of 1.73 (95% CI: 1.20–2.48), with both moderate EORD (<33%; OR: 1.75, 95% CI: 1.20–2.55) and high EORD (≥33%; OR: 1.65, 95% CI: 1.04–2.64) showing significant associations independently of cardiovascular risk factors, age, education, income, and parity. At baseline, 86.0% of Black women and 37.1% of White women reported discrimination, with the overall discrimination exposure rate at 60.6% of the cohort; women reporting EORD were more likely to be Black, unmarried, and have a higher BMI. Critically, the association between racial discrimination and HDP was significant in both Black and White women, suggesting a psychosocial stress-mediated pathway independent of race-specific structural factors, with important implications for universal stress-reduction interventions in prenatal care. Limitations include reliance on self-reported outcomes for both discrimination and HDP with no medical record validation, potential residual confounding by unmeasured psychosocial stressors, and the predominantly American social context limiting direct global extrapolation.

Implications Medical Affairs : These findings highlight psychosocial stress as an underrecognized modifiable driver of hypertensive disorders of pregnancy, supporting the development of integrated care pathways that combine antihypertensive pharmacological management with structured stress reduction and social support programs. For Medical Affairs teams, these data substantiate social determinants of health as targets for comprehensive hypertension management programs, creating new engagement angles with maternal-fetal medicine specialists, social epidemiologists, and health equity researchers as non-traditional KOLs. Future research should investigate whether targeted psychosocial interventions in high-discrimination exposure groups reduce HDP incidence and whether specific antihypertensive regimens offer differential protective effects in chronically stressed obstetric populations.

Association of Placental Morphological Abnormalities with Adverse Pregnancy Outcomes in Hypertensive Disorders of Pregnancy: A Propensity Score-Matched Cohort Study

Revue : International journal of women's health | Tier : NA | IF estime : NA

Date de publication : 2026-10-27

Resultats principaux : This retrospective propensity score-matched cohort study enrolled 1,928 patients with hypertensive disorders of pregnancy (HDP) delivering at Wuhan Union Hospital between May 2020 and June 2025, of whom 111 (5.8%) had abnormal placental morphology (thickened or small placenta) on prenatal ultrasound; matching at a 1:2 ratio balanced baseline characteristics. In the primary analysis (Model 1), abnormal placental morphology was significantly associated with higher odds of preterm birth, early preterm birth, low birth weight, very low birth weight, 1-minute Apgar score <7, and NICU admission compared to normal morphology. After additional adjustment for abnormal umbilical artery or middle cerebral artery Doppler findings and ultrasound-estimated fetal growth lag (Model 2), these associations were attenuated and no longer statistically significant, suggesting that placental morphology mediates neonatal risk largely through concurrent Doppler and fetal growth abnormalities rather than independently. Subgroup analyses showed that small-placenta cases had more frequent abnormal Doppler findings and suspected fetal growth restriction with generally less favorable outcomes than the isolated thickening subgroup; landmark analyses at 28 weeks identified early preterm birth and very low birth weight as significant, with the latter persisting after full adjustment. The retrospective single-center Chinese tertiary hospital design, potential for residual confounding, variability in ultrasound operator technique, and the relatively low prevalence of abnormal morphology are key limitations affecting generalizability.

Implications Medical Affairs : The attenuation of associations after Doppler adjustment positions abnormal placental morphology as a component of a multiparameter placental risk assessment rather than an independent predictor, with implications for clinical decision-making on when to escalate antihypertensive treatment intensity in HDP. Medical Affairs can use these findings to support educational programs for obstetricians on integrated placental surveillance protocols that optimize treatment timing in severe HDP, potentially strengthening the evidence base for early pharmacological intervention to prevent catastrophic perinatal outcomes. Prospective multicenter studies are needed to define validated thresholds for integrated placental-Doppler assessment that should trigger treatment modification, representing a critical evidence gap for guideline development in high-risk obstetric hypertension management.

HELLP (Hemolysis, Elevated Liver Enzymes, and Low Platelets)-Associated Subcapsular Hepatic Hematoma Presenting as Postoperative Hemorrhagic Shock: A Case Report

Revue : Cureus | Tier : NA | IF estime : NA

Date de publication : 2026-10-31

Resultats principaux : This case report documents a 23-year-old primigravida at 27 weeks and 3 days of gestation who presented with severe frontal headache, nausea, vomiting, and hypertension initially diagnosed as preeclampsia with severe features, with HELLP syndrome confirmed on the third hospital day after emergency cesarean delivery. On postoperative day 1, the patient developed acute hemodynamic instability with signs of acute abdomen; point-of-care ultrasonography identified free intra-abdominal fluid, prompting emergency surgical exploration that revealed an extensive subcapsular hepatic hematoma with active bleeding requiring damage control surgery with hepatic packing and temporary abdominal closure. After 48 hours of clinical stabilization, definitive abdominal wall closure was completed with pack removal, and liver enzymes progressively normalized through hospital discharge without requiring hepatic resection or embolization. The case highlights the critical importance of high clinical suspicion for hepatic complications following HELLP syndrome, particularly in patients showing abrupt postoperative cardiovascular deterioration, and demonstrates that multidisciplinary rapid response — even without definitive cross-sectional imaging — can achieve favorable maternal outcomes. As a single case report, the evidence is inherently limited with no comparative data and no formal systematic review of management outcomes; however, the management approach described is consistent with current literature on damage control surgery in obstetric hemorrhage.

Implications Medical Affairs : This case reinforces the potential for catastrophic hepatic complications of severe hypertensive disorders of pregnancy and underscores the need for intensive peripartum monitoring protocols and clear escalation pathways in obstetric units, creating opportunities for Medical Affairs to support multidisciplinary severe preeclampsia management programs. The bridging of hypertension management with surgical and critical care expertise highlighted in this case creates advocacy opportunities for Medical Affairs to engage a broader multidisciplinary KOL network — obstetric surgeons, intensivists, and hepatologists — in developing consensus guidelines for managing HELLP-associated complications. Future registry-based studies characterizing the incidence, risk factors, and optimal management of hepatic complications in HELLP syndrome are needed to guide risk stratification and to assess whether more aggressive early antihypertensive treatment prevents hepatic injury progression.

Clinical Symptom Burden and Its Association With Echocardiographic Parameters and Comorbidities in Heart Failure With Preserved Ejection Fraction: A Retrospective Study

Revue : Cureus | Tier : NA | IF estime : NA

Date de publication : 2026-10-18

Resultats principaux : This retrospective observational cross-sectional study enrolled 216 patients aged 18 years or older with heart failure with preserved ejection fraction (HFpEF, LVEF ≥50%) at a government hospital in Chennai, India (March 2025–March 2026), assessing the association between clinical symptom severity (NYHA class) and echocardiographic parameters and comorbidities. Hypertension was the most prevalent comorbidity at 80.6% of patients, with 76.9% having two or more major comorbidities; 55 patients (25.5%) were classified as NYHA III–IV with a mean age of 67.8 ± 10.4 years and 53.7% female. The severe symptom group (NYHA III–IV) had significantly higher E/e' ratio (17.4 ± 5.0 vs 12.6 ± 4.0), left atrial volume index LAVI (44.5 ± 12.1 vs 34.2 ± 9.1 mL/m²), left ventricular mass, and pulmonary artery systolic pressure PASP (43.9 ± 12.4 vs 36.8 ± 8.7 mmHg) compared to NYHA I–II patients. On multivariable analysis, E/e' (adjusted OR 1.18), LAVI (adjusted OR 1.05), and PASP (adjusted OR 1.04) were independently associated with NYHA III–IV symptoms, highlighting abnormal filling pressures and left atrial remodeling as dominant drivers of symptom burden in this hypertension-predominant population. Key limitations include the single-center retrospective cross-sectional design in a resource-limited Indian hospital setting and the potential for selection bias, precluding causal inference about the direction of hypertension-driven echocardiographic remodeling.

Implications Medical Affairs : The overwhelming predominance of hypertension (80.6%) as the primary driver of HFpEF reinforces the central role of antihypertensive therapy — especially agents reducing LV filling pressures and left atrial remodeling — in HFpEF prevention and management, supporting the positioning of RAAS inhibitors, SGLT2 inhibitors, and MRAs in Medical Affairs messaging for this indication. These findings justify engagement with cardiologists specializing in heart failure as KOLs, positioning hypertension control as the most critical modifiable upstream target in HFpEF prevention strategies and providing data to support guideline-driven treatment escalation discussions with payers. Companies should support prospective randomized trials examining whether intensive blood pressure control reduces echocardiographic markers (E/e', LAVI, PASP) and translates into patient-reported symptomatic improvement and reduced hospitalizations in HFpEF.

Clinico-Angiographic Profile of Paramilitary Forces Personnel Presenting With Acute Coronary Syndrome at a Tertiary Care Institute in Jammu and Kashmir, India

Revue : Cureus | Tier : NA | IF estime : NA

Date de publication : 2026-10-01

Resultats principaux : This retrospective observational study analyzed 173 paramilitary personnel diagnosed with acute coronary syndrome (ACS) at a tertiary care institute in Jammu and Kashmir, India, evaluating cardiovascular risk factors, clinical presentation, angiographic findings, treatment strategies, and in-hospital outcomes; the mean age was 46.5 ± 7.52 years and 98.8% were male. Hypertension was the second most prevalent cardiovascular risk factor at 54.3% (n=94), after smoking at 57.2% (n=99), with dyslipidemia in 33.5% and diabetes mellitus in 28.3%; these prevalence rates highlight that hypertension management is suboptimal in this high-stress occupational group. Anterior wall myocardial infarction was the most common presentation (42.8%), coronary angiography revealed predominantly single-vessel disease (53.6%), and the left anterior descending artery was the most frequently involved vessel (61.7%); mean ejection fraction was 45.6 ± 7.49% with regional wall motion abnormalities in 76.9%. Pharmaco-invasive therapy was the preferred strategy in 68.8% of patients (thrombolysis in 72.3%), percutaneous coronary intervention was performed in most eligible cases, with complications including complete heart block in 4.6% and ventricular tachycardia in 4.0%; no in-hospital mortality was reported. The single-center retrospective design, exclusively male military population, and the specific high-stress occupational context significantly limit generalizability to the broader general hypertensive population seeking routine cardiovascular care.

Implications Medical Affairs : The high prevalence of undertreated hypertension (54.3%) in this relatively young working-age military population illustrates the substantial cardiovascular burden of uncontrolled blood pressure in high-stress occupational groups and supports Medical Affairs initiatives targeting hypertension awareness in occupational health settings. Engagement with military and occupational health physicians as non-traditional KOLs presents an opportunity to expand antihypertensive treatment program reach, with data from this study supporting cardiovascular risk reduction messages in a workforce context where medication adherence may be particularly challenging. Prospective occupational cohort studies examining the impact of systematic hypertension screening and treatment protocols on ACS incidence in high-stress professions are needed to build the evidence base for antihypertensive prescribing guidelines in occupational medicine.

Clinical Evaluation of Patients With Premature Canities and Its Association With Metabolic Syndrome

Revue : Cureus | Tier : NA | IF estime : NA

Date de publication : 2026-10-01

Resultats principaux : This observational case-control study at an Indian tertiary care center enrolled 240 age- and sex-matched participants (120 cases with premature canities and 120 controls) to evaluate the prevalence of metabolic syndrome (NCEP ATP III criteria) in individuals with early hair graying, with a mean age of onset for graying of 16.58 ± 4.20 years and a strong male predominance of 70.8%. Cases showed significantly higher mean systolic blood pressure (123.63 vs 119.35 mmHg; p=0.001), larger waist circumference (84.44 vs 81.75 cm; p=0.020), lower protective HDL cholesterol (50.10 vs 53.34 mg/dL; p=0.045), and elevated triglycerides (115.98 vs 103.40 mg/dL; p=0.023) compared to controls, indicating a clustering of metabolic risk features. The biological plausibility for this association is grounded in shared oxidative stress pathways: depletion of follicular melanocytes in premature graying mirrors the accelerated vascular aging and endothelial oxidative stress underlying early hypertension and metabolic dysfunction. A positive family history of premature graying was reported in 38.3% of cases, suggesting a genetic predisposition potentially overlapping with familial hypertension and metabolic syndrome susceptibility. Key limitations include the small sample size of 240 participants, single-center design in a tertiary care population prone to selection bias, cross-sectional nature precluding causal inference, and the incomplete reporting of formal full NCEP ATP III metabolic syndrome classification rates.

Implications Medical Affairs : The association between premature canities and elevated blood pressure, dyslipidemia, and central obesity suggests that dermatological clinicians could serve as an untapped early detection channel for hypertension and metabolic syndrome risk, creating novel cross-specialty educational opportunities for Medical Affairs teams targeting early cardiovascular prevention. Collaboration with dermatology KOLs to develop integrated screening algorithms combining blood pressure assessment with dermatological presentations of early metabolic risk could expand the reach of hypertension prevention programs to patient populations not yet engaged with cardiovascular care. Larger multicenter prospective studies are needed to validate premature canities as a formal cardiovascular risk marker and to determine whether early antihypertensive and metabolic intervention in this population reduces long-term cardiovascular event rates.

Renin-angiotensin system inhibitors and arterial stiffness in chronic kidney disease: results from the chronic renal insufficiency cohort

Revue : Journal of human hypertension | Tier : Tier 3 | IF estime : ~3

Date de publication : 2026-10-31

Resultats principaux : This prospective observational analysis used linear mixed-effects modeling in 1,201 participants from the Chronic Renal Insufficiency Cohort (CRIC) Study — of whom 982 were on renin-angiotensin system inhibitors (RASI: primarily ACE inhibitors and ARBs) and 219 on non-RASI antihypertensives — to assess the differential impact on carotid-femoral pulse wave velocity (cfPWV), the gold-standard measure of aortic arterial stiffness. The primary outcome showed a non-significant difference in annual cfPWV change between RASI users (adjusted 0.19 m/s per year, 95% CI: 0.14–0.25) and non-RASI users (0.15 m/s per year, 95% CI: 0.00–0.30; p=0.61), indicating no meaningful differential benefit of RASI on slowing arterial stiffness progression in CKD. In the monotherapy subgroup analysis (N=283), the annual cfPWV change was 0.11 m/s for RASI versus 0.03 m/s for non-RASI users (p=0.41), with still no significant difference and no meaningful difference in systolic blood pressure trajectories over time between the two groups. These null findings challenge the hypothesis that RASI confer blood-pressure-independent vascular protective effects on arterial stiffness in CKD, suggesting that alternative mechanisms beyond the renin-angiotensin system are responsible for the accelerated large-artery aging characteristic of CKD. Key limitations include the observational design with potential confounding by indication (sicker patients more likely on RASI), lack of randomization, use of single cfPWV measurement timepoints, inability to assess individual drug dosing and adherence, and potential unmeasured confounders.

Implications Medical Affairs : The null finding for differential RASI benefit on arterial stiffness in CKD challenges the extrapolation of ACE inhibitor and ARB benefits to the vascular stiffness endpoint, potentially limiting their labeling differentiation on this marker and highlighting an unmet need for antihypertensive agents with proven arterial stiffness benefits in CKD. Medical Affairs teams should monitor upcoming randomized trials of SGLT2 inhibitors, endothelin receptor antagonists, and novel renal protective agents targeting arterial stiffness in hypertensive CKD patients, assessing whether these classes can capture this evidence gap and differentiate from established RASI therapy. KOL engagement with nephrologists and vascular biology researchers to explore mechanistic pathways — including inflammation, oxidative stress, and fibrosis — beyond the renin-angiotensin system is warranted to identify actionable research directions for next-generation antihypertensive development in CKD.

Reported Life-Threatening Polymorphic Ventricular Arrhythmia With Torsade-Like Morphology in a Patient With Gordon Syndrome and Hyperkalaemia

Revue : Cureus | Tier : NA | IF estime : NA

Date de publication : 2026-10-01

Resultats principaux : This case report describes a 37-year-old woman with genetically confirmed CUL3-variant Gordon's syndrome (pseudohypoaldosteronism type II) — characterized by salt-sensitive hypertension, hyperkalaemia, and metabolic acidosis with preserved renal function — who developed clinically significant hyperkalaemia (laboratory K+ 6.1 mmol/L; point-of-care K+ 4.8 mmol/L) following a three-week interruption of her usual antihypertensive and potassium-lowering treatment with bendroflumethiazide. During pre-hospital assessment, paramedics documented a self-terminating rhythm they identified as torsade de pointes, though the original ECG recording could not be retrieved, precluding independent verification of rhythm classification, preceding QT interval measurement, or exclusion of artefact or alternative rhythm. Extensive cardiac evaluation — including echocardiography, CT coronary angiography, cardiac MRI, ambulatory monitoring, exercise testing, and electrophysiology assessment — did not identify a definitive alternative arrhythmia substrate, though an intermittent electrical disorder could not be excluded with certainty. Following multidisciplinary cardiology and electrophysiology review, an extravascular implantable cardioverter-defibrillator was implanted, and bendroflumethiazide was successfully resumed with subsequent improvement in potassium control, demonstrating the dual antihypertensive and hyperkalaemia-corrective role of thiazide therapy in Gordon's syndrome. The single case format, inability to retrieve the original arrhythmia tracing, the diagnostic uncertainty regarding true torsade de pointes versus alternative rhythm or artefact, and the inability to establish direct causality between hyperkalaemia and the arrhythmia are fundamental limitations of this report.

Implications Medical Affairs : This case powerfully illustrates the potentially life-threatening cardiac consequences of thiazide diuretic discontinuation in rare genetic forms of salt-sensitive hypertension such as Gordon's syndrome, providing an important educational opportunity for Medical Affairs teams to communicate medication adherence and interruption risks to cardiologists, nephrologists, and endocrinologists managing these rare patients. The report highlights thiazide diuretics as the cornerstone of precision hypertension management in WNK-pathway-mediated genetic hypertension syndromes, reinforcing the sustained clinical relevance of this drug class even in the era of newer antihypertensive agents and supporting targeted Medical Affairs messaging on genetic hypertension subtypes. Future research should establish consensus perioperative and acute illness management protocols for patients with Gordon's syndrome and similar potassium-sensitive hypertension syndromes to prevent thiazide interruption-related arrhythmic events, representing a high-impact niche area for specialist KOL collaboration and guideline advocacy.

Revue : Zhonghua yi xue za zhi | Tier : NA | IF estime : NA

Date de publication : 2026-11-03

Resultats principaux : Note: This article concerns portal hypertension (a complication of liver cirrhosis), which is a distinct entity from arterial hypertension; it appeared in the search because the query included the term 'hypertension' in combination with 'guideline.' This 2026 updated edition of Chinese clinical practice guidelines for transjugular intrahepatic portosystemic shunt (TIPS) in portal hypertension was developed by more than 30 Chinese experts in interventional radiology, hepatobiliary surgery, hepatology, and gastroenterology under the Chinese College of Interventionalists. TIPS is the primary therapeutic modality for portal hypertension complications — including variceal bleeding and refractory ascites — and serves as a bridging therapy for decompensated cirrhosis patients awaiting liver transplantation; the 2026 edition updates the 2019 guidelines with advances in TIPS techniques, evolving treatment concepts, and new high-level evidence. The recommendations cover patient selection, technical aspects, and post-procedure management, aiming to standardize TIPS practice across Chinese healthcare institutions based on international evidence supplemented by Chinese consensus data and high-quality clinical studies. As a Chinese-language guideline targeting portal (not arterial) hypertension, its direct relevance to arterial hypertension drug development is limited, though co-morbidity with cardiovascular risk factors, including arterial hypertension, is relevant context for hepatic medicine programs.

Implications Medical Affairs : Although this guideline addresses portal hypertension rather than arterial hypertension, it represents the 2026 Chinese standard of care for a major cirrhosis complication, which has co-morbidity relevance for pharmaceutical companies with programs in hepatic or cardiometabolic diseases where both conditions may coexist in the same patient population. Medical Affairs teams monitoring Chinese regulatory and clinical practice trends should note this updated guideline as context for market access strategy in China, where practice guidelines from national medical societies heavily influence formulary decisions and reimbursement pathways. Future surveillance searches should employ stricter arterial hypertension-specific query terms (e.g., 'essential hypertension' or 'systemic arterial hypertension') to exclude portal hypertension literature and improve signal-to-noise ratio in this monitoring program.
