Predictors of genetic polymorphisms in patients with arterial hypertension and ischemic heart disease.

Revue : Endocrine Regulations | Tier : NA | IF estime : NA

Date de publication : 2026-09-19

Resultats principaux : This cross-sectional study enrolled 86 patients with arterial hypertension (45 also with ischemic heart disease) and 30 healthy controls from a single center in Ukraine, aiming to develop a multivariate regression model for predicting pathological alleles of the AGTR1 (C1166C) and eNOS (T786C) genes using routinely available clinical and demographic parameters without laboratory genetic testing. Eight significant predictors were identified: age, sex, disease duration, BMI, smoking, alcohol consumption, systolic blood pressure, and diastolic blood pressure. The regression model explained 98.42% of the variance in gene polymorphism status (R² = 0.9842), with high statistical significance confirmed by ANOVA (p < 0.001) and normally distributed residuals confirming model adequacy. No subgroup analyses were reported, though the hypertension-plus-IHD subgroup formed a distinct clinical stratum within the study design. Key limitations include the small sample size (n = 86), single-center Ukrainian setting, and lack of external validation on an independent dataset, which substantially restricts generalizability.

Implications Medical Affairs : This study offers a novel approach to genetic risk stratification in hypertension without requiring laboratory genetic testing, potentially enabling broader screening in resource-limited settings and identifying patients likely to respond differently to RAAS-targeting agents. From a Medical Affairs standpoint, the ability to predict AGTR1 and eNOS polymorphism status using clinical parameters could inform the development of personalized antihypertensive regimens and support positioning of drugs targeting the renin-angiotensin-nitric oxide pathway. Engaging KOLs in pharmacogenomics and precision medicine to validate this model prospectively in larger, multicenter cohorts would represent a high-impact research priority and strengthen the evidence base for genotype-guided treatment selection.

Development and temporal validation of a machine learning-based prediction model for long-term depressive symptoms in older adults with cardiovascular disease or hypertension: A longitudinal cohort study.

Revue : Medicine (Baltimore) | Tier : NA | IF estime : NA

Date de publication : 2026-09-18

Resultats principaux : This longitudinal cohort study used data from the China Health and Retirement Longitudinal Study (CHARLS, 2011-2020) to develop and temporally validate a machine learning-based model predicting long-term depressive symptoms in older adults with cardiovascular disease or hypertension. The development cohort comprised 548 participants followed from 2011 to 2015, and the non-overlapping temporal validation cohort comprised 523 participants followed from 2015 to 2020. Among seven machine learning algorithms tested with the Boruta algorithm for predictor selection, logistic regression demonstrated the best performance, achieving an AUC of 0.844 (95% CI: 0.773-0.915) in development and 0.840 (95% CI: 0.799-0.881) in temporal validation, indicating excellent discrimination and calibration stability. SHAP (Shapley Additive Explanations) was used to ensure model interpretability, and seven key predictors were selected. Limitations include reliance on self-reported depression screening instruments, potential residual confounding by unmeasured variables, and the need for prospective external validation in non-Chinese populations before clinical implementation.

Implications Medical Affairs : The high predictive accuracy of this model for identifying hypertensive and cardiovascular patients at risk for depression highlights the importance of integrated cardiometabolic-mental health management and could drive development of co-management pathways that extend beyond blood pressure control. From a Medical Affairs perspective, this evidence underscores an opportunity to position antihypertensive therapies within holistic cardiovascular risk frameworks that include mental health endpoints, potentially differentiating products through depression-related quality-of-life outcomes in older populations. Collaborating with KOLs in geriatric cardiology and psychiatry to validate this model in Western cohorts and incorporate it into digital health decision-support platforms represents a key near-term strategic opportunity.

Analysis of incidence characteristics and influencing factors of metabolic syndrome among rural residents in Southern Xinjiang Uygur Autonomous Region.

Revue : Zhonghua Liu Xing Bing Xue Za Zhi | Tier : NA | IF estime : NA

Date de publication : 2026-09-10

Resultats principaux : This prospective rural population cohort study enrolled 12,813 permanent residents aged 18 years and above in southern Xinjiang Uygur Autonomous Region, established in 2016, to examine metabolic syndrome (MS) incidence and risk factors using Latent Class Analysis (LCA) and Cox proportional hazards regression. The overall MS incidence density was 32.06 per 1,000 person-years, with higher rates in men (37.25) than women (27.89 per 1,000 person-years). Cox regression identified age >30 years, overweight/obesity, abnormal LDL-C, abnormal gamma-glutamyl transferase (GGT), high triglyceride-high waist circumference phenotype, and hypertension-high waist circumference phenotype as independent risk factors (all p < 0.05). Sex-specific subgroup analyses revealed that men bore higher MS risk from abnormal GGT and high triglyceride-high waist circumference phenotypes, while women showed higher risk from abnormal LDL-C, overweight/obesity, and hypertension-high waist circumference phenotypes. Limitations include the observational design with potential for residual confounding, restriction to a single geographic ethnic minority population, and article published in Chinese limiting accessibility.

Implications Medical Affairs : The high MS incidence density and distinct hypertension-associated phenotypes identified in this large underserved rural Chinese cohort point to a substantial and growing treatment-naive population for antihypertensive and metabolic risk-reduction therapies in China's western regions. The sex-specific and phenotype-specific risk profiles provide a compelling basis for targeted patient segmentation when developing educational programs and advisory boards around metabolic syndrome and hypertension co-management. KOL engagement with Chinese public health researchers and regional hospital networks in Xinjiang should be prioritized to build local evidence and strengthen product positioning in metabolic risk clusters among ethnic minority populations.

Office Blood Pressure and Arteriolosclerosis in Biopsy-Proven Glomerulonephritis: Association With Renal Function Decline and Clinical Outcomes.

Revue : Journal of Clinical Hypertension | Tier : Tier 3 | IF estime : ~3.9

Date de publication : 2026-09

Resultats principaux : This retrospective longitudinal study from Sapienza University of Rome enrolled 66 adults with biopsy-proven glomerulonephritis followed for 36 months to evaluate the association between standardized office blood pressure measurements (OBPM), renal histopathological lesions, and cardiorenal outcomes. Patients with arteriolosclerosis had significantly higher systolic blood pressure (SBP) compared to those without (130 vs. 120 mmHg, p = 0.032), and SBP >130 mmHg was independently associated with arteriolosclerosis (p = 0.004), arterial intimal fibrosis (p = 0.005), crescentic lesions (p = 0.033), and glomerulosclerotic changes (p = 0.048), while diastolic blood pressure showed no such associations. Over follow-up, arteriolosclerosis was associated with a significantly steeper eGFR decline (interaction beta = -4.91 mL/min/1.73m² per follow-up interval, p = 0.023) and independently predicted the composite renal and cardiovascular outcome (OR 6.67, 95% CI: 1.01-44.31; p = 0.049). Subgroup analyses within histopathological lesion categories reinforced the primacy of SBP as the driver of vascular pathology. Major limitations include the small sample size (n = 66), retrospective single-center design, and absence of ambulatory blood pressure monitoring data.

Implications Medical Affairs : These data reinforce the specific prognostic role of systolic hypertension in driving renal vascular damage in glomerulonephritis and support tighter SBP targets (<130 mmHg) in this population, directly aligned with current cardiorenal guidelines. From a Medical Affairs perspective, the finding that arteriolosclerosis independently predicts composite cardiorenal outcomes provides an opportunity to position antihypertensive agents with demonstrated renoprotective properties (particularly RAAS inhibitors) as the preferred option in the glomerulonephritis-hypertension overlap. This evidence should be leveraged in KOL discussions targeting nephrologists and supports investment in prospective cardiorenal outcomes research combining histopathology with ambulatory blood pressure metrics.

Effects of a lung recruitment maneuver combined with individualized positive end-expiratory pressure on postoperative pulmonary complications and perioperative hemodynamics in hypertensive patients undergoing pulmonary resection.

Revue : Zhong Nan Da Xue Xue Bao Yi Xue Ban | Tier : NA | IF estime : NA

Date de publication : 2026-07-28

Resultats principaux : This single-center RCT enrolled 100 hypertensive patients undergoing thoracoscopic pulmonary resection with one-lung ventilation (OLV) at a Chinese hospital between November 2024 and August 2025, randomized 1:1 to conventional protective ventilation (control) or a lung recruitment maneuver (LRM) combined with dynamic lung compliance-guided individualized PEEP (intervention). The primary outcome of postoperative pulmonary complications (PPCs) within 5 days was significantly lower in the intervention group (22% vs. 42%, p < 0.05). However, intraoperative hypotension was significantly more frequent in the intervention group (52% vs. 30%, p < 0.05), as was use of vasoactive drugs (54% vs. 20%, p < 0.05), with multivariable logistic regression confirming a 2.8-fold higher adjusted risk of intraoperative hypotension (OR 2.8, 95% CI: 1.2-6.9, p < 0.05). Subgroup analyses identified patients aged ≥65 years, BMI >24 kg/m², grade 3 hypertension, and ASA class III as particularly high-risk for intraoperative hypotension. Limitations include the single-center Chinese design, exclusively hypertensive population, and lack of long-term follow-up beyond 5 days postoperatively.

Implications Medical Affairs : This RCT demonstrates that optimized intraoperative ventilation meaningfully reduces pulmonary complications in hypertensive surgical patients, but at the cost of increased hemodynamic instability, underscoring the critical need for tailored perioperative antihypertensive management protocols in this growing surgical population. From a Medical Affairs perspective, the findings highlight an underexplored clinical niche at the intersection of perioperative medicine and hypertension, presenting an opportunity to position short-acting intravenous antihypertensive agents as essential components of perioperative care bundles for hypertensive patients undergoing thoracic surgery. Engaging anesthesiology and thoracic surgery KOLs through advisory boards focused on perioperative hemodynamic optimization in hypertensive patients could generate valuable real-world evidence and strengthen product differentiation.

Clinical course and management of adrenal endocrine hypertension: A single-center experience.

Revue : Endocrine Regulations | Tier : NA | IF estime : NA

Date de publication : 2026-09-19

Resultats principaux : This retrospective observational descriptive study at a Tunisian tertiary referral center included 55 patients with adrenal endocrine hypertension: primary aldosteronism (PA, 52.8%), pheochromocytoma (PHEO, 29.1%), and ACTH-independent Cushing's syndrome (CS, 18.1%), with a mean age of 47.0 ± 14.9 years and a slight female preponderance (60%). PA patients had significantly younger age at diagnosis, more severe and resistant hypertension, and required a higher number of antihypertensive drugs; 72.4% were treated medically with mineralocorticoid receptor antagonists (MRAs), with HTN remission achieved in only 21.8% after surgical treatment. All PHEO patients underwent adrenalectomy with 37.5% achieving HTN remission, while CS patients were surgically managed in 50% of cases with 40% achieving HTN remission. HTN-mediated organ damage (HMOD) was predominantly cardiovascular and ocular across all etiological groups. Limitations include the small sample size (n = 55), single-center retrospective design in a North African setting, and potential selection bias inherent to a tertiary referral center.

Implications Medical Affairs : The persistently low HTN remission rates following etiological treatment, particularly in PA (21.8%) and PHEO (37.5%), highlight a clinically significant residual hypertension burden in secondary hypertension patients even after specific intervention, justifying continued and optimized antihypertensive pharmacotherapy long-term in this population. From a Medical Affairs perspective, this study reinforces the therapeutic role of mineralocorticoid receptor antagonists in PA management and opens a strategic discussion around positioning next-generation antihypertensive agents in the residual risk population following adrenal surgery, where current treatment algorithms remain inadequately defined. Engaging endocrinology KOLs managing secondary hypertension through multidisciplinary care pathway development and real-world burden studies could strengthen advocacy for earlier, more aggressive treatment and support product differentiation in this niche indication.

Psychological intervention for disputing irrational beliefs associated with work stress amongst tourism educators and mathematicians with hypertension.

Revue : Medicine (Baltimore) | Tier : NA | IF estime : NA

Date de publication : 2026-09-18

Resultats principaux : This randomized controlled study enrolled 93 university staff members with hypertension from private, state, and federal universities in Nigeria, evaluating the effect of rational emotive behavior therapy (REBT) versus control on work-related stress and irrational beliefs, assessed at baseline (Time 1), post-intervention (Time 2), and follow-up (Time 3). Participants included lecturers in tourism and hospitality management, educational scientists, and home economics specialists from multiple faculties. Repeated measures statistical analysis demonstrated that REBT significantly reduced both stress levels and irrational beliefs compared to the control group (p < 0.05 at both Time 2 and Time 3), with follow-up confirming sustained long-term benefit of the intervention. Subgroup analyses by faculty specialty were not reported, and importantly, objective blood pressure measurements as a direct physiological hypertension endpoint were not assessed as a primary outcome. Key limitations include the absence of objective blood pressure change data, relatively small sample size (n = 93), restriction to specific Nigerian academic faculties, and risk of performance bias in a behavioral intervention.

Implications Medical Affairs : This RCT provides evidence supporting psychosocial interventions as evidence-based complements to pharmacological treatment for hypertension in occupationally stressed individuals, relevant to the growing debate around non-pharmacological adjunct therapies in current hypertension management guidelines. From a Medical Affairs perspective, these findings can be leveraged to position antihypertensive therapies within comprehensive treatment programs that integrate stress management components, appealing to payers and guideline committees that increasingly emphasize holistic cardiovascular risk reduction. Partnering with KOLs in behavioral cardiology and occupational medicine to design larger trials measuring hard blood pressure outcomes alongside psychological endpoints would substantially strengthen this body of evidence and differentiate a product's value proposition.

The association between the Global Diet Quality Score and all-cause mortality in Black South African adults.

Revue : The Journal of Nutrition | Tier : NA | IF estime : NA

Date de publication : 2026-09-19

Resultats principaux : This prospective cohort study from the PURE-NWP-SA study used baseline dietary data from Black South African adults aged 30-70 years to assess the association between the Global Diet Quality Score (GDQS) and all-cause and CVD mortality over a 13-year follow-up. The overall GDQS was low in both survivors and non-survivors, with only whole grains and liquid oils reaching desirable intake levels reflecting the high dietary monotony in this low-income population. Cox proportional hazards models showed that participants in the highest tertile of the GDQS+ (healthy food sub-score) had a significantly lower risk of all-cause mortality (HR = 0.69, 95% CI: 0.56-0.86) compared to the lowest tertile, while those in the highest tertile of the GDQS- (reverse-scored unhealthy food avoidance) had a 39% higher risk of all-cause mortality (HR = 1.39, 95% CI: 1.12-1.73). No statistically significant associations were found for CVD-specific mortality, potentially reflecting limited statistical power for this secondary endpoint. Limitations include potential dietary measurement error, observational design precluding causal inference, restriction to a single South African cohort, and the GDQS-minus metric's limitation in settings of food insecurity where avoiding unhealthy foods may paradoxically score high due to poverty.

Implications Medical Affairs : This study provides strong prospective evidence linking overall diet quality to all-cause mortality in a sub-Saharan African population with a high hypertension burden, reinforcing dietary intervention as a critical complementary component of cardiovascular prevention strategies in these underrepresented populations. From a Medical Affairs perspective, these findings support positioning antihypertensive and cardiovascular therapies within lifestyle modification programs tailored to sub-Saharan African populations, where dietary risk profiles and food security contexts differ substantially from Western cohorts used in guideline evidence. Engaging public health, nutrition, and hypertension KOLs in Africa for collaborative evidence generation on the interaction between dietary quality, blood pressure control, and cardiovascular outcomes could substantively inform product positioning and broader health equity messaging.

What causes colorectal cancer in the young? Insights from an age-specific attributable fraction analysis.

Revue : European Journal of Cancer Prevention | Tier : NA | IF estime : NA

Date de publication : 2026-09-17

Resultats principaux : This attributable fraction analysis estimated population attributable fractions (PAFs) for lifestyle and medical risk factors for colorectal cancer (CRC) across three age groups (30-49, 50-69, ≥70 years) in the United States in 2022, using NHANES birth-cohort prevalence data, meta-analytic relative risks, and Global Cancer Observatory incidence estimates. The combined contribution of all lifestyle and medical risk factors was greatest in early-onset CRC (30-49 years), explaining 61.4% of young-onset cases vs. 55.2% and 56.4% in older groups, largely driven by lifestyle-related exposures. In the young-onset group, the leading attributable risk factors were low-fiber intake (PAF 16.7%), tobacco smoking (16.3%), and alcohol drinking (15.6%), followed by obesity (7.8%), while hypertension and diabetes contributed smaller but age-increasing fractions. Gender-specific analyses revealed higher tobacco-attributable PAF in young men and higher obesity-related PAF in young women. Key limitations include the ecological design, reliance on cross-sectional prevalence data to estimate lifetime exposure, the inherent lag between exposure and cancer diagnosis, and inability to assess combined exposure interactions.

Implications Medical Affairs : Although primarily a colorectal cancer study, the characterization of hypertension as a documented and age-progressive contributor to young-onset CRC highlights its role as a broader cardiometabolic risk factor with multisystem oncological implications, reinforcing the urgency of early hypertension control well beyond traditional cardiovascular endpoints. From a Medical Affairs perspective, these findings support expanding the narrative around antihypertensive therapy to encompass multisystem risk reduction, strengthening value propositions directed at payers and health technology assessment bodies that evaluate broader health economic benefits of blood pressure control. Collaboration with oncology, gastroenterology, and preventive medicine KOLs to develop cross-disciplinary evidence on hypertension's role in young-onset cancer risk could build novel and compelling scientific exchange platforms.

Revue : Health Science Reports | Tier : NA | IF estime : NA

Date de publication : 2026-09-17

Resultats principaux : This institution-based cross-sectional study enrolled 283 hypertensive adults attending cardiac follow-up at two federal teaching hospitals in Addis Ababa, Ethiopia (February to August 2022), assessing stroke prevention knowledge using the 32-item Stroke Prevention Knowledge Questionnaire and preventive practice using the 52-item Health-Promoting Lifestyle Profile II (HPLP-II). Association of priori dietary patterns with metabolic syndrome and its components: a systematic review and meta-analysis.

Revue : Frontiers in Nutrition | Tier : NA | IF estime : NA

Date de publication : 2026-09-04

Resultats principaux : This systematic review and meta-analysis incorporated 39 observational studies (cross-sectional, cohort, and case-control) retrieved from Embase, Web of Science, and PubMed through March 2025, examining the association between a priori dietary patterns and metabolic syndrome (MetS) and its components, including hypertension. A higher Dietary Inflammatory Index (DII) was significantly associated with greater MetS prevalence (OR 1.33, 95% CI: 1.23-1.43, p < 0.001), while a higher Oxidative Balance Score (OBS) was robustly associated with lower MetS prevalence (OR 0.48, 95% CI: 0.41-0.58, p < 0.001). Categorical adherence to the Mediterranean diet, DASH diet, Dietary Diversity Score, and Healthy Eating Index showed no statistically significant associations with MetS prevalence. Continuous OBS and Mediterranean diet analyses confirmed significant inverse associations (OBS: OR 0.94, 95% CI: 0.92-0.96; MD: OR 0.89, 95% CI: 0.83-0.96). Limitations include significant heterogeneity across studies, predominantly cross-sectional designs limiting causal inference, variable MetS definitions used across studies, and diverse dietary assessment methodologies reducing comparability.

Implications Medical Affairs : The robust inverse association between oxidative balance and MetS prevalence, combined with the strong pro-inflammatory diet risk, provides compelling evidence that anti-inflammatory and antioxidant-rich dietary patterns meaningfully reduce metabolic syndrome burden including hypertension, strengthening the rationale for recommending lifestyle co-intervention alongside antihypertensive pharmacotherapy. The surprising absence of significant benefit from categorical Mediterranean and DASH diet adherence in this meta-analysis challenges some widely promoted dietary guidelines and could be leveraged to advocate for more individualized and nuanced patient nutrition counseling programs that emphasize inflammatory index reduction. Collaborating with KOLs in nutritional medicine, preventive cardiology, and diabetology to generate prospective intervention data on combined dietary-pharmacological approaches to MetS and hypertension control could yield a differentiated and clinically compelling real-world evidence portfolio.

Identification of novel and known variants in sarcomere and cardiomyopathy-related genes in coronary artery disease patients by Whole-exome sequencing.

Revue : Frontiers in Cardiovascular Medicine | Tier : NA | IF estime : NA

Date de publication : 2026-09-04

Resultats principaux : This whole exome sequencing (WES) study enrolled 28 patients with coronary artery disease (CAD) at a Saudi Arabian medical center, characterizing the genetic variant landscape in this population where hypertension, hyperlipidemia, obesity, and type 2 diabetes were prevalent comorbidities. A total of 13,289 variants were identified across 108 genes (median 545 variants per patient); the most frequently mutated gene was TTN, followed by GAA, SVIL, NRAP, and DMD, all related to sarcomere structural integrity and cardiomyopathy pathways. Among 58 genes with high-impact variants, 341 variants were identified including 122 novel variants (36%) not previously reported in public databases; 21 variants were classified as pathogenic (n=3) or likely pathogenic (n=18) per ACMG criteria. Eight novel likely pathogenic variants were identified in GAA, MYH6, NEXN, ATA3A, RHBDF1, and ACTA1 genes, with functional significance confirmed by gene ontology and human phenotype ontology enrichment analyses. Key limitations include the very small sample size (n = 28), single-center Saudi Arabian design, and the observational nature of WES without functional validation of identified variants.

Implications Medical Affairs : The identification of novel likely pathogenic variants in structural cardiac genes in hypertensive CAD patients advances understanding of gene-environment interactions in cardiovascular disease, with emerging implications for pharmacogenomics-guided antihypertensive and cardioprotective treatment in patients with concomitant genetic cardiomyopathy risk. From a Medical Affairs perspective, these findings underscore the growing importance of precision cardiology and the potential to stratify antihypertensive and cardiovascular therapy selection based on genomic risk profiles in the hypertensive CAD population. Association between dietary folic acid intake and diabetic retinopathy among patients with diabetes: An analysis of 2005-2008 NHANES data.

Revue : Zhong Nan Da Xue Xue Bao Yi Xue Ban | Tier : NA | IF estime : NA

Date de publication : 2026-07-28

Resultats principaux : This cross-sectional study used 2005-2008 NHANES data to investigate the association between dietary folic acid (FA) intake and diabetic retinopathy (DR) in 3,220 patients aged ≥40 years with previously diagnosed type 2 diabetes mellitus in the United States, with hypertension as an adjusted covariate and subgroup stratification variable. Multivariable logistic regression demonstrated that higher dietary FA intake was significantly associated with a lower prevalence of DR (OR = 0.394, 95% CI: 0.193-0.804, p = 0.011) after full adjustment for confounders. Smooth curve fitting revealed a predominantly linear inverse dose-response relationship with no significant non-linearity (p for non-linearity = 0.6535). Subgroup analyses stratified by sex, age, BMI, education, smoking, alcohol consumption, hypertension, and hypercholesterolemia confirmed consistent inverse associations across all strata, with no significant interaction effects (all interaction p > 0.05), suggesting the effect is robust across hypertensive and non-hypertensive diabetic patients alike. Limitations include the cross-sectional design precluding causal inference, reliance on self-reported dietary recall subject to measurement error, and potential for residual confounding by unmeasured dietary and lifestyle variables.

Implications Medical Affairs : The robust inverse association between dietary folic acid intake and diabetic retinopathy prevalence, with consistent findings across hypertensive subgroups, suggests that folate-related homocysteine reduction and oxidative stress attenuation may contribute to microvascular protection relevant to hypertension-driven end-organ damage extending beyond retinopathy to renal and cerebrovascular complications. From a Medical Affairs perspective, this study supports the emerging concept that nutritional strategies targeting homocysteine modulation complement antihypertensive pharmacotherapy in preventing hypertension-associated microvascular complications, offering a differentiated educational angle for patient support materials and health economic arguments around comprehensive risk management. Engaging endocrinology, ophthalmology, and hypertension KOLs in collaborative research evaluating folate supplementation as adjunctive intervention in the diabetic-hypertensive patient population could yield clinically meaningful real-world evidence supporting a broader and more holistic cardiovascular prevention story.
