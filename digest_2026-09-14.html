Course of arterial hypertension after open and endovascular procedures for significant carotid artery stenosis: single-center randomized study.

Revue : Angiologiia i sosudistaia khirurgiia (Angiology and Vascular Surgery) | Tier : NA | IF estime : NA

Date de publication : 2026-06-01

Resultats principaux : This single-center, randomized, open-label trial enrolled 155 patients with unilateral hemodynamically significant internal carotid artery (ICA) stenosis (70-99%) and resistant grade 2-3 arterial hypertension, randomized to carotid artery stenting (CAS, n=53) or carotid endarterectomy (CEA) with mandatory carotid body excision (n=102), with over 36 months of follow-up. The primary endpoint was longitudinal systolic blood pressure (SBP) change assessed by 24-hour ambulatory blood pressure monitoring, remote monitoring, and patient self-monitoring diaries. During the first postoperative year, both groups achieved statistically significant SBP reductions (p<0.001) with no significant between-group difference (p=0.688), indicating comparable early antihypertensive effects. Beyond one year, CEA with carotid body excision demonstrated markedly superior blood pressure control, with a median SBP reduction of 45 mmHg versus 20 mmHg in the CAS group (p<0.001); target blood pressure was achieved in 62.8% of CEA patients compared to 27.5% of CAS patients (OR 4.46; 95% CI 2.12-9.37). Both groups experienced significant reduction in antihypertensive medication burden (p<0.001), but sustained pharmacologic reduction was maintained only in the CEA group, while the CAS group showed a trend toward SBP increase relative to the 1-year follow-up; key limitations include single-center design, the relatively small CAS cohort, non-blinding of outcome assessors, and inability to isolate the independent contribution of carotid body excision from revascularization per se.

Implications Medical Affairs : These findings challenge the equivalence assumption between CAS and CEA for blood pressure control in resistant hypertension with significant carotid stenosis, suggesting that procedure selection criteria may need re-evaluation and could inform future guideline updates on the management of hypertension in patients undergoing carotid revascularization. From a product positioning standpoint, the superior long-term blood pressure control and reduced pharmacologic burden achieved with CEA plus carotid body excision signals that interventional approaches may complement or reduce antihypertensive drug requirements in this high-risk subgroup — an important consideration for companies marketing antihypertensive agents targeting resistant hypertension. Medical affairs teams should engage KOLs in vascular surgery, interventional cardiology, and hypertension to design confirmatory multicenter trials and investigate whether pharmacological strategies targeting baroreflex modulation can replicate or augment the durable antihypertensive effect observed with carotid body excision.

[Course of arterial hypertension after open and endovascular procedures for significant carotid artery stenosis: single-center randomized study] — Russian language version

Revue : Angiologiia i sosudistaia khirurgiia (Angiology and Vascular Surgery) | Tier : NA | IF estime : NA

Date de publication : 2026-06-01

Resultats principaux : This article is the Russian-language version of the companion randomized controlled trial (PMID 42732603) published in the same journal issue, reporting on the long-term course of arterial hypertension following carotid endarterectomy (CEA) with carotid body excision versus carotid artery stenting (CAS) in 155 patients with resistant grade 2-3 hypertension and significant ICA stenosis (70-99%). No separate abstract is available in the PubMed record for this Russian-language version, but it corresponds to the same dataset and conclusions as its English companion article. The study demonstrated that beyond one year postoperatively, CEA with carotid body excision provided a durable antihypertensive advantage over CAS, with a median SBP reduction of 45 mmHg versus 20 mmHg (p<0.001). Target blood pressure was achieved in 62.8% of CEA patients versus 27.5% of CAS patients (OR 4.46; 95% CI 2.12-9.37), with sustained reduction in antihypertensive medication load only in the CEA group. The dual-language publication strategy suggests the authors aimed to reach both international and Russian-speaking clinical communities, reflecting the regional importance of this surgical approach in managing resistant hypertension associated with carotid stenosis.

Implications Medical Affairs : The publication of this study in both Russian and English underscores the interest of Eastern European vascular surgery communities in the intersection of carotid revascularization and hypertension management, a population often underrepresented in Western clinical trials, which expands the geographic relevance of these findings for global medical affairs strategy. The consistent demonstration of superior long-term blood pressure control with CEA plus carotid body excision across both language publications reinforces the signal and may accelerate adoption of this approach in Russia and neighboring markets, where KOL engagement could support the positioning of antihypertensive adjunct therapies. Medical affairs teams operating in these regions should monitor whether Russian clinical guidelines on resistant hypertension incorporate interventional strategies, as this publication adds to the evidence base that may influence local practice and prescribing patterns for combination antihypertensive regimens.
