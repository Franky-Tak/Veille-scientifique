Bangladesh HEARTS: Lessons Learned from a National Hypertension Control Program, 2018-2026.

Revue : Global heart | Tier : NA | IF estime : NA

Date de publication : 2026-10-21

Resultats principaux : The Bangladesh Hypertension Control Initiative (BHCI) implemented the WHO-HEARTS package starting in 4 primary care facilities (Upazila Health Complexes, UHCs) in 2018 and scaled nationally to 320 UHCs by May 2026, covering approximately 54 million of 108 million adults (~43% national coverage). By May 2026, the program had enrolled 739,057 hypertensive patients and achieved 59% blood pressure control (defined as <140/90 mmHg based on BP measured in the prior three months among all enrolled patients). Key endpoints included BP control rate, enrollment scale, and facility coverage, all demonstrating continuous improvement over the eight-year implementation period. No formal subgroup analysis by age, sex, or comorbidity was reported, limiting understanding of differential control rates. The primary limitations include use of enrolled patients as the denominator rather than all hypertensive adults in the population, absence of a comparator group, and reliance on program data which may not fully capture patients lost to follow-up.

Implications Medical Affairs : This study provides compelling real-world evidence that the WHO-HEARTS standardized hypertension control package can be successfully deployed at national scale in a low-middle income country, achieving clinically meaningful BP control in over 700,000 patients, a benchmark relevant to pharmaceutical access strategies in LMIC markets. For Medical Affairs, the experience highlights critical success factors including standardized treatment protocols, uninterrupted medicine supply, and electronic medical records, all of which are directly relevant to product access planning and pharmacovigilance in emerging markets. Engaging with program leaders and WHO-HEARTS implementing organizations as KOLs could support positioning of antihypertensive agents within similar LMIC hypertension control programs globally.

Self-reported hypertension in adults: a cross-sectional study using VIGITEL data, southern region of Brazil, 2023.

Revue : Epidemiologia e servicos de saude : revista do Sistema Unico de Saude do Brasil | Tier : NA | IF estime : NA

Date de publication : 2026-08-28

Resultats principaux : This cross-sectional study analyzed 2,255 adults from the state capital cities of the Southern Brazilian region (Curitiba, Florianopolis, Porto Alegre) using the 2023 VIGITEL telephone surveillance survey with complex sampling design. Overall hypertension prevalence was 28.0% (95%CI 25.4-30.8%), with significant city-level variation ranging from 22.7% in Florianopolis to 32.8% in Porto Alegre. Multivariate Poisson regression identified three factors independently associated with hypertension: age >=70 years (PR 13.15; 95%CI 5.93-29.15), obesity (PR 2.01; 95%CI 1.70-2.38), and diabetes (PR 1.53; 95%CI 1.31-1.79). No subgroup analysis by sex or ethnicity was presented despite potential differential prevalence in these groups. Study limitations include reliance on self-reported diagnosis which may underestimate true prevalence, telephone survey methodology that may introduce selection bias among older and lower-income populations, and cross-sectional design that precludes causal inference.

Implications Medical Affairs : These surveillance data confirm a substantial ~28% hypertension burden in urban Southern Brazil with robust associations with obesity and diabetes, providing actionable epidemiological evidence for targeting antihypertensive treatment strategies within the growing Brazilian cardiometabolic market. The significant inter-city variation in prevalence (22.7% to 32.8%) underscores the need for geographically tailored health promotion and commercial strategies rather than uniform national approaches. Medical Affairs teams should leverage VIGITEL data and engage Brazilian epidemiologists and public health KOLs to support advocacy for expanded BP monitoring programs and combination antihypertensive and cardiometabolic therapy access.

Early-Life Predictors of Obesity and High Blood Pressure Comorbidity at Midlife: Findings From the 1958 National Child Development Study (NCDS).

Revue : Journal of obesity | Tier : NA | IF estime : NA

Date de publication : 2026

Resultats principaux : This longitudinal cohort study analyzed 9,150 participants from the 1958 National Child Development Study (NCDS) to identify early-life predictors of obesity combined with high blood pressure (BP>140/90 mmHg) as a comorbid outcome at age 44. Five pre-hypothesized childhood domains were assessed: prenatal/antenatal/neonatal/birth factors, developmental attributes and behavior, education and academic ability, socioeconomic factors, and parental/family environment. A multivariable prediction model including all domain-specific risk scores, sex, and ethnicity achieved an area under the curve of 0.70 (95%CI 0.67-0.72). Socioeconomic factors were the strongest domain predictor (OR 1.28; 95%CI 1.19-1.38), consistent with findings from the 1970 British Cohort Study (BCS70). Parental/family environment domain was not a significant predictor (OR 1.08; 95%CI 0.94-1.24), differing from BCS70 results. The principal limitations include the historical birth cohort (1958) that may not reflect contemporary socioeconomic conditions, a moderate AUC suggesting incomplete prediction, and restriction to a single UK birth cohort.

Implications Medical Affairs : These findings reinforce the developmental origins of hypertension and obesity, demonstrating that childhood socioeconomic disadvantage is a robust and reproducible predictor of midlife cardiovascular risk, which strengthens the scientific rationale for life-course prevention strategies that include antihypertensive therapy. The consistency of socioeconomic factors as the dominant predictor across two UK birth cohorts provides high-quality epidemiological evidence that Medical Affairs teams can leverage in health economics arguments for early intervention and prevention programs in socially disadvantaged populations. Engaging KOLs in developmental epidemiology and social determinants of cardiovascular health could enhance the scientific positioning of antihypertensive therapies as part of a broader life-course cardiometabolic risk reduction narrative.

Revue : Epidemiologia e servicos de saude : revista do Sistema Unico de Saude do Brasil | Tier : NA | IF estime : NA

Date de publication : 2026-07-18

Resultats principaux : This cross-sectional study used baseline data from a randomized controlled community trial of the Health Academy Program (Academia da Saude) in Belo Horizonte, Brazil, including 3,264 participants (88.0% women, 56.6% adults aged >=20 years). The prevalence of key cardiometabolic comorbidities was notable: hypertension 53.0%, dyslipidemia 44.3%, and diabetes 16.8%; nutritional status showed 41.4% overweight and 28.9% obese. Implications Medical Affairs : The high prevalence of hypertension (53%) in a community health promotion program population in Brazil highlights the substantial uncontrolled cardiometabolic burden in urban low-income Brazilians and the need for more aggressive pharmacological intervention alongside lifestyle programs. The demonstrated association between obesity and poorer self-rated health suggests that patient-reported outcomes may be influenced by weight status, which has implications for designing patient support programs that address weight management alongside antihypertensive adherence to improve overall health perception and treatment engagement. Medical Affairs teams should engage with Brazilian primary care and community health KOLs to develop integrated antihypertensive and metabolic treatment strategies for community program participants, using these data to advocate for comprehensive cardiovascular risk management in public health settings.

Building Healthy Temples With Less Pressure: A Faith-Based Hypertension Control Pilot Study.

Revue : Health promotion practice | Tier : NA | IF estime : NA

Date de publication : 2026-10-24

Resultats principaux : This pilot feasibility study evaluated Building Healthy Temples with Less Pressure (BHT-LP), a 12-session faith-based hypertension self-management intervention delivered by trained lay church leaders in two Hispanic churches in San Antonio, Texas between 2021 and 2022. Forty adults with hypertension or elevated blood pressure were recruited, of whom 29 (72.5%) completed both baseline and post-intervention assessments. Mean systolic and diastolic blood pressure decreased modestly, but the changes were not statistically significant. Aerobic physical activity increased significantly by 72 minutes per week (p<0.01), and participants with excess weight experienced significant reductions in body weight and waist circumference. Key limitations include the small sample of completers (n=29), absence of a control group, short follow-up period, and potential self-selection bias.

Implications Medical Affairs : This pilot study demonstrates the feasibility of culturally tailored, lay leader-facilitated hypertension interventions in Hispanic faith communities, an approach that could complement pharmacological treatment by enhancing self-management behaviors and lifestyle modification in a population at high cardiovascular risk and historically underserved by the healthcare system. For Medical Affairs, faith-based community partnerships offer novel channels for patient education initiatives, real-world evidence generation, and brand-neutral awareness campaigns targeting Hispanic populations with high hypertension prevalence. Revue : American journal of lifestyle medicine | Tier : NA | IF estime : NA

Date de publication : 2026-10-23

Resultats principaux : This secondary analysis of the Healthy Food First randomized clinical trial (NCT05048836) evaluated whether change in food insecurity mediated the effect of community health worker (CHW) coaching on physical activity (PA) among food-insecure adults with hypertension from 364 clinical sites in North Carolina, using a 2x2x2 factorial design. CHW assignment was not significantly associated with total PA at 12 months (ATE = -197.9 MET-min/week; 95%CI -855.6 to 500.8). Implications Medical Affairs : These null findings confirm that CHW-mediated improvements in food insecurity alone are insufficient to drive meaningful increases in physical activity in food-insecure hypertensive patients, reinforcing the irreplaceable role of pharmacological management in this complex population where lifestyle modification barriers remain substantial. For Medical Affairs, this evidence supports the positioning of antihypertensive medications as the primary intervention backbone for hypertension management in socially complex patient populations where lifestyle modification programs may have limited effectiveness as standalone strategies. Revue : Journal of the American Heart Association | Tier : NA | IF estime : NA

Date de publication : 2026-10-23

Resultats principaux : This GWAS and transcriptome-wide association study (TWAS) in the Singapore Chinese Health Study prospective cohort (n=27,308 participants) evaluated genetic associations for five blood pressure traits: hypertension status, systolic BP, diastolic BP, mean arterial pressure, and pulse pressure. Ten blood pressure loci were validated at genome-wide significance, and an East Asian-specific splice donor variant at pulse pressure was identified (rs149344559; p=6.78x10-8). Implications Medical Affairs : The identification of East Asian-specific BP genetic variants and tissue-specific regulatory pathways (kidney cortex, pituitary) represents important precision medicine groundwork relevant to drug target identification and patient stratification for antihypertensive development in the large Asian cardiovascular market. Resultats principaux : This PRISMA-compliant meta-analysis evaluated the impact of metformin on preeclampsia risk in obese pregnant women without diabetes, including 3 randomized controlled trials identified from a search across 2022 total citations. The primary analysis showed metformin did not significantly reduce preeclampsia risk (OR 0.42; 95%CI 0.10-1.68; p=0.22; I2=81%), with high heterogeneity driven by methodological differences. Subgroup analysis excluding a methodological outlier suggested potential benefit (OR 0.20; 95%CI 0.10-0.38; p<0.00001; I2=0%), though this finding is exploratory. Secondary outcomes including gestational hypertension, gestational diabetes, preterm birth, cesarean delivery, postpartum hemorrhage, and NICU admission showed no significant differences between groups. The review concluded that metformin should not be routinely recommended for preeclampsia prevention in this population. Key limitations include very few included trials (n=3), high heterogeneity in the primary analysis, insufficient statistical power for secondary outcomes, and publication bias risk.

Implications Medical Affairs : These findings confirm that metformin is currently insufficient for routine preeclampsia prevention in obese non-diabetic pregnant women, maintaining antihypertensive and antiplatelet strategies (aspirin, calcium) as the current standard pharmacological approaches for hypertensive pregnancy complication prevention and leaving a substantial unmet need for effective novel agents. Medical Affairs should monitor emerging clinical trial data on novel antihypertensive and preventive agents targeting preeclampsia, as this indication remains an area of active development with significant regulatory and clinical opportunity. Engaging maternal-fetal medicine KOLs, perinatologists, and obstetric pharmacologists in research discussions around gestational hypertension prevention could position pharmaceutical companies at the forefront of this important and underserved therapeutic area.
