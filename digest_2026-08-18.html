Characteristics of Adults Participating in Community Blood Pressure Screening Events: An Exploratory Cross-Sectional Analysis.

Revue : Journal of Health Care for the Poor and Underserved | Tier : NA | IF estimé : NA

Date de publication : 2026

Résultats principaux : This exploratory cross-sectional study enrolled 272 adults who participated in community blood pressure screening events held at health fairs in Nashville, Tennessee, between May and September 2025, with a focus on low-income and underserved populations. The study population was predominantly elderly (43.4% aged 65 years or older) and female (51.8%), and 46.3% of screenings were conducted at public housing communities. The primary endpoint was detection of possible hypertension, with 26.8% of participants meeting the clinical criteria. Significant sex-based venue differences were observed: men were more likely to attend health workshops (OR 3.59; 95% CI 1.92–6.71) and women were more likely to attend church-based screenings (OR 2.12; 95% CI 1.15–3.90). Key limitations include the cross-sectional convenience sampling design precluding causal inference, the observation that most attendees were already diagnosed with hypertension or considered themselves high-risk, and limited generalizability beyond similar community screening settings.

Implications Medical Affairs : These findings reveal that community blood pressure screening events primarily attract already-diagnosed or self-identified high-risk individuals, significantly limiting their utility for identifying undiagnosed hypertensives in underserved populations. From a Medical Affairs standpoint, venue-specific demographic patterns (churches for women, workshops for men) provide actionable segmentation for designing more targeted hypertension awareness and treatment adherence campaigns in low-income communities. This evidence supports KOL partnerships with community health organizations in underserved settings to enhance hypertension detection pipelines and optimize patient journey mapping for antihypertensive therapy initiation.

Impact of Blood Donation Frequency on the Prevalence of Cardiovascular Risk Factors Among Donors.

Revue : Global Health, Epidemiology and Genomics | Tier : NA | IF estimé : NA

Date de publication : 2026

Résultats principaux : This cross-sectional study conducted in Kenya compared cardiovascular risk factor profiles between regular and occasional voluntary blood donors, measuring blood pressure, BMI, fasting blood glucose, and total cholesterol using standard clinical criteria. Regular donors demonstrated significantly lower mean systolic BP (118 ± 12 mmHg) and diastolic BP (77 ± 8 mmHg), along with a lower mean BMI (24.5 ± 3.1 kg/m²) compared to occasional donors. Hypertension prevalence was significantly lower in regular donors (22% vs. 39%, p=0.04) as was elevated cholesterol (21% vs. 35%, p=0.04), with no significant difference in fasting glucose or diabetes rates. Multivariate logistic regression confirmed lower odds of hypertension (OR 0.52; 95% CI 0.30–0.89) and elevated cholesterol (OR 0.56; 95% CI 0.33–0.95) among regular donors. Key limitations include the cross-sectional design preventing causal inference, likely healthy donor selection bias, small and single-center sample, and limited generalizability of this Kenyan cohort to other populations.

Implications Medical Affairs : The association between regular blood donation and reduced hypertension prevalence suggests a possible cardioprotective effect potentially mediated through iron depletion and reduced oxidative stress, though causality cannot be established from this cross-sectional design. From a Medical Affairs perspective, these exploratory findings contribute to the growing evidence base on modifiable lifestyle and physiological factors influencing blood pressure, relevant for understanding the complexity of hypertension risk factor profiles in diverse global populations. KOL engagement should emphasize the critical need for prospective longitudinal studies and mechanistic research before any clinical or public health recommendation regarding blood donation as a blood pressure intervention can be considered.

Attenuated Impact of Gestational Diabetes Mellitus (GDM) With Hypertensive Disorders of Pregnancy (HDP) on Adverse Pregnancy Outcomes in Twin Versus Singleton Gestations: A Single-Center Real-World Study.

Revue : Journal of Diabetes Research | Tier : NA | IF estimé : NA

Date de publication : 2026

Résultats principaux : This retrospective single-center study at a maternal and child health hospital in Hubei Province, China enrolled 132,314 pregnant individuals who delivered at or beyond 20 weeks of gestation, categorized by the presence or absence of hypertensive disorders of pregnancy (HDP) and gestational diabetes mellitus (GDM). The incidence of HDP in twin pregnancies was 23.0% and GDM was 21.0%, both markedly higher than in singleton pregnancies; twin pregnancies overall showed higher stillbirth rates (1.79% vs. 0.67%, p<0.001). Across all subgroups, twin pregnancies were consistently associated with preterm birth, cesarean section, and anemia. Strikingly, the combined effect of concurrent HDP-GDM showed an attenuated impact on adverse outcomes in twin pregnancies, where 15 complications showed comparable rates between twins and singletons compared to only 3 in the non-HDP-GDM group, suggesting the expected synergistic risk amplification seen in singletons is blunted in twins. The study is limited by its retrospective single-center design, potential selection bias, absence of matched controls, and limited external validity outside Chinese tertiary obstetric centers.

Implications Medical Affairs : The attenuation of combined HDP-GDM risk in twin pregnancies challenges existing risk stratification models, which are largely derived from singleton data, and has direct implications for how antihypertensive treatment thresholds and monitoring protocols should be applied in multiple gestations. From a Medical Affairs perspective, this creates an opportunity to highlight an underaddressed clinical need: differentiated pharmacological guidance for HDP management specifically in twin pregnancies, a therapeutic area with significant unmet needs and few dedicated clinical trials. KOL engagement should prioritize perinatologists and obstetric cardiologists, encouraging investigator-initiated research on optimal antihypertensive regimens in multiple gestations to build evidence supporting future label expansions or specialized guidelines.

Pendrin inhibitor PDSinh-C01 reverses salt-sensitive hypertension and metabolic acidosis in the 5/6 nephrectomy rat model.

Revue : Pflugers Archiv: European Journal of Physiology | Tier : NA | IF estimé : NA

Date de publication : 2026-08-17

Résultats principaux : This preclinical study evaluated PDSinh-C01, a pharmacological inhibitor of pendrin (the chloride-bicarbonate exchanger SLC26A4), in the 5/6 nephrectomy (5/6Nx) rat model of chronic kidney disease (CKD), which replicates hallmark features including reduced eGFR, salt-sensitive hypertension, proteinuria, and hyperchloremic metabolic acidosis. Female Sprague Dawley rats were maintained on a high-salt (2% NaCl) diet and randomized (n=7–9 per group) to subcutaneous injections of vehicle, PDSinh-C01 (10 mg/kg), furosemide (6 mg/kg), or their combination for 3 days, with continuous telemetric blood pressure monitoring. The primary endpoint of mean arterial pressure (MAP) reduction was greatest in the combination group (-11 ± 2 mmHg) compared to vehicle (-3 ± 2 mmHg), furosemide alone (-6 ± 1 mmHg), and PDSinh-C01 alone (-6 ± 2 mmHg), demonstrating additive antihypertensive efficacy. Regarding metabolic acidosis, both PDSinh-C01 alone and in combination with furosemide significantly increased venous bicarbonate concentrations (24.5 ± 2.1 and 24.3 ± 1.4 mmol/L, respectively, vs. vehicle 21.4 ± 1.4 mmol/L), indicating meaningful acid-base correction. Limitations include the exclusively preclinical and female-only animal design, the short 3-day treatment window preventing evaluation of long-term efficacy and safety, and the absence of human pharmacokinetic or pharmacodynamic data for PDSinh-C01.

Implications Medical Affairs : The dual mechanism of PDSinh-C01 in simultaneously addressing salt-sensitive hypertension and metabolic acidosis in CKD represents a clinically compelling differentiation from existing diuretics, which lack meaningful acid-base correcting properties, and positions pendrin inhibition as a potentially transformative target in renal hypertension management. From a Medical Affairs standpoint, this proof-of-concept data provides a scientific rationale to engage nephrologists and cardiorenal specialists around the concept of pendrin as a therapeutic target, particularly for the sizable CKD patient population with concurrent hypertension and metabolic acidosis that remains inadequately controlled on loop diuretics alone. Investment in KOL strategy around this emerging therapeutic class should begin now, focusing on academic nephrology centers and translational research collaborations that could accelerate the path toward first-in-human studies.

Chronic psychosocial stress: A primary driver of sleep apnea onset in rats and humans.

Revue : Proceedings of the National Academy of Sciences of the United States of America | Tier : NA | IF estimé : NA

Date de publication : 2026-08-17

Résultats principaux : This translational study combined preclinical experiments in rats and Mendelian randomization (MR) analysis in humans to test the hypothesis that chronic psychosocial stress is a primary causal driver of sleep apnea onset rather than merely a consequence, with secondary analysis of associated hypertension. In adult rats, 3 weeks of social isolation (single housing) activated stress pathways, increased apnea frequency during natural sleep, and augmented ventilatory responses to O2 and CO2 challenges. In supine anesthetized male rats, obstructive apneas with oxygen desaturations were 5 times more frequent in socially isolated animals than in controls, an effect not observed in females; social isolation also induced increased blood pressure and augmented fat gain, particularly in males. In humans, MR analysis using GWAS data from European cohorts (approximately 100,000 to 1,096,458 participants) demonstrated significant causal effects of loneliness on sleep apnea risk, independent of adiposity, using genetic instruments. Key limitations include the restriction of MR analysis to European-ancestry populations limiting ethnic generalizability, sex-specific effects in animal models with the obstructive apnea findings predominantly in males, and the indirect nature of Mendelian randomization as a causal inference tool.

Implications Medical Affairs : By positioning psychosocial stress as a causal upstream driver of sleep apnea — itself a well-established contributor to hypertension — this study opens a new mechanistic pathway linking social determinants of health to cardiovascular risk, with implications for identifying high-risk hypertensive patient subgroups. From a Medical Affairs perspective, this evidence supports the concept that antihypertensive pharmacological strategies may need to be embedded within broader cardiopsychosocial management frameworks, particularly for patients with concomitant sleep apnea and social isolation risk factors. KOL engagement should prioritize sleep medicine specialists and preventive cardiologists, and this study is relevant for companies exploring combinations of cardiovascular and behavioral health interventions or those positioning antihypertensives in high-risk comorbidity populations.

Rationale, design, and methods of the dietary approaches to stop hypertension for diabetes (DASH4D) controlled feeding trial.

Revue : Contemporary Clinical Trials | Tier : NA | IF estimé : NA

Date de publication : 2026-08-17

Résultats principaux : This publication describes the rationale, design, and methodology of the DASH4D trial — a randomized, controlled, crossover controlled feeding study conducted at Johns Hopkins University evaluating the DASH4D dietary pattern (an adaptation of the DASH diet optimized for safety and efficacy in type 2 diabetes) and sodium content on blood pressure in adults with type 2 diabetes. The trial randomized participants to four 5-week feeding periods comparing the DASH4D diet with low sodium, DASH4D diet with high sodium, comparison (typical US) diet with low sodium, and comparison diet with high sodium, with calories adjusted to maintain stable weight and the primary contrast between DASH4D-low sodium and comparison-high sodium. The primary outcome was systolic blood pressure and the secondary outcome was diastolic blood pressure, with additional outcomes including glycemia markers, lipids, proteinuria, and diet acceptability measures. The study addressed significant design challenges including conducting rigorously controlled feeding in a medically complex diabetic population, maximizing dietary adherence, and implementing the protocol during the COVID-19 pandemic. As a design and methods paper, no efficacy results are reported; results are pending publication, and the study's generalizability will depend on the demographic composition of the enrolled cohort.

Implications Medical Affairs : The DASH4D trial will generate the most rigorous controlled feeding evidence to date specifically addressing the dual burden of hypertension and type 2 diabetes, a highly prevalent and cardiovascular high-risk combination for which optimal dietary strategies remain poorly defined in current guidelines. From a Medical Affairs standpoint, the trial results will be critically important for defining the residual treatment gap that pharmacological antihypertensive therapy must address in diabetic patients, which is essential for product positioning in the cardiometabolic space. This study design paper is a key reference for KOL engagement with clinical nutritionists, diabetologists, and cardiometabolic specialists, particularly when discussing the complementary role of dietary and pharmacological interventions in achieving BP targets in type 2 diabetes.

Effects of ultraviolet light exposure on blood pressure: a systematic review.

Revue : Photochemical & Photobiological Sciences | Tier : NA | IF estimé : NA

Date de publication : 2026-08-17

Résultats principaux : This systematic review conducted in accordance with PRISMA guidelines examined the available evidence on the effects of ultraviolet (UV) radiation exposure on blood pressure regulation in human populations. A total of 14 studies were included, comprising randomized controlled trials, clinical trials, and observational studies investigating UVA and/or UVB exposure effects on BP. The majority of included studies demonstrated that UV exposure, particularly UVA, was associated with reductions in blood pressure, with the proposed primary mechanism being nitric oxide (NO) mobilization from cutaneous photolabile stores, which functions independently of vitamin D synthesis pathways. Several studies reporting no significant BP effects were attributed to variability in study design, population characteristics, and exposure protocols, leading to substantial heterogeneity across the 14 included publications. Key limitations include the small total number of studies (n=14), marked methodological heterogeneity precluding formal meta-analysis, lack of standardized UV exposure protocols across studies, and absence of long-term safety data addressing carcinogenic and dermatological risks of therapeutic UV exposure.

Implications Medical Affairs : The emerging evidence for UVA-mediated NO mobilization as a blood pressure-lowering mechanism introduces a novel, non-pharmacological pathway in hypertension pathophysiology that is conceptually distinct from the vitamin D hypothesis and potentially relevant for environmental and seasonal BP variation research. From a Medical Affairs perspective, while the current evidence is insufficient to support clinical recommendations, this review is relevant for companies with assets targeting the NO-cGMP-sGC pathway (e.g., soluble guanylate cyclase stimulators or PDE5 inhibitors), as environmental photobiological factors may modulate the same pathway in hypertensive patients. KOL strategy should include cardiovascular preventive medicine researchers and photobiology experts, and Medical Affairs teams should monitor this space for larger, better-standardized trials that may eventually influence environmental health recommendations in hypertension guidelines.
